Solution to Activity 1a

Common law can be described as the set of rules that originate from social customs. As a result,
common  law  can  be  developed  through  judicial  precedent.  On  the  other  hand,  civil  law  can  be
regarded as a written codification of laws that clearly express the rights and dut ies of natural and
juristic persons.

Substantive laws are the written laws that define rights and duties, formalised by way of legislation.
For example, a substantive law would be the law prescribed by the Consumer Protection Act, which
grants a consumer with the right of returning faulty, damaged or poor quality goods to a supplier
within six months of the purchase date.

Adjective law constitutes the procedures put in place to enforce the substantive laws. For example,
if  someone  is  charged  with theft,  the  adjective  law  would  be  the  criminal  procedures in place  to
enforce  the  substantive  laws  governing  theft.  These  laws  determine  how  an  accused  would  be
charged and brought to trial, should there be sufficient evidence. Adjective law would also set out
the sanction to be imposed if the accused is found guilty.

Solution to Activity 1c

(i)

An  application  involves  a  point  or  dispute  of  law  (as  opposed  to  fact).  A  notice  of  motion  and
founding  affidavit  is  then  filed.  The  respondent  files  a  notice  of  intention  to  oppose,  and  the
respondent is afforded an opportunity to respond by way of an answering affidavit. The applicant
can then respond with a replying affidavit, and once the affidavits have been exchanged, the matter
is set down for a hearing where, after hearing arguments from both sides, the magistrate/judge will
hand down a judgement.

An action, on the other hand, involves a factual dispute that culminates in a trial and involves the
exchange of witness evidence. The plaintiff in an action commences proceedings with a summons,
to which the defendant responds by way of a notice of intention to defend, and thereafter a plea.
The plaintiff can respond by way of a replication. Once pleadings are closed, the matter will be set
down for trial. Discovery will take place and a pretrial conference can be scheduled before trial. At
trial, witness evidence may be led.

(ii)

For criminal proceedings to start, the first step is to lay a charge against the person in question.
While it is a private person (Jane) who lays the complaint against another (John), it is the state’s
responsibility to prosecute the individual accused of having committed a crime. Because the state
does  not  possess  the  resources  to  prosecute  each  complaint,  it  determines  whether  there  is
enough evidence to prosecute. This is done by the public prosecutor examining the merits of each
complaint. Once the public prosecutor has decided to prosecute the accused, the next step is to
charge, arrest  and detain the  accused  (John).  John  is  entitled to apply for ba il, such application
being  heard  within  48  hours  of  his arrest.  Provided  bail  is  granted, and  the  requisite  amount of
money has been paid, John will be released until the date of his trial.

At  trial, John pleads  to  the charge.  In  other  words,  he  states  whether  he is  guilty  or  not.  Should
John  plead  the  former,  the  magistrate/judge  hands  down  a  verdict  of  guilty,  and  arguments  for
mitigation  commence.  However,  if  John  pleads  not  guilty,  the  state  leads  evidence.  Thereafter,
John  is  provided  with  an  opportunity  to  lead  evidence.  After  hearing  both  cases,  the
magistrate/judge hands down a judgement.

If  John  is  found  not  guilty,  he  is  released  and  the  charges  are  dropped.  However,  should  the
magistrate/judge find John guilty, he is presented with the opportunity to lead evidence in mitigation.
In other words, he may present evidence that justifies a lesser sentence. In contrast, the state may
present evidence that justifies a harsher sentence.

Finally, the magistrate/judge hands down a sentence. The final step involves John seeking leave
to appeal or review, if that is his choice.

Lesedi has limited contractual capacity as she is married in community of property. The Matrimonial
Property Act provides that spouses married in community of property first require the consent of
the other in certain instances, before concluding a juristic act that binds the joint estate. However,
when the juristic act relates to a spouse’s normal course of business, occupation or trade, consent

Sarah  has  limited  contractual  capacity  as  she  is  prodigal.  This  means  that  she  requires  the
assistance or consent of an administrator or curator, in order to perform a juristic act that binds her

as  a marriage. Therefore,  Sarah’s marriage is valid  as  she  does not  require  her administrator’s
consent.

Solution to Activity 2c

Contractual capacity constitutes the ability to form a will, and the ability to act with sound judgement in
accordance with that will. Some persons have no contractual capacity at all, such as an  infans, persons
lacking mental capacity and intoxicated persons. Other persons have limited contractual capacity (e.g.
minors,  persons  married  in  community  of  property,  prodigals  and  insolvent  persons).  Other  natural
persons and juristic persons, as well as the state, have full contractual capacity.

There must be consensus. In other words, the parties must have the intention to enter into a contract with
one another, and agree on the terms of the contract. The following elements are necessary for consensus:

•  The parties must possess a true intention to create certain consequences .
•  They must agree that these consequences are juristic.
•  They must be aware that there is agreement between them.

Solution to Activity 3a

(i)

A condition is an uncertain future event, upon which either the start or the continued existence of
the contract is made dependent. A condition therefore refers to something in the future, which may
or may not happen.

A time clause refers to a certain future event. In other words, the duty to perform is either suspended
or  terminated until,  or  at  the  occurrence of,  a certain  future  event,  although it may be uncertain
when exactly the event will occur.

An  assumption  (or  supposition)  refers  to  an  event  that  has  already  occurred.  Alternatively,  an
assumption may refer to a fact or situation that was already in existence at the time of contracting.

take place. However, this is not the case in respect of an unlawful contract. Aside from any other
requirements, this particular contract is unlawful as it concerns the sale of stolen prescription drugs.

The ex turpi causa rule confirms that the courts will not come to the assistance of the parties to an
unlawful contract. The second rule that is applicable is the par delictum rule, which confirms that a
party cannot recover performance in terms of an unlawful contract, where the parties are equally
guilty of the unlawfulness.

In this case, John should have known that he was purchasing the prescription drugs illegally, as
such transactions should only take place via a pharmacy. It is therefore likely that a court will hold
that both parties were equally guilty of unlawful conduct. As a result, John cannot seek to recover
the payment that he has made to Darren.

Sam,  as  the seller  of  the  car,  is  entitled  to  receive  payment  from  Aggie.  If  he  does  not  receive
payment, the requirement of performance is absent, and there will be breach of contract. However,
Aggie, as the buyer (and debtor), has the right to receive a receipt, as it serves as prima facie proof
that she has made payment, and therefore performed in terms of the contract. If the seller (creditor)
refuses to provide a receipt, the debtor is entitled to refuse to make payment. Therefore, Sam does
not have a case against Aggie.

Solution to Activity 3d

(i)  Merger:  This  occurs  where  the  same  person becomes both  the debtor  and  creditor  of  the same
debt. For example, Judith, who is the debtor in the scenario, also becomes the creditor when she
and her fiancé marry in community of property, as their estates then become one.

(ii)

Extinctive prescription: This occurs when legal obligations terminate through the lapse of time. For
example,  more  than  three  years  have  passed  since  Andrew  borrowed  R  15  000  from  Mpho.
According to the Prescription Act, all other debts not set out in the Act prescribe after three years.
Furthermore, Andrew has not acknowledged the debt. This means that the debt will not run anew.

(iii)  Novation: This occurs when the contracting parties conclude a second contract with the intention
of terminating and substituting the original contract with the second contract. For example, Zinhle
and  Sanjay  enter  into  a  second  contract  that  states  that  Sanjay  will  pay  R  450  per  month  with
regard to the table, laptop and PVR. This terminates the first contract, which included the payment
of R 500 per month for the tablet and laptop.

(iv)  Performance:  This  occurs  where  the  contracting  parties  fulfil  their  contractual  obligations.  For
example, Thandi pays the specified amount for the water fountain. In turn, Green House Interior
delivers the water fountain to Thandi.
