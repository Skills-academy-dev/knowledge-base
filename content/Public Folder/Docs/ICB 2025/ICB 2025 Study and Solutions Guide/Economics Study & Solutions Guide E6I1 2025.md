Feedback to Integration task 1.1
(a) A strike of transport workers might mean that ordinary people have no transport to work. This
could mean that fewer goods are produced in that time, and that companies lose money. This, in
turn, could mean that companies will be unable to give their workers a good increase in the
forthcoming year. This could cause a ripple effect resulting in reduced wages and spending,
nervousness of investors and negative sentiment.
© EDGE Education (Pty) Ltd – 2025
1

Study Phase
(b) An increase in the petrol price means that companies that produce food and clothes will have to
pay more to have their products transported to the retail outlets. To avoid making a loss, they
may increase the prices of their products. Consumers would then have to pay more for food and
clothes. Taxi, bus and train fares might also increase.
(c) Until recently, Telkom has been the only provider of landline telephone services in the country.
Because we need phones and they were the only service provider, they could charge what they
liked for telephone calls. If their new competitors charge less than this, everyone may want to use
the competitors’ services and Telkom may have to lower their rates. The fact that Telkom is
getting competitors may mean that in the future people will pay less for their telephone calls.
(d) An increase in the repo-rate is usually introduced to counter rising inflation rates. When interest
rates are low, it becomes cheaper to borrow money. People therefore spend more money on
goods and services as real income levels increase, resulting in higher inflation rates. By raising
the repo-rate (the rate at which the central bank lends funds to commercial banks), the SARB is
often able to stem the tide in an inflationary environment. People borrow less, spend less and the
reduced demand causes a drop in prices (or smaller increases).
From this exercise, we begin to see how protests, an increase in competitors in the market, interest
rates and prices, and so on, affect us all. In economics we try to analyse these problems in order to
understand them better, and sometimes in order to try and suggest solutions for them. Thus, economics
is about what is happening around us. Learning more about the subject can help you to better
understand your own situation, and help you plan better for the future.

(e) Cars and fuel are complementary products. The more cars are sold, the more fuel will be sold –
at any price. There is a movement along the demand curve for cars due to the price change. We
say that the quantity demanded of cars has increased. The movement in the market for cars has
a direct impact on fuel, its complementary product. The demand curve for fuel will shift to the right
(note that there is no movement along the demand curve for fuel).
Solution to Activity 1d
(i) The following are possible causes of a rightward shift in the demand curve:
• An increase in the number of female consumers in the economy
• Consumers expecting an increase in the price of bikinis in the near future
• An increase in income of consumers of bikinis
(ii) The following are possible causes of a rightward shift in the supply curve:
• A sudden increase in the number of competitive suppliers of beef in the market
• An increase in rainfall and in the conditions for breeding of cattle
• A drop in the price of chicken
