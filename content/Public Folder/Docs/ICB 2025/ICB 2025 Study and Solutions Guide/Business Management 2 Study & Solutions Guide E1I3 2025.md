AQUA Corporation should implement a proper team and reporting structure in the company. The

Practice exercise 4b
(i) Leadership refers more to a state of mind compared to management, which refers to actions and
deeds. Management skills are something that can be taught, while leadership is something more
inherent to a person’s personality.
(ii) All managers are not necessarily leaders. The have been placed in a position where it is
expected of them to lead a team (usually because of their skills and experience) and are not
necessarily capable of influencing a team positively. All leaders are also not necessarily
managers. It could be that an employee working on the factory floor is a natural leader and that
others in the factory look to him for advice and motivation even though he is not in a management
position.
Based on the above, it is more accurate to state that some managers are leaders and some
leaders are managers.
Practice exercise 4c
(i) It is important for managers to be aware of different leadership styles, as different situations
require different styles. For example, if a manager works with a highly experienced team a more
democratic leadership style will be appropriate, while an inexperienced team will be led using a
more autocratic style where they are told exactly what to do.
(ii) It appears as though Tesco encourages democratic leadership styles where employees are
involved in the decision-making process and where they are encouraged to provide input.

1. Six Hats Method:
Colour hat Description
• The colour of caution or negativity.
Black
• It can typically be used by asking a question such as ‘What can go wrong?’
• This colour is for exclusive use by the facilitator.
Blue • It represents overview and reflection.
• Facilitators will use the colour to summarise a meeting from time to time.
• The colour of creative thinking.
Green
• It invites people to generate ideas, and is similar to brainstorming.
• The colour of intuition, emotion or feeling.
Red • It allows all people to express how they feel about a specific point or issue.
• It can be used to explore the feeling of others.
• The colour of facts.
White
• It can be used immediately after red, green, black and yellow to support opinions/statements.
• The colour of optimism.
Yellow
• It can typically be used by asking a question such as ‘What value or positive outcome could there be?’
2. Five characteristics of budgets:
• They are mostly stated in monetary terms.
• They cover a specific period.
• They contain elements of management commitment.
• They are reviewed and approved at a higher level than the level that created the budget.
• They are used to identify variances (overspending or underspending).
• They can only be changed under exceptional circumstances.

3. Principles of organising work through a structure:
• Division of work: this is an attempt to divide work logically and by function – people doing
the same type of work are grouped together.
• Reporting structure: workers are assigned to a supervisor or manager within a specific
function or speciality – accounting, manufacturing etc.
• Departmentalisation: the logical grouping of work gives rise to different business
departments.
• Coordination: through a reporting structure, it becomes possible to integrate people’s
activities into a coherent whole that serves the best interests of the business.
