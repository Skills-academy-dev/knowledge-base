Solution to Activity 1a
First-line managers:
• Maintain general discipline in a business unit or department. For example, they ensure that staff
do the work assigned to them and that staff do not disrupt the overall harmony in the workplace.
• Ensure that targets are met within a business unit or department.
• Make decisions (such as giving discounts) within specific guidelines that the first-line manager
received from their seniors.
• Advise staff on how to do things better and more quickly.
• Assign tasks to workers so that the flow of work can be optimised.
• Provide an insight to seniors regarding the job performance of workers who report to the first-line
manager.

Middle managers:
• Traditionally, middle managers receive specific instructions or goals from top managers, and mid-
dle managers have to ensure that workers and supervisors achieve those targets.
• Middle managers have a semi-executive role. They are no longer responsible for detailed work,
but they are responsible for the implementation of business strategies.
• Middle managers do not manage workers. They manage those who supervise the workers.
• Middle managers depend on the results of others (at lower levels) to achieve goals.
• Middle managers motivate and inspire others to achieve goals, but they do not necessarily control
and organise work.
• Middle managers are sometimes referred to as ‘paper pushers’ because they receive reports
from first-line managers. These reports have to be repackaged to create specific reports that top
management wants.
Top managers:
• Designing an organisation’s strategy. They must also make sure that the strategy is
communicated to:
▪ the board of directors; and
▪ the whole organisation.
• Sourcing operating capital for the organisation.
• Drafting budgets so that income and expenditures can be tracked.
• Finding the right people who can add the right value to the organisation.
• Communicating matters relating to the organisation’s performance to shareholders and other
stakeholders.
• Establishing and maintaining relationships with shareholders.
• Ensuring compliance with regulations.
• Ensuring that the organisation’s code of conduct is followed.
• Guiding the organisation through difficult times.
• Developing staff potential. For example, a worker may show all the attributes of a good leader. It
would be the responsibility of top management to train and educate that worker to fulfil a manage-
rial role.
• Developing and implementing business continuity plans.
• Top managers have the additional responsibility to ensure that the organisation remains
sustainable over a long period of time.
Solution to Activity 1b
• Plan
Planning refers to an intention to do something. It also refers to the drafting of proposals, which detail
how things need to be done. Most plans are future-oriented and describe how organisations will meet
© EDGE Education (Pty) Ltd – 2025

specific goals at specific times. Planning is important for management to stay focused and to ensure
that they know where the organisation is going.
• Decide
Deciding on something is an intention to act, and has usually been considered for some time. Typically,
decisions follow after many facts and options are considered by the decision-maker. Decision-making is
an important tool to put words into action.
• Organise and delegate
Organising refers to systematically arranging things or coordinating activities of people as efficiently as
possible in a business. In other cases, organising refers to making preparations or arrangements for
something that is going to occur at some point in the future. Organising is important to ensure that the
resources available to management is used efficiently and that responsibility can be allocated to
different individuals.
To delegate means that one entrusts a task or responsibility to someone else. In other words, the act of
delegation is an authorisation to do something on behalf of someone else. Managers normally delegate
tasks from a higher level to a lower level. Delegation is important to ensure that all tasks get done, as
one person cannot be held responsible for doing everything. It also serves as a training opportunity for
inexperienced staff.
• Lead
Leading refers to actions taken by seniors that will inspire juniors to behave in a certain way. Leading
also means that seniors are guiding others to take certain actions, which will generate results that the
seniors want; and it means being in charge of something. Lastly, leading can refer to an act to organise
and direct something (such as a meeting). Leadership is important to illustrate to employees what the
organisation stands for and how it is expected of them to behave. It also shows them the way through
times of uncertainty.
• Motivate
To motivate someone means to provide a reason for doing something. It is important for management
to motivate employees to ensure that productivity is high and that staff morale is kept at an optimum
level.
• Communicate
Communication is about the sharing of information between people. Organisations make use of
different modes of communication such as oral, written, visual and non-verbal communication.
Management should communicate properly to ensure that employees always know what is expected of
them and what is happening in the organisation.

• Control
Control refers to the power of managers to influence or direct people’s behaviour over time. It also
refers to the ability to take into account factors that could influence an organisation positively or
negatively. Finally, control refers to a manager’s ability to deal with situations successfully. Through
controls, organisations can create a sense of order and structure, which aids in the achievement of
optimum results.

Feedback to Integration task 2.1
Instead of appointing specialist managers, companies can make use of consultants to serve the same
purpose. Consultants work across a range of different industries and specialities, which means that
they can bring more experience and greater perspective to companies who have to solve specific
problems. A manager working for a specific company may be narrow-minded in his or her thoughts,
while a consultant is objective and may bring more creativity to the table.
It might also be more cost-effective for a company to focus on its core business and outsource other
activities that do not form part of their main business (such as accounting services).
Solution to Activity 2b
Managers use the immediate competitive environment to shape their businesses so that they are able
to survive amid competitor actions. Within the competitive environment, managers will:
• analyse competitors and work out how to respond to their actions;
• assess how to adjust prices and product/service offerings so that they can retain customers;
• maintain relationships with investors and creditors so that they can maintain favourable cash flow
levels;
• maintain relationships with customers;
• take actions to attract and retain employees who are essential to the success of the business; and
• maintain relationships with suppliers so that there are no disruptions in the delivery of goods or
services to customers.

Solution to Activity 5a
The most important aspect to making an information system more useful is creating structure. A folder
should be created for every project, and this folder should be saved on the central server. All files
related to the project should be saved inside this folder by creating sub-folders (e.g. contracts, work-in-
progress etc.)
A naming convention should be put in place to ensure that everyone knows what a folder or file
contains. For example, all project folders should contain the name of the project and the year in which
the project started i.e. ABC Company 20.3.
Different naming conventions could be used for files, for example ABC_Contract1_20.3. The important
thing here is that someone browsing through the files should immediately be able to see what is in a file
and there should be no duplications.
It is also very important that all information relating to a project is saved in one central location where all
relevant parties have access to it.
