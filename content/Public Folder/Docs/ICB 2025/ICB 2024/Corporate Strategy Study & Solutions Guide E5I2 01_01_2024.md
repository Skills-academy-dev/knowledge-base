Solution to Question 1c
(i) The first definition is one by Lynch (2009: 5):
‘Strategic management can be described as the identification of the purpose of the organisation
and the plans and actions to achieve that purpose’.
This definition takes a high level view by approaching strategy from the point of the purpose of
the organisation. Purpose can be captured in the following question: ‘Why do we exist and what
do we want to contribute to society? However, strategic management is also about the plans and
action steps that will help an organisation to realise their purpose.
The second definition comes from Thompson et al. (2005: 3) when they say:
‘Strategy is the game plan indicating the choices a manager needs to make, for example how to
attract and meet customer needs, how to compete successfully, how to grow the organisation,
how to manage the organisational architecture and develop dynamic capabilities, how to achieve
performance targets by implementing strategies successfully’.

Solution to Question 2c
(i) Vision: This is your dream for the business. It is meant for you and the other members of your
company, not for your customers or clients.
Mission: The reason for the business’s existence in commercial terms.
Strategic intent: This is the intention of the organisation, i.e. what the organisation wants to do in
the long term. The vision and mission express the strategic intent of an organisation.
(ii) Criteria for assessing a ‘good’ vision:
• Is it clearly formulated?
• Is it focused on the future?
• It is idealistic?
• Is it challenging?
• Will it excite people – the management, employees and other stakeholders?
• Will it unify people in putting their energy into the organisation?
(iii) Benefits of a well-formulated mission:
• An organisation achieves a heightened sense of purpose when strategists, managers, and
employees develop and communicate a clear business mission.
• A good mission statement reveals an organisation’s customers, products or services,
markets, technology, concern for survival, philosophy, self-concept, concern for public image,
and concern for employees.
• The mission statement provides direction for all planning activities.
• A good mission statement forces introspection on an organisation: ‘Why do we exist?’’, ‘What
is our business?’ and ‘What are we trying to accomplish’?
(iv) Very often the strategic vision and mission will not stand on its own but will be supported by a set
of core values. Values are an expression of ‘the way things ought to be’. Top management, in
particular, has a responsibility to proactively shape and manage a company’s values. The basic
beliefs and values of an organisation could be perpetuated – to become its guiding ‘constant’
through time. Values therefore force choices: whom a business hires, the ways they serve the
customer, how they develop talent at all levels, which businesses they create, enter and exit, and
when; how much risk taking they promote.

Solution to Question 3b
In terms of identifying the industry, the following questions can be asked:
• Which organisations have similar goals to ours?
• In what industry are these organisations currently competing?
• What are the critical success factors for this particular industry?
It is important to have a solid understanding of the structure of the industry, which depends on enduring
characteristics that give the industry its unique appeal and can be identified by examining four variables:
• Concentration – the degree to which industry sales are controlled by only a select few.
• Economies of scale – the savings attained due to increased volume.
• Product differentiation – the extent to which customers perceive goods and services offered by
organisations in an industry as different from one another.
• Barriers to entry – the difficulties that an organisation must overcome in order to enter an industry.
