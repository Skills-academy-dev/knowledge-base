Feedback on Integration task 1.1

• Royalties: Previously a common law system applied. The court decided in the case of Millan v CIR – 1928 AD that the originating cause of income from the writing of novels was the exercise of the wits, skill and labour of the author. If a book was written in South Africa the author would be taxed on any royalty income from such a book in South Africa, irrespective of where the book was published or sold. If the book was written elsewhere and sold in South Africa, the royalties received would not be taxed in South Africa, unless the author was a South African resident. With effect for years of assessment commencing on or after 1 January 2012, this common law principle will no longer apply. From this date a royalty will only be from a South African source if it is incurred by a South African resident i.e. the person paying the royalty is a South African resident or the intellectual property is used in South Africa. In all other cases s 9(4) will deem a royalty to be from a non-South African source.

• Interest on investment: In the case of CIR v Lever Brothers and Unilever Ltd (1946) it was decided that the source of interest on a loan is the granting of the credit. The granting of the credit is normally situated where the creditor’s business is situated. In terms of s 9(2)(b) which came into effect for years of assessment commencing on or after 1 January 2012, interest is deemed to be from a South African source if it is paid by a South African resident or is earned on funds invested or used in South Africa.

• Rent from the letting of fixed assets: Because it is the asset itself which gives rise to the income, the source of the income will be where the asset is situated. In the case of property, this seems logical but in the case of machinery which can be rented in South Africa and then moved to a neighboring country, as in the case of COT v British United Shoe Machinery (SA) Ltd – 1964 FC the source of the income is where the machines are used.

• Dividends from shares: Previously the source of a dividend was where the share register was located and not where the dividends are received (Boyd v CIR – 1951 AD). For years of assessment commencing on or after 1 January 2012, this common law principle will no longer apply. Section 9(2)(a) deems dividends from South African resident companies to be from a South African source. Section 9(4) deems ‘foreign dividends’ to be from a non-South African source.

• Annuities: The source of income from annuities will be in the country where the contract for such an annuity was entered into (Boyd v CIR 1951 AD).

Feedback on Integration task 1.3

The reason why taxable income often differs from accounting profit is that different amounts and items of income and deductions are taken into account in determining the two amounts. For example, certain income of a capital nature may be fully included for accounting purposes, while only a portion thereof may be included for tax purposes. On the deduction side, there may be timing differences in respect of the depreciation of capital assets or special deductions/allowances for tax purposes which will cause differences in the deductions between accounting and taxation.

Nevertheless, the determination of net profit from an accounting point of view is an important building block in the determination of the business’s taxable income. Every business must first prepare an income statement or a profit and loss account, after which certain adjustments are made for tax purposes so that the taxable income can be ascertained.
