Introduction
Understanding Personnel Management
Personnel Management is key in any workplace. It's a part of what the human resources (HR) team
and the bosses of a company do. Without people, a business can't succeed. It’s important to have
the right staff, with the right skills, in the right jobs, and at the right time. Managing people well is a
big part of a company's success and how much money it makes.
What is Personnel Management?
Personnel Management means getting, keeping, and making sure we have happy and content staff.
It’s about looking after the people at work and how they get along with each other and the company.
It's up to the managers to guide, watch over, and help staff grow. They need to manage everyone
who works under them well, so the business can work like a well-oiled machine. They are in charge
of making sure the staff are learning, doing their best, and being recognised for their work.
The main goal of a business is to make a profit. This can only happen when resources, like people,
are managed well. Human Resources departments are there to help by giving advice on the best
ways to manage staff so the business can meet its aims and do well.
Module Breakdown
This module has two parts, and both are about different parts of managing a business. In Part 1, we
talk about:
 Basic management tasks
 Additional management tasks
 Enterprise functions
Learning about these will help future HR managers work alongside other parts of the business to
make the most of the staff.
1.1 Basic management tasks
The leaders of any company have to make sure it works well and makes money. They have to
combine and manage different things like work, money, resources, and new ideas in the right
amounts. This mix is very important for any company to do well.
What is Management?
Management is when you run and look after a company or similar group.
Leaders in a company have different jobs and duties in every part of the company. What they do
depends on their job level and which part of the company they work in. They all help the company
do its best, but each team or department has different things they are responsible for.

1.1.1 Levels of management
There are three main levels of management.
1. Top Management
People at the top make big decisions for the whole company and decide on the main plans and
goals. For example, this includes the Chief Executive Officer, the director, or the chairman.
2. Middle Management
This includes all the managers between the top and the bottom. They manage the work of the man-
agers below them. For example, department heads or plant managers.
3. First-line Management
These are the managers at the beginning level. They manage the day-to-day work of people who are
not managers and make sure the products or services are made. For example, supervisors or team
leaders.
Managers have a lot of responsibilities. Doing these well is very important for the company to be
successful. The main jobs they have are planning, organising, leading, and making sure everything
is going right.

1.2 Additional management tasks
Alongside the main tasks of management, there are some extra important jobs that help a company
do really well. These extra jobs are about making choices, talking to people, encouraging your team,
getting everyone to work together, and sharing work. Let's talk about each of these tasks:
1. Decision-making
 What is it? It's like choosing the best route on a map. Managers decide on the important
stuff that needs to happen in a company.
 Why is it important? Making smart choices can help the company do well. Bad choices
might lead to losing money or even closing down.
 What should managers do? They need to think carefully and make the best decisions
for the company's future.
2. Communication
 What is it? It's all about sharing ideas and news with people at work.
 Why is it important? Good communication helps everything run smoothly. Without it,
there can be confusion and mistakes.
 How does it happen? Talking face-to-face, writing emails, or even using body language.
 What's the goal? To make sure managers and staff understand each other and work
well together.
8 | Page

3. Motivation
 What is it? It's the feeling that makes you want to do your job well, like wanting to score
a goal in football because you love the game.
 Why is it important? Being motivated helps you work towards goals and do better at
your tasks.
 How does it work? It's something inside you that pushes you to act. It's not always
about money; sometimes it's about enjoying what you do or wanting to help your family.
4. Coordination
 What is it? It's like being a conductor of an orchestra where every part must work
together.
 Why is it important? Good coordination means everyone is working together smoothly
to reach the company's goals.
 How does it work? It ties all the different parts of managing a company, like planning
and leading, into one big effort.
5. Delegation
 What is it? It's when a manager gives some of their work to other people in the team.
 Why is it important? It helps get more work done and lets other people learn new
things.
 How does it work? It's about choosing the right tasks to give to others and trusting them
to do it well.
1.3 Enterprise functions
When you have a big company, the person in charge can't do everything by themselves. They need
a team because there's a lot to look after, especially if the company is to make money.
The more people working in a company, the harder it is to manage everything. That's why big
companies split the work into different areas, each with its own team that focuses on certain jobs.
These areas are called enterprise functions.
What's an Enterprise Function?
It's a special part of a company that handles certain jobs, like making things, selling them, or keeping
track of the money.
Each area has to make sure its part of the company is working well. Smaller companies might have
just a few of these areas, while big ones have many.
The company decides which areas it needs based on what's important for the business. Some areas
are really necessary for the company to work, and others are there to help when needed.
These functions are there to make sure the company can do its best work. They're like different
pieces of a puzzle that fit together to make the whole picture.

Introduction
In this part of the module, we take a closer look at organisations and the critical role of managing
people within them. We will cover:
 The Organisation: Understanding what an organisation is and its purpose.
 Organisational Culture: How a company’s culture affects the way it works.
 Organisational Change: Managing changes within a company.
 Organisational Effectiveness: What makes an organisation perform well.
2.1 The organisation
An organisation is like a team that comes together to achieve a common goal, usually to make
money. When any part of the company or its environment changes, it affects everyone involved.
Knowing what a company is all about helps us understand how managing people helps the company
succeed.
Why Organisations Matter
Without organisations, there would not be jobs. People join companies to work towards shared goals
and to earn money. They become part of the company's story and contribute to achieving its aims.
Understanding Organisations
An organisation is a group of people who form a business to reach a goal, often to make a profit.
Changes within the company or its surroundings can impact everyone connected to it.
Comprehending what an organisation represents helps clarify the role of Human Resources in reach-
ing company goals.
Remember, without organisations aiming for specific targets, there would be no employment
opportunities. People contribute their time and skills to organisations for income, helping them
achieve their personal goals, which in turn helps the company's objectives. These people are the
employees who drive the organisation forward.
2.1.1 Organisational concepts
The key concepts in understanding organisations is explained below:
Mission and Vision
a) Mission
 Definition: A mission statement tells us what a company is here to do right now. It
explains the company's main goal and why it exists.
 Purpose: It's written clearly to show the company's immediate goals. Everyone who
works for the company should know the mission because it helps them understand their
role and how they fit into the bigger picture.
 Importance: A clear mission can help avoid confusion at work and make it easier for
everyone to get along and work together.
15 | Page

b) Vision
 Definition: A vision statement is about the company's future and its big dreams. It's what
the company is aiming for in the long run.
 Purpose: It's meant to inspire and guide everyone in the company, showing them what
they're all working towards, maybe five or ten years down the line.
 Importance: It helps everyone remember the big goal, even as people come and go in
the company. It keeps everyone focused on what's really important.
Why Mission and Vision Matter
 For the Company: They help make sure that the company knows where it is going and how to
get there.
 For the Employees: They help staff understand their jobs and how to do them well, aiming for
the company's goals.
Real-World Examples
 Mission Example: A food company might have a mission to give people tasty and
healthy food choices all day long.
 Vision Example: A brewery in South Africa might aim to be the most respected company
in the beer industry, chosen first by customers, partners, and investors.
Benefits of Having Clear Statements
 They attract, keep, and motivate skilled people.
 They create a strong company culture.
 They lead to better work and use of resources, helping the company reach its goals.

Values and Principles
a) Values
Values are like the compass that guides a company. They are the beliefs about what's right and
important for the company. These values help everyone know how to act and make decisions at
work. For example, a famous drinks company believes in leadership, working together, being honest,
taking responsibility, having passion, including everyone, and doing things well.
Why Values Matter
Values are important because they stick with the company for a long time. They tell everyone what
the company thinks is good behaviour and what it doesn't like. Values influence how all employees,
including managers, act every day. They make sure that even as things change, the company keeps
its unique identity.
Building a Values-Driven Culture
It is really important for a company to have its own clear values from the start. This helps everyone
make better decisions every day and keeps the company moving towards its goals. When everyone
understands and follows the values, it can lead to less arguing and better teamwork.
b) Principles
Principles are like the rules or the big ideas that help decide what's good or bad in a company. They
take the company's values and help turn them into real actions. Principles make sure that the way
the company works matches its values and mission.
How Principles Help
Principles are there to help companies think about how their values affect big choices. They are the
foundation of the company and give everyone a clear guide on how to build the company's future.
Principles tell us how to treat all the people who have a stake in the company (like employees,
customers, and the community) in a fair and honest way, sticking to the company's vision and
mission.
Examples in Practice
One supermarket chain in South Africa follows strong principles like keeping their core values even
when things get tough, respecting everyone, telling the difference between right and wrong, and
always sticking to their principles, even if it might seem like it will cost them.
By understanding values and principles, you will see how they shape the way a company works and
its culture. They are key to making a company a great place to work and helping it succeed.
Policies and Procedures
a) Policies:
 What They Are: Policies are the rules and guidelines that everyone in a company
follows.
 Purpose: They tell us what the company wants to achieve and how everyone should
work together to do that.
17 | Page

 Importance: Policies are important because they help keep the company running
smoothly. They make sure that all the employees know what to do and how to do it.
b) Procedures:
 What They Are: Procedures are the step-by-step instructions that explain how to carry
out the policies.
 Purpose: They give details about how to do things in the company, like how to handle a
complaint or solve a problem.
 Importance: Procedures are like a recipe for making sure things are done the right way
in a company. They help people understand exactly what steps to take.
The Difference Between Policies and Procedures
 Policies set out the aims and principles of a company. They're like a map for how to reach the
company's goals.
 Procedures are more like a GPS system, giving turn-by-turn directions so that the company's
policies are followed correctly.
Why Having Both is Important
Together, policies and procedures help make sure that a company is fair to everyone and successful
in what it does. They are based on the company's values and help make sure that the company
stays on the right path.
Examples of Common Policies:
 Code of Conduct: Rules about how employees should behave at work.
 Disciplinary Policy: What happens if someone breaks the rules.
 Leave Policy: Rules about taking time off from work.
 Recruitment Policy: How the company hires new people.
Examples of Key Procedures:
 Disciplinary Procedure: Steps for managing employee discipline.
 Grievance Procedure: How employees can raise concerns.
 Dispute Resolution Procedure: How the company solves arguments or disagreements.
By understanding policies and procedures, you'll know how a company makes sure that its work is
done well and its goals are met. They are crucial for a well-run company and happy employees.

SWOT Analysis
What is SWOT Analysis?
SWOT Analysis is a tool that helps management plan out a company's goals and activities. It stands
for Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats.
Why Conduct a SWOT Analysis?
 To get a clear picture of where the company stands.
 To make informed decisions about where the company should go next.
Components of SWOT Analysis
Importance of SWOT Analysis:
It is important for companies to know their strengths and weaknesses inside and out. They also need
to keep an eye on what's happening outside that could bring new chances to grow or new
challenges. This understanding helps the company be more effective and successful.
Real-World Example:
For instance, a SWOT analysis might show that a company has a really strong product but is not
very good at telling people about it. It might also show that there's a big new market opening up be-
cause people are shopping online more than ever.
By using SWOT Analysis, a company can make a plan that uses its strengths to make the most of
new opportunities, improve its weaknesses, and protect against threats. This helps the company
focus on the right things and stay on track for success.

Goals and Objectives
Understanding Goals and Objectives
Every organisation aims to be successful and stay ahead in the market. This can be achieved by
setting clear goals and objectives for the workplace.
Definitions:
 Goals: These are specific targets that can be seen and measured, which an employee or
company wants to hit within a certain timeframe.
 Objectives: These are the detailed aims or milestones that you are working to accomplish.
Importance of Goals and Objectives:
For a company to be on top of its game, it needs to know what it's working towards. Goals and
objectives can be for the short term or the long term and must be aligned with the company's vision,
mission, and purpose. They should also be SMART, which means:
Specific: Clear and precise.
Measurable: You can track progress.
Attainable: They can realistically be achieved.
Relevant: They matter to what the company wants to do.
Timely: There's a deadline for achieving them.
Aligning Goals and Objectives:
Everyone in a company should be moving in the same direction, and their individual goals should
match the company's overall goals. This ensures that everyone's efforts support the company's big
picture.
Feedback and Adaptation:
It's essential to review goals and see if they're being met. If not, the company might need to change
them to stay on track.
Examples:
 A company goal might be to increase coffee production by 60% by the end of the year.
 A department's goal could be to reduce machine breakdowns by 75% before the end of the

 An employee’s goal might be to up their daily production by 50% by the year's end.

Business Plans
What is a Business Plan?
A business plan is a comprehensive document that outlines how a company intends to achieve its
goals and objectives. It includes a roadmap for the organisation, incorporating marketing, financial,
and operational strategies.
Purpose of a Business Plan:
 To give a clear direction for the company's future.
 To help business owners make informed decisions and plan for potential costs and risks.
Elements of a Business Plan:
 Organisation Overview: A brief description or summary of the company.
 Vision, Mission, and Values: The core beliefs and long-term aspirations of the company.
 Products/Services: Details about what the company offers to the market.
 SWOT Analysis: An evaluation of the company's Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and
Threats.
 Goals and Objectives: Specific aims the company plans to achieve and how it intends to do
so.
 Financial Plan: An overview of current and projected financing, including needs and
strategies.
 Market Analysis: Research on market conditions, target audience, and competitive
landscape.
 Competitor Analysis: An assessment of competitors within the market.
 Revenue and Expenses Forecast: Predictions of income and expenditures over a specific
period.
Why It's Critical:
A business plan acts as the organisation's guide, helping navigate the management team through
the company's journey, avoiding potential pitfalls, and ensuring that every step taken aligns with the
company's overall purpose.
Remember, a well-thought-out business plan isn't just a formality – it's a living document that guides
the company's strategic decisions and growth. It needs to be well-planned, structured, and ready to
be adapted as the company evolves.

Strategies
What are Strategies?
 Definition: Strategies are detailed plans created to reach a specific goal.
 Purpose: They guide an organisation towards achieving its goals, objectives, and ideals by
outlining necessary changes and actions over time.
Developing Effective Strategies:
 Assessment: Understand the current state of the company and where it needs improvement.
 Direction: Clearly define where the company wants to go and what changes are needed to get
there.
 Action Plan: Strategies should result in a set of actions that aim to achieve the company's
goals and objectives, considering resources, scope, and the core business.
Implementation of Strategies:
 Action Translation: Turning strategic plans into real company actions to achieve goals.
 Responsibility Allocation: Assigning clear responsibilities to ensure effective strategy
execution.
 Continuous Review: Regularly evaluate and adjust the strategic plan to stay aligned with
goals.
The Importance of Implementation Planning:
Having a strategic plan is essential, but without an implementation plan, strategies may not lead to
desired outcomes.
Plan Execution: Ensures that the company doesn't just have good ideas but also follows through on
them to see results.
Remember, strategies are more than just ideas. They're the blueprint for action that helps a company
move from where it is now to where it wants to be. It's crucial to have both a strategic vision and a
practical way to put that vision into practice.
2.1.2 Organisational structure and design
What is Organisational Structure?
It's the hierarchy within a company that defines job roles, responsibilities, and the flow of information
between them.
Why is Organisational Structure Important?
 It ensures that a company's activities are managed efficiently.
