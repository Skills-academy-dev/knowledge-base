1.1 Introduction to the concepts of ICTs, the digital world
and digital citizenship
1.1.1 Explain the term ICT
Information and Communication Technology (ICT) is typically regarded as a broad domain focusing
on the convergence of technologies facilitating communication and the dissemination of information.
This spectrum includes but is not limited to computers, the internet, broadcasting mediums, and
telecommunication tools.
In essence, ICT is a bridge in the global village, drawing together the ends of the world closer than
ever. It’s almost as if we have sculpted a "web" that binds the entire planet, making use of this vast
array of technologies. As illustrated below, ICT is at the heart of many everyday devices and
platforms we often take for granted.
Source: Computer Pracice Office 2016
1.1.2 Differentiate between different types of ICT systems
ICT can be segmented into information, control, and communication systems.
Information systems:
These ICT frameworks are centred on data processing and the management of information. They
are commonly found at the core of sectors such as banking, healthcare, education, and public
services.
Control systems:
Control mechanisms, whether mechanical or electronic in nature, oversee and regulate the
operations of other devices or entire systems through systematic controls. Due to their ability to
boost productivity, ensure safety, and streamline operations, they are integral in fields like chemical
production, mining, and pharmaceutical manufacturing.
7 | Page

ICT Communication systems:
 Traditional telecommunication pathways, for instance, landline telephones and mobile
communication networks, exemplify these.
 Internet-based platforms cater to both auditory and visual communication, spanning from radio
and television broadcasts to Subscription Video On Demand (SVOD) platforms, such as
Netflix, Showmax, Apple TV, and Amazon Prime Video.
 Progressive modes of communication, like video conferencing, represent the next frontier in
this category.
Advanced Communication:
ICT communication infrastructures play a pivotal role in transmitting data from one endpoint to
another, handling tasks from storage and retrieval to broadcasting information. They are the
backbone that enables businesses, institutions, and individuals to interact through a myriad of ICT
devices, typically connected via intricate networks.
1.1.3 Discuss examples of computer usage and applications as party of
society
Computers have become a fundamental cornerstone in the lives of families, communities, and
businesses alike. As technological innovations advance, the manner in which we operate and
interact has transformed. From streamlining business processes to enhancing academic research,
the role of computers, underpinned by ICT, has been pivotal. They not only facilitate speedy and
efficient operations but also play a significant role in increasing both efficacy and productivity in
various sectors.
Cloud computing
Cloud computing represents a method of offering diverse services via the internet. This includes a
variety of tools and applications like data storage solutions, server access, networking capabilities,
and a broad range of software.
In essence, cloud computing capitalises on shared digital resources instead of relying on direct local
server connectivity. A straightforward way to visualise this is understanding cloud computing as a
service - imagine "borrowing" digital services and then returning them, all happening remotely.
Regardless of whether one is accessing these potent systems via a humble desktop at home or a
sophisticated terminal, the actual computational work is executed by computers stationed globally.
Social media
Gone are the days when community was purely a physical concept. In this digital age, platforms of
social media have rejuvenated the essence of community. Devices, be it PCs, laptops, or tablets,
act as gateways, enabling individuals to share, connect, and narrate their life stories to a global
audience.
The surge in social media's popularity is deeply rooted in human desire: the innate need to connect,
to be acknowledged, and to interact. Computers, complemented by intricate networks, are the
enablers of these rich, diverse, and expansive digital conversations and communities.

company premises.

1.2 Explain what a personal computer is and how it is
used
1.2.1 Describe the concept personal computer in terms of hardware,
software, memory, storage
The realm of computing has transformed the modern world, with the personal computer (PC)
standing as a testament to this revolution. But what makes a PC tick? Let's delve into the core
components that drive these technological wonders.
A PC is best understood as an electronic apparatus that:
1. Takes in electronic information via various input means.
2. Processes the incoming information using a predetermined set of coded instructions.
3. Outputs the processed results for display or further operations.
4. Alternatively, the system might convert the processed information into a tangible format, like
printing business summaries, timetables, or analytical documents.
Source: Computer Practice Office 2016
Components of a computer
While from the outside, a computer might seem like a singular entity, its functionality relies on a
harmony of intricate components. Breaking down a microcomputer offers insight into its sophisticated
makeup.
12 | Page

The makeup of a microcomputer is categorised as follows:
1. Hardware:
 Motherboard: The main board housing all essential computer components.
 CPU: The brain of the computer, governing logic and instructions.
2. Memory:
 RAM: Temporary storage actively used during operations.
 ROM: Permanent storage containing critical system information.
3. Peripherals:
 Hard disk drives: Storing substantial amounts of data.
 CD-ROM drive: Reading optical storage media.
 Keyboard & Screen: Primary input and output devices, respectively.
 Printer: Producing physical copies of electronic documents.
4. Software:
 Programs: Individual applications tailored for specific tasks.
 Operating System: Governing the interaction between hardware and user.
 Application Programs: Tailored tools for tasks such as word processing or graphic de-
sign.
 Language Systems: Enables the creation of new software and applications.
5. Storage Media:
 Hard disks: Long-term, high-capacity data storage.
 USB flash drive/CD-R/DVD: Removable storage mediums.
 Data cartridges: Compact storage for specific uses.
The intricate dance between the CPU, ROM, and RAM, along with auxiliary components, takes place
on the motherboard. This central hub ensures that all parts of the PC work in tandem, creating the
cohesive experience we associate with personal computers.
The term 'hardware' in computing encompasses the tangible parts of a computer. This includes
components designed for data input, data processing, data storage, and data output. The specific
hardware a computer possesses often depends on the software and applications it's intended to run.

How a computer functions
It's fascinating to think that our daily interactions with computers boil down to a series of systematic
steps. Let's break down this intricate dance.
The operational cycle of a microcomputer can be delineated into the following phases:
1. INPUT: Data introduction, commonly through devices like a keyboard.
2. PROCESSING: The realm of the CPU, where input data is interpreted and acted upon.
3. STORAGE: Preservation of data in mediums like flash drives or hard disks.
4. OUTPUT: Presenting processed data, whether on a screen, through a speaker, or on paper via
a printer.
One of the standout qualities of computers is their ability to perform these tasks at breathtaking
speeds.
Computers can seamlessly handle different data types, be it numeric, graphic, or textual. This
adaptability shines in various realms, from scientific research and technical projects, which often
require a blend of numeric and graphic processing, to everyday tasks like word processing, which
can integrate both textual and visual elements.
Note to Remember:
Computers are incredibly efficient, but they strictly adhere to the principle of 'garbage in, garbage
out' (GIGO). It means a computer will process exactly what it receives. Therefore, if the input data is
flawed, the output will mirror those inaccuracies.

1.2.2 Differentiate between system software vs application software
In the vast ocean of software, two categories stand prominent: system software and application
software. Let's set sail and understand their distinct roles in our digital voyage.
Software
At its core, software is essentially a collection of organised instructions, also known as programmes,
which guide the computer's hardware to perform desired tasks. These programmes streamline the
complex task of data processing, enabling our devices to run smoothly and efficiently. Typically, soft-
ware resides within the computer's storage, ready to spring into action.
Application software
Picture this: You've just powered up your computer. Beyond the essential functions that make the
machine run, what do you wish to do next? Perhaps pen a novel or create a spreadsheet? This is
where application software enters the scene.
Application software refers to those dedicated programmes designed for specific tasks that cater to
the end-user's needs. Examples include:
 Word processing tools: to craft and format documents.
 Graphics applications: for visual creativity and design.
 Multimedia platforms: to engage with videos, music, and other media.
System software
Before you dive into drafting that novel or piecing together a graphic design, your computer needs a
foundation—a set of basic instructions to function. Welcome to the world of system software.
This category of software serves as the backbone for the computer's operations. It manages and
controls the hardware components, ensuring they harmonise to provide a conducive environment for
other software to run. A familiar example of system software is the operating system (OS), the
master programme that orchestrates the computer's functions.
Distinguishing Features
To draw a clear line of distinction:
 System software: Primarily focuses on regulating the hardware and creating a user-friendly
interface. It operates in the background, unnoticed but indispensable.
 Application software: Offers a vast suite of tools tailored for particular tasks, from document
editing and graphic designing to audio editing. Examples include programmes like Microsoft
Word for word processing and tools like Sony Audio Master for refining soundtracks.
Systems software
System software acts as the bedrock, providing a framework for a computer to utilise its resources
effectively. This includes the likes of operating systems, utility programmes, and user interfaces. It's
the silent force that springs to life the moment you power up a computer, ensuring all components
harmoniously interact. In essence, it lays the groundwork for application software to function
seamlessly, painting a cohesive picture of the digital world.
15 | Page

Operating system software versus application software
The operating system, often termed the OS, is the bridge between the user and the computer's
hardware. It not only facilitates user-hardware communication but also oversees the computer's
operations, ensuring applications run smoothly. Moreover, it's the custodian of the files on your hard
disk, providing an organised storage system. Without an OS, a computer's hardware remains just a
collection of components, much like a ship without its captain. It's the OS that gives life to these
components, allowing them to work in harmony.
Application software
Application software is tailor-made to meet specific user needs. These programmes, whether they
come pre-installed or are later added, ensure users can accomplish their objectives on a computer.
From word processors and media players to specialised design tools, they cater to varied demands,
enriching our computing experience.

1.2.4 Describe general model of a computer in relation to the information
processing cycle: input, processing, output, storage as well as
communication
Information functions as the backbone of the digital realm. When we interact with computers, we are
essentially engaging in a dance of data transformation. The original raw data undergoes a
transformation into meaningful information through a series of systematic steps. It's this processed
data, now termed as information, which is comprehended and acted upon by the user or another
device. It's imperative to understand this flow to comprehend how computers aid in decision-making.
Four phases of the information processing cycle
Source: Computer Practice Office 2016
The harmony between these stages is pivotal for effective computer operations.

A computer's core functions can be likened to a well-coordinated orchestra, encompassing input,
processing, and output. Delve a bit deeper, and you'll discern these components:
 Input: This involves feeding raw data into the system. Examples include pressing keys on a
keyboard or clicking a mouse.
 Processing: The computer's central processing unit (CPU) serves as its brain. It interprets the
data using intricate algorithms.
 Output: Post-processing, the computer presents the information in a digestible format, be it
text on a monitor or a printed document.
 Storage: Computers come equipped with memory spaces like RAM (temporary storage) and
hard drives (long-term storage) where data can be securely kept.
Peripherals
When discussing computers, we often overlook the importance of external devices termed
peripherals. These essential components facilitate the input, output, and storage processes. For
instance:
 Input Devices: Keyboards and mice allow us to provide raw data to the computer.
 Output Devices: Monitors showcase the results while printers materialise the digital data onto
paper.
 Storage Devices: Hard drives or USBs ensure our information remains intact for subsequent
access.
Source: Computer Practice Office 2016
To encapsulate, while the core computer is the brain of the operation, peripherals function as the
limbs, enabling a seamless flow of the information processing cycle.

Keyboard
The keyboard acts as a vital interface allowing users to interact directly with the computer. It's the
primary tool for most when conversing with a machine.
Mouse
Serving as a navigation tool, the mouse lets users pinpoint and interact with on-screen elements.
Think of it as a digital hand that moves the cursor, making it easier to navigate various interfaces,
from operating systems like Windows to various software applications.
Distinguishing Mouse Types: Optical and Mechanical
There are predominantly two categories of mice:
 Optical mice: These leverage electronic sensors for detection and are relatively smoother in
movement. Bid farewell to the old rolling ball with this technology.
 Mechanical mice: This variety has a tangible ball that rolls, ensuring cursor movement. It
demands periodic cleaning to ensure consistent operation.
Diverse Mouse Variants
 Electronic mice: Connect these directly to the computer, and you're set. Generally, they come
with two or three buttons, transmitting signals to perform a variety of tasks. The days when a
mouse had just a single function are long gone.
 Mouse functionalities: Scroll, right-click, left-click - these are just a few of the many
operations possible with modern mice. The adaptability and diversity are vast, catering to
various user requirements.
