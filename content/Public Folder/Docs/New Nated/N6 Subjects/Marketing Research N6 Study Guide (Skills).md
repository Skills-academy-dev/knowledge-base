Unit 1: Mission and Aims of a Marketing Research Enterprise

Marketing research does not operate independently from the organisation. It must support the

company’s purpose, goals, and long-term direction.

● 1.1 Mission statement

A mission statement defines the organisation’s purpose and overall direction. Marketing

research must support this purpose by ensuring that all research activities contribute to

achieving the company’s vision and strategic intent.

● 1.2 Organisational goals

Organisational goals describe what the company aims to achieve, such as increased

profitability, market share, or customer satisfaction. Marketing research provides the

information needed to measure performance and guide improvements toward these

goals.

● 1.3 Research objectives

Research objectives are specific statements that outline what a research project intends

to accomplish. They guide the study and ensure that data collection remains focused

and relevant to management’s needs.

● 1.4 Alignment with business strategy

For research to be effective, it must align with the organisation’s strategic plans. Proper

alignment ensures that findings support decision-making and strengthen the company’s

competitive position.

Read pages 10—15 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 2: Definition of Marketing Research

This Unit explains what marketing research is and outlines its essential characteristics.

● 2.1 Systematic and objective process

Marketing research follows a structured and organised process to ensure accuracy. It

must remain objective, meaning that conclusions are based on facts and data rather

than personal opinions.

● 2.2 Key elements of marketing research

The key elements include identifying a problem, collecting relevant data, analysing the

information, interpreting results, and presenting findings. Each element contributes to

producing reliable and useful information.

Read pages 15—20 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 3: Marketing Research and Decision Making

Effective decisions require reliable information. This unit explains how research supports

managerial decision-making.

● 3.1 Strategic and tactical decisions

Strategic decisions focus on long-term organisational direction, while tactical decisions

deal with short-term operational matters. Marketing research provides information to

support both types of decisions.

● 3.2 Controllable vs uncontrollable variables

Businesses operate within environments influenced by controllable variables, such as

price and promotion, and uncontrollable variables, such as economic conditions.

Research helps managers understand and respond to both.

Unit 4: Application of Marketing Research

Marketing research can be applied in different situations depending on the organisation’s needs.

● 4.1 Problem identification research

Problem identification research focuses on recognising issues or opportunities in the

performance.

● 4.2 Problem-solving research

Once a problem has been identified, problem-solving research is conducted to find

appropriate solutions. It evaluates alternatives and supports corrective action.

Unit 2: Research Designs

Research design is the overall plan used to conduct the study. The type of design depends on

the research problem.

● 2.1 Exploratory research

Used when the problem is unclear and more understanding is needed.

● 2.2 Descriptive research

Describes characteristics of a market or group and answers who, what, when, and where

questions.

● 2.3 Causal research

Examines cause-and-effect relationships between variables.

● 2.4 Predictive research

Forecasts future trends or outcomes to assist planning.

Read pages 70—74 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 3: Potential Sources of Error in Research Design

Research may contain errors that affect results. Identifying these errors improves accuracy.

● Problem definition error: Occurs when the problem is not clearly defined.

● Sampling error: Happens when the sample does not represent the population properly.

● Measurement error: Caused by unclear questions or poor research tools.

● Response error: Occurs when respondents give incorrect or dishonest answers.

● Researcher bias: Happens when personal opinions influence the research process or

results.
