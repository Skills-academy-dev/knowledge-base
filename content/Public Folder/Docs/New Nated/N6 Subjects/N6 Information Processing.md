SYLLABUS: INFORMATION PROCESSING N6
1. GENERAL AIMS
1.1 To improve the keying in and processing skills of the competent student in
such a way that he/she will become the personal assistant and the right
hand of his/her employer.
1.2 To polish and hone the acquired skills of the student to a level of near
excellence which an employer would pursue in his/her search of the perfect
personal assistant.
1.3 To instil a sound understanding of the importance of neat and accurate
keyed in documentation by students for the business world and the
important contribution each student can make to the smooth operation of an
office.
2. SPECIFIC AIMS
2.1 Typing Technique
2.1.1 To develop keyboarding skills in students in order to prepare them to
attain keying in excellence.
2.1.2 To develop accuracy and speed in students in preparation for production
of documents.
2.1.3 To develop a speed of 50 to 55 w.p.m.
2.1.4 To enable students to study and analyse problem situations, i.e.
complicated tabular work or financial statements which should be
completed according to certain methods of work.
2.1.5 To encourage students to follow a systematic and sensible approach to
deal with challenging assignments: the acquisition of skills and the
development of brain, vision, listening and finger co-ordination; good
judgement and perception of display work, proofreading, spelling;
systematic methods of work; the improvement of standards to establish
insight and principles, e.g. advanced tabular work on one landscape
paper.
2.1.6 To enable students to acquire and master the necessary techniques, skills
and efficiency so that challenging work is produced at a high speed.
2.1.7 To prepare the students for tasks related to a personal assistant – the
know-how, skills, knowledge and techniques required for more advanced
work than the content prescribed on the N5 level.
2.1.8 To develop and promote in students a logical way of thinking and the
ability to transfer basic principles in new situations instead of relying on
preconceived ideas.

2.1.9 To contribute to the development in students of characteristics such as
self-discipline, concentration, loyalty and the ability to work independently.
2.1.10 To promote the students' perception and comprehension of typing and
word processing and their relationship to other instructional offerings, e.g.
Communication, Financial Accounting, Office Practice, etc.
2.2 Word Processing
2.2.1 To further improve the knowledge and skills acquired by students at the
N4 level and to enable them to use these functionally, at an increased
tempo and at a more advanced level.
2.2.2 To provide students with a much higher proficiency level in the production
of typing assignment at a pre-set processing speed, which is far higher
than that required on the N4 or N5 level.
2.2.3 To enable students to use the computer independently by teaching them
to use the word processing program and also to apply this knowledge
when using other programs.
3. EQUIPMENT AND APPLICATION PROGRAMS
Educational institutions presenting this instructional offering must be in
possession of the following:

3.1.1 Computers
 One computer/work station (in the case of a network) per student,

(Desk must be of the correct height and adjustable chairs are strongly
advised).
 A sufficient number of printers or a network printer with the capacity to
handle the printing of the entire group when the computer room is fully

 A suitable application program according to the needs of the module

 At least one competent IT technician on each campus offering the
subject.

4. PREREQUISITE
A student must have passed Information Processing N5.
5. DURATION
This instructional offering is offered full- or part-time over one semester of
17 weeks (7 hours per week). The time allocation for the two sections is
as follows: Typing Technique 4 hours and Word Processing 3 hours.
6. EVALUATION
6.1 Internal Continuous Assessment (ICASS)
Evaluation is conducted continuously by means of assignments, tests and
an internal examination. An ICASS mark of at least 40% as well as 80%
class attendance as per DHET Punctuality and Attendance policy to qualify
to write the national examination.
6.2 Examinations
 Typing Technique and Word Processing is combined in one question
paper and it is marked externally.
 The examination consists of practical applications.
 Students will not fail merely because they could not complete or pass
the timed accuracy test.
 The mark for Typing Technique (200) is added to the Word Processing
mark (100) to obtain a mark out of 300 which is processed to a mark
out of 100 (percentage).
6.3 Pass requirements
To pass this instructional offering, a candidate must obtain a final mark of
40% when the ICASS mark and the examination mark are added together.
A minimum examination mark of 40% is required to pass the instructional
offering. The ICASS mark and the examination mark will be calculated
together in a ratio of 40:60 to be resulted.

timed accuracy test) is determined by the duration of the question paper

and proofreading signs, a rebate of 15% of the total required number of

for this section is therefore: 110 (minutes) x 12 (w.p.m.) x 5 (average
strokes per word) = 6 600 strokes minus 15% rebate = 5 610 strokes.

5 (average strokes per word) = 4 500 strokes minus 15% rebate = 3 825

8.4 General Information
8.4.1 The practical components have as their purpose the preparation of
students for industry in general, and for typing and word processing tasks
in particular. The students must be able to enter at the level for which this
training has prepared them and be productive from the outset. It is
therefore essential that students in this subject must obtain ample work
related learning on the computer.
8.4.2 Lecturers will be required to maintain close contact with industry in order
to remain conversant with latest developments.
8.4.3 The word processing assignments can be similar to the usual typing
assignments students have to do, and must be aimed at reflecting work
related simulation. To ensure that the training is industry related as
possible, the work produced by students must be presentable to industry,
the ability to proofread all work and correct errors quickly and accurately
and the efficient use of all corrective methods must be emphasised
throughout.
8.4.4 The spell checker function must be used in the typing technique and word
processing modules.

9. MODULES: TYPING TECHNIQUE
9.1 MODULE 1: ACCURACY AND SPEED DEVELOPMENT
General aim
On completion of this module the student should be able to key in the timed
accuracy test accurately and meet the minimum required speed of 50 w.p.m. using
a word processing program according to typing technique rules.
LEARNING OUTCOMES
LEARNING CONTENT
The student must be able to:
1. Accuracy and speed drills  Achieve a minimum speed of 50 w.p.m. with
a high degree of accuracy in daily keyboard
mastering exercises as well as accuracy
and speed development drills.
2. Timed accuracy test  Achieve a minimum speed of 50 w.p.m. with
a degree of accuracy of no more than 26
typing errors in timed accuracy test for a
duration of 10 minutes.
DIDACTICAL DIRECTIVES
1. Note that according to typing technique rules a usable document must be
produced at the first attempt. This requires that all instructions must be
carried out by the student at the highest degree of speed and accuracy.
Students however are allowed to correct their errors by utilising the spell
checker. This didactic guideline applies to all the modules for typing
technique and word processing.
2. Special attention should be given to correct sitting posture, height of seat,
correct fingering and the rules for touch typing. Reasons for this should be
given in order to motivate students to improve their typing skills.
3. The software programs used for keyboard development e.g. any typing
tutor may be used to teach the keyboard on the computer as well as any
other typing methods. Regular timed daily drill exercises MUST be done by
using a timing device. There are other teaching aids that can be used to
improve rhythm.
4. During the accuracy and speed drill a timing device must be used and the
drill times should vary – 15 seconds, 20 seconds, 45 seconds and one
minute. Concentrate more on the 15 to 30 second drills.
5. Speed drills of 1 minute, 2 minutes and 3 minutes should be given for
practising purposes.
6. A timed accuracy test of 10 minutes must be given once a week.
