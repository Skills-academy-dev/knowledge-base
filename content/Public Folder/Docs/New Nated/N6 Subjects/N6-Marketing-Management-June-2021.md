Derived demand:
The demand for industrial goods is derived from the demand for consumer goods. For
example, animal hides are in demand because consumers need to buy shoes, wallets,
and other leather goods. If the demand for consumer goods slackens, so will the demand
for all industrial goods used in their production. For this reason, industries must closely
monitor the buying patterns of the ultimate consumers.
For example, the demand for steel and cement does not exist by itself. It exists because
of the demand for houses. Because of a huge demand for flats in the mid-90s, the
construction industry boomed. Consequently, the real-estate industry in general and the
construction industry in particular have to be monitored to understand the demand for steel
and cement.
Concerning capital goods such as the equipment and machinery required to produce other
goods, purchases are made not only for cement requirements but also in anticipation of
profits from future usage. Therefore, when businessmen foresee a recession in the near
future, they curtail their purchases drastically.

Advertisement:
During a recession or when a drop in consumer demand is evident, industrial firms reduce
either their inventories or their production or both. On the other hand, during a period of
prosperity, an increase in the production and sales of consumer goods results in an
increased demand for industrial goods. This is the right time for price increases or building
stock thanks to a ready availability and shorter delivery periods.
An industrial marketing firm must monitor its customers, purchases, finances, quality
standards, and so on closely to detect changes in customer demand.

| Marketers should plan for every scenario, not only for  |     |     |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- |
the most likely one
The year 2020 is behind us. Marketers have set their eyes firmly on the year
ahead and are currently devising their marketing plans and drafting their
strategy documents for 2021. Most of them will plan for only one scenario:
business as usual.  

Planning for business as usual makes sense. It is, after all, impossible to
predict the future. So it wouldn't make sense to base one’s marketing plan on
an event that may or may not occur.
Consider the following:
– The future is neither pre-determined nor predictable.
– If it were, there would be no point in taking precautions today because it

would have no effect on the future.

– Full information about the future is never available.

Because the future cannot be predicted, you shouldn't plan for only one
scenario (businesses as usual). Rather have several strategies in place to
deal with any possible scenario that may cause a crisis.
'Don't wait till you're in a crisis to come up with a crisis plan.' – Dr Phil
