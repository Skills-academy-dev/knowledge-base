Introduction
In a rapidly changing workplace, equipping employees with the right skills and knowledge is
essential for organisational success. Competency-based training has emerged as a modern and
effective approach to workforce development. Unlike traditional training methods, which often
emphasise attendance and completion, competency-based training focuses on the ability to perform
specific tasks or demonstrate particular skills under defined conditions.
This module introduces you to the principles of competency-based training and its advantages over
traditional norm-based approaches. You will explore what it means to be competent, learn about the
modular structure of this training method, and understand its emphasis on achieving clear,
measurable outcomes. By the end of this module, you will have a strong foundation in the concepts
and characteristics of competency-based training, as well as the ability to compare it with more
traditional training systems.
1.1 Describing Competency-Based Training
Competency-based training is an approach designed to meet the practical needs of individuals and
organisations. Its main goal is to develop skills and abilities that ensure learners are fully prepared
to perform specific tasks effectively. This section explores the foundational concepts of competency,
the modular structure of competency-based training, and its key characteristics.
1.1.1 What is competency?
Competency refers to the combination of knowledge, skills, and attitudes that enables a person to
perform tasks successfully under specific conditions. It goes beyond theoretical understanding and
focuses on the ability to apply what one knows in practical settings. Competency is an essential
foundation for achieving measurable and consistent performance standards in the workplace.
The Sequence from Knowledge to Effective Task Performance
The development of competency follows a logical sequence:
1. Acquisition of Knowledge: Understanding theories, facts, and processes.
2. Development of Skills: Applying the knowledge practically to perform specific tasks.
3. Cultivation of Attitudes: Adopting the right mindset to approach tasks ethically and
responsibly.

The sequence from knowledge to effective task performance
This sequence ensures that individuals are not only technically capable but also equipped to handle
real-world challenges effectively.
The Three Key Components of Competency
1. Essential Skills Required
Essential skills are the practical and interpersonal abilities required to perform tasks effectively.
They can be broadly categorised as:
• Technical Skills: Specific, job-related skills such as operating machinery, using software, or
performing specialised procedures.
• Soft Skills: Interpersonal and behavioural skills such as teamwork, communication,
problem-solving, and adaptability.
Examples:
• Graphic Designer: Must know how to use design software (technical skill) and collaborate
effectively with clients (soft skill).
• Healthcare Worker: Needs to administer medication accurately (technical skill) while
showing empathy towards patients (soft skill).
10 | Page

2. Standards of Performance
Performance standards serve as measurable benchmarks to evaluate whether a learner has
achieved competency. These standards are essential for ensuring consistency and quality in task
performance. They typically include:
• Quantity: The amount of work completed.
• Quality: How well the work meets predefined expectations.
• Time: The speed at which tasks are performed.
• Cost Effectiveness: Performing tasks within budgetary and resource constraints.
Examples:
• Call Centre Employee: Expected to handle 20 calls per hour while maintaining a customer
satisfaction score of 90%.
• Construction Worker: Required to complete a structure within specific time limits and meet
safety and quality standards.
How Standards are Measured
• Quantity: Measures how many tasks are completed. Example: A salesperson must sell 10
products daily.
• Quality: Ensures work meets the required standard. Example: An accountant must submit
error-free financial reports.
• Time: Evaluates how efficiently a task is completed. Example: A chef must prepare a dish
within 30 minutes during a busy service.
3. Conditions for Assessment
Competency is assessed under specific conditions that mimic real-world scenarios. This ensures
that learners can demonstrate their skills effectively in relevant and practical contexts.
Key Conditions:
• The environment should reflect actual workplace challenges.
• Assessments should be task-specific and realistic.
• Evaluators should observe performance under controlled conditions.
Examples:
• Lifeguard: Demonstrates a water rescue in a simulated emergency within a set time frame.
• Plumber: Repairs a leaking pipe under conditions simulating an actual household setup

1.1.2 Competency-based modular training
Competency-based modular training is a structured learning approach designed to break down
education into smaller, focused units called modules. Each module concentrates on a single
competency or skill, allowing learners to develop mastery in a systematic and measurable way. This
method ensures that learners can progress only after demonstrating proficiency in one
Key Features of Competency-Based Modular Training
1. Customised Learning: Each learner progresses at their own pace based on their specific
needs and abilities. This flexibility allows learners to spend additional time on areas where
they need improvement.
• Example: In a language course, a student may spend extra time practising basic
grammar before advancing to modules on conversational fluency.
Benefits:
• Promotes individualised learning paths.
• Accommodates varying levels of prior knowledge and skill.
Customised Learning Path
2. Clear Progression: Learners advance only after demonstrating competence in the current
module. This ensures they build a solid foundation of knowledge and skills before tackling
more advanced topics.
• Example: In a driving course, a learner must pass a parking skills test before
progressing to highway driving modules.

Benefits:

• Builds learner confidence as they achieve milestones.
3. Practical Focus: Modules prioritise hands-on, real-world applications of knowledge over

• Example: In a culinary programme, a module might require learners to prepare a

• Improves job readiness.

Steps in Competency-Based Modular Training
Competency-based modular training follows a structured process to ensure learners achieve
mastery in their desired skills.
Step 1: Identify Competencies
Determine the specific skills, knowledge, and attitudes required for the role or task.
• Purpose: To clearly define the expected outcomes for each module.
• Example: For a customer service training programme, the competencies might include conflict
resolution, effective communication, and time management.
• Output: A list of core competencies required for success.
Step 2: Design Modules
Develop focused units, each addressing a single competency. Modules are structured to facilitate
step-by-step learning and assessment.
• Purpose: To create manageable learning chunks that align with the identified competencies.
• Example: A nursing training programme might include separate modules for patient
communication, administering medication, and emergency response.
• Output: A modular curriculum with clear learning objectives for each unit
Step 3: Deliver Training
Provide learners with instruction, opportunities for practice, and the necessary support to master
each module.
• Purpose: To equip learners with the tools they need to succeed.

• Example: A trainer might conduct interactive workshops, provide real-life scenarios, or use

• Output: Learners receive consistent and targeted training aligned with module goals.
Step 4: Assess Competence

the next module.
• Purpose: To confirm mastery of each competency and identify areas for improvement.
• Example: A learner in an IT training programme may be required to troubleshoot and fix a

• Output: Competent learners advance to the next module, while others receive additional

1.1.3 Characteristics of competency-based training
Competency-based training (CBT) focuses on measurable outcomes and learner-centric
approaches to equip individuals with the knowledge, skills, and attitudes needed for practical
application in real-world settings. By adapting to the needs of individual learners and following
systematic training methods, CBT ensures a structured and effective learning experience.
1. Customised Learning
Competency-based training is highly adaptable to the pace and needs of individual learners. By
recognising that learners have diverse backgrounds, experiences, and learning styles, trainers tailor
their content and delivery methods.
Description:
• Training is customised to suit individual learners' pace, cultural backgrounds, and preferred
learning styles.
16 | Page

• Different learning styles—Visual, Auditory, and Tactile (VAT)—are considered.
Learning Styles:
• Visual Learners: Prefer seeing information through charts, videos, or demonstrations.
• Auditory Learners: Retain information best when it is spoken or explained verbally.
• Tactile Learners: Learn best through hands-on experiences and physical interaction with
tasks.
Example:
A visual learner may be given detailed diagrams for understanding equipment operation, while a
tactile learner is encouraged to practise using the equipment directly.
Benefits:
• Accommodates diverse learner needs.
• Enhances retention and understanding of content.
2. Regular Feedback
Feedback plays a vital role in competency-based training. It helps learners identify their strengths
and areas for improvement and ensures continuous progress.
Description:
• Feedback is provided frequently throughout the learning process, during and after tasks or
assessments.
• Trainers encourage informal and formal feedback to maintain motivation and correct errors.
How Feedback Works:
• Trainers use activities like “What if” discussions to help learners reflect on tasks.
• Feedback includes highlighting strengths and areas requiring further practice.
Example:
After a simulated sales pitch, a trainer provides constructive feedback on communication style,
confidence, and persuasiveness.
Benefits:
• Encourages self-improvement and accountability.
• Strengthens the trainer-learner relationship, fostering trust and support.
17 | Page

3. Exit Criteria Focus
In CBT, progression is based on mastery of specific outcomes rather than time spent in the
programme. Learners advance only after meeting defined performance standards.
Description:
• Entry requirements are less critical compared to achieving defined outcomes.
• Emphasis is placed on mastering tasks required in professional roles.
Example:
To complete a presentation skills module, learners must successfully create and deliver a
presentation using tools like Microsoft PowerPoint.
Benefits:
• Guarantees that learners are job-ready.
• Prevents learners from progressing without mastering essential skills.
4. Systematic Programme Design
CBT programmes are organised step-by-step to ensure logical progression. Each stage builds on
the last, creating a cohesive and structured learning experience.
Description:
Programmes follow six main steps:
1. Identify training needs.
2. Set training objectives.
3. Develop the training programme.
4. Create training materials.
5. Conduct the training.
6. Evaluate results.
Example:
A customer service training programme begins with modules on greeting customers, followed by
modules on resolving complaints and ensuring satisfaction.
Benefits:
• Reduces learner confusion with clear progression.
• Ensures consistent outcomes across different learners.

1.2 Competency-Based Modular Training vs Traditional
Norm-Based Training
Training methods play a vital role in developing a skilled and competent workforce. Two popular
approaches are competency-based modular training and traditional norm-based training. This
section compares their key features, benefits, and limitations.
A. Competency-Based Modular Training
Competency-based modular training focuses on developing specific skills and abilities in smaller,
manageable units called modules. Learners progress by demonstrating mastery in each module
before advancing.
20 | Page

Key Features:
1. Skill Mastery Focus: Training is centred around acquiring practical skills relevant to the job.
2. Learner-Centric: Progression is based on individual learners demonstrating competency,
allowing for personalised learning paths.
3. Practical Assessments: Continuous assessments focus on real-world applications.
4. Modular Structure: Content is broken into focused units, enabling learners to master one
competency at a time.
Example:
In a nursing programme:
• Module 1: Effective communication with patients.
• Module 2: Administering medication safely.
• Module 3: Responding to emergencies.
Learners cannot advance to Module 2 until they have demonstrated competency in patient
communication.
B. Traditional Norm-Based Training
Norm-based training evaluates learners by comparing their performance to an average or "norm"
within a group. It is typically based on time-bound courses and theoretical assessments.
Key Features:
1. Completion Focus: Learners progress based on time spent rather than skill mastery.
2. Fixed Curriculum: The same content and timeline apply to all learners.
3. Periodic Assessments: Assessments are often theoretical and conducted at set intervals.
4. Ranking System: Performance is compared to peers, with learners ranked as below average,
average, or above average.
Example:
In a traditional business management course, all learners are assessed at the end of a semester
through a theoretical examination, regardless of whether they have mastered practical applications.

Strengths and Weaknesses of Each Approach
Competency-Based Training
Strengths:
• Ensures learners are job-ready.
• Focuses on practical and measurable skills.
• Provides flexibility and personalisation.
Weaknesses:
• Requires more resources and time for personalised training.
• May need skilled trainers to provide continuous feedback and assessments.
Norm-Based Training
Strengths:
• Easier to implement across large groups.
• Provides a standardised measure for comparison.
Weaknesses:
• Often lacks practical focus.
• Progression does not guarantee skill mastery.

Example: Application in a Workplace Context
Competency-Based Training in IT:
A training programme for web developers includes separate modules on HTML, CSS, JavaScript,
and debugging. Learners must demonstrate proficiency in one skill (e.g., writing clean HTML code)
before advancing to the next.
Norm-Based Training in IT:
A standard course on web development includes all topics taught simultaneously over six months,
with a final exam determining overall competence, regardless of practical application.
Competency-Based Training prioritises the individual learner by focusing on measurable skills and
practical application, making it an ideal choice for preparing learners for real-world job
requirements. Norm-Based Training, while more traditional, offers a standardised approach suitable
for theoretical learning. By understanding these differences, organisations can select the method
best suited to their training objectives and workforce needs.
