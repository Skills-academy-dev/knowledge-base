Introduction

Marketing research is the foundation of effective marketing decision-making. Organisations

operate in competitive and uncertain environments where managers must make strategic and

tactical decisions. Without reliable information, these decisions may lead to financial loss or

reduced competitiveness.

This module introduces the fundamental principles of marketing research. You will learn what

marketing research is, how it supports decision-making, and why it is essential in modern

organisations. These concepts are frequently examined in both Section A and Section B of the

N6 examination and form the foundation for all subsequent modules.

Unit 1: Mission and Aims of a Marketing Research Enterprise

Before any marketing research project is conducted, the organisation must clearly understand

its purpose and direction. Marketing research does not operate independently from the

organisation. It supports the broader goals and strategy of the business.

In this unit, you will examine the mission statement, organisational goals, research objectives

and the importance of aligning research with business strategy. These concepts are frequently

tested in the N6 examination, particularly where you are required to define, explain, discuss, or

distinguish between related terms.

1.1 Mission statement

Every organisation exists for a specific reason. This reason is formally expressed in its mission

statement. The mission statement provides overall direction and influences all major decisions,

including marketing research activities.

Quick Definition

Mission statement: A written declaration that describes the purpose, values and long-term

objectives of an organisation.

10

A mission statement:

● Explains why the organisation exists

● Identifies the target market

● Reflects the organisation’s values

● Guides long-term strategy

Marketing research must support the mission of the organisation. If research activities do not

align with the mission, resources may be wasted and decisions may not contribute to long-term

success.

Exam focus

In the N6 exam, you may be asked to:

● Define a mission statement

● Explain the importance of a mission statement

● Discuss how research activities support the mission

When answering, always link the mission statement to organisational direction and

decision-making.

Practical example

Marileze owns a cosmetic company in Cape Town. Her mission is to provide high-quality, locally

produced skincare products. When she conducts marketing research, she focuses on

understanding customer perceptions of quality and natural ingredients. She does not research

unrelated markets, such as cleaning products. This shows alignment between research and

mission.

1.2 Organisational goals

While the mission statement describes the overall purpose, organisational goals translate that

purpose into measurable targets. Goals provide direction and allow management to evaluate

performance.

11

Quick Definition

Organisational goals: Specific measurable targets that an organisation aims to achieve in

order to fulfil its mission.

Examples of organisational goals include:

● Increasing sales by 10%

● Expanding into new provinces

● Improving customer satisfaction ratings

● Increasing market share

Marketing research supports these goals by providing information that helps management make

informed decisions.

For example, if the goal is to increase market share, research can identify:

● Competitor strengths

● Customer preferences

● New market opportunities

Practical example

Jason owns a small electronics store in Bloemfontein. His goal is to increase repeat customers.

Through research, he discovers that customers prefer extended warranties. He introduces

improved after-sales service to support his organisational goal.

1.3 Research objectives

Research objectives are more specific than organisational goals. They focus on the exact

information that must be collected during a research project.

12

Quick Definition

Research objectives: Clear and specific statements describing what a research project aims

to achieve.

Research objectives must:

● Be directly related to the research problem

● Be clear and measurable

● Support organisational goals

● Guide data collection

For example, if sales are declining, research objectives might be:

● To determine whether price increases have affected demand

● To assess customer satisfaction levels

● To identify reasons for switching to competitors

Marketing research must align with the organisation’s overall business strategy. Strategy refers

to the long-term plan for achieving competitive advantage.

If research is not aligned with strategy:

● Decisions may not support long-term growth

● Information gathered may be irrelevant

Marketing research supports business strategy by:

● Identifying market opportunities

● Analysing competitor behaviour

● Monitoring customer trends

● Reducing uncertainty in strategic decisions

Mohammed owns a furniture manufacturing company in Cape Town. His strategy is to focus on

premium-quality furniture. When conducting marketing research, he investigates customer

perceptions of quality and willingness to pay higher prices. This ensures that research supports

strategic positioning.

Unit 2: Definition of Marketing Research

Before you can apply marketing research in practice, you must clearly understand what it

means. The definition of marketing research is frequently examined in the N6 examination,

especially in Section A where students are required to define key concepts, and in Section B

where they must discuss or explain the importance of marketing research.

In this unit, you will examine the formal definition of marketing research, understand why it is

described as systematic and objective, and analyse the key elements contained in the definition.

15

2.1 Systematic and objective process

Marketing research is not based on guessing or personal opinion. It follows a structured

procedure and must remain unbiased.

Examination-ready definition

Marketing research: The systematic and objective identification, collection, analysis and

dissemination of information to assist management in decision-making related to marketing

problems and opportunities.

When answering a “define marketing research” question, it is important to include the words

systematic, objective, and decision-making.

Meaning of systematic

The term systematic means that marketing research follows a planned and organised process.

It involves clearly defined steps such as:

● Identifying the problem

● Collecting data

● Analysing information

● Reporting findings

Research must be carefully structured to ensure reliable results.

Quick Definition

Systematic: Conducted according to a structured and organised process.

For example, Rajesh owns a clothing store in Durban. Sales have declined. Instead of guessing

the cause, he follows a structured research process to identify the problem, collect customer

feedback and analyse competitor activity. This demonstrates systematic research.

16

Meaning of objective

The term objective means that marketing research must be free from bias. The researcher

must report findings honestly, even if the results are unfavourable.

If research is not objective, decisions may be based on incorrect information.

For example, Mohammed conducts a customer survey but only asks satisfied customers to

participate. This would produce biased results and would not be objective.

Did You Know?

Many research projects fail because the research was influenced by management

expectations rather than factual findings.

2.2 Key elements of marketing research

The definition of marketing research contains several important elements. These elements are

often tested in short questions where students must list or explain them.

The key elements are:

● Identification

● Collection

● Analysis

● Dissemination

● Decision support

Each element is explained below.

1\. Identification of information

The first step is to identify the marketing problem or opportunity. If the problem is not clearly

defined, the research will not provide useful information.

17

Quick Definition

Problem identification: The process of clearly defining the marketing issue that requires

investigation.

For example, Zanele notices that sales of her skincare product have decreased. She must first

identify whether the problem relates to price, competition or customer preferences.

2\. Collection of data

Once the problem is identified, relevant data must be collected. Data may be:

● Primary data (newly collected)

● Secondary data (existing information)

Quick Definitions

Primary data: New data collected specifically for the research problem.

Secondary data: Existing data that was originally collected for another purpose.

For example, Lebo collects customer survey responses (primary data) and reviews past sales

records (secondary data).

3\. Analysis of data

After data collection, the information must be organised and interpreted. Analysis allows

management to identify patterns and relationships.

Without proper analysis, raw data has little value.

For example, Jason analyses monthly sales data to identify seasonal trends in customer

purchases.

18

4\. Dissemination of findings

The results must be communicated clearly to management. This is usually done through a

written report or presentation.

If findings are not properly communicated, they cannot assist in decision-making.

5\. Assistance in decision-making

The ultimate purpose of marketing research is to support management decisions. Research

reduces uncertainty and improves the quality of decisions.

Quick Definition

Decision support: The use of research findings to assist management in selecting the best

course of action.

For example, Mohammed uses research findings to decide whether to reduce prices or improve

product quality.

Marketing research is important because it:

● Reduces uncertainty

● Identifies opportunities

● Supports strategic planning

● Improves customer satisfaction

● Strengthens competitive advantage

Without marketing research, managers may rely on assumptions rather than evidence.

Unit 3: Marketing Research and Decision Making

Managers are required to make decisions daily. Some decisions affect the long-term direction of

the organisation, while others relate to short-term operations. In all cases, effective decisions

require reliable information. Marketing research provides this information and reduces the risk

associated with business decisions.

In this unit, you will examine how marketing research supports strategic and tactical decisions,

the difference between controllable and uncontrollable variables, and how research reduces

uncertainty. These topics are frequently examined in both short and long N6 questions.

20

3.1 Strategic and tactical decisions

Organisations operate at two main decision-making levels: strategic and tactical. Marketing

research plays an important role at both levels.

Strategic decisions

Strategic decisions determine the long-term direction of the organisation. These decisions

usually involve large investments and high levels of risk.

Examples of strategic decisions include:

● Entering a new market

● Launching a new product line

● Repositioning a brand

● Expanding into another province

Marketing research supports strategic decisions by:

● Assessing market potential

● Analysing competitor strengths

● Identifying customer needs

● Evaluating long-term demand

Practical example

Rajesh owns a chain of clothing stores in Johannesburg. He is considering expanding into

Namibia. Before making this strategic decision, he conducts marketing research to assess

market size, income levels and competitor presence.

21

Tip

In an exam, you may be asked to discuss how marketing research supports strategic

decisions. Always link research findings to risk reduction and informed planning.

Tactical decisions

Tactical decisions are short-term decisions that support strategic objectives. These decisions

are usually operational and less risky.

Quick Definition

Tactical decisions: Short-term decisions made to implement strategic plans.

Examples include:

● Running a promotional campaign

● Offering price discounts

● Changing packaging design

● Selecting advertising media

Marketing research assists tactical decisions by evaluating:

● Promotional effectiveness

● Price sensitivity

● Customer response

● Distribution efficiency

22

Practical example

Lebo introduces a weekend discount promotion at her supermarket in Pretoria. She uses

previous sales data and customer feedback to determine which products to discount. This is a

tactical decision supported by marketing research.

3.2 Controllable vs uncontrollable variables

When making decisions, managers must consider internal and external factors. Marketing

research helps managers understand both.

Controllable variables

Controllable variables are factors that management can directly influence. These are commonly

known as the marketing mix or the 4Ps.

Quick Definition

Controllable variables: Marketing factors that management can directly control or change.

23

The main controllable variables are:

● Product

● Price

● Place (distribution)

● Promotion

Marketing research provides information that helps management adjust these variables

effectively.

For example, Mohammed manufactures furniture in Cape Town. Through research, he learns

that customers prefer modern designs. He adjusts his product design accordingly.

Uncontrollable variables

Uncontrollable variables are external environmental factors that management cannot directly

Quick Definition

Uncontrollable variables: External factors that influence business performance but cannot

be directly controlled by management.

Examples include:

● Economic conditions (inflation, unemployment)

● Political and legal regulations

● Technological developments

● Social and cultural trends

● Competitor actions

Marketing research helps organisations monitor these variables and adapt accordingly.

Practical example

Zanele operates a cosmetic company in Durban. Due to rising inflation, customers are spending

less. Although she cannot control inflation, she uses marketing research to identify affordable

3.3 Role in reducing uncertainty

Business decisions always involve risk because managers rarely have complete information.

Marketing research reduces uncertainty by providing reliable data.

Quick Definition

Uncertainty: A situation where management lacks complete or reliable information about a

decision.

25

Marketing research reduces uncertainty by:

● Identifying market trends

● Measuring customer satisfaction

● Evaluating competitor activities

● Testing new products before launch

It is important to understand that marketing research does not eliminate risk completely.

However, it reduces the level of uncertainty and improves the quality of decisions.

Practical example

Jason plans to introduce a new range of gaming consoles in his electronics store in

Bloemfontein. Instead of ordering large quantities immediately, he conducts a customer survey

to measure demand. This reduces the risk of overstocking.

Did You Know?

Many business failures occur because decisions were made without reliable market

information.

Importance of marketing research in decision-making

Marketing research:

● Supports evidence-based decisions

● Improves strategic planning

● Assists in adapting to environmental changes

● Enhances competitive advantage

● Reduces financial risk

Unit 4: Application of Marketing Research

Marketing research is conducted to solve real business problems. It is not an academic

exercise, but a practical tool used by managers to identify issues and develop solutions. In

practice, marketing research is applied in two main areas: identifying problems and solving

problems.

In this unit, you will learn the difference between problem identification research and

problem-solving research. This distinction is frequently tested in the N6 examination, particularly

where students are required to define, explain or distinguish between these two types of

research.

4.1 Problem identification research

Problem identification research is conducted to detect existing or potential problems in the

market before they become serious.

27

Quick Definition

Problem identification research: Research undertaken to identify existing or potential

marketing problems and opportunities.

This type of research focuses on identifying symptoms and warning signs, such as:

● Declining sales

● Decreasing market share

● Negative brand image

● Changing customer preferences

● Increased competition

Problem identification research answers questions such as:

● Why are sales decreasing?

● Why are customers switching brands?

● Is customer satisfaction declining?

● Are competitors gaining market share?

It allows management to recognise problems early and take corrective action.

Exam focus

In exam questions, you may be asked to:

● Define problem identification research

● Discuss its importance

● Provide practical examples

Always explain that this type of research focuses on identifying problems before solutions are

developed.

28

Practical example

Mohammed owns a small furniture business in Cape Town. Over the past six months, his sales

have steadily declined. He is unsure whether the problem is pricing, product design or increased

competition. He conducts research to analyse customer satisfaction and competitor prices. This

is an example of problem identification research.
