Unit 1: Mark Schedule for Timed Accuracy Tests
Timed accuracy tests are an essential part of your typing practice in Information Processing
N6. These tests are designed to measure both your speed (words per minute) and accuracy
(the number of errors made while typing).
The goal is not only to type faster but also to type correctly and neatly, just as you would in a
real business environment.
In the National Certificate (N6) examination, students are required to complete a 10-minute
timed accuracy test with a speed target of 50–55 words per minute and no more than 26
errors. This unit will guide you through how the test is conducted, how marks are calculated,
and how to improve your performance through regular practice.
11

Definitions
Accuracy: The degree to which your typing is free from errors such as spelling, spacing, or
punctuation mistakes.
Speed (WPM): The number of words typed per minute, calculated using a standard word
length of five keystrokes (including spaces and punctuation).
Timed Accuracy Test: A formal typing test conducted over a fixed period (usually 10
minutes) to assess typing skill.
Error Deduction: The process of subtracting marks for each mistake made during a typing
test.
1.1 Understanding the timed accuracy test
The Timed Accuracy Test evaluates two main areas:
● Typing Speed (Words per Minute)
● Typing Accuracy (Error Count)
The test typically involves typing a pre-prepared passage of text in Microsoft Word, using
correct margins, font, and spacing. You must type continuously for 10 minutes, without stopping
to correct errors during the test.
Test Conditions
● Time allowed: 10 minutes
● Paper size: A4 Portrait
● Margins: 2.5 cm (1 inch) all around
● Line spacing: 1.5 or double
● Font: Courier New, size 12 pt
● Spell checker: Disabled during the test (to measure manual accuracy)
12

Tip
Practise with the same layout and font settings used in the exam to feel comfortable with
spacing and line flow.
1.2 Marking criteria and calculation of words per minute
After the test, your examiner will calculate your typing speed and accuracy using the official
DHET Marking Schedule.
Step-by-Step WPM Calculation
1. Count the total number of characters typed, including spaces and punctuation.
2. Divide by 5 to convert characters into standard words.
Total Characters ÷ 5 = Total Words
3. Divide total words by 10 (minutes typed) to get words per minute (WPM).
Total Words ÷ 10 = WPM
Example:
If you typed 2 250 characters in 10 minutes:
2250 ÷ 5 = 450 words
450 ÷ 10 = 45 words per minute (WPM)
Your gross speed would be 45 WPM.

Unit 2: Improving Speed and Accuracy – Touch-Typing Skills
Touch-typing is the foundation of professional keyboarding. It refers to typing without looking
at the keyboard, using the correct fingers for each key. This skill allows you to focus on your
work rather than searching for letters, resulting in greater speed, accuracy, and consistency.
In this unit, you will learn how to improve your typing technique by using correct posture,
maintaining a steady rhythm, and avoiding common typing errors. These skills will help you
achieve the N6 goal of typing 50–55 words per minute with high accuracy, which is essential
for both your examination and future office work.
2.1 The importance of correct posture and hand placement
Good typing posture and correct hand placement form the foundation for professional typing
performance. Proper technique prevents fatigue, reduces errors, and supports long-term
comfort when typing for extended periods.
Step-by-Step Guide: Correct Posture
1. Sit upright with your back straight and shoulders relaxed.
2. Keep both feet flat on the floor, slightly apart.
3. Position your chair height so your elbows are bent at a 90-degree angle.
4. Sit at a comfortable distance from the keyboard — about 25–30 cm.
5. Keep wrists level, not resting heavily on the keyboard or desk.
Definition
Home Row Keys: The middle row of the keyboard where fingers rest — A, S, D, F, J,
K, L, and ;

Tip
Keep your fingers curved and always return them to the home row after each key press.
This helps maintain speed and reduces hand strain.
2.2 Maintaining a steady rhythm
Typing rhythm means maintaining a consistent pace and equal spacing between words and
letters. The goal is to type smoothly, not to rush.
A good rhythm allows you to type faster with fewer mistakes, just like a steady beat in music.
20

Steps to Build Typing Rhythm
1. Start Slowly: Begin at a manageable speed to prioritise accuracy.
2. Maintain Even Pressure: Press all keys with the same light touch — no banging.
3. Use a Metronome or Timer: Practise typing to a rhythm or use a timer to monitor
consistency.
4. Avoid Backspacing: During practice, do not stop to correct mistakes. Review them
only after the exercise.
5. Increase Speed Gradually: Raise your speed by 5 WPM once you can maintain 95%
accuracy.
Did You Know?
Professional typists maintain rhythm by keeping both hands balanced — each hand performs
an almost equal number of keystrokes per line!

Unit 3: The Importance of Using the Spell Check Function
Spelling accuracy is a key part of professional document presentation. Even one spelling
mistake in a business letter or report can create a negative impression and reduce credibility.
The spell check function in Microsoft Word is a built-in tool that helps you detect and correct
spelling, grammar, and typing errors before printing or submitting a document.
In this unit, you will learn how to use the spell checker effectively, when to trust its suggestions,
and why manual proofreading is still necessary.
These skills will help you improve your accuracy in both Section A (Typing Technique) and
Section B (Word Processing) of the N6 examination.
3.1 Using automatic spelling and grammar tools
The spelling and grammar check feature in Microsoft Word automatically scans your
document for errors and underlines them for review.
How to Use the Spell Checker
Step-by-Step: Running a Spelling and Grammar Check
1. Open your document in Microsoft Word.
2. Go to the Review tab on the Ribbon.
3. Click on Spelling & Grammar.
4. The Editor Pane (or dialogue box) appears, showing each detected error.
5. Choose one of the following options:
○ Change – Corrects the error using Word’s suggested word.
○ Change All – Corrects all similar errors in the document.
○ Ignore – Skips that word (use this for proper nouns or abbreviations).
○ Add to Dictionary – Saves a word so Word recognises it in the future.

3.3 Manual proofreading
Even though Microsoft Word can detect most spelling and grammar mistakes, it is not perfect.
Some errors are context-based — meaning a word is correctly spelt but used incorrectly.
For example, typing “The meeting will take place their” instead of “there” will not always be
caught by automatic spell check.
Steps for Manual Proofreading
1. Read through your entire document carefully after spell check.
2. Check names, figures, and dates — the spell checker may skip them.
3. Verify punctuation such as full stops, commas, and spacing.
4. Ensure consistent capitalisation, especially in headings and titles.
5. Read your document aloud or line by line — this helps catch missed errors.
Tip
Always run a final manual check before printing or submitting your work — especially in
professional documents like CVs, letters, or invoices.
Practical Example
Scenario:
Mohammed is preparing a report for his supervisor. After using the spell checker, he notices that
some names (like “Thabo” and “Lebo”) are marked as errors. Instead of changing them, he
selects Add to Dictionary to prevent future flagging. He then proofreads the document
manually and corrects spacing errors that the spell checker missed.

Unit 4: Punctuation Spacing
Punctuation and spacing are small details that make a big difference in document presentation
and readability. In professional typing, even one extra space or misplaced punctuation mark can
make a document look untidy or incorrect.
This unit will teach you how to apply correct spacing after punctuation marks, maintain
consistent letter and word spacing, and avoid common layout mistakes. Mastering these
simple rules will improve your accuracy, neatness, and marks in the N6 Information
Processing examination.
4.1 Correct spacing after punctuation marks
Correct spacing ensures your work looks clean and easy to read. Microsoft Word automatically
adjusts spacing between words and sentences, but typists must still know the rules for
professional document layout.
Table: Spacing Rules
Punctuation Mark Spacing Rule
Full Stop (.) One space after a full stop.
Comma (,) One space after a comma.
Colon (:) One space after a colon.
Semicolon (;) One space after a semicolon.
Question Mark (?) One space after a question mark.
Exclamation Mark (!) One space after an exclamation mark.
Quotation Marks (“ ”) One space after closing quotation marks.
29

Tip
Use only one space after punctuation marks. The old rule of using two spaces after a full
stop applies to typewriters, not modern word processors.
Steps to Apply Spacing in Microsoft Word
1. Open your document and select the text you wish to check.
2. On the Home tab, click Show/Hide to display spacing and paragraph marks.
3. Review for double spaces between sentences and remove extras manually.
4. Press Ctrl + H to open the Find and Replace dialog box.
○ In Find what: type two spaces.
○ In Replace with: type one space.
○ Click Replace All to correct the entire document.

4.2 Rules for letter and word spacing
Letter and word spacing affect how neatly your document appears. Consistency helps improve
legibility and professional appearance.
Key Rules for Spacing
1. Use one space between words.
○ Incorrect: Please check the figures.
○ Correct: Please check the figures.
2. Do not insert spaces before punctuation marks.
○ Incorrect: Thank you , Lebo.
○ Correct: Thank you, Lebo.
3. Avoid uneven spacing in justified text.
○ Use left alignment for short letters or memos to prevent large gaps between
words.
4. Keep spacing consistent in tables or lists.
○ Use the Tab key instead of multiple spaces to align text neatly.
Definition
Justification: A formatting option in Microsoft Word that aligns text evenly along both
the left and right margins.
