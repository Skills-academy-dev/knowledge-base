teams communicate and how organisations achieve their goals. From hiring and training staff to
managing performance, maintaining workplace fairness and supporting employee wellbeing —
every aspect of personnel management contributes to a productive and positive work
environment. In simple terms, Personnel Management is the study of how to organise, lead and
support employees so that both the organisation and its people can succeed.

● Understand your role in the workplace – By studying Personnel Management, you

Introduction
Motivation is one of the most important concepts in personnel management because it directly
influences how employees behave, perform, and respond to their work. An organisation cannot
achieve its goals unless employees are willing to put in the right amount of effort and maintain
positive attitudes. Understanding motivation helps managers create conditions that encourage
employees to work willingly and effectively.
According to the Personnel Management N6 textbook, employees are motivated by a
combination of internal factors, such as personal satisfaction, and external factors, such as
recognition or rewards. Managers must therefore understand that people differ in their needs,
expectations and experiences, and these differences affect how they respond to different forms
of motivation.
Motivation is closely linked to job content, working conditions, communication, and leadership.
When motivation is high, employees show commitment, creativity and responsibility. When
motivation is low, organisations experience low productivity, conflict, absenteeism and poor
performance — challenges reflected in past N6 exam scenarios.

Unit 1: Understanding Employee Motivation
This unit introduces the meaning of motivation, how the motivation cycle works, and why
motivated employees are essential for organisational success. It builds the foundation for the
theories and motivation methods covered later in the module.
Figure 2: Employee Motivation

1.1 Meaning and Purpose of Motivation
Motivation refers to the internal and external forces that influence people to behave in certain
ways at work. It includes a person’s desires, needs, actions, and reasons for choosing a
particular behaviour. According to the N6 marking guideline, motivation is what causes a person
to act and repeat certain behaviours.
10

Purpose of Motivation in Organisations
● Encourages employees to perform tasks willingly.
● Helps employees achieve organisational and personal goals.
● Builds positive work attitudes, responsibility and commitment.
● Reduces negative behaviour such as absenteeism, conflict and poor performance.
T I P
Motivation differs among individuals. Managers should never assume that the same strategy
will work for everyone.
1.2 The Motivation Cycle and How It Works
The motivation cycle explains how a person moves from having a need to taking action to
satisfy that need. It includes the following stages:
1. Need or Drive – The employee feels a desire or lack (e.g., need for recognition).
2. Tension – The need creates internal pressure.
3. Action or Behaviour – The employee chooses an action to satisfy the need (e.g.,
working harder).
4. Goal Achievement – The need is satisfied if the behaviour is successful.
5. Feedback – The employee assesses whether the behaviour helped to meet the need.
This cycle repeats as new needs arise.
DID YOU KNOW?:
The motivation cycle restarts automatically whenever a person develops a new need. This is
why motivation is a continuous process, not a once-off event.
1.3 Benefits of Motivation for Organisational Performance
Motivation plays a major role in organisational success. Past N6 scenarios and exam questions
highlight how motivated employees show commitment, responsibility and better performance.
11

Key Benefits
1. Higher Productivity – Motivated employees work faster and more accurately.
2. Better Job Satisfaction – They enjoy their work and feel fulfilled.
3. Reduced Absenteeism – They are more committed to being present at work.
4. Improved Quality of Work – Motivated workers take pride in doing tasks well.
5. Better Teamwork – Motivated individuals communicate more openly.
6. Lower Staff Turnover – Employees are less likely to leave the organisation.
7. Positive Work Climate – A motivated workforce creates a healthy
organisational culture.

Unit 2: Major Motivation Models
Understanding employee behaviour is key to effective personnel management. People are
motivated by different needs, expectations and perceptions of fairness. This unit outlines four
major motivation theories that help managers design better workplaces. These theories clarify
what employees value, how they react to rewards, and why fairness boosts performance. Applying
them can improve productivity, reduce conflict and create a supportive work environment.
2.1 Maslow’s Hierarchy of Needs
Maslow’s theory is a well-known motivation model often tested in N6 exams. It explains that people
are driven by a hierarchy of needs, starting with basic survival and moving to psychological growth.
Lower-level needs must be met before higher-level needs can motivate behaviour. This is why
employees may not value recognition or development opportunities if they still feel unsafe or
insecure in their jobs.
Figure 3: Maslow’s Hierarchy of Needs
Source: simplypsychology.org
13

Explanation of the Five Levels
1. Physiological Needs
These are the most basic needs necessary for survival. Examples include:
● Fair wages for food and shelter
● Adequate rest breaks
● A comfortable working environment
If employees cannot meet these needs, motivation will drop sharply.
2. Safety Needs
These involve physical and emotional security, such as:
● Stable employment
● Safe working conditions
● Clear organisational policies
● Protection from unfair treatment
For example, if workers fear retrenchment, they may not perform well, even if they enjoy their
work.
3. Social Needs
This level focuses on belonging and relationships. Employees need:
● Teamwork
● Acceptance by colleagues
● Friendly interactions
● A sense of community
This is why organisations encourage team-building and open communication.
4. Esteem Needs
These involve feelings of worth and accomplishment. Examples include:
● Recognition for achievements
● Respect from supervisors
● Increased responsibility
14

● Job titles reflecting status
Employees at this level want to feel valued and competent.
5. Self-Actualisation
The highest level of need. It involves becoming the best version of oneself. Examples:
● Creative work
● Challenging tasks
● Opportunities for leadership
● Professional growth and learning
Few employees reach this level, but those who do tend to contribute greatly to the organisation.
T I P
If employees constantly worry about their salary or job security, higher-level motivators will not
work. Meet lower-level needs first.
DID YOU KNOW?:
The N6 textbook emphasises that self-actualisation is ongoing — once employees grow, they
continue seeking new challenges.
2.2 Herzberg’s Motivation–Hygiene Theory
Herzberg’s theory explains why some factors lead to satisfaction while others only prevent
dissatisfaction. His research found that motivation is strongly influenced by job content—what
employees actually do—not just by working conditions. This theory plays a major role in exam
questions focused on job enrichment.
Hygiene Factors (Prevent Dissatisfaction)
Hygiene factors do not create motivation, but if they are poor, they cause dissatisfaction.
Examples include:
● Salary
● Company policies
● Working conditions
15

● Supervision quality
● Job security
● Relationships at work
For instance, if the office environment is unsafe or the manager is unfair, motivation will drop
even if the employee loves the job.
Motivators (Increase Satisfaction)
Motivators relate directly to the job itself and lead to long-term satisfaction.
Examples include:
● Achievement
● Recognition
● Responsibility
● Opportunities for advancement
● Meaningful and challenging work
These factors truly inspire employees to work harder.
Why Herzberg’s Theory Matters
● Fixing hygiene issues prevents complaints but does not increase motivation.
● To motivate employees, managers must provide meaningful tasks and responsibility.
● Job enrichment programmes are built on Herzberg’s model.

2.3 Vroom’s Expectancy Theory
Vroom believed that motivation depends on how strongly employees believe that effort leads to
performance and performance leads to rewards. This theory is useful for understanding bonus
systems, promotion opportunities and performance appraisals — all tested in N6 examinations.
The Three Key Elements
1. Expectancy – “If I try harder, I can perform better.” This depends on:
● Training
● Resources
● Support from supervisors
● Personal confidence
If employees feel unprepared for tasks, expectancy decreases.
2. Instrumentality – “If I perform well, I will receive the promised reward.” This depends on:
● Fair reward systems
● Trust in management
● Clear communication
● Honest performance evaluations
Instrumentality is weak when employees do not trust managers to keep their promises.
3. Valence – “The reward has value to me.”
Employees must genuinely want the reward. For example:
● Some prefer more money
● Others prefer recognition or flexible hours
If the reward is not attractive, motivation decreases — even if performance is high.
Putting Vroom’s Theory Into Practice
Managers can improve motivation by:
● Providing resources to help employees succeed (expectancy)
● Making reward systems transparent and fair (instrumentality)
17

● Offering rewards employees actually value (valence)
2.4 Adams’ Equity Theory
Adams’ theory is based on fairness. According to this model, employees constantly compare
their input–output ratio with that of others. If they believe they are being treated unfairly,
motivation decreases, and negative behaviours develop — a common theme in N6 group
conflict exam questions.
Inputs
These are what employees contribute to the organisation:
● Skills
● Qualifications
● Time
● Experience
● Loyalty
● Effort
● Commitment
Outputs
These are what employees receive in return:
● Salary
● Benefits
● Praise
● Promotion
● Work conditions
● Status
Why Equity Matters
If an employee believes another worker is receiving more rewards for similar or even less input,
they may:
● Reduce their effort
● Withdraw from teamwork
18

● Complain
● Seek employment elsewhere
This theory explains workplace disputes, low morale and resistance to organisational change.
T I P
Fairness does not mean everyone gets the same reward; it means rewards match each
person’s input.
REFLECTION QUESTION:
1. How do you typically respond when you feel something is unfair—speak up, reduce
effort, or withdraw?
2. Why do you think fairness plays such a powerful role in workplace motivation?
3. Can you think of someone who reacted strongly to unfairness? What does this show
about how equity influences behaviour?

Unit 3: Methods For Enhancing Motivation
Motivation relies not only on theories but also on how organisations design jobs and systems
that encourage motivated behaviour. This unit outlines practical methods managers use to make
work more meaningful, reduce boredom and help employees see the impact of their efforts.
Effective job design, Management by Objectives (MBO), and productivity improvement systems
create a workplace where employees feel valued and motivated to perform well. These methods
are closely linked to improved performance and are frequently tested in the Personnel
Management N6 examinations.

Job design involves structuring the tasks and responsibilities in a way that improves motivation
and performance. When jobs match employees’ skills, provide variety, and include meaningful
responsibilities, employees are more likely to feel committed and satisfied.

Job Simplification
Job simplification removes complexity by breaking tasks into smaller, standardised duties. It
helps beginners or employees in repetitive industries like packaging, cleaning, retail stocking
and factory production.
21

Advantages:
● Reduces training time
● Improves speed and accuracy
● Helps maintain uniform quality
Disadvantages:
● High repetition → boredom
● Low challenge → low motivation
● Employees feel replaceable
Workplace Scenario:
In a chicken-processing plant, workers may only trim, pack or seal. This increases output but
may cause frustration over time.
Job Rotation
Job rotation helps employees understand different tasks and prevents monotony. It is widely
used in administrative offices, banks, retail stores and hospitals.
Benefits:
● Reduces fatigue and boredom
● Builds teamwork
● Helps with absenteeism (employees can replace each other)
● Broadens experience for promotions
Workplace Scenario:
In an office, a junior admin assistant rotates between document scanning, receptionist duties
and filing. This keeps work refreshing and builds confidence.
T I P
Rotation is effective for young employees who are still discovering their strengths.
22

Job Enlargement
Job enlargement widens the number of tasks an employee performs—without increasing
authority.
Benefits:
● More variety reduces monotony
● Employees experience a fuller workflow
● Helps employees feel useful
Limitations:
● Can feel like “extra work for the same pay”
● May not increase motivation unless combined with recognition
Real Example:
A cashier who only handles payments now also stocks shelves and handles price
checks—more interesting, but responsibility stays the same.
Job Enrichment
This is the most motivational job design method. It involves adding decision-making, planning,
and problem-solving tasks, which help employees feel valued.
Key Features:
● Independence
● Responsibility
● Full ownership of tasks
● Opportunities for growth
● More meaningful work
Examples:
● Allowing employees to decide how to complete tasks
● Handling a process from start to finish
● Assessing own work quality
● Making suggestions for improvement
23

DID YOU KNOW?:
Job enrichment is based on Herzberg’s motivators such as achievement, recognition and
personal growth. It is not about increasing workload—it is about increasing quality of work.

Case Study: Motivation at Kayisela Furniture Workshop
CASE STUDY: MOTIVATION AT KAYISELA FURNITURE WORKSHOP
Kayisela Furniture Workshop is a small manufacturing business in Limpopo that produces
custom wooden tables and chairs. The business has grown quickly over the past year, but
productivity has recently dropped. Several employees have complained that their work feels
repetitive, and they do not receive recognition when they meet targets.
The supervisor, Mr. Phakathi, notices that employees arrive late, take long breaks and show
little interest in learning new skills. Some workers say they are unhappy because the workload
has increased but their responsibilities have not changed. Others feel that they work harder
than their colleagues but receive the same rewards, which they believe is unfair.
After reviewing the situation, management decides to introduce job rotation so that employees
can learn different tasks such as sanding, varnishing and assembling. They also include
employees in planning daily production targets using a simple MBO approach. In addition, the
workshop starts a small gain-sharing system: if the team reduces waste and completes orders
on time, they will receive a shared bonus.
Within two months, absenteeism decreases, quality improves and workers begin to take pride
in their roles. Employees say the work feels more meaningful, and they enjoy gaining new
skills and having a say in planning the workload.
QUESTIONS
1. Identify two motivation problems faced by Kayisela Furniture Workshop before
management introduced changes.
2. Explain how job rotation and MBO helped improve employee motivation in the workshop.
3. Which motivation theory (Maslow, Herzberg, Vroom or Adams) best explains why
employees felt the situation was unfair? Briefly explain your answer.

SUMMARY
Motivation is the force that drives employees to act, perform and remain committed. A motivated
workforce improves productivity, job satisfaction and organisational success, while low
motivation leads to absenteeism, conflict and poor performance.
Motivation begins with a need that leads to action, followed by satisfaction. Because people
have different needs, managers must understand what motivates each employee.
Key motivation theories explain this:
● Maslow: needs move from basic survival to personal growth.
● Herzberg: hygiene factors prevent dissatisfaction; motivators create satisfaction.
● Vroom: motivation depends on effort, performance and valued rewards.
● Adams: fairness in effort and rewards influences motivation.
Motivation improves through practical methods such as:
● Job design (rotation, enlargement, enrichment) to make work meaningful.
● MBO, where goals are jointly set and progress is reviewed.
● Productivity improvement and gain-sharing, which reward teams for better
performance.
In summary, employees feel motivated when their needs are considered, their work has
meaning, goals are clear, and rewards are fair.

Introduction
Communication is an essential part of every organisation. It is the process through which people
share information, express ideas, give instructions and work towards common goals. Without
clear communication, organisations struggle to operate because employees become confused,
mistakes increase and teamwork becomes difficult. Good communication helps to build trust,
reduce conflict and ensure that everyone understands their role in achieving organisational
objectives.
In modern workplaces, communication is not only about speaking. It includes listening, writing,
reading, body language and the use of technology. Communication flows through different levels
of the organisation and takes many forms, such as face-to-face conversations, emails, meetings
and reports. Each form has its strengths and weaknesses, which managers must understand to
communicate effectively.
This module explores how communication works, what influences it, and how organisations can
improve it. You will learn about communication processes, barriers, personal differences,
workplace structures and formal procedures such as grievances and discipline. Understanding
these concepts helps you communicate confidently, avoid misunderstandings and contribute
positively to the workplace.
