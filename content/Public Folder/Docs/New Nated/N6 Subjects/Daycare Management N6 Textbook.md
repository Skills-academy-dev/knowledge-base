INTRODUCTION

Daycare management is all around you. It influences how children grow, how families work, and

how communities support early learning and development. From the nutritious meals served

each day to the safety procedures, budgeting, and communication with parents — every aspect

of daycare management contributes to creating a secure and stimulating environment for young

children. In simple terms, daycare management is the study of how to organise, lead, and

maintain a childcare centre that promotes the well-being and development of every child in your

care.

By learning Daycare Management, you will:

● Understand how management supports early childhood care – Explore how daycare

centres are structured and managed, the responsibilities of caregivers and

administrators, and how effective leadership ensures a well-run, nurturing environment

for young children.

● Think critically about daily operations – Learn to plan nutritious menus, maintain

hygiene and safety standards, manage finances and staff, and create systems that

promote accountability and professionalism.

● See South Africa in context – Discover how daycare management in South Africa is

guided by national legislation, community needs, and cultural values, and how these

influence the care and education of children from diverse backgrounds.

● Prepare for your future – Develop essential skills in organisation, communication,

leadership, and teamwork that are vital for managing or supporting a daycare centre —

or even starting your own.

● Stay connected to your community – By studying daycare management, you will

better understand the vital role that early childhood care plays in family life, social

development, and the building of a stronger, more caring society.

Module 1

Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you should be able to:

1\. Explain the importance of nutrition in the healthy growth and development of children

2\. Describe the components of a balanced diet and the factors to consider when planning

Introduction

Nutrition plays a vital role in the growth, development, and overall well-being of children from

birth to six years. During these early years, the body and brain develop rapidly, requiring a

balanced diet rich in essential nutrients to support physical growth, cognitive development, and

strong immune function. In a daycare environment, caregivers play a key role in ensuring that

every child receives meals that are both nourishing and suited to their individual health needs.

Understanding basic nutrition principles allows caregivers to create menus that promote

concentration, energy, and healthy habits from an early age.

This module explores how to plan, prepare, and manage food safely and effectively within an

early childhood setting. It covers the principles of balanced diets, ration scales for different age

groups, and the management of food allergies and special dietary conditions such as diabetes,

lactose intolerance, and celiac disease. Learners will also gain practical skills in menu planning,

food preparation, hygiene, and kitchen management, while learning how to communicate

professionally with parents about children’s dietary needs. By the end of this module, you will

understand how good nutrition and effective food management contribute to a happy, healthy,

and productive daycare environment.

years, focusing on planning balanced meals, understanding ration scales, and recognising

special dietary needs. Caregivers who understand how to meet children’s nutritional

1.1 Diet plan for children aged 1–6 years

Children between one and six years are at a stage of rapid physical and mental growth. They

require meals that are balanced, frequent, and suited to their smaller stomachs and higher

energy needs. A balanced diet includes foods from all main food groups: carbohydrates,

proteins, fats, vitamins, and minerals, along with sufficient water.

Guidelines for a Healthy Diet Plan:

● Offer three main meals and two to three snacks daily.

● Meals should include foods from at least four of the five major food groups.

● Limit sugar, salt, and processed foods.

● Encourage water and milk as the main drinks instead of fizzy or sugary beverages.

● Prepare meals that are colourful and varied to make eating appealing.

● Involve children in simple tasks such as setting the table or serving fruit to promote

independence and enjoyment during mealtimes.

1.3 Special diets and feeding challenges

Some children require special diets because of allergies, intolerances, or medical conditions.

Understanding these needs helps prevent serious health complications and ensures inclusivity

in the daycare environment.

Common Dietary Challenges:

● Food Allergies: Caused by an immune reaction to specific foods like milk, eggs,

peanuts, or fish. Symptoms may include rashes, swelling, or stomach cramps.

● Food Intolerances: Occur when the digestive system cannot properly break down

certain foods, e.g., lactose intolerance.

● Medical Conditions: Conditions such as diabetes, celiac disease, or anaemia require

dietary adjustments and careful menu planning.

Managing a Child with Multiple Food Allergies

Scenario:

Little Stars Daycare has 22 children enrolled in the 3–4-year age group. One of the new

children, Aiden, has recently joined the group. His parents inform the caregiver, Thandi, that

Aiden has severe allergies to eggs and peanuts. He also develops mild rashes when he

drinks regular cow’s milk.

Thandi reviews the daycare’s weekly menu and realises that many of the planned meals

include foods that could trigger Aiden’s allergies — such as peanut butter sandwiches and

scrambled eggs. She immediately meets with the centre’s cook and principal to discuss safe

substitutions. They decide to replace peanut butter with mashed avocado or hummus and to

substitute eggs with other protein sources like beans or chicken.

Thandi also ensures that Aiden’s meals are prepared separately from other children’s

meals to avoid cross-contamination. She keeps an emergency allergy action plan in

Aiden’s file, including his parents’ contact numbers and instructions for responding if he has

an allergic reaction. Thandi regularly communicates with Aiden’s parents to review his

progress and check for any new food sensitivities.

Unit 2: Kitchen and Meal Management

An efficiently organised kitchen is essential for ensuring that food is prepared safely,

hygienically, and in a way that promotes healthy eating for children. This unit focuses on setting

up and maintaining a clean, functional kitchen, planning balanced menus, serving meals

correctly, and maintaining high standards of cleanliness. Effective kitchen and meal

management not only supports children’s nutrition but also builds trust with parents and ensures

compliance with health regulations.

2.1 Kitchen setup and hygiene

A well-planned kitchen ensures safety, cleanliness, and smooth food preparation. In a daycare

setting, kitchen layout, equipment, and hygiene practices must meet health standards to prevent

contamination and illness.

Guidelines for Kitchen Setup:

● Design the kitchen for efficiency with separate areas for storage, prep, and cleaning.

● Provide sufficient storage for dry goods, refrigerated items, and cleaning materials

● Surfaces should be easy to clean — stainless steel or tile is ideal.

2.2 Menu planning and balanced diets

Menu planning ensures that children receive a variety of foods that meet their nutritional needs.

A well-planned menu also helps control costs and avoid food waste.

Principles of Menu Planning:

● Include foods from all major food groups (carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, and

minerals).

● Consider age, portion sizes, and dietary restrictions.

● Plan meals that are colourful, appealing, and easy to eat.

● Use local and seasonal ingredients to reduce costs and ensure freshness.

● Rotate menus on a 3–5 week cycle to maintain variety.

2.4 Feeding Guidelines for Different Age Groups

Feeding practices in a daycare centre should always match the child’s developmental stage

and individual needs. The goal is to ensure that each child receives adequate nutrition while

developing independence, social skills, and positive attitudes toward food. The following

guidelines outline appropriate feeding methods for babies, toddlers, and children aged 3–6

years.

Feeding Babies (Birth to 12 Months)

Babies need gentle, safe, and hygienic feeding practices. Educators must handle feeding times

calmly and attentively to promote bonding and a sense of security.

23

Guidelines for Feeding Babies:

● Always wash hands thoroughly before preparing or handling bottles.

● Hold the baby comfortably and securely, supporting the head and shoulders while

feeding — never prop the bottle or leave the baby unattended.

● Maintain eye contact and talk softly to the baby during feeding to build trust and comfort.

● Sterilise bottles and teats after each use by boiling them or using a sterilising solution.

● Label bottles clearly with the baby’s name, feeding time, and contents to prevent

mix-ups.

● Prepared milk formula should be stored in the refrigerator and only removed when it’s

time to feed.

● Discard any unused milk left in the bottle after feeding to prevent bacterial growth.

● Never feed babies while they are lying flat — always hold them at a slight angle to

prevent choking and ear infections.

● After feeding, gently burp the baby to release swallowed air.

feeding

Feeding Toddlers (1 to 3 Years)

Toddlers are learning to eat independently. Feeding times should be patient, unhurried, and

positive to encourage self-feeding and confidence.

Guidelines for Feeding Toddlers:

● Serve food in small, manageable portions that are easy to chew.

● Use child-safe eating ware — plastic or melamine plates, cups, and spoons (no sharp or

breakable items).

● Always use bibs or washable aprons to keep clothes clean.

● Offer a variety of textures and flavours to help develop food preferences.

● Encourage children to feed themselves, even if they spill.

● Do not bribe or force a toddler to eat or finish their food — appetite varies daily.

● Avoid pressuring them into perfect table manners; focus instead on basic habits like

sitting at the table and using utensils.

● Monitor for choking hazards such as nuts, popcorn, grapes, or hard sweets.

● Keep the atmosphere calm and pleasant — feeding should never feel like punishment or

a chore.

Figure 8: Always encourage toddlers to feed themselves

(Source: Pinterest, Accessed: 2025)

Feeding 3–6-Year-Old Children

Children in this age group are developing social skills and independence. Mealtimes are

opportunities for them to learn cooperation, responsibility, and basic etiquette in a friendly

setting.

Guidelines for Feeding 3–6-Year-Olds:

● Ensure children wash their hands before and after meals.

● Involve them in simple mealtime responsibilities, such as helping to set the table,

carrying trays, or wiping surfaces after eating.

● Encourage children to sit properly at the table and enjoy their food together.

● Allow children to serve themselves small portions (family-style serving) when possible,

promoting independence.

● Offer second servings to children who are still hungry, rather than forcing large portions

initially.

● Encourage conversation and social contact during meals — this helps with language and

social development.

● Do not insist that children wait until everyone is finished before leaving the table; they

should be allowed to leave politely once done and continue with quiet play.

● Reinforce positive table manners gently, without criticism.

● Praise children for trying new foods, but never punish or shame them for refusing certain

items.

Tip

Allow toddlers and 3-6 year olds to eat outside (in the shade) if there is nice weather.

These age-appropriate feeding practices ensure that every child in the daycare centre is

healthy eating, good hygiene, and positive social interaction.

2.5 Responsibilities and cleanliness in the food service area

Maintaining a clean and orderly food service area is the responsibility of all staff involved in food

preparation and service. A clean environment prevents illness, ensures safety, and sets a good

example for children.

Responsibilities of Staff:

● Wash hands before and after handling food or feeding children.

● Check that all dishes, utensils, and surfaces are sanitised before and after use.

● Serve food promptly and at the correct temperature.

● Report any signs of pests or contamination immediately.

● Store leftovers safely and discard expired items.
