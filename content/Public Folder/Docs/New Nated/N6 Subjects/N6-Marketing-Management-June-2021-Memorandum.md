4.1 4.1.1 Licensing – is a relatively flexible work agreement that can be
customised to fit the needs and interests of both the licensor and
the licensee.
4.1.2 Foreign trade policies – policies by a government regarding its
domestic economy to discourage imports from and to encourage
exports to foreign countries.
4.1.3 Direct export – a producer or supplier sells its product directly to the
international market, either through intermediaries such as sales
representatives, distributors or foreign retailers, or to the end user.
4.1.4 Indirect export – a producer or supplier sells goods and services to
an intermediary who later sells them directly to importing
wholesalers or customers. 
4.1.5 Price determination – the interaction between demand and supply in
the free market that is used to determine the cost of goods and
services.
(5 × 2) (10)
4.2 • Bilateral trade – a trade agreement in terms of which two countries
exchange goods and services.
• Multilateral trade – a type of international trade when a country trades with
two or more other countries. (6)
4.3 • Direct export– selling directly to the market you have chosen by using you
own resources. Agents and distributors who work closely with you,
promote your interests and become the face of your company.
• Licensing – a firm transfers the rights to the use of a product or service to
another firm. It is a useful strategy if the purchaser of the licence has a
relatively large share in the market you want to enter.
• Franchising – It works well in firms that have a repeatable business model
that can be easily transferred to another market, e.g. food outlets. Your
business model should either be quite unique or have a strong brand
recognition that can be utilised internationally.
• Partnerships – are indispensable when companies wish to enter foreign
markets. In some parts of the world, e.g. Asia, a partnership is a
prerequisite. It can take a variety of forms, from a simple co-marketing
alliance to a sophisticated strategic alliance for manufacturing.
• Joint venture – is a type of partnership that involves the creation of a third
independently managed company. Two companies agree to work together
in a particular market, either a geographical or a product market, and create
a third company in order to do this.

• Buying company – In some markets, buying an existing local company
may be the most appropriate entry strategy. The reason may be that a
company has a substantial market share, is in direct competition with you
or, because of regulations, that this is the only option for your firm to enter a
market. Since it is the most costly method and determines the true value
of a firm in a foreign market, it requires a substantial due diligence.
• Piggybacking – is one of the ways of entering the international arena. If you
have an interesting and unique product or service that you sell to large
domestic firms that are involved in a foreign market, you request them to
include your product or service in their inventory for international markets.
• Turnkey projects – are particular to companies that provide services such
as environmental counselling, architecture, construction and engineering. A
facility is built from the ground up and turned over to the client who is ready
to go. This is a very effective way to enter a foreign market because the
client is normally a government and the project is often financed by an
international financial agency such as the World Bank, so the risk of not
being paid is eliminated. (18)

QUESTION 5
5.1 • Ask for referrals. 
Don’t wait for your current customers to refer their friends and family
members to you because you could be waiting far too long. Take action
by implementing a system for actively soliciting referrals from your satisfied
customers and building referral generating activity in the sales process
by, e.g., collecting information about customers from sales people.
• Network. 
Generate good, old-fashioned word of mouth by participating in networking
organisations and events relevant to your industry and customers. Be
sure to approach your networking with the correct attitude. Ask yourself
'How can I help others?' instead of 'What is in it for me?' By being of use to
others, you build relationships that will lead to new customers.

• Offer discounts and incentives to new customers only.
Introductory offers such as a two-week course at your karate studio for only
R100, can lure curious customers to your door. It is a low-risk way to try
your product or services. Track the customers who take up the special
offer and target them with a marketing message that encourages them to
keep buying from you.
• Reconnect with old customers.
Everything old can be new again, including old customers who haven’t
done business with you for some time. Go through your customer
contacts on a regular basis and reach out to dormant customers who
haven’t bought anything from you for six months or a year with a special
offer via e-mail, direct mail or phone. They will be glad that you still
remember them and want to win them back.
• Improve your website.
These days customers and business-to-business buyers find new business
primarily by searching online. Therefore, your website has to do some
heavy lifting to attract new customers. Give your website a once-over to
make sure that the design, content, graphics and SEO are up to date. If
website improvement is not your strong suit, enlist the services of a website
design company.
• Partner with complementary businesses.
Team up with businesses that have the same customers base as you, but
are not directly competitive. Think of ways to target each other’s customers
to drive new business to each other. For example, a maternity clothing
website and a baby product website could pair up to offer discounts and
deals to each other’s customers.
• Promote your expertise.
Generate interest and new customers by publicising your expertise in your
industry. Impress potential customers with your expertise by participating
in industry panel discussions or webinars, by speaking at industry events
or to groups that your target customers belong to or by holding educational
sessions or workshops.
• Use online reviews to your advantage.
Cultivate your reviews and make the most of them. Link to reviews on your
website and post signage in your location by urging customers to check
you out on where the reviews are. Social proof is powerful and new
customers are likely to give your business a try if others praise it.
• Participate in community events.
Most people like to support independent business in their communities.
Raise your profile in your community by participating in charity events and
organisations. Sponsor a local fun run, organise holidays, donate 'toys for
kids', or equip a little league team in your city. It gets your name out there
and brings in new customers.
