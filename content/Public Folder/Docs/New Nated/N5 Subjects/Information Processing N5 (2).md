Introduction

Typing accurately and at a fast pace is an essential skill in today’s professional environment. Whether you are preparing business documents, reports, or correspondence, speed and accuracy ensure efficiency and professionalism. Employers value individuals who can produce well-formatted documents quickly without compromising on quality.

This module focuses on improving your typing speed to at least 45 words per minute (w.p.m.) while maintaining high accuracy. Through daily keyboarding drills and timed accuracy tests, you will develop better finger coordination, minimise errors, and enhance your overall typing efficiency. By practising consistently, you will build the skills needed for effective document processing and transcription tasks.

1.1 Importance of Speed and Accuracy

Typing quickly and accurately is essential in modern workplaces where documents must be produced efficiently. Many businesses rely on employees to complete reports, emails, and business correspondence without delays or errors.

Having a fast typing speed means that tasks can be completed in less time, increasing productivity. However, speed should not come at the cost of accuracy. Errors in business documents can lead to misunderstandings, legal issues, or loss of credibility. A balance between speed and accuracy ensures high-quality documents with minimal errors.

Benefits of Good Typing Skills:

• Increased work efficiency and productivity.

• Fewer mistakes, reducing the need for corrections.

• Improved confidence when handling large volumes of text.

• Better job opportunities in administration, finance, and legal fields.

Example: A secretary who types at 30 w.p.m. but makes frequent errors will take longer to correct mistakes than a colleague who types at 45 w.p.m. with high accuracy.

To achieve the best results, focus on both speed and accuracy by developing a consistent typing rhythm and minimising errors through practice.

1.2 Daily Keyboarding Drills

Developing strong typing skills requires consistent practice. Regular keyboarding drills help to improve both speed and accuracy by reinforcing correct finger placement and hand coordination.

Types of Keyboarding Drills

1\. Home Row Practice

• Place fingers on the home row keys (ASDF JKL;).

• Type simple word patterns: "sad, lad, fad, jal, ask, desk".

• Keep your eyes on the screen, not the keyboard.

2\. Speed and Accuracy Exercises

• Short bursts: Type a set of words in 15-second or 30-second intervals to increase typing speed.

• Full sentence drills: Type common business phrases like "Please find attached the re- quested report."

3\. Number and Symbol Drills

• Practise typing numbers (1234567890).

• Include special characters used in business: (@\#$%&\*()).

Example Drill:

Type the following passage as quickly and accurately as possible:

"The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog. Accuracy is key to success in typing."

Best Practices for Daily Typing Drills:

• Sit in a comfortable position with good posture.

• Place fingers correctly on the keyboard without looking down.

• Maintain a steady typing rhythm instead of rushing.

• Use typing software to track your progress.

1.3 Timed Accuracy Tests (10 minutes)

A timed accuracy test measures how well you balance speed and accuracy under time constraints. These tests help monitor progress and highlight areas that need improvement.

Test Requirements:

• Type at a minimum speed of 45 w.p.m.

• Limit errors to a maximum of 23 mistakes per test.

• Complete the test within 10 minutes.

How to Take a Timed Accuracy Test:

1\. Open a word processor or use an online typing tool.

2\. Set a 10-minute timer.

3\. Type a 400-word passage as quickly and accurately as possible.

4\. Calculate your words per minute (w.p.m.) and error count.

Formula to Calculate Typing Speed:

Example Calculation:

• Words Typed: 470

• Errors: 20

• Time: 10 minutes

Tips to Improve Accuracy in Timed Tests:

• Focus on typing steadily, not rushing.

• Correct mistakes immediately instead of waiting until the end.

• Use spell check after completing the test to review errors.

• Repeat tests weekly to track progress.

2.1.1. Components of a Computer System

A computer system consists of hardware and software. Both work together to process information and perform tasks.

A. Hardware

Hardware refers to the physical parts of a computer that you can touch. These include input devices, output devices, storage devices, and processing units.

2.1.2. Handling and Care of Storage Devices

Storage devices are used to save files and ensure data is not lost. Proper handling and care of storage devices increase their lifespan and prevent data corruption.

Best Practices for Handling Storage Devices:

Storage Device Handling and Care Guidelines

USB Flash Drives • Avoid dropping or exposing to extreme heat.

• Always eject safely before removing.

External Hard Drives • Store in a protective case.

• Keep away from magnetic fields.

Internal Hard Drives (HDD/SSD) • Avoid sudden movements when in use.

• Regularly defragment HDDs for performance.

CDs/DVDs • Handle by the edges, not the surface.

• Keep in protective cases.

Example:

If you accidentally remove a USB drive without ejecting it safely, files can become corrupted, leading to data loss.

2.2 Common Proofreading Signs

Proofreading is an essential skill in document processing to ensure that final copies are error-free, well-structured, and professional. Errors in business documents can lead to misunderstandings, legal issues, and loss of credibility.

Proofreading signs are standard symbols used to mark corrections on a typed or handwritten document before finalising it. This section covers:

• The importance of proofreading

• A list of common proofreading symbols and their meanings

• Examples of how to apply proofreading marks

Mastering proofreading signs will help improve your document accuracy and efficiency in office and business environments.

2.2.1 Importance of Proofreading

Proofreading ensures that documents are:

• Free from spelling, grammatical, and formatting errors

• Clear and professional in business communication

• Correctly formatted with proper spacing and alignment

Types of Errors That Proofreading Fixes

Error Type Examples

Spelling Mistakes "Recieve" → "Receive"

Grammar Errors "He go to work." → "He goes to work."

Punctuation Errors "Lets eat, Grandpa." vs. "Let’s eat Grandpa."

Formatting Issues Incorrect spacing, indentation, or alignment

Typographical Errors "Teh" → "The"

2.2.2 Common Proofreading Signs

Proofreading marks are internationally recognised symbols that help indicate corrections quickly and clearly.

List of Common Proofreading Symbols

Proofreading Sign Meaning Example

sp Spelling mistake Recieve → Receive

/ (Slash mark) Remove extra space "The report" → "The report"

^ (Caret) Insert a missing letter/word "She going home." → "She ^is going

\~\~\~ (Wavy line) Mark a word or phrase that

needs to be rewritten

¶ (Paragraph sign) Start a new paragraph "The report is due tomorrow. We need

\= (Double line under text)

lc (Lowercase mark) Convert uppercase to lower-

case

delete (Strike-through line)

Remove a word/letter "The the meeting" → "The the

\[ \] (Brackets around text)

Replace with better wording "\[Big\]" → "\[Significant\]"

. (Circle around punctuation)

Incorrect punctuation "The report is due, tomorrow." → "The

2.2.3 How to Apply Proofreading Signs

The flowchart below outlines the step-by-step process of proofreading a document using proofreading signs:

Examples of Using Proofreading Signs in Sentences

Example 1: Spelling and Grammar Corrections

Before Proofreading: "Their going to the meeting at three o'clock."

Proofreading with Signs Applied: "Their^ going to the meeting at three o’clock." (Correction: "Their" should be changed to "They’re" using a caret ^ mark to insert the missing word.)

2.3 Formatting Guidelines (line spacing, indentation, alignment)

Formatting is an essential part of document preparation. Proper formatting ensures that text is easy to read, well-structured, and professionally presented. It also helps maintain consistency in business documents such as reports, letters, and meeting minutes.

This section covers three key formatting elements:

• Line Spacing – Adjusting the vertical space between lines of text.

• Indentation – Positioning text away from the margin to organise content.

• Alignment – Positioning text within a document to enhance readability.

Mastering these formatting techniques will help produce clear and professional documents suitable for business and administrative use.

2.3.1 Line Spacing

Line spacing is the vertical distance between each line of text in a paragraph. It helps control how tightly or loosely lines of text are placed on the page. Appropriate line spacing improves readability, enhances the professional appearance of documents, and ensures that content is easy to follow and visually balanced.
