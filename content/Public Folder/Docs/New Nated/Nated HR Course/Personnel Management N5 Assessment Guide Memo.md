Question 2: Short Answer (40 Marks)

Each sub-question includes a breakdown of marks based on clarity, relevance, and completeness.

Define personnel research and its importance in the workplace. (5 marks)
Answer: Personnel research involves systematically gathering and analysing workplace data to inform HR decisions. It improves recruitment, retention, and performance management.
Mark allocation:

Definition: 2 marks

Importance (e.g., decision-making, identifying trends, improving policies): 3 marks

Differentiate between internal and external recruitment methods. (5 marks)
Answer:

Internal recruitment involves promoting or transferring existing employees within the organisation.

External recruitment involves sourcing candidates from outside the organisation.
Mark allocation:

Definition of internal recruitment: 2 marks

Definition of external recruitment: 2 marks

Example or additional point: 1 mark

List and describe three elements of a recruitment policy. (6 marks)
Answer:

Job descriptions: Outline the roles and responsibilities.

Equal opportunity guidelines: Ensure fair and inclusive hiring practices.

Legislative compliance: Adhere to relevant labour laws.
Mark allocation:

Each element: 2 marks

Explain the steps involved in conducting a survey as part of personnel research. (8 marks)
Answer:

Planning and designing the survey (define objectives, choose methods).

Testing and modifying the survey.

Distributing the survey to the target audience.

Analysing and interpreting results.
Mark allocation:

Each step: 2 marks

Describe the components of a SWOT analysis with examples. (8 marks)
Answer:

Strengths (e.g., skilled workforce).

Weaknesses (e.g., outdated technology).

Opportunities (e.g., emerging markets).

Threats (e.g., economic downturn).
Mark allocation:

Each component: 2 marks

What are two advantages of using internal recruitment over external recruitment? (4 marks)
Answer:

Cost-effective (reduces advertising expenses).

Boosts employee morale (provides growth opportunities).
Mark allocation:

Each advantage: 2 marks

List three factors that influence recruitment strategies. (4 marks)
Answer:

Labour market conditions.

Organisational image.

Economic conditions.
Mark allocation:

Each factor: 1.33 marks

Discuss how personnel research supports strategic HR decision-making, using examples. (10 marks)
Answer: Personnel research provides data for workforce planning, identifies training needs, improves retention strategies, and supports diversity initiatives. Examples include using employee surveys for policy improvements or analysing turnover rates to develop retention strategies.
Mark allocation:

Explanation of strategic HR decision-making: 5 marks

Examples: 5 marks

Create a recruitment plan for hiring a customer service representative, ensuring alignment with the Employment Equity Act. (15 marks)
Answer:

Identify job requirements and align with equity goals.

Advertise the position through diverse channels.

Conduct fair screening and selection processes.

Ensure compliance with Employment Equity Act regulations.
Mark allocation:

Recruitment process (steps): 10 marks

Compliance with the Employment Equity Act: 5 marks

Design a job advertisement for a marketing manager role. Ensure compliance with recruitment policy guidelines. (15 marks)
Answer:

Job title and description: Clearly define responsibilities.

Requirements: Include qualifications and experience.

Equal opportunity statement: Highlight inclusivity and fairness.
Mark allocation:

Clarity and structure: 10 marks

Compliance with policy guidelines: 5 marks

Question 2: Short Answer (40 Marks)

Define personnel research and its importance in human resources. (5 marks)
Answer:

Definition: Personnel research involves gathering and analysing workforce data to inform HR decisions. (2 marks)

Importance: Improves decision-making, identifies trends, enhances workforce planning, and supports policy development. (3 marks)

Differentiate between qualitative and quantitative research methods. (6 marks)
Answer:

Qualitative: Focuses on non-numerical data, exploring behaviours and motivations (e.g., interviews, focus groups). (3 marks)

Quantitative: Focuses on numerical data, measuring trends and relationships (e.g., surveys, statistical analysis). (3 marks)

List and explain three key components of a recruitment policy. (6 marks)
Answer:

Job descriptions: Define responsibilities and requirements for roles. (2 marks)

Equal opportunity guidelines: Ensure fairness and inclusivity in hiring. (2 marks)

Legislative compliance: Adhere to laws like the Employment Equity Act. (2 marks)

Describe the purpose of performance appraisals. (5 marks)
Answer:

Assess employee performance against objectives. (2 marks)

Provide feedback, identify training needs, and support career development. (3 marks)

What are two main strategies to reduce absenteeism in the workplace? (4 marks)
Answer:

Flexible work hours: Allow work-life balance and reduce stress. (2 marks)

Wellness programs: Address mental and physical health needs. (2 marks)

Explain how a SWOT analysis supports decision-making. (4 marks)
Answer:

Strengths: Identify internal advantages. (1 mark)

Weaknesses: Highlight internal limitations. (1 mark)

Opportunities: Recognise external growth potential. (1 mark)

Threats: Address external risks. (1 mark)

What are three types of job evaluation techniques? (6 marks)
Answer:

Point factor method: Assigns points to job factors. (2 marks)

Ranking method: Compares jobs based on difficulty. (2 marks)

Classification method: Groups jobs into predefined classes. (2 marks)

Define career development and its importance in organisations. (4 marks)
Answer:

Definition: Improving employee skills and preparing them for future roles. (2 marks)

Importance: Boosts satisfaction, retention, and productivity. (2 marks)
