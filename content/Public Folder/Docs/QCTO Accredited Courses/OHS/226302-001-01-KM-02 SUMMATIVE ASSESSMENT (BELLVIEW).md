KM-02KT03: LEADING PRACTICES FOR EFFECTIVE EMPLOYEE REPRESENTATION.
Question 7
IAC0301: Given descriptions of typical work situations learners will be able to describe effective ways of
making the work of the OHS representative work well. [12]
1. Active Engagement with Employees:
o Situation: Employees express concerns about a potential hazard in the workplace, such
as unsafe machinery.
2. Thorough Workplace Inspections:
o Situation: An OHS representative is tasked with identifying potential hazards in a factory
setting.
3. Collaborative Problem-Solving:
o Situation: A new piece of equipment is introduced, and workers are unsure how to
operate it safely.
4. Advocacy and Support:
o Situation: An employee reports a safety concern but fears retaliation from management.
13

KM-02KT04: METHODS OF MAKING EFFECTIVE REPRESENTATIONS AND GIVING
FEEDBACK.
Question 8
IAC0401: Given typical feedback scenarios learners will be able to identify successful and unsuccessful
feedback situations and indicate how feedback should be improved for each of the given scenarios. [12]
Scenario 1: Successful Feedback
● Description: An OHS representative provides feedback to management after conducting a
workplace safety audit. The feedback is specific, highlighting both strengths and areas for
improvement. The representative also suggests actionable steps for addressing identified issues.
Scenario 2: Unsuccessful Feedback
● Description: An OHS representative provides vague feedback to employees about their safety
practices, stating only that "improvements are needed" without specifying what those
improvements should be.
Scenario 3: Unsuccessful Feedback
● Description: An OHS representative gives feedback to an employee in a public setting, leading
to embarrassment and defensiveness from the employee.

KM-02KT05: THE PROCEDURE FOR SUBMISSION OF COMPENSATION CLAIMS FOR
INJURIES AND OCCUPATIONAL DISEASES
Question 9
IAC0501: Given various scenarios regarding compensation claims learners will be able to analyse the
situations and determine how to ensure that claims are successfully submitted. [10]
1. Scenario 1: Workplace Injury Claim
o Description: An employee suffers a back injury while lifting heavy equipment. The
employee reports the injury to the supervisor but does not immediately seek medical
attention.
2. Scenario 2: Occupational Disease Claim
o Description: An employee develops a respiratory condition after prolonged exposure to
harmful chemicals at work. The condition is diagnosed several months after the exposure.
3. Scenario 3: Rejected Compensation Claim
o Description: An employee's claim for a repetitive strain injury is rejected due to
insufficient evidence.
