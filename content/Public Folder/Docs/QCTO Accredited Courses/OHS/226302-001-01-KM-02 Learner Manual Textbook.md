Welcome

Dear Student,

Welcome to your journey in Fundamentals of Representing Employees Regarding

Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) Issues\! This course will help you understand how to

represent employees effectively, promote workplace safety, and ensure compliance with South

Africa’s OHS legislation. The ability to recognise, report, and address health and safety

concerns is vital for creating a fair and secure working environment — one where every

employee’s voice is heard and respected.

This manual combines theory and practice to help you become a confident and informed

workplace representative. You’ll learn how to interpret key sections of the Labour Relations Act

(LRA) and the Occupational Health and Safety Act (OHSA), understand the role of the OHS

representative, and apply effective communication and feedback strategies when addressing

safety concerns. You’ll also explore how to make representations to management, avoid

common pitfalls, and promote collaboration between employers and employees to resolve

workplace issues constructively.

Throughout this manual, you’ll find real-life South African examples, case studies, and practical

activities that connect what you learn to everyday workplace experiences. Take your time,

complete each activity thoughtfully, and reflect on how strong OHS representation contributes to

a safer, healthier, and more productive workforce.

By the end of this course, you’ll understand how to apply legislation, represent your colleagues

effectively, and contribute to a culture of safety and accountability within your organisation. Each

action you take and each representation you make helps protect workers’ rights and supports

South Africa’s broader goal of promoting safe and equitable workplaces for all.

We wish you every success as you begin this empowering journey — strengthening workplace

democracy, one representation at a time.

Introduction

Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) is part of every workplace — from the construction site

and factory floor to the office and retail store.

It’s present in the careful inspection of equipment, the open discussions between employers and

employees, and the shared commitment to ensuring that everyone goes home safely at the end

of the day.

Representation is a key part of this process. It ensures that employees have a voice in health

and safety matters and that workplace democracy is upheld in line with South Africa’s Labour

Relations Act (LRA) and the Occupational Health and Safety Act (OHSA).

This manual will guide you through the essential principles, responsibilities, and best practices

that make employee representation both effective and professional.

By learning about Fundamentals of Representing Employees Regarding OHS Issues, you will:

● Understand how to represent employees effectively – You’ll learn about your rights

and duties as an OHS representative, how to raise safety concerns appropriately, and

how to participate in health and safety committees.

● Make a positive difference in your workplace – By identifying hazards, monitoring

compliance, and collaborating with management, you’ll help create a safer, healthier

environment for all employees.

● Promote fairness and equality – You’ll discover how representation supports

workplace democracy, ensuring that all employees — regardless of their role or position

— are treated fairly and have equal access to safe working conditions.

● Develop professional and communication skills – You’ll practise accurate reporting,

effective feedback, conflict resolution, and ethical representation — essential tools for

gaining trust and credibility as a safety leader.

● Shape the future of safe and responsible work – As an OHS representative, your

actions and advocacy help build safer workplaces, strengthen compliance with national

legislation, and support South Africa’s vision of zero harm in the workplace.

Introduction

Workplace democracy is a cornerstone of fair and inclusive labour relations in South Africa. It

ensures that employees are not just workers but active participants in shaping the conditions

under which they work. Through the Labour Relations Act (LRA), employees are given the right

to organise, form trade unions, and engage in collective bargaining — creating an environment

where their voices can influence workplace policies, safety standards, and overall well-being.

This module introduces you to the concept of workplace democracy and explains how it

connects to the Occupational Health and Safety Act (OHSA). You will learn how employee

representatives and safety committees function within this framework to promote a culture of

participation, accountability, and shared responsibility. By understanding the legislative

foundations and practical applications of employee representation, you will gain the knowledge

needed to foster safe, fair, and collaborative workplaces that meet both legal and ethical

standards.

Unit 1: KT0101 – Concept of Workplace Democracy as per the LRA Act

Workplace democracy lies at the heart of South Africa’s labour legislation. It ensures that

employees have a voice in decisions that affect their working conditions, safety, and well-being.

This unit explores the concept of workplace democracy, the role of the Labour Relations Act

(LRA) in promoting it, and how these principles apply specifically to the Occupational Health and

Safety (OHS) environment.

1.1 Understanding Workplace Democracy

Workplace democracy refers to systems and practices that allow employees to participate

actively in decision-making processes that affect their work and the operation of their

organisation. It is based on the belief that employees are key stakeholders and should have a

voice in shaping workplace policies and practices.

Key features of workplace democracy include:

● Shared decision-making between employers and employees.

● Open communication and consultation.

● Fair representation through elected employee representatives or trade unions.

● Transparent and participatory workplace structures.

This inclusive approach fosters equality, accountability, and collaboration within the workplace. It

improves morale, strengthens relationships, and leads to safer and more productive work

environments.

1.2 The Labour Relations Act (LRA) and Workplace Democracy

The Labour Relations Act (Act 66 of 1995) provides the foundation for workplace democracy in

South Africa. It promotes employee participation and fairness in the workplace by establishing

systems that encourage negotiation, consultation, and cooperation between employers and

employees.

1.3 Application of Workplace Democracy in the OHS Environment

Workplace democracy plays a vital role in maintaining safe and healthy workplaces. The LRA

and the Occupational Health and Safety Act (OHSA) work together to ensure that employees

can participate meaningfully in health and safety decision-making.

Key applications include:

1\. Employee Representation on Health and Safety Committees

● OHS committees must include employee representatives elected by their peers.

● Representatives identify hazards, assess risks, and recommend solutions.

● Their participation ensures that safety measures are practical and reflect real

working conditions.

2\. Collective Bargaining on Health and Safety Matters

● Trade unions can negotiate safety-related provisions such as PPE, training,

and emergency procedures.

● This process ensures that safety policies align with workers’ needs and are

implemented consistently.

3\. Workplace Forums and OHS Discussions

● Workplace forums provide a structured setting for reviewing safety

procedures, new machinery risks, or work redesigns.

● Employee feedback helps prevent accidents and improves compliance.

4\. Dispute Resolution and OHS Compliance

● If an employer fails to meet safety obligations, employees can use LRA

dispute resolution channels (such as the CCMA).

● This ensures fair handling of safety grievances and encourages accountability.

1.4 Debating the Application of Workplace Democracy in the OHS Environment

While workplace democracy offers clear benefits, its implementation within the OHS context

presents both opportunities and challenges.

Advantages:

● Encourages employee engagement and ownership of safety practices.

● Leads to more practical and effective safety solutions.

● Builds a stronger safety culture based on mutual respect and shared responsibility.

Challenges:

● Differences in priorities: management may focus on cost control, while employees

prioritise safety.

● Delays in decision-making when consensus is required.

● Risk of tokenism, where employee participation is symbolic rather than meaningful.

13

Balanced Approach:

To make workplace democracy truly effective in OHS:

● Both management and employees must commit to open, honest communication.

● Safety committees and forums should have real influence on policy and

decision-making.

● Continuous training and monitoring are needed to ensure active participation and

compliance.

Unit 2: KT0102 – Determinations Regarding Representatives in the

Appropriate OHS Act

The Occupational Health and Safety Act (OHSA) of 1993, as amended, forms the foundation of

workplace safety and health in South Africa. One of its central features is the appointment and

functioning of Health and Safety Representatives and Committees, who ensure that workers’

voices are heard in all matters relating to workplace safety.

This unit explores the legislative framework governing these representatives — their

appointment, powers, and responsibilities — and examines how their role contributes to

compliance, communication, and safety culture within the workplace.

2.1 Appointment and Role of Health and Safety Representatives

Legislative Requirements for Appointment

Under Section 17 of the OHSA, employers with 20 or more employees are legally required to

appoint one or more Health and Safety Representatives. This ensures that every workplace,

regardless of size, maintains a structure for employee participation in health and safety matters.

Key aspects include:

● Scope of Representation: Representatives are appointed in all designated workplaces

where the number of employees exceeds the legal threshold.

● Election Process: Representatives are elected by their fellow employees. The process

must be transparent and democratic, allowing for participation from all eligible workers.

● Qualifications and Training: Representatives must receive appropriate training to

perform their duties effectively. Training typically covers:

○ Hazard identification and risk assessment

○ Basic legal and safety responsibilities

○ Emergency response and reporting procedures

○ Communication and representation skills

Properly trained representatives serve as an essential link between employees, management,

and regulators, ensuring that health and safety standards are upheld throughout the

organisation.

15

2.2 Functions and Responsibilities

Health and Safety Representatives play a vital role in promoting and maintaining a safe working

environment. Their duties extend beyond routine checks to active participation in safety planning

and employee consultation.

Their main functions and responsibilities include:

● Hazard Identification and Risk Assessment: Regularly inspecting the workplace to

identify hazards and assess associated risks.

● Incident Reporting: Investigating incidents, near misses, and unsafe conditions, and

reporting findings to the employer.

● Consultation with Employees: Gathering feedback from employees about safety

issues and relaying this information to management.

● Safety Promotion: Encouraging safe practices through awareness campaigns, toolbox

talks, and safety meetings.

● Participation in Committees: Representing employees’ concerns during Health and

Safety Committee meetings.

2.3 Establishment and Functions of Health and Safety Committees

Legislative Framework

Under Section 19 of the OHSA, any workplace employing 50 or more workers must establish

a Health and Safety Committee. This committee provides a structured platform for

collaboration between employees and management on safety matters.

Composition and Structure

● Representation: Both employer and employee representatives must serve on the

committee.

● Proportionality: The number of employee representatives must reflect the size of the

workforce to ensure fair participation.

● Chairpersonship: A chairperson is appointed to lead meetings and ensure effective

communication and decision-making.

Functions of the Committee

● Review existing safety policies and procedures.

● Monitor accident reports and discuss corrective measures.

● Advise management on potential improvements to safety standards.

● Promote awareness of OHS rights and responsibilities among employees.

● Facilitate communication between employees and management on safety issues.

Meeting and Documentation Requirements

● Committees must meet regularly (at least once every three months).

● Detailed minutes of each meeting must be kept, including:

○ Issues raised and decisions taken

○ Assigned responsibilities and deadlines

○ Follow-up actions for subsequent meetings

These records serve as legal evidence of compliance and continuous safety improvement.

2.5 Documentation and Record-Keeping

Accurate documentation is central to compliance with the OHSA. Records serve both as a

management tool and as evidence of due diligence during inspections or audits.

Essential records include:

● Hazard Registers and Risk Assessments: Identify hazards, classify risks, and

record mitigation measures.

● Inspection Reports: Detail findings from regular workplace inspections

conducted by representatives.

● Incident Reports: Record the nature, cause, and corrective action following

accidents or near misses.

● Committee Meeting Minutes: Provide an official record of discussions, decisions,

and follow-up actions.

Maintaining and Updating Records

Records must be reviewed and updated regularly, particularly when:

● New machinery or materials are introduced.

● Work processes change.

● New risks or incidents occur.

Proper record-keeping supports transparency, facilitates learning from past incidents, and

ensures ongoing compliance with legislation.

20

2.6 Legal Obligations and Compliance

Regulatory Oversight

The Department of Employment and Labour (DEL) monitors compliance with the OHSA.

Employers and employees alike must adhere to the Act’s provisions to ensure that health and

safety representatives function effectively.

Compliance includes:

● Appointing trained representatives where required.

● Establishing functional committees in workplaces with 50+ employees.

● Ensuring regular inspections, risk assessments, and reporting.

● Providing the DEL with access to all safety records upon request.

Audits and Inspections

Labour inspectors have the authority to:

● Enter any workplace and review safety practices.

● Examine relevant documents, including committee minutes and risk assessments.

● Issue compliance orders where deficiencies are found.

● Failure to comply may lead to penalties, fines, or, in severe cases, temporary closure

of the workplace.

2.7 Application and Impact in the Workplace

The proper implementation of OHSA provisions on health and safety representation delivers

measurable improvements in workplace safety and compliance.

Positive impacts include:

● Enhanced Safety Culture: Representatives foster shared responsibility for safety,

encouraging workers to report hazards early.

● Improved Communication: Regular engagement between employees and

management promotes trust and responsiveness.

● Proactive Risk Management: Early detection and corrective action prevent

accidents and reduce downtime.

● Regulatory Compliance: Meeting legislative standards protects both the

organisation and its workforce from legal and financial consequences.

Real-Life Example: OHS Representation in South African Mining

In the South African mining sector, health and safety representatives play a pivotal role in

maintaining compliance with the Mine Health and Safety Act (MHSA), which aligns closely

with the OHSA.

At a platinum mine in Rustenburg, elected representatives conduct daily inspections before

each shift. When an unsafe rock face was identified, the representative halted operations and

alerted management. Immediate corrective measures were implemented, preventing a

potential collapse.

This example demonstrates the value of trained and empowered representatives in protecting

lives and ensuring operational continuity through proactive action.

Case Study

Read the case study below and answer the questions that follow.

Strengthening Workplace Democracy at Ubuntu Engineering

Background:

Ubuntu Engineering, a medium-sized manufacturing company in Port Elizabeth, employs 120

workers. The company recently experienced a series of minor workplace incidents involving

machine operators. Following an inspection by the Department of Employment and Labour,

management was advised to strengthen employee participation in health and safety matters.

In response, Ubuntu Engineering established a Health and Safety Committee in line with the

Occupational Health and Safety Act (OHSA) and held democratic elections to appoint three

employee representatives. The newly elected representatives were given training in hazard

identification, reporting procedures, and employee consultation.

Over the next three months, the committee met regularly to review safety concerns. Workers

reported issues such as poor lighting and limited access to personal protective equipment

(PPE). After discussions between the committee and management, new lighting was installed,

and a PPE inventory system was introduced. Incident reports dropped significantly, and

employee satisfaction improved.

Summary

● Workplace democracy is the foundation of fair and participatory labour relations in

South Africa. It ensures that employees have a voice in decision-making processes that

affect their working conditions, safety, and well-being.

● The Labour Relations Act (LRA) of 1995 promotes workplace democracy by enabling

employee representation through collective bargaining, workplace forums, and dispute

resolution mechanisms such as the Commission for Conciliation, Mediation, and

Arbitration (CCMA).

● Democratic participation enhances occupational health and safety by empowering

employees to identify risks, influence safety policies, and collaborate with management

in creating safer work environments.

● The Occupational Health and Safety Act (OHSA) of 1993 outlines the legal

requirements for appointing Health and Safety Representatives in workplaces with 20

or more employees and mandates the establishment of Health and Safety Committees

in workplaces with 50 or more employees.

● Health and Safety Representatives are responsible for conducting inspections,

identifying hazards, reporting risks, and serving as a communication link between

employees and management.

● Health and Safety Committees facilitate consultation, policy review, and safety

planning, ensuring that both employee and employer perspectives are represented.

● Employers are legally obligated to support representatives by providing adequate

training, time, information, and resources, and by ensuring protection from victimisation.

● Accurate record-keeping and documentation — including hazard registers, risk

assessments, and meeting minutes — are vital for demonstrating compliance and

continuous improvement.

● The Department of Employment and Labour monitors compliance through audits and

inspections, ensuring adherence to safety standards and representative functions.

● When effectively implemented, the OHSA framework leads to improved safety culture,

stronger communication, and greater trust between management and workers.

● Real-life evidence, particularly from industries such as mining, manufacturing, and

logistics, shows that active employee representation significantly reduces workplace

incidents and enhances compliance with national safety legislation.
