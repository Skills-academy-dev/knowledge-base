IAC0505  Given  scenarios  relating  to  the  work  of  Occupational  Health  and  Safety  representatives  in

various occupational settings be able to:

a.  Identify  where  the  legal  rights  and  duties  of  the Occupational Health and Safety representative

are being ignored and/or exceeded.

b.  Indicate  if  the  correct  processes  are  being  used  to  select  and appoint the Occupational Health

and safety representative and how these processes can be improved.

c.

Identify  where  the  incorrect  structuring  of  Occupational  Health  and  Safety  Committees  are

hampering  the  effective  functioning  of  the  Occupational  Health  and  Safety  representative  and

how these deviations present a risk to the organisation. [15]

Scenario  1:  In  a  manufacturing  plant,  the  Occupational  Health and Safety Representative (OHS Rep)

identifies  that  the  machinery  maintenance  records  are  incomplete  and  that  safety  guards  on  several

machines are missing. The OHS Rep has reported these issues to management, but no corrective action

has been taken. Additionally, the plant's safety committee meetings are irregular and lack representation

from all relevant departments.

Scenario  2:  In an office environment, the OHS Rep notices that emergency exit routes are blocked by

office  equipment  and  that  some  employees  are  not  following  the  prescribed  ergonomic practices.  The

OHS Rep has attempted to address these issues through informal conversations with management but

has not been successful. The safety committee is well-structured but meets infrequently.

Case Study: Manufacturing Facility Fire

Scenario Description: A large manufacturing facility specializing in the production of chemical products

experienced a significant fire due to a chemical reaction in one of the production lines. The fire quickly

spread through the facility, causing dense smoke and making it difficult to see. The facility was equipped

with automated fire alarms, sprinklers, and fire extinguishers, and had a designated evacuation plan. An

alarm was triggered by the manager after being informed by an employee working near the area. The

manager immediately informed the Emergency Response Team (ERT) to attend to the fire whilst

informing line managers and HSE Reps to evacuate people. However, the ERT did not manage to put it

out as it was spreading rapidly. The manager then informed the fire department after the emergency

response team informed him the fire was too big for their equipment.
