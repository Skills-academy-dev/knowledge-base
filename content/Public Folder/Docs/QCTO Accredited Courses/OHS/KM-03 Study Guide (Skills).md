Introduction to the Fundamentals of communication

within the OHS Discipline Study Guide

Welcome to Your Introduction to the Fundamentals of Communication within the OHS

Discipline Study Guide! This study guide has been developed to support you as a distance

learning student. It is designed to help you understand your textbook and prepare for your

assessments — even if you are studying from home.

Clear and respectful communication is at the heart of Occupational Health and Safety (OHS). It

ensures that vital information about hazards, procedures, and responsibilities is shared

accurately and that collaboration between employees, management, and representatives

promotes a culture of safety and mutual respect. Through this course, you will learn how to:

●  Apply key communication principles and models to strengthen understanding in

workplace safety contexts.

●  Develop ethical communication habits that build trust and accountability.

●  Use assertive communication to prevent or manage conflict effectively.

●  Provide constructive feedback and facilitate professional dialogue that supports

cooperation and compliance.

●  Apply communication skills that promote inclusivity, professionalism, and teamwork in

South African workplaces.

This guide is your companion throughout the course. It is designed to:

●  Explain key communication principles, models, and processes in clear, simple language.

●  Summarise the main topics in each module for easier study and revision.

●  Help you prepare for assignments, tests, and final assessments.

●  Strengthen your ability to apply communication and ethical principles in real workplace

situations.

Remember: You are not alone on this journey. Stay focused, plan your study time carefully, and

reach out to your tutor or student support team whenever you need help or clarification.

Let’s begin your journey into the world of OHS communication — where every message shared

with honesty and clarity helps protect lives, strengthen relationships, and build safer workplaces

for all.

How to Use the Fundamentals of communication

within the OHS Discipline Study Guide

This study guide is designed to make your learning journey easier — especially as a distance

learning student. It will help you understand your Learner Manual, stay organised, and prepare

effectively for your assessments. Here’s how to use it successfully:

1. Start with the Module Overview

Each module begins with a short introduction that explains the key focus areas and learning

outcomes. Read this section first to understand what you will be learning and why it matters in

the context of OHS communication, teamwork, and ethical behaviour. Knowing the purpose of

each module helps you connect the theory to real workplace safety and leadership situations.

2. Read the Unit Summaries

Every module is divided into smaller units. These unit summaries simplify key concepts such as

communication models, feedback methods, coaching techniques, and ethical auditing. You can

read them before studying your Learner Manual to get an overview, or afterward to reinforce

what you’ve learned. They make complex ideas easier to understand and remember.

3. Highlight Key Concepts

As you read, use a highlighter or make notes on key terms, definitions, and examples — such

as types of communication, ethical principles, or conflict resolution strategies. Highlighting helps

you focus on what’s most important and makes revision faster when preparing for assessments.

4. Take Notes and Reflect

Leave space in this guide for your own notes, examples, or personal reflections. Writing in your

own words helps you process information more deeply and apply it to real OHS contexts —

such as safety meetings, internal audits, or employee consultations. Reflection also strengthens

your self-awareness and professional communication skills.

5. Prepare for Assessments

Use this study guide to review before your tests, assignments, and final assessments. Each

module includes summaries, reflection activities, and practical exercises to help you practise

what you’ve learned. You’ll be able to apply communication techniques, ethical reasoning, and

conflict management strategies to realistic workplace situations.

5

6. Refer to Your Learner Manual for More Detail

This study guide supports your prescribed Learner Manual — it does not replace it. Always refer

back to your manual for detailed explanations, legislation examples, and case studies. The

manual provides in-depth context on topics such as effective communication, internal consulting,

and auditing ethics.

7. Manage Your Time Wisely

Plan your study schedule carefully by setting realistic weekly goals. Work through one module at

a time, review your progress regularly, and stay consistent. Steady progress helps you retain

key information about communication processes, ethical principles, and professional standards

— all vital for success in OHS roles.

8. Ask for Help When Needed

If you find something unclear, make a note of your question and reach out to your tutor or

academic support team. Asking for help shows initiative and commitment — two qualities that

define effective communicators and OHS professionals. Remember, support is always available

to help you stay on track.

6

7 Study Tips

1.  Break Down Big Topics into Small Parts: Don’t try to learn everything at once. Focus

on one concept or unit at a time, making sure you understand it clearly before moving

on. This approach helps you build a strong foundation step by step.

2.  Practise Applying Knowledge to Real Workplace Scenarios: Use the case studies,

exercises, and reflection questions in this study guide to practise analysing

communication challenges, identifying barriers, and suggesting practical solutions — just

as you would in a real OHS environment.

3.  Make Flashcards for Key Terms and Concepts: Create flashcards (digital or paper) to

test yourself regularly. Reviewing key definitions and examples often will make it easier

to recall them during assessments and real-life discussions.

4.  Link Learning to Real-Life Practice: Think about how each concept applies to your

daily work or community interactions. For example, when studying conflict management,

reflect on how you could use assertive communication to resolve a disagreement or

prevent misunderstanding in your workplace. Linking theory to experience makes your

learning more meaningful and memorable.

5.  Test Yourself Often: After completing each module, challenge yourself by answering

the practice questions and reflection activities without looking at your notes. This will

help you see how well you understand topics— and identify areas that need more

attention.

6.  Use the Study Guide for Quick Reviews: Before starting a new module in your

Learner Manual, read through the study guide summaries to get a clear overview of what

you’ll be learning. When revising, use these summaries again to refresh your memory

before assignments or exams

7.  Stick to a Study Schedule: Good time management is essential for success as a

distance learner. Create a weekly study plan with specific goals — for example,

completing one unit per week or setting aside a set time each day for reading and

revision. Consistency will help you stay motivated, organised, and confident as you

develop your professional communication skills.

Unit 1: KT0101 Basic Communication Model and Describe Workplace

Strategies to Overcome Typical Barriers to Communication

This unit introduces the key concepts of the communication model and explains how people

send, receive, and understand messages in the workplace. You will also learn to identify and

overcome common barriers that interfere with effective communication.

1.1 Understanding the Communication Model

Communication is the process of exchanging information between a sender and a receiver. It

helps build understanding, relationships, and cooperation in the workplace. The communication

model shows how messages are created, transmitted, and interpreted.

1.2 The Communication Process

The process includes several steps: the sender forms a message, encodes it into words or

actions, sends it through a channel, and the receiver interprets it. Feedback ensures that the

message has been understood correctly.

1.3 Types of Communication Models

Different models explain how communication works — such as linear, interactive, and

transactional models. Each model highlights how people share meaning, respond to feedback,

and build mutual understanding.

Figure 2: Models of communication

Source AI Generated

10

1.4 Workplace Barriers to Communication

Barriers can occur when messages are misunderstood or unclear. Common barriers include

language differences, noise, emotional stress, and poor listening. These challenges can lead to

confusion and conflict.

1.5 Overcoming Barriers to Communication

To improve communication, use clear language, listen actively, and check for understanding.

Encourage feedback, remain calm, and adapt your style to suit your audience.

Unit 5: KT0105 The Difference Between Assertiveness and

Aggressiveness

This unit explains how to distinguish between assertive and aggressive communication and why

assertiveness is essential for healthy workplace relationships.

5.1 Assertiveness

Assertiveness means expressing thoughts and feelings clearly, calmly, and respectfully. It

involves standing up for your rights without violating the rights of others. Assertive

communication promotes confidence, cooperation, and mutual understanding.

Figure 8: Assertiveness means standing firm — without raising your voice.”

Source: coursera, active-listening

5.2 Aggressiveness

Aggressiveness occurs when someone expresses their views in a forceful or hostile manner. It

often involves blaming, interrupting, or ignoring others. Aggressive communication damages

trust, increases conflict, and reduces teamwork.

Unit 6: KT0106 Basic Strategies for Preventing and Managing Conflict

This unit explores proactive ways to prevent conflict and constructive strategies to manage it

when it arises in the workplace.

6.1 Preventing Conflict (Proactive Strategies)

Conflict prevention begins with open communication, active listening, and respect for different

opinions. Setting clear expectations and addressing issues early reduces tension and

misunderstanding.

6.2 Post-Conflict Reflection

After a conflict, reflection helps identify what caused the problem and how it was resolved.

Reflecting allows individuals and teams to learn from the experience and prevent future

disagreements.

6.3 Quick Tips for a Peaceful Workplace

Stay calm, listen before responding, and focus on solutions rather than blame. Show empathy,

take responsibility when needed, and maintain professionalism in all interactions.

Introduction

Ethical behaviour is the foundation of trust and accountability in the workplace. This module

explores the principles of ethics and their importance in Occupational Health and Safety (OHS)

monitoring systems and organisational practices.

Unit 1: KT0201 Ethics and Why Ethical Behaviour Is Always Important

This unit introduces the concept of ethics and explains why ethical behaviour is essential in

maintaining professionalism, trust, and compliance within the OHS environment.

1.1 Definition of Ethics

Ethics refers to the principles of right and wrong that guide human behaviour. It helps individuals

make responsible choices that reflect honesty, fairness, and respect for others.

1.2 The Role of Ethics in Professional Practice

Ethics ensures that decisions and actions in the workplace are fair and transparent.

Professionals use ethical judgement to protect the interests of employees, employers, and the

public.

1.3 Importance of Ethical Behaviour

Ethical behaviour builds credibility, promotes teamwork, and strengthens trust between

colleagues. It also supports compliance with laws and company policies.

23

1.4 Consequences of Unethical Behaviour

Unethical behaviour can damage reputations, cause mistrust, and lead to legal or disciplinary

action. It can also create unsafe working conditions and harm organisational integrity.

Unit 2: KT0202 The Consequences of Unethical Behaviour on

Occupational Health and Safety

This unit explains how unethical actions affect workplace safety, employee well-being, and

overall organisational performance.

2.1 Impact on Workplace Safety

When ethical standards are ignored, safety rules may be bypassed. This increases the risk of

accidents, injuries, and non-compliance with OHS regulations.

2.2 Effects on Employee Well-Being

Unethical conduct such as dishonesty, favouritism, or negligence can lower morale and create

stress, leading to reduced productivity and job satisfaction.

24

2.3 Consequences for the Organisation

Unethical behaviour can result in penalties, financial loss, and reputational damage. It

undermines public trust and weakens workplace relationships.

2.4 Ethical Implications for Auditors

Auditors must remain impartial, truthful, and professional when reviewing safety systems. Any

bias or dishonesty can compromise the accuracy of audit results.

2.5 Building an Ethical Safety Culture

An ethical safety culture encourages employees to act responsibly, report hazards honestly, and

support fair decision-making. Integrity and accountability help sustain a safe and transparent

workplace.

Unit 3: KT0203 An Occupational Health and Safety Monitoring System

vs Ethical Behaviour to Ensure Its Effectiveness

This unit explores how ethical behaviour supports the success of OHS monitoring systems and

helps maintain compliance and continuous improvement.

3.1 Understanding Ethical Behaviour in OHS Monitoring

Ethical behaviour in monitoring means collecting, analysing, and reporting safety data honestly

and accurately. It ensures that safety assessments reflect the true condition of the workplace.

3.2 The Role of Ethical Behaviour in Effective OHS Monitoring

When auditors and employees act ethically, safety systems remain reliable and transparent.

Ethical practices promote accuracy, accountability, and continuous improvement.

3.3 Consequences of Unethical Behaviour in OHS Monitoring

Dishonesty or manipulation of safety reports can lead to false results, accidents, and legal

penalties. It also weakens trust between management and employees.

3.4 Ethics and the Path to Effective OHS Systems

Strong ethical standards ensure that monitoring processes are fair, consistent, and trustworthy.

Ethical leadership inspires responsible behaviour and strengthens the organisation’s overall

safety culture.
