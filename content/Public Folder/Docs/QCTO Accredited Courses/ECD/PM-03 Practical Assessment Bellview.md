PM-03-PS01: Observe and assess the child's behaviour and progress
Complete the following activities in the presence of your facilitator/instructor who must observe
the activity and complete the attached observation evaluation form.
Activity 1
Simulation Scenario:
You are a practitioner in an early childhood setting, working with children aged 0-6 years.
Task: Identify and Describe Aspects of Children's Learning and Development (PA0101)
Identify and describe the aspects of children's learning and development, aligned with the Early
Years Foundation Stage (EYFS) or relevant framework.
Aspects of Children's Learning and Development:
1. Physical Development
2. Communication and Language
3. Personal, Social and Emotional Development
4. Literacy
5. Mathematics
6. Understanding the World
7. Expressive Arts and Design
Deliverables:
Provide the following:
1. Written Report (approx. 500 words): Describe the aspects of children's learning and
development.
2. Child Development Checklist (approx. 2-3 pages): Identify key milestones for each
aspect.
3. Case Study (approx. 250 words): Illustrate how you applied your knowledge in a
real-life scenario.
11

Example Template:
Written Report:
Children's learning and development encompass various aspects, including:
1. Physical Development: Gross and fine motor skills
2. Communication and Language: Verbal and non-verbal communication
3. Personal, Social and Emotional Development: Self-awareness and relationships
Child Development Checklist:
Physical Development:
● 12-18 months: Sits unaided
● 2-3 years: Dresses/undresses self
● 4-5 years: Demonstrates balance and coordination
Case Study:
While observing a 3-year-old child, I noticed she struggled with sharing toys. I supported her
social-emotional development by modeling sharing and encouraging empathy.
Assessment Criteria:
1. Depth of understanding of children's learning and development
2. Accuracy of identification and description
3. Relevance to EYFS or relevant framework
4. Effectiveness of application in real-life scenario
5. Clarity and organization of written report
Grading:
● Satisfactory (40-60%): Meets minimum requirements
● Good (61-75%): Demonstrates good understanding of children's learning and
development
● Excellent (76-90%): Shows exceptional ability to identify and describe aspects
● Outstanding (91-100%): Exceeds expectations, demonstrating innovative and
child-centered approach
Please provide your submissions for the written report, child development checklist, and case study.

Activity 2
Simulation Scenario:
You are a practitioner in an early childhood setting, working with children aged 0-6 years.
Task: Assess Children's Progress, Performance, and Participation (PA0102)
Assess the progress, performance, and participation of at least two children in each of the two broad
phases:
Phase 1: Early Years (0-3 years) Phase 2: Foundation Years (4-6 years)
Assessment Requirements:
1. Conduct observations of at least two children in each phase.
2. Assess progress, performance, and participation.
3. Consider diverse learners (e.g., children with special needs, English language learners).
Deliverables:
Provide the following:
1. Observation Records (approx. 2-3 pages per child): Document observations.
2. Assessment Reports (approx. 250 words per child): Summarize progress, performance,
and participation.
3. Reflection and Analysis (approx. 300 words): Discuss implications for practice.
Example Template:
Observation Record (Child 1, Phase 1):
Date: [Insert Date]
Age: 2 years 6 months Activity: Block Play
Observations: Demonstrated problem-solving skills, used verbal communication.
Assessment Report (Child 1, Phase 1):
Progress: Developing problem-solving skills
Performance: Demonstrates curiosity Participation: Engages in group activities
Reflection and Analysis:
13

Observing children in different phases highlighted the importance of adapting assessment strategies.
Diverse learners require tailored approaches.
Assessment Criteria:
1. Accuracy and thoroughness of observations
2. Effectiveness of assessment reports
3. Consideration of diverse learners
4. Depth of reflection and analysis
5. Alignment with early childhood frameworks (e.g., EYFS, )
Grading:
● Satisfactory (40-60%): Meets minimum requirements
● Good (61-75%): Demonstrates good understanding of assessment
● Excellent (76-90%): Shows exceptional ability to assess progress
● Outstanding (91-100%): Exceeds expectations, demonstrating innovative and
child-centered approach
Please provide your submissions for the observation records, assessment reports, and reflection and
analysis.
Additional Tips:
● Use standardized assessment tools (e.g., Early Years Foundation Stage Profile)
● Consider cultural and individual differences
● Emphasize the importance of ongoing observation and assessment
● Provide specific examples from your experience
● Demonstrate knowledge of child development theories

Activity 3
Simulation Scenario:
You are a practitioner in an early childhood setting, working with children aged 0-6 years.
Task 1: Inform Practice, Referrals, and Individual Support Plans (PA0103)
Use observation and assessment records and reports to:
1. Inform practice and planning
2. Identify referrals to specialists (e.g., speech therapist)
3. Develop individual support plans/programs
Deliverables:
1. Case Study (approx. 300 words): Illustrate how observation and assessment informed
practice.
2. Individual Support Plan (approx. 2-3 pages): Develop a plan for a child requiring
additional support.
3. Referral Letter (approx. 1 page): Write a letter referring a child to a specialist.
Example Template:
Case Study:
Observations indicated a child struggled with communication. I adapted my practice, incorporating
visual aids and referring the child to a speech therapist.
Individual Support Plan:
Child's Name: [Insert Name] Age: [Insert Age]
Goals: Improve communication skills
Strategies: Visual aids, small group instruction
15

Referral Letter:
To: Speech Therapist From: [Your Name]
Subject: Referral for [Child's Name] Reason: Communication difficulties
Task 2: Reflect and Review Observation, Assessment, and Reporting Practices (PA0104)
Reflect on and review your observation, assessment, and reporting practices.
Deliverables:
1. Reflective Journal (approx. 300 words): Reflect on strengths and areas for
improvement.
2. Self-Assessment Checklist: Evaluate your practices.
3. Action Plan (approx. 200 words): Outline strategies for improvement.
Example Template:
Reflective Journal:
I realized the importance of regular observations and assessments. I will improve my reporting
by incorporating more specific examples.
Self-Assessment Checklist:
Criteria Strengths Areas for Improvement
Observation Regular observations Increase frequency
Assessment Accurate assessments Use more standardized tools
Action Plan:
1. Attend professional development workshops on assessment.
2. Implement standardized assessment tools.
3. Increase observation frequency.

Activity 4
Here's a detailed simulation for Practical Task Assessment on Recording and Reporting
Children's Progress, focusing on PA0201:
Simulation Scenario:
You are a practitioner in an early childhood setting, working with children aged 0-6 years.
Task: Monitor and Record Children's Progress (PA0201)
Monitor and record the progress of at least two children, considering their age and stage.
Recording Requirements:
1. Use standardized recording tools (e.g., learning journals, progress tracking sheets)
2. Record observations, assessments, and evaluations
3. Monitor progress across various areas (e.g., physical, cognitive, social-emotional)
Deliverables:
1. Child Progress Records (approx. 2-3 pages per child): Document progress.
2. Progress Tracking Sheets (approx. 1-2 pages per child): Illustrate progress.
3. Reflective Report (approx. 300 words): Discuss monitoring and recording processes.
Example Template:
Child Progress Record (Child 1):
Name: Emily
Age: 3 years 6 months Date: [Insert Date]
Observations: Demonstrated improved fine motor skills.
18

Progress Tracking Sheet (Child 1):
Area Initial Current Target
Fine Motor Emerging Developing Secure
Reflective Report:
Monitoring and recording children's progress helps identify areas for support. Standardized tools
ensure consistency.
Assessment Criteria:
1. Accuracy and thoroughness of records
2. Effectiveness of recording tools
3. Depth of reflection and self-awareness
4. Alignment with early childhood frameworks (e.g., EYFS, NAEYC)
5. Clarity and organization of records
Grading:
● Satisfactory (40-60%): Meets minimum requirements
● Good (61-75%): Demonstrates good understanding of recording progress
● Excellent (76-90%): Shows exceptional ability to monitor and record progress
● Outstanding (91-100%): Exceeds expectations, demonstrating innovative and
child-centered approach
Please provide your submissions for the child progress records, progress tracking sheets, and
reflective report.
Additional Tips:

● Involve children and parents in recording processes
● Consider cultural and individual differences
● Emphasize the importance of ongoing monitoring and recording
● Demonstrate knowledge of child development theories

ACTIVITY 5
Simulation Scenario:
You are a practitioner in an early childhood setting, working with children aged 0-6 years.
Task: Report on Children's Progress (PA0202)
Report on the progress of at least two children, using appropriate templates and considering
additional factors.
Reporting Requirements:
1. Use standardized reporting templates (e.g., progress reports, parent-teacher
conference forms)
2. Include feedback to parents and relevant support structures (e.g., speech therapists)
3. Consider additional factors (e.g., cultural background, special needs)
Deliverables:
1. Progress Report (approx. 2-3 pages per child): Complete a standardized template.
2. Parent-Teacher Conference Report (approx. 1-2 pages per child): Document feedback.
3. Additional Considerations Report (approx. 200 words): Discuss factors not included in
templates.
Example Template:
Progress Report (Child 1):
Name: Emily
Age: 3 years 6 months
Progress:
● Fine motor skills: Developing
● Social-emotional development: Secure
20

Date: [Insert Date]
Attendees: Parent, Practitioner
Discussion: Emily's progress, areas for support.
Additional Considerations Report:
For Child 1, cultural background influences learning styles. Adaptations include incorporating
traditional music.
Assessment Criteria:
1. Accuracy and completeness of reports
2. Effectiveness of reporting templates
3. Depth of consideration for additional factors
4. Clarity and organization of reports
5. Alignment with early childhood frameworks (e.g., EYFS, NAEYC)
Grading:

● Excellent (76-90%): Shows exceptional ability to report progress
● Outstanding (91-100%): Exceeds expectations, demonstrating innovative and
child-centered approach
Please provide your submissions for the progress report, parent-teacher conference report, and

Additional Tips:
