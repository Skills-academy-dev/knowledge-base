Welcome
Welcome to Your Promotion of Health, Safety, and Well-being of Children Study Guide!
This study guide is designed especially for distance learning students — to make your study
journey smooth, engaging, and empowering, even when you’re learning from home.
Studying Promotion of Health, Safety, and Well-being of Children will equip you with the
essential knowledge and practical skills to create safe, nurturing, and healthy environments for
young children. You’ll learn how to identify potential risks, promote positive health practices, and
support children’s physical, emotional, and social well-being every day.
This subject explores key areas such as health promotion, nutrition, hygiene, safety
procedures, emotional well-being, and child protection. You’ll also discover how your
actions as an Early Childhood Development (ECD) practitioner directly influence children’s
overall growth and happiness — helping them build confidence and resilience in a caring
environment.
This guide will help you:
● Understand the key principles of children’s health, safety, and well-being.
● Review module and unit summaries for easier revision.
● Prepare effectively for tests, assignments, and final assessments.
● Apply health and safety strategies in real-life ECD settings.
Each module follows the structure of your textbook and summarises the main ideas in clear,
easy-to-understand language designed for self-paced study.
Remember: Prioritising children’s safety and well-being is the foundation of quality ECD
practice. Stay organised, keep a positive attitude, and reach out to your tutor or support team
whenever you need guidance.
Let’s begin your journey into Promoting the Health, Safety, and Well-being of Children —
because when children feel safe and cared for, they can truly flourish and grow!

How to Use the Study Guide
This study guide is designed to make your learning journey easier, especially as a distance
learning student. It will help you understand your textbook, stay organised, and prepare
effectively for assessments.Here’s how to use it for Planning and Programme Development
in Early Childhood Settings:
1. Start with the Module Overview
Each module begins with a short introduction outlining the main focus areas and learning
outcomes. Read this first to understand what the module covers and how it connects to planning
and programme design in ECD.
2. Read the Unit Summaries
Each module is divided into smaller units. These unit summaries explain key concepts — such
as planning, routines, play-based learning, and evaluation — in clear, simple language. Read
them before your textbook for a preview or after to reinforce your understanding.
3. Highlight Key Concepts
Use a highlighter or sticky notes to mark important terms, frameworks, and principles. This helps
you focus during revision and quickly find information related to planning, development, and
evaluation.
4. Take Notes and Reflect
Leave space to write your own notes, examples, and reflections. Try linking what you learn to
real-life ECD settings — such as planning a daily routine or designing an inclusive classroom
activity. Writing in your own words strengthens understanding.
5. Prepare for Assessments
Use the guide to revise before assignments or tests. Each module includes key points, reflection
prompts, and practical examples to help you apply what you’ve learned to realistic ECD
planning situations.
6. Refer to Your Textbook for More Detail
This guide summarises and supports your textbook — it does not replace it. Always refer back
to the textbook for full explanations, visuals, and examples related to programme development
and daily planning.
6

7. Manage Your Time Wisely
Work through one module at a time. Create a study plan, set weekly goals, and review what
you’ve learned regularly. Consistency helps you stay organised and confident in your progress.
8. Ask for Help When Needed
If something is unclear, write down your questions and discuss them with your tutor or study
group. Asking questions shows curiosity and commitment — both essential qualities of an
effective ECD practitioner and planner.
7

7 Study Tips
1. Break Down Big Topics into Smaller Parts: This subject covers many ideas such as
programme planning, daily routines, play-based learning, and the use of ICT. Don’t try to
learn everything at once — focus on one concept at a time, like developmentally
appropriate practice or flexible routines, and make sure you understand it before moving
on.
2. Apply What You Learn to Real Settings: Many assessments ask you to apply theory to
real ECD situations. Practise by thinking about how you would design a daily
programme, plan a play activity, or adapt lessons for children with different needs.
3. Make Flashcards for Key Terms: There are important terms to remember, such as
holistic development, curriculum framework, and programme evaluation. Use flashcards
or apps like Quizlet to test yourself and strengthen your memory.
4. Connect Theory to Practice: Think about how what you learn affects real children and
classrooms. For example, when studying routines, consider how predictable schedules
help children feel safe and ready to learn.
5. Test Yourself Often: After each module, answer the practice questions or reflection
prompts in your study guide without checking your notes. This helps you identify what
you know and what needs more review.
6. Use the Study Guide for Quick Reviews: Before starting a new module, read its
summary for an overview. Revisit it again before tests or assignments for quick revision.
7. Manage Your Time Well: Create a simple study plan and set small weekly goals.
Consistent study, even for short periods each day, will help you stay organised and
confident throughout your course.

MODULE 1:
HEALTH AND NUTRITIONAL NEEDS
OF CHILDREN AND PREGNANT
WOMEN
Learning Outcomes
After studying this module, you should be able to:
1. Explain the concepts of health, safety, and well-being as they apply to children and
pregnant women.
2. Identify and describe the basic health needs of children and how to support them in a
safe, nurturing environment.
3. Discuss the key health requirements of pregnant women, including rest, exercise, and
breast care.
4. Demonstrate understanding of the importance of proper nutrition during pregnancy and
early childhood.
5. Apply guidelines for child nutrition and menu planning that are sensitive to cultural and
developmental needs.
6. Encourage healthy habits that promote physical, emotional, and social well-being for
mothers and children.
9

Introduction
This module focuses on promoting the health and well-being of children and pregnant women
through understanding their specific needs.
Health, safety, and well-being are closely linked — each contributing to overall quality of life.
Learners explore how proper nutrition, exercise, and emotional support influence growth,
development, and positive outcomes for both mothers and children.
Refer to Learner Manual: Pages 8
Unit 1: KT0101 — Age-Appropriate Approaches to Health Needs
Understanding the health needs of children and pregnant women helps prevent illness and
promote wellness. Both groups depend on safe, stable environments and balanced lifestyles for
growth and development.
Key Concepts
● Health: Complete physical, mental, and social well-being.
● Safety: Protection from harm or danger.
● Well-being: Overall balance between physical, mental, and social health.
Children’s Basic Health Needs
1. Stable, loving environments.
2. Balanced nutrition and hydration.
3. Consistent discipline and structure.
4. Regular physical exercise and outdoor play.
5. Emotional support and affection.
10

Pregnant Women’s Health Needs
1. Balanced nutrition (iron, calcium, folate, protein).
2. Adequate rest and sleep.
3. Gentle exercise for circulation and strength.
4. Breast care and hygiene in preparation for breastfeeding.
Example:
Thandi, who is five months pregnant, follows a healthy routine that includes nutritious meals,
morning walks, and rest after lunch.
Where to find in Learner Manual: Pages 8-11
Unit 2: KT0102 — Age-Appropriate Approaches to Nutritional Needs
Nutrition supports healthy growth for both children and pregnant women. Balanced diets, meal
planning, and cultural sensitivity are central to promoting well-being.
Key Concepts
● Food Groups:
○ Grains and starches – energy
○ Fruits and vegetables – vitamins and minerals
○ Dairy – calcium
○ Protein – growth and repair
○ Fats and treats – moderation
Meal Planning Guidelines
● Provide variety and colour.
● Include water or milk as main drinks.
● Limit sugar and processed snacks.
● Use affordable, local foods.
Example:
A crèche in KwaZulu-Natal serves maize porridge, apples, and samp with beans — a balanced,
affordable menu.
11

Fun Facts
● Taste preferences develop in the womb.
● Children who eat breakfast perform better in school.
● Water is the most essential nutrient.
Where to find in Learner Manual: Pages 12-14
Summary
This module explains that health involves physical, emotional, and social well-being — not just
the absence of illness. It highlights how stability, affection, rest, nutrition, and exercise all
contribute to development. Learners understand how to plan healthy meals and routines for
children and pregnant women, promoting lifelong wellness.

Introduction
This module explores how ECD practitioners and caregivers promote the health, safety, and
protection of children through planned interventions.
It focuses on maintaining clean, safe environments and ensuring that children receive proper
nutrition and emotional care. You will learn how prevention, early action, and collaboration with
families help ensure every child thrives.
Refer to Learner Manual: Pages 21
Unit 1: KT0201 — Interventions to Promote Health and Nutrition
Health promotion includes all the daily practices that keep children healthy and well-nourished.
These involve hygiene routines, nutritious meals, rest periods, and regular health checks.
Key Concepts
● Health Promotion: Actions that improve or maintain health (e.g., handwashing, physical
activity).
● Nutrition Intervention: Providing balanced meals and teaching good eating habits.
● Preventive Care: Identifying and reducing risks before illness develops.
Health Promotion Activities
1. Daily hygiene routines – handwashing, tooth brushing, clean clothing.
2. Health checks – monitoring growth, noting changes in appetite or energy.
3. Physical activity – free play, dancing, or outdoor time.
Example:
An ECD teacher encourages children to wash their hands before eating and after play. Posters
remind them of healthy habits.
14

Nutrition Interventions
● Provide nutritious meals with proteins, fruits, and vegetables.
● Educate parents about healthy lunchboxes.
● Avoid sugary drinks and snacks at the centre.
● Include children in simple food-preparation activities to build awareness.

Unit 2: KT0202 — Interventions for Safety, Protection and Well-being
Child safety and protection are central to all ECD environments. Practitioners have both ethical
and legal duties to prevent accidents, recognise signs of abuse, and maintain secure spaces for
children.
Safety Practices
1. Conduct daily safety inspections of the classroom and play area.
2. Store cleaning products and sharp objects safely out of reach.
3. Keep emergency numbers visible.
4. Ensure gates and doors are locked during operating hours.
Child Protection Responsibilities
● Be alert to changes in behaviour or appearance.
● Record and report suspected abuse to the appropriate authorities.
● Follow the Children’s Act (Act 38 of 2005) guidelines.
● Maintain confidentiality and compassion at all times.
Example:
After noticing bruises on a child, the teacher records the observation confidentially and reports it
to the principal, following the centre’s policy.
15

Promoting Emotional Well-being
● Encourage open communication and respect.
● Praise effort and kindness.
● Create predictable routines to help children feel safe and secure.
● Build positive relationships between staff, parents, and children.

Unit 3: KT0203 - Roles and Responsibilities of Early Childhood
Practitioners in Recognising Signs of Illnesses, including Chronic
Conditions
Early Childhood Practitioners play a vital role in ensuring children’s health and safety by
identifying early signs of illness and responding appropriately. Practitioners must understand
common and chronic conditions to provide suitable support and reduce risks.
Key responsibilities include:
● Observation and Recognition: Identify symptoms such as fatigue, rashes, coughing, or
changes in behaviour that may indicate illness.
● Training and Knowledge: Understand different illnesses and their effects on children’s
physical, emotional, and cognitive development.
● Partnership with Families and Professionals: Share relevant health information with
parents, health practitioners, and support agencies.
● Inclusive Practice: Ensure all children, including those with chronic conditions, are
supported and included in activities.
● Safe Working Practice: Maintain hygiene, record and report illnesses, prevent
cross-infection, and seek emotional support when managing challenging cases.
Remember: Practitioners are the first line of defence in keeping children healthy and safe
through awareness, communication, and care.

Unit 4: KT0204 Various Types of Injuries and Signs of Life-threatening
Illnesses
Children are naturally active and curious, which increases the risk of injuries and illnesses.
Understanding the types, symptoms, and emergency responses is essential for all ECD
practitioners.
Common Injuries:
● Burns: Caused by heat, steam, or electricity. Prevent through supervision and safety
barriers.
● Poisoning: Occurs from swallowing or inhaling toxic substances; store chemicals safely.
● Falls: Common in toddlers learning to walk; prevent with soft flooring and child-safe
furniture.
● Choking and Suffocation: Avoid small objects and ensure food is age-appropriate.
Signs of Life-threatening Illnesses:
● Meningitis: Stiff neck, high fever, and rash that doesn’t fade under pressure.
● Sepsis: Bluish skin, rapid breathing, and lethargy.
● Asthma: Severe wheezing or difficulty breathing.
● Anaphylaxis: Swelling, difficulty breathing, and collapse after allergen exposure.
Early recognition saves lives — knowing when and how to act is key to protecting children’s
well-being.
Where to find in Learner Manual: Pages 39
