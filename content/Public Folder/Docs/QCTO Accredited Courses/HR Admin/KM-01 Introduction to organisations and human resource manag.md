Introduction
Organisations and Human Resource Management Are All Around You
They’re in the teamwork that drives a successful business, the motivation that keeps
employees performing at their best, and the leadership that guides a company through
change. In simple terms, Human Resource Management (HRM) is about understanding
people at work — how they’re recruited, developed, supported, and inspired to achieve both
personal and organisational success.
By learning about organisations and HRM, you will:
● Understand how organisations work – You’ll explore how businesses are
structured, managed, and coordinated to achieve their goals. You’ll learn about
organisational culture, communication systems, and the roles different departments
play.
● Learn how to manage and support people – You’ll discover the principles of
human resource management, from recruitment and selection to performance
management, training, and employee relations.
● Develop strong leadership and teamwork skills – You’ll learn how to motivate
employees, manage conflict, and build positive, productive workplace relationships.
● Promote fairness and ethical behaviour – You’ll understand employment laws,
equity principles, and how to create inclusive workplaces where everyone feels
respected and valued.
● Build professional and managerial skills – Through HRM, you’ll gain practical
knowledge in communication, problem-solving, planning, and leadership — preparing
you for rewarding careers in management, administration, or human resources.
● Shape successful organisations – As an HR or management professional, you’ll
play a key role in helping organisations grow, adapt, and succeed in an
ever-changing business environment.

Introduction
Every business, school, hospital, or community project is an organisation — a group of
people working together to achieve a shared goal. Some organisations aim to make a profit,
while others focus on providing services or improving society. Regardless of their purpose,
all organisations depend on people, structure, and systems to function effectively.
This unit introduces what an organisation is, how it operates, and how different activities
within it create value for customers and stakeholders. Understanding how organisations
work is essential for anyone entering management, administration, or human resources.
Unit 1 KT0101 Definition of an organisation and the generic
organisational value chain
1.1 Definition of an Organisation
An organisation is a structured group of people who work together to achieve shared goals
and objectives. It exists within a defined framework of systems, structures, and relationships
that guide how tasks are performed and decisions are made.
At its core, an organisation brings together people, resources, and processes to produce
goods or deliver services efficiently.

Key Components of an Organisation
1. Structural Framework – Defines how work is divided, coordinated, and reported.
Structures may be hierarchical, flat, or matrix-based, depending on the size and
goals of the organisation.
2. Purpose and Objectives – The organisation’s mission, vision, and goals provide
direction and help measure success.
3. Culture and Values – The shared beliefs and behaviours that shape how employees
interact and make decisions. A positive culture builds unity and motivation.
4. Processes and Systems – Procedures and technologies that ensure efficient
workflow, coordination, and quality control.
5. People and Human Resources – The employees who bring the organisation to life.
Human Resource Management ensures they are recruited, trained, and supported
effectively.
6. Leadership and Governance – Leaders provide direction, make decisions, and
ensure accountability through proper management and oversight structures.
7. Communication and Collaboration – Clear communication builds trust, encourages
innovation, and supports teamwork.
8. Adaptability and Change Management – Successful organisations remain flexible
and responsive to internal and external changes.
9. External Relations and Stakeholder Engagement – Building strong relationships
with clients, suppliers, regulators, and communities ensures sustainability and
reputation.
10. Performance Evaluation and Improvement – Measuring progress using key
performance indicators (KPIs) helps identify strengths and areas for improvement.
An organisation is a dynamic system where people and resources work together in a
coordinated way to achieve common goals. Its success depends on effective leadership,
teamwork, communication, and the ability to adapt to change.

Unit 3 KT0103 The concept of staff, line, and professional work in
organisations
Every organisation depends on people working together — but not everyone does the same
kind of work. Some employees focus on the core operations (like producing or selling
products), while others provide specialised support or professional expertise.
To keep everything running smoothly, organisations divide work into three main
categories:
1. Line Work – the core operations directly linked to production or service delivery.
2. Staff Work – supportive functions that assist and guide line operations.
3. Professional Work – specialised roles requiring advanced qualifications or expert
knowledge.
Understanding how these three roles work together helps managers coordinate activities,
delegate authority, and achieve organisational goals effectively.
3.1. Line Work
Line work forms the backbone of every organisation. These are the roles directly
responsible for achieving the organisation’s primary mission — producing goods, delivering
services, and interacting with customers.
Key Features of Line Work
● Directly connected to the organisation’s core purpose.
● Involves decision-making authority over day-to-day operations.
● Focuses on achieving targets, productivity, and efficiency.
● Often involves frontline roles that impact customer experience.
3.2. Staff Work
While line employees carry out the core operations, staff functions provide vital support,
advice, and expertise. They ensure that line departments operate efficiently and comply with
organisational standards.
Key Features of Staff Work
● Offer specialised services such as HR, finance, or IT.
● Function in an advisory capacity to guide and support line management.
● Focus on efficiency, compliance, and long-term strategy.
● Often involved in planning, coordination, and analysis rather than direct
production.
21

3.3. Professional Work
Professional work involves roles that require specialised expertise, advanced education,
or professional accreditation. These individuals provide deep technical or creative insight
that supports both line and staff activities.
Key Features of Professional Work
● Involves intellectual or creative problem-solving.
● Requires higher education or certification in a specific field.
● Adheres to professional ethics and standards.
● Promotes innovation, improvement, and knowledge development.
3.4. How Line, Staff, and Professional Work Interact
These three types of work don’t operate in isolation — they depend on one another for the
organisation to function effectively.
● Line functions drive operations and service delivery.
● Staff functions provide the tools, policies, and support systems.
● Professional functions add specialised expertise and innovation.
Example of Interaction
In a hospital setting:
● The doctors and nurses (line) provide direct patient care.
● The HR and Finance departments (staff) handle recruitment, payroll, and
budgeting.
● The specialist consultants (professional) offer expert medical or technical input.

3.5. The Importance of Balancing These Roles
A well-functioning organisation must maintain balance among all three types of work:
● Too much line focus can lead to burnout and poor compliance.
● Too much staff control can cause bureaucracy and delays.
● Too much professional independence can reduce teamwork.
Effective managers know how to integrate these roles to enhance efficiency, quality, and
innovation.
Did You Know?
Modern organisations often use cross-functional teams — mixing line, staff, and
professional members — to improve coordination and decision-making.
Together, they form a balanced system that ensures smooth operations, compliance,
creativity, and growth.

Unit 4 KT0104 Definition of management and the functions and
activities of management
4.1. What is Management?
Management is the process of coordinating people, resources, and activities to achieve
organisational goals effectively and efficiently. It involves planning, organising, leading,
and controlling to ensure success.
In simple terms, management is about getting work done through others — using
available resources wisely while maintaining balance between performance and people.
Did You Know?
The word “manage” comes from the Italian “maneggiare”, meaning “to handle”. It originally
referred to training horses — later evolving into handling people and organisations!
4.2. Importance of Management
Good management is the heartbeat of every organisation. It ensures that goals are achieved
efficiently, people are motivated, and operations run smoothly.
Why Management Matters
● Ensures effective use of resources
● Builds teamwork and discipline
● Helps in decision-making and coordination
● Encourages innovation and problem-solving
● Maintains quality, morale, and productivity
Fun Fact:
Even in your daily life — planning your day, budgeting your money, or organising your
studies — you’re applying management principles without realising it!

4.4.1 Planning
Planning is the first and most important management function. It defines what the
organisation aims to achieve and how it will get there.
Key Activities:
● Setting SMART goals (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound)
● Analysing internal and external environments (SWOT analysis)
● Developing strategies and budgets
● Reviewing and adjusting plans regularly

4.4.2 Organising

Key Activities:
● Dividing work into departments or teams
● Assigning tasks and delegating authority
● Establishing reporting relationships
● Setting up communication channels

● Mentoring and resolving conflicts
Effective leaders:
● Lead by example
● Recognise achievements
● Promote collaboration and creativity
