Introduction

This module builds the foundation for understanding how data functions within an

depends on accurate, secure, and well-governed data processes. A strong grasp of these

basics ensures that HR professionals can handle information responsibly and effectively.

Unit 1- KT0101 Definitions of Terms and Concepts Associated with

Data and Data Management

1.1 Understanding Data

Data consists of raw, unprocessed facts. It does not carry meaning until it is interpreted. HR

departments rely on different types of raw data—like attendance logs, survey answers, or job

application details—to later generate insights that support decision‑making.

1.2 Information

Information is the result of organising or analysing data so it becomes meaningful. HR uses

information to make decisions about staffing, performance, training, and compliance—for

example, converting attendance data into absenteeism rates.

1.3 Data Management

Data management is the structured handling of data throughout its entire journey. It ensures

that information remains accurate, updated, and secure. Good data management reduces

errors, supports compliance, and helps HR deliver reliable reports.

1.4 Data Lifecycle

The data lifecycle shows how data moves through stages—from creation to disposal. Each

stage requires correct handling to maintain security, accuracy, and legal compliance. HR

must ensure that sensitive information (like IDs or medical records) is stored, used, and

removed properly.

1.5 HR Data

HR data includes any information about employees or HR processes. This can include

personal records, payroll details, training results, and performance scores. Because this data

is sensitive, it must always be handled with confidentiality and care.

1.6 Data Quality

High‑quality data is reliable and useful. To maintain quality, HR checks for accuracy,

completeness, consistency, and timeliness. Poor data quality can lead to incorrect payroll,

unfair decisions, or failed audits.

10

1.7 Data Security

Data security protects information from being accessed or changed by unauthorised

individuals. HR uses passwords, restricted access, encryption, and secure storage to protect

confidential employee information.

1.8 Data Privacy

Privacy ensures that personal information is used lawfully and ethically. HR collects only

necessary data and informs employees how it will be used. POPIA requires organisations to

protect personal data and respect employee rights.

1.9 Data Governance

Data governance includes the policies, roles, and controls that guide how data is handled. It

ensures compliance, accuracy, and ethical use of information. Governance helps prevent

errors, misuse, and data breaches.

Where to find in Learner Manual: Pages 9-14

Unit 2 – KT0102 The Data Management Process

2.1 Overview of the Data Management Process

The process includes sequential steps that guide how data is handled from the moment it is

collected until it is securely disposed of. Each step ensures that HR’s information remains

trustworthy and useful.

2.2 Data Collection

This step gathers data from various sources such as applications, attendance systems,

performance forms, and digital platforms. Correct collection methods ensure that HR

captures the right information for the right purpose.

2.3 Data Storage

Storage ensures that collected data is kept safely and remains accessible when needed.

This can include physical storage (cabinets), digital storage (HRIS), or a combination of

both.

2.4 Data Processing

Processing turns raw data into usable formats. It includes capturing, editing, sorting,

organising, and cleaning data. HR often standardises formats to maintain consistency across

systems.

2.5 Data Analysis

Analysis helps HR interpret information and identify trends. By analysing data, HR can

detect issues such as high absenteeism, low training completion, or turnover spikes.

11

2.6 Data Reporting / Presentation

Reporting shares analysed findings through written or visual formats. HR uses reports to

inform managers and ensure compliance with statutory requirements.

2.7 Data Archiving

Archiving stores inactive or historical data for future reference. Archived HR records support

audits, investigations, and compliance checks.

2.8 Data Disposal

Secure disposal ensures that outdated or unnecessary data is permanently destroyed. HR

follows strict procedures to prevent privacy breaches.

2.9 The Data Management Cycle

The cycle is continuous. Once data is disposed of, new data is collected again. HR must

consistently apply the same processes to maintain reliable and ethical data handling.

Where to find in Learner Manual: Pages 15-20

Unit 3 - KT0103 General Data Management Governance

3.1 Understanding Data Governance

Data governance establishes responsibility, oversight, and structure in the handling of

organisational data. It reduces risk and strengthens transparency.

3.2 Principles of Data Governance

Core principles include accountability, accuracy, security, compliance, ethical use, and

transparency. These principles guide all decisions and actions taken when working with

data.

3.3 Policies and Procedures for Data Governance

Policies translate governance principles into practical rules. They guide how data is

collected, stored, accessed, and shared. Procedures ensure consistency and prevent

misuse.

3.4 Data Quality Standards

Data must meet specific standards to be considered high quality. HR regularly checks for

errors, missing information, outdated records, and incorrect entries.

3.5 Data Security and Privacy

Governance requires organisations to protect employee information from internal and

external threats. Security and privacy policies ensure compliance with POPIA and other

laws.

12

3.6 Roles and Responsibilities in Data Governance

Different departments share responsibility: HR manages employee records; IT secures

systems; managers ensure accurate submissions; employees provide truthful information.

3.7 Monitoring and Auditing

Monitoring ensures ongoing accuracy and compliance. Auditing identifies weaknesses,

gaps, or risks in data handling practices.

3.8 Risks of Poor Governance

Weak governance leads to legal penalties, data breaches, incorrect reporting, reputational

harm, and operational inefficiencies.

Unit 1 – KT0201 Sources of HR Data

HR data is gathered from both inside and outside the organisation. Each source provides

unique information that supports HR operations.

1.1 Internal Sources of HR Data

Internal sources come directly from within the organisation. These sources offer real-time,

employee-specific, and process-related information.

●  Personnel Files: Contain personal details, qualification documents, disciplinary

records, contracts, and employment history.

●  HR Information Systems (HRIS): Centralised digital systems storing updated

employee data.

●  Attendance and Timekeeping Systems: Biometric systems, electronic clock-ins,

and attendance registers.

●  Payroll Systems: Salary, benefits, overtime, deductions, and tax information.

●  Performance Records: Ratings, development plans, KPIs, and evaluation

outcomes.

●  Training Records: Courses attended, skills acquired, and training results.

●  Recruitment Records: CVs, interview notes, screening outcomes, and appointment

details. Internal data is essential because it reflects the organisation’s actual

workforce activities.

15

1.2 External Sources of HR Data

External sources provide additional insights about labour trends, legal requirements, and

industry benchmarks.

●  Government Reports (e.g., Stats SA): Labour force trends, unemployment rates,

and demographic statistics.

●  Legislation and Regulations: BCEA, LRA, EEA, POPIA, and SARS requirements.

●  SAQA: Validates qualifications and accreditation.

●

Industry Reports: Salary surveys, recruitment trends, and benchmarking data.

●  Professional Bodies: Standards and best practices for HR (e.g., SABPP). External

data helps HR compare internal performance with labour market expectations.

Unit 2 - KT0202 Principles of HR Data Collection

Accurate HR decisions depend on high-quality data. This unit explains the principles that

guide ethical and reliable data collection.

2.1 Importance of HR Data Collection

Correct data collection supports fair decision-making, legal compliance, reporting (e.g., EE

and WSP/ATR), performance monitoring, and workforce planning.

2.2 Principles of Effective HR Data Collection

Each principle ensures that collected data is trustworthy and useful.

●  Reliability: Using consistent methods and tools so results are stable over time.

●  Validity: Ensuring that data measures what it is supposed to measure.

●  Accuracy: Capturing correct and error-free information.

●  Relevance: Collecting only the information needed for a specific purpose.

●  Completeness: Ensuring all required fields and documents are provided.

●  Timeliness: Capturing and recording data at the appropriate time.

●  Consistency: Using uniform formats, terms, and standards across departments.

2.3 Ethical Considerations in Data Collection

HR must protect employee rights by:

●  Collecting only essential information

●  Notifying employees about how their data will be used

●  Keeping personal data confidential

●  Respecting cultural and personal sensitivities Ethical collection prevents

discrimination and supports trust.

2.4 Legal Considerations

Several laws guide what information HR may collect and how it must be handled:

●  POPIA: Protects personal information and sets rules for processing.

●  BCEA: Requires accurate recordkeeping of hours worked, leave, and remuneration.

●  LRA: Requires documented disciplinary and grievance procedures.

●  EEA: Requires demographic and workforce movement records.

●  Skills Development Act: Requires training-related information for reporting. Failure

to follow these laws results in penalties and compliance risks.

17

2.5 Factors That Influence HR Data Collection

Multiple factors guide HR’s choice of data collection method:

●  Purpose of the data (e.g., payroll, compliance, performance)

●  Type of data (numerical or descriptive)

●  Resources (time, staff, technology)

●  Sensitivity of the information

●  Legal restrictions

●  Urgency and volume of data needed Understanding these factors ensures effective

and ethical collection.

Unit 3 - KT0203 Collecting HRM Data Using a Variety of Methods

HR uses several methods to collect data depending on the type of information and its

purpose.

3.1 Methods of HR Data Collection

Each method has advantages and limitations.

●

Interviews: Provide detailed information but require time and skill.

●  Questionnaires/Surveys: Gather large amounts of feedback quickly.

●  Observation: Allows real-time assessment of behaviour or job tasks.

●  Document and Record Review: Uses existing data for accuracy and verification.

●  Focus Groups: Explore employee experiences and opinions.

●  Electronic/Automated Systems: Provide accurate, immediate digital records.

3.2 Comparing HR Data Collection Methods

Each method supports different HR needs. Interviews provide depth, surveys provide reach,

observations reveal behaviour, documents confirm facts, and automated systems improve

accuracy.

3.3 Factors Influencing the Choice of Data Collection Method

HR considers the type of information required, available resources, urgency, and

legal/privacy requirements.

18

3.4 Ethical and Legal Considerations

All methods must follow POPIA, avoid bias, maintain confidentiality, and protect sensitive

information.

Unit 4 - KT0204 Storing and Managing Collected HR Data

Once collected, HR must store and manage data in a secure and organised manner.

4.1 Purpose of Storing HR Data

HR stores data for recordkeeping, payroll accuracy, legal compliance, reporting, tracking

employee development, and supporting audits.

4.2 Methods of Storing HR Data

●  Manual Storage: Physical files and cabinets.

●  Digital Storage: HRIS, payroll software, cloud systems.

●  Hybrid Storage: Combination of manual and digital systems.

4.3 Data Organisation Methods

Data can be organised alphabetically, numerically, departmentally, or digitally using folders

and categories.

4.4 Managing Collected HR Data

HR regularly updates, verifies, and backs up data. Effective management also includes

limiting access and maintaining accuracy.

4.5 Data Access and Security

Access is restricted according to roles. HR uses passwords, encryption, locked storage, user

permissions, and confidentiality agreements.

4.6 Legal Requirements for Storing HR Data

HR must comply with POPIA, BCEA, LRA, EEA, and SARS. These laws require safe

storage, accurate records, and secure access.

4.7 Data Retention and Archiving

HR keeps data for legally required timeframes and archives older information securely for

audits or reference.

19

4.8 Data Disposal

Disposal must be secure—shredding, secure deletion, or destruction of storage media. Poor

disposal risks privacy breaches and legal penalties.

Unit 1– KT0301 Prepare Collected Data for Analysis

Before analysis begins, data must be checked, cleaned, and organised. This ensures results

are trustworthy and meaningful.

1.1 Why Data Preparation Is Important

Correct preparation ensures that data used for reports and decisions is reliable. Poorly

prepared data leads to inaccurate findings, incorrect conclusions, and organisational risks.

1.2 Steps in Preparing HR Data for Analysis

1.2.1 Data Cleaning

Removes mistakes such as spelling errors, incorrect dates, and duplicate entries. This

ensures the data reflects real information.

1.2.2 Checking for Completeness

Ensures every required field is filled in. Missing values weaken analysis and can lead to

misleading results.

1.2.3 Data Validation

Confirms that data follows rules and makes sense. HR checks formats, logical

sequences, and acceptable ranges.

1.2.4 Standardising Data

Puts data into one consistent format. This makes it easier to compare and combine

information from different sources.

1.2.5 Coding and Categorising Data

Groups information into categories or numerical codes so it can be analysed more

easily.

1.2.6 Organising Data into Tables or Spreadsheets

Information is arranged neatly into rows and columns, which makes it easier to analyse

and compare.

22

1.2.7 Verifying Data Accuracy

Cross-checks information against original sources to confirm it is correct and up to date.

1.2.8 Protecting Data During Preparation

Ensures data remains confidential. Files are password protected and only authorised

HR staff may access information.

Unit 2 – KT0302 Analyse the Data Using Various Methods

Analysis helps HR transform numbers and records into insights that answer organisational

questions.

2.1 Purpose of Data Analysis

Analysis highlights trends, identifies challenges, measures performance, and helps HR make

informed decisions.

2.2 Basic Methods of HR Data Analysis

2.2.1 Counting and Summarising Data

Shows how often something happens, such as the number of resignations or training

days.

2.2.2 Using Tables to Organise and Analyse Data

Tables allow quick comparison between departments, months, or employee groups.

2.2.3 Using Graphs and Charts

Visual tools make it easier to identify trends, differences, and patterns.

2.2.4 Calculating Basic Percentages

Percentages help HR compare groups of different sizes fairly.

2.2.5 Calculating Averages

Averages show typical values and help identify normal and abnormal patterns.

2.2.6 Identifying Patterns and Trends

Helps HR understand long-term issues such as seasonal absenteeism.

2.2.7 Comparing Groups or Categories

Reveals differences in performance or experience between teams or departments.

2.2.8 Cross-Tabulation

Compares two variables at the same time to see how they relate to each other.

23

2.2.9 Using HRIS and Software Tools

HRIS and spreadsheet tools automate calculations, generate reports, and reduce

human error.

Unit 3 -– KT0303 Interpret the Data, Draw Conclusions and Make

Recommendations

Interpretation explains what the analysed data actually means and guides HR in taking

action.

3.1 What Is Data Interpretation?

It involves explaining the significance of the results and understanding the underlying issues.

3.2 Steps in Interpreting HR Data
