Introduction

Human Resource Management is all around you.

It’s in the recruitment interview that shapes a career opportunity, the HR policy that ensures

fairness in the workplace, and the support systems that help employees perform at their best. In

simple terms, Human Resource Management (HRM) is about managing people effectively,

ethically, and strategically — ensuring that employees and organisations can grow, perform, and

succeed together.

Delivering effective HR services is a critical part of this process. It ensures that HR practices are

consistent, compliant, and aligned with organisational goals while supporting employees

throughout their employment journey. This learner manual will guide you through the key

principles, approaches, and tools used in delivering Human Resource Management services in

a professional and value-adding way.

By learning about Delivering Human Resource Management Services, you will:

● Understand how HR services are delivered within organisations – You’ll learn about

different HR service delivery approaches and models, and how they are used to support

employees, managers, and organisational objectives.

● Add value to organisations and employees – By applying effective HR service

delivery practices, you’ll help improve employee experience, operational efficiency, and

organisational performance.

● Promote professionalism, ethics, and compliance – You’ll explore professional

practice standards, ethical decision-making, and compliance with labour laws, HR

policies, and procedures.

● Build practical and strategic HR skills – You’ll develop skills in monitoring HR

services, managing HR technology and service agreements, using people analytics

responsibly, and implementing HR policies consistently.

● Support sustainable and effective workplaces – As an HR practitioner, your ability to

deliver reliable, ethical, and well-managed HR services will contribute to positive

workplace cultures, engaged employees, and long-term organisational success.

KT0101: Different Approaches and Models of HR Service Delivery

(Unit 1)

Human Resource (HR) service delivery plays a key role in how organisations support their

employees and achieve their business objectives. The way HR services are structured and

delivered affects efficiency, consistency, employee experience, and the ability of HR to

contribute strategically to the organisation.

In this unit, you will explore different approaches and models used to deliver HR services. You

will examine how organisations choose between decentralised and centralised approaches, as

well as the various HR service delivery models commonly used in practice. By understanding

these approaches and models, you will be better equipped to recognise how HR service

delivery can be aligned with organisational needs, resources, and workforce expectations.

1.1 What Is HR Service Delivery?

HR service delivery refers to the way in which the Human Resources (HR) function provides

services to employees and management within an organisation. It includes the strategies,

processes, systems, and tools used to deliver HR support in an efficient, effective, and

consistent manner.

1.2 Approaches to HR Service Delivery

The approach an organisation uses to deliver HR services has a direct impact on employee

satisfaction, operational efficiency, and organisational culture. Two common approaches to HR

service delivery are the traditional decentralised approach and the centralised shared

service approach.

Traditional (Decentralised) HR Service Delivery Approach

In this approach, HR services are delivered by HR generalists who are based within specific

departments, regions, or business units. This model is commonly found in smaller organisations

or those with less complex HR needs.

HR generalists typically manage a wide range of HR functions, including:

● Recruitment and onboarding, Employee relations and discipline

● Payroll, compensation, and benefits administration

● Training and employee development as well as performance management and feedback

Advantages of the decentralised approach:

● Personalised service tailored to local needs

● Direct access to an HR contact who understands the work environment

● Quick response to local HR issues and strong relationships between HR, employees,

and managers

● Strong relationships between HR, employees, and managers

13

Disadvantages of the decentralised approach:

● HR staff may become overloaded due to multiple responsibilities

● Limited access to specialised HR expertise

● Inconsistent HR policies and practices across departments

● Less time available for strategic HR planning

Did You Know?

Decentralised HR structures were more common before digital HR systems became widely

available, as technology now makes centralisation easier.

Centralised (Shared Service) HR Delivery Approach

In a centralised approach, HR services are consolidated into a single HR unit or shared service

centre. HR specialists manage specific HR functions for the entire organisation, either from a

central location or virtually.

Specialists may focus on areas such as:

● Recruitment and talent acquisition

● Compensation and benefits

● Training and development

● HR systems and analytics

● Employee relations and compliance

Advantages of the centralised approach:

● Standardised HR processes and policies across the organisation

● Improved efficiency through economies of scale

● Greater use of technology and automation

● Access to specialised HR expertise

● Increased focus on strategic HR initiatives

14

Disadvantages of the centralised approach:

● Reduced personal interaction between HR and employees

● Possible lack of understanding of local or departmental needs

● Risk of slower response times

● More complex approval processes

1.3 Choosing the Right Approach

There is no single best approach to HR service delivery. The most suitable approach depends

on the organisation’s specific context, needs, and resources.

Key factors to consider when choosing an HR service delivery approach include:

● Organisation size and complexity

Smaller organisations may benefit from a decentralised approach, while larger

organisations often require centralisation to manage complexity.

● HR budget and available resources

Decentralised models may require fewer specialists, while centralised models often need

greater investment in systems and infrastructure.

● Employee needs and expectations

Some employees value personalised, face-to-face HR support, while others prioritise

fast and consistent service delivery.

● Level of standardisation required

Organisations seeking consistency and efficiency may favour a centralised approach,

while those requiring flexibility may prefer decentralisation.

● Strategic HR priorities

If the organisation wants HR to focus on long-term planning, talent management, and

organisational development, a centralised model may be more effective.

Selecting the right approach ensures that HR services are aligned with organisational goals

while meeting the needs of both employees and management.

Tip

Always align the HR service delivery approach with the organisation’s strategy, not just its

current size.

reinforce the organisation’s overall business objectives. When HR operates in alignment with

organisational strategy, it moves beyond isolated HR activities and becomes an integrated

business partner.

Strategic HR alignment involves translating business goals into people-focused strategies that

● Clearly outlining employee rights and responsibilities
