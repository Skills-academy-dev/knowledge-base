Unit 1: Number Formats and Conventions

Numbers are part of daily life. We see them on price tags, bank statements, bus timetables, and

By the end of this unit, you will be able to recognise and use whole numbers, decimals, and

1.1 Whole Numbers, Decimals, and Fractions

In Mathematical Literacy, you will work with numbers in many forms. Whole numbers, decimals,

and fractions are the most common formats you will encounter in daily life. Understanding these

formats helps you read information correctly and calculate accurately.

Whole Numbers

Whole numbers are numbers without fractions or decimals. They include 0, 1, 2, 3…

Examples in Daily Life:

● Counting money: R100, R250, R1 000

● Counting items: 12 apples, 25 learners

● Bus seat numbers: Seat 1, Seat 2

9

Decimals

Decimals are numbers with a decimal point separating the whole number part from the fractional part.

Examples in Daily Life:

● Money: R45.50 (forty-five rand and fifty cents)

● Fuel price: R23.75 per litre

● Weight: 1.25 kg of sugar

Tip

Always check the number of decimal places—for money, it is usually two decimal places.

Fractions

Fractions represent a part of a whole, written as one number over another.

Examples in Daily Life:

● Cooking: ½ cup of sugar

● Sales: A store offering ¼ off the price

● Measurements: 2½ metres of fabric

Did You Know?

Fractions can be converted to decimals:

● 12 = 0. 5

● 14 = 0. 25

1.2 Converting Between Forms

Sometimes it is easier to work with a number in a different format. For example, prices are often

given as decimals (R23.75), but discounts might be given as fractions or percentages. You must

know how to convert between these forms.

Fractions to Decimals

Divide the numerator (top number) by the denominator (bottom number).

Example:

● 34 = 3 ÷ 4 = 0. 75

Decimals to Fractions

Write the decimal as a fraction with a power of 10, then simplify.

Example:

● 0. 6 = 610 = 35

Decimals to Percentages

Multiply the decimal by 100.

Example:

● 0. 75 × 100 = 75%

11

Percentages to Decimals

Divide the percentage by 100.

Example:

● 25% ÷ 100 = 0. 25

Unit 2: Operations on Numbers and Calculator Skills

In Mathematical Literacy, calculations are part of almost every problem you solve—whether you

are working out a budget, calculating a discount, or measuring materials for a project.

This unit will help you:

● Apply the correct order of operations (BODMAS) to get accurate answers.

● Use calculator functions efficiently to save time and reduce errors.

These skills are essential for both your exam success and everyday decision-making.

2.1 Order of Operations (BODMAS)

When solving a calculation, you cannot simply work from left to right—you must follow the order

of operations. Using the correct order ensures that everyone arrives at the same answer for

the same problem.

Definition

BODMAS stands for:

● B – Brackets

● O – Orders (powers and roots)

● D – Division

● M – Multiplication

● A – Addition

● S – Subtraction

This is the order in which calculations must be done.

BODMAS Example

Solve: 8 + 4 × 28 + 4

Step 1: Multiplication before addition

8 + (4 × 2)

8 + 8 = 16

15

Answer: 16

More Complex Example with Brackets

Solve: (10 + 5) × 22

Step 1: Brackets

(15) × 22

Step 2: Orders (powers)

15 × 4

Step 3: Multiplication

60

Answer: 60

Flow chart: The BODMAS Sequence

Tip

If there are multiple multiplication and division steps, work from left to right. The same

applies for addition and subtraction.

Did You Know?

Many exam mistakes happen because learners forget to apply BODMAS. Always check the

order before writing your final answer.

2.2 Using Calculator Functions

Your calculator is a powerful tool in Mathematical Literacy. Knowing how to use its functions

correctly will save time and help prevent mistakes.

Basic Calculator Functions

● Addition (+) – for adding amounts.

● Subtraction (–) – for calculating differences or reductions.

● Multiplication (×) – for repeated addition (e.g. cost per item × number of items).

● Division (÷) – for splitting amounts or finding rates.

Advanced Functions

● Square (x²) – for calculating area of squares or other formulas involving powers.

● Square root (√) – for calculations involving roots.

● Percentage (%) – for discounts, increases, or tax.

● Memory keys (M+, M–, MR) – for storing and recalling values.

Unit 3: Rounding

Rounding is used to make numbers simpler to read and work with. It is a skill you will use in

shopping, budgeting, travel planning, construction, and exams.

In this unit, you will learn how to round numbers correctly to the required accuracy—whether to

the nearest rand, nearest whole number, or to a set number of decimal places—depending on

the context.

3.1 Rounding Off to Required Accuracy

In Mathematical Literacy, the level of accuracy you use depends on the situation. Sometimes

you round to make numbers easier to communicate, but in other cases (like medicine doses or

exam marks) accuracy is critical.

Definition

Rounding: Adjusting a number to make it simpler, while keeping it close to its original value.

Rules for Rounding

● If the next digit is 5 or more, round up.

● If the next digit is 4 or less, round down.

20

● Types of Rounding

1\. Rounding to the Nearest Whole Number

Example:

● 45.6 rounds to 46 (because 6 ≥ 5)

● 45.3 rounds to 45 (because 3 ≤ 4)

2\. Rounding to Decimal Places

Example:

● 2.746 rounded to 2 decimal places = 2.75

● 2.742 rounded to 2 decimal places = 2.74

3\. Rounding Money (to the Nearest Rand or Cent)

Example:

● R45.78 rounds to R46 (nearest rand)

● R45.78 stays as R45.78 (nearest cent)

4\. Rounding to Significant Figures

Example:

● 12 347 rounded to 2 significant figures = 12 000

● 0.004563 rounded to 2 significant figures = 0.0046

4.1 Comparing Quantities

A ratio shows the relationship between two or more quantities. It tells you how much of one

thing there is compared to another.

Definition

Ratio: A way to compare quantities, often written with a colon (:) or as a fraction.

Writing Ratios

● Example: A recipe needs 2 cups of flour for 1 cup of sugar.

● The ratio of flour to sugar is 2:1.

Simplifying Ratios

● Ratios can be simplified by dividing both sides by the same number.

● Example: 10:5 simplifies to 2:1 (dividing both by 5).

● Education: A class of 25 learners has 10 boys and 15 girls. The ratio of boys to
