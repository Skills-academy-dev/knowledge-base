Conflict, Transition, and Memory: Using the Past to Understand the Present

From superpower rivalry to community-led resistance and negotiated democracy, the modern era invites comparisons across places and times. Understanding these patterns means examining decisions under pressure, tracing their outcomes, and recognizing how memory shapes identity and policy. Such study links global structures to everyday life.

This textbook balances concise content with purposeful skills practice. Each module offers short sections, visual timelines, and annotated sources that teach reliability, bias, and corroboration. Activities and summaries build confidence for source-based questions and extended writing in Matric assessments.

In this textbook, you will learn about:

The Cold War: Explain ideological conflict and rivalry, analyse diplomacy and proxy wars, and use regional case studies to test arguments about power and security. Independent Africa (1960s–1980s): Examine ideas shaping independence, evaluate political and economic choices in selected states, and assess Angola as a Cold War flashpoint. Civil Society Protests (1950s–1970s): Understand drivers of mobilisation, compare methods from boycotts to direct action, and assess outcomes in the Civil Rights and Black Power movements. Civil Resistance in South Africa (1970s–1980s): Explore Black Consciousness, study the crisis years of apartheid, and judge the effectiveness of international responses. Transition and Memory: Trace negotiations to the GNU, analyse the TRC’s approach to truth and justice, and consider how remembrance shapes national identity. World After the Cold War: Describe transformations from 1989 - 1991, define characteristics of the post-Cold War order, and situate South Africa within global and regional forums.

Taken together, these chapters aim to build informed, confident readers who can connect evidence to claims and see why the past still matters. You are encouraged to use these skills beyond the classroom - in discussion, decision-making, and community life.
