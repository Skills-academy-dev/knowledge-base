HOW TO APPROACH YOUR STUDIES

We are sure that you are excited to dive right into your studies. This is a very important

journey and you should be enthusiastic! After all, this is the first step to reaching your goals.

Before you get started, take some time to read through these guidelines. How you approach

your studies is just as important as what you do when studying.

This study guide includes a practice assessment, a memo and assignments. You might feel

tempted to start on the practice assessment as soon as possible. We advise that you work

through your textbook first and complete the activities that can be found there. Read through

your textbook slowly and make as many notes as possible. This will make the studying

process much easier.

Once you have completed the content and the activities in the textbook, you can take a shot

at the practice test. Practice tests are a great way to prepare for your assessments. So use

the opportunity wisely.

Set some time aside to complete this practice test. Read through the questions carefully and

complete it to the best of your ability. Once completed, use the memo to see how well you

did. If you find that you have not gotten the marks you desire, then simply revise the sections

you did not fare so well in. Then have a go at the test again!

If you are happy with your results for the practice test then you can move onto the

assignments provided by us. Manage your time well so you can give the right amount of

attention to the assignments. Once you have completed these, you must submit them to your

tutors to be marked.

Only once you have completed and submitted the assignments to your tutor will you be

eligible for your next round of books. Your books are dispatched in this way to ensure that you

do not become too overwhelmed with your studies.

Remember this journey is yours. So work at your own pace. If you need any assistance, don’t

hesitate to reach out to your tutors. They are there to help you.

Good luck and happy studies!

5.2) The five forces are:

1) Supplier power. An assessment of how easy it is for suppliers to drive up prices. This is

driven by the: number of suppliers of each essential input; uniqueness of their product or

service; relative size and strength of the supplier; and cost of switching from one supplier to

another.

2) Buyer power. An assessment of how easy it is for buyers to drive prices down. This is driven

by the: number of buyers in the market; importance of each individual buyer to the organisation;

and cost to the buyer of switching from one supplier to another. If a business has just a few

powerful buyers, they are often able to dictate terms.

3) Competitive rivalry. The main driver is the number and capability of competitors in the market.

Many competitors, offering undifferentiated products and services, will reduce market

attractiveness.

4) Threat of substitution. Where close substitute products exist in a market, it increases the

likelihood of customers switching to alternatives in response to price increases. This reduces

both the power of suppliers and the attractiveness of the market.

5) Threat of new entry. Profitable markets attract new entrants, which erodes profitability.

Unless incumbents have strong and durable barriers to entry, for example, patents, economies

of scale, capital requirements or government policies, then profitability will decline to a

competitive rate.

Arguably, regulation, taxation and trade policies make the government a sixth force for many

industries.
