Introduction

Business Studies is part of everyday life. It explores how businesses are created, how they

function, and how they shape the communities and economies we live in. From the corner shop

in your neighbourhood to international companies that operate across the globe, businesses

play a vital role in providing goods, services, and jobs. This subject helps us understand not

only how businesses are managed and sustained, but also how they respond to challenges,

grow over time, and contribute to society. At its heart, Business Studies is about learning how

people organise resources, make decisions, and find innovative ways to meet human needs

and wants.

By learning Business Studies, you will:

● Understand how businesses function in real life – You’ll explore how laws affect

businesses, how managers lead and motivate employees, how strategies are

developed, and how different business environments influence operations.

● Make informed decisions – Whether it’s about managing finances, investing, choosing

a form of ownership, or solving ethical dilemmas, Business Studies equips you with

knowledge that helps you balance risks and benefits.

● Tackle South Africa’s challenges – You will learn to think critically about issues such

as unemployment, inequality, consumer rights, and social responsibility, and suggest

solutions that businesses can apply.

● Prepare for future careers – From entrepreneurship and management to finance,

marketing, and human resources, Business Studies provides skills and knowledge that

employers and universities value highly.

● Stay connected to your world – You’ll be able to make sense of news about economic

changes, business strategies, and government policies, and understand how these

affect your community and daily life.

Introduction

Every business operates within an environment that shapes the way it functions and grows. In

South Africa, this environment is influenced by laws, human rights, inclusivity, environmental

issues, and the different economic sectors. Businesses must also adapt to changing conditions

in the micro, market, and macro environments while developing strategies to remain

competitive.

In this module, you will explore key legislation such as the Skills Development Act, the Labour

Relations Act, and the Consumer Protection Act, and examine their impact on employers,

employees, and consumers. You will also learn about diversity and inclusivity in the workplace,

the importance of protecting the environment, and the challenges businesses face in different

environments. Finally, you will study how businesses use strategies and ownership structures to

ensure success and sustainability.

By the end of this module, you will understand how businesses interact with their environment

and how effective management of these factors leads to long-term growth and competitiveness.

Did You Know?

South Africa’s Broad-Based Black Economic Empowerment (BBBEE) scorecard is often

used by government and large companies when awarding tenders. This means businesses

that invest in empowerment initiatives, like MTN and Shoprite, can access more opportunities

and grow faster.

Unit 1: Impact of Legislation on Businesses

South African businesses must follow several laws that regulate how they operate. These laws

protect workers, consumers, and society, while also ensuring fair competition and sustainable

growth. In this unit, you will learn about eight important Acts and how they affect employers,

employees, and consumers. Understanding these laws helps businesses avoid penalties,

comply with legislation, and build a positive reputation.

1.1 Skills Development Act (SDA)

South Africa faces a shortage of skilled workers, which limits economic growth. The SDA was

introduced to ensure that businesses provide training opportunities so that employees can

develop skills and improve their employability.

Purpose

● To develop the skills of the workforce.

● To improve workers’ quality of life and chances of employment.

● To make South African businesses more competitive.

Definition

Skills Development Act (SDA): A law that promotes training, education, and development to

improve skills.

Role of SETAs (Sector Education and Training Authorities)

● Approve workplace skills plans.

● Provide grants to businesses for training.

● Manage learnerships, apprenticeships, and internships.

● Align training with national industry needs.

Conflict between workers and employers has historically been a challenge in South Africa. The

Labour Relations Act was created to protect workers’ rights, encourage fair labour practices,

and promote stability in the workplace.

Purpose

● To promote labour peace and fair employment practices.

● To encourage collective bargaining between employers and employees.

● To prevent unfair dismissal and discrimination.

1.3 Employment Equity Act (EEA)

South Africa’s history of discrimination has left inequalities in the workplace. The Employment

Equity Act was introduced to promote fairness and diversity by preventing unfair discrimination

and applying affirmative action measures.

Purpose

● To achieve equity in the workplace.

● To promote fair treatment of all workers.

Definition

Employment Equity Act (EEA): A law that promotes fairness and equality at work.

15

Prevents

● Discrimination based on race, gender, disability, religion, or age.

Affirmative Action

● Provides opportunities for disadvantaged groups.

● Promotes diversity in hiring and promotions.

Employer Duties

● Prepare employment equity plans.

● Submit annual reports to the Department of Labour.

● Eliminate barriers to equity in the workplace.

Impact

● Improves representation of women, people with disabilities, and black South Africans.

● Encourages diversity and fairness.

● Penalties, fines, or exclusion from government tenders.

Remember that equity = fairness. It’s about giving everyone a fair chance, not treating

1.4 Basic Conditions of Employment Act (BCEA)

The BCEA sets the basic rules for employment in South Africa. It protects workers from

exploitation by setting minimum standards for working hours, leave, and termination procedures.

Purpose

● To establish fair labour practices.

● To regulate working conditions.

Definition

BCEA: A law that sets minimum standards for working conditions.

Key Provisions

● Working Hours: Maximum 45 hours per week.

● Leave:

○ 21 consecutive days of annual leave.

○ 6 weeks of sick leave in 36 months.

○ 4 months unpaid maternity leave.

● Termination: Requires notice and severance pay if retrenched.

Impact

● Protects workers from unfair conditions.

● Ensures decent working standards.

● Increases costs for employers.

Non-Compliance Consequences

● Fines, penalties, or Department of Labour intervention.

17

Example

BCEA ensures Woolworths employees get proper annual leave and rest days.

Some jobs expose workers to health and safety risks. The COIDA was created to ensure that

Purpose

● To compensate employees for injuries or illnesses caused by work.

Definition

Employer Duties

● Report workplace accidents.

● Keep records of injuries.

Benefits for Employees

● Covers medical expenses.

● Disability and death benefits for affected workers.

Impact

● Protects employers from lawsuits.

Non-Compliance Consequences

1.6 Broad-Based Black Economic Empowerment Act (BBBEE)

To correct economic imbalances of the past, BBBEE was introduced. It provides a framework for

businesses to include black South Africans in the economy.

Purpose

● To promote participation of black South Africans in the economy.

BEE vs BBBEE

● BEE: Narrow, mainly focused on ownership transfer.

● BBBEE: Broader, includes five pillars: ownership, management, skills, supplier

development, socio-economic development.

Definition

BBBEE: A government policy that promotes economic transformation and inclusivity.

Five Pillars of BBBEE

1\. Ownership – Shareholding by black South Africans.

2\. Management Control – Representation in senior positions and boards.

3\. Skills Development – Training and education opportunities.

4\. Enterprise & Supplier Development (ESD) – Support for black-owned suppliers.

5\. Socio-Economic Development – Community upliftment projects.

Unit 2: Human Rights, Inclusivity and Environmental Issues

A fair and sustainable workplace is built on respecting human rights and embracing diversity.

This unit explains the constitutional rights of employees, the importance of inclusivity, and

strategies businesses can use to reduce discrimination and promote fairness. You will also learn

about environmental responsibilities and how businesses can operate sustainably.

2.1 Constitutional Rights in the Workplace

The Constitution of South Africa is the highest law in the land and provides protection to all

citizens. These rights extend to the workplace, where employees must be treated fairly and with

dignity. Businesses must respect these rights to maintain compliance with the law and to create

a safe, fair environment.

Key Constitutional Rights in the Workplace

● Right to Privacy – Personal information (emails, phone calls, records) must be

protected.

● Right to Dignity – Employees must be treated with respect. No degrading treatment is

allowed.

● Right to Equity – Equal opportunities regardless of race, gender, disability, or

background.

● Right to Freedom of Expression and Information – Employees can express opinions

and access information as long as it does not harm others or the business.

● Right to Safety, Security, and Protection of Life – Employers must provide a safe,

healthy workplace free from hazards.

● Economic, Social, and Cultural Rights – Employees have the right to fair wages, rest,

cultural respect, and equal access to opportunities.

Definition

Constitutional Rights: The basic rights and freedoms protected by South Africa’s

Constitution, including in the workplace.

2.3 Inclusivity Strategies in Business

Inclusivity means creating a workplace where everyone feels respected, valued, and able to

contribute. Businesses must develop strategies to ensure all employees have fair access to

opportunities.

Strategies to Promote Inclusivity

● Anti-Discrimination Policies – Clear policies against racism, sexism, and harassment.

● Employment Equity Programmes – Ensuring fair representation of previously

disadvantaged groups.

● Diversity Training – Educating staff about cultural awareness and respect.

● Accessible Workplaces – Providing facilities for people with disabilities (e.g. ramps,

adapted software).

● Mentorship Programmes – Supporting young and inexperienced employees.

● Open Communication – Encouraging dialogue to reduce conflict.

sectors depending on whether they extract raw materials, produce goods, or provide services.

services.

Definition

Business Environments: All the internal and external forces that affect how a business

operates and performs.

Examples Across Environments

● Micro: Woolworths decides to train cashiers (employees).

● Market: Woolworths competes with Pick n Pay for customers.

● Macro: Woolworths cannot control a VAT increase that raises prices.

Tip

Think of the environments like circles:

● Micro = inside the business.

● Market = close to the business.

● Macro = big external world.

Example

A restaurant in Cape Town (micro: menu planning, staff hiring) faces competition from nearby

restaurants (market), while dealing with loadshedding (macro).

Did You Know?

The COVID-19 pandemic showed how the macro environment can force entire industries

(like tourism and airlines) to shut down overnight, even though their internal operations were

strong.
