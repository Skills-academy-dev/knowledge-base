1.1 What is Financial Management?
Financial management is about making sure a business uses its money wisely. It includes all
the activities involved in planning, controlling, organising, and monitoring how money flows in
and out of the business. When a business manages its money well, it can grow, make a profit,
and survive even in difficult times.
Definition of Financial Management
Financial management is the process of planning, organising, controlling, and monitoring
financial resources to achieve business goals.
In simple terms, it is how a business makes decisions about money.
Main Activities in Financial Management
Financial management includes the following key activities:
Activity Description
Planning Deciding how much money is needed and how it will be used.
Organising Putting systems and people in place to handle the finances.
Controlling Checking if the business is spending according to plan.
Monitoring Keeping track of the financial progress of the business over time.
Decision-making Choosing how to spend, save, or invest money to meet financial goals.
Why is Financial Management Important?
Good financial management helps a business:
• Make enough money to cover costs.
• Avoid financial problems like debt or cash shortages.
• Grow and invest in new products or services.
• Pay workers and suppliers on time.
• Survive during tough times.

Functions of Financial Management
Here are the main functions of financial management explained with examples:
• Capital Budgeting
Deciding what to spend money on and whether it will help the business grow.
Example: Should we buy a delivery van or rent one?
• Financing Decisions
Choosing how to get money (own savings, bank loan, investors).
Example: Should we borrow from the bank or use company savings?
• Working Capital Management
Making sure the business can pay for day-to-day costs like stock, salaries, and rent.
Example: Do we have enough cash to pay for next month’s stock?
• Profit Planning
Setting targets for how much profit the business wants to make
Example: We want to earn R5 000 profit this month—how can we do that?
Benefits of Financial Management
Some benefits of financial management include the following:
• Helps the business avoid overspending
• Makes it easier to attract investors or get a loan
• Increases the chances of success and long-term survival
• Gives the owner control over the business finances
• Ensures timely payments and a good reputation

1.4 Financial Goals: Profitability, Liquidity, Solvency
Every business needs clear goals to succeed. In financial management, the main goals help
the business stay strong, stable, and successful. These goals include making a profit, keeping
enough cash available, and being able to pay off long-term debts.
In this section, you will learn about the three key financial goals: profitability, liquidity, and
solvency. These goals help business owners and financial managers make smart decisions.
What Are Financial Goals?
Financial goals are the main money-related targets that businesses aim to achieve to stay
healthy and successful.
There are three main financial goals in every business:
1. Profitability
Profitability means that a business earns more money (income) than it spends (expenses).
When a business is profitable, it can grow and stay in operation.
Why It Matters
• Shows how well the business is doing
• Attracts investors and banks
• Helps the business expand
Formula to Calculate Profit:
Profit = Income − Expenses
Example:
A bakery earns R12 000 from selling bread and cakes, but spends R8 000 on ingredients,
wages, and electricity.
Profit = R12000 − R8000 = R4000
The business is profitable.
20 | Page

2. Liquidity
Liquidity refers to the business’s ability to pay its short-term debts and daily expenses using the
cash or assets it can quickly turn into cash.
Why It Matters
• Ensures bills, wages, and rent are paid on time
• Keeps the business running smoothly
• Avoids late payment penalties
Examples of Liquid Assets:
• Cash in the bank
• Money owed by customers (debtors)
• Stock that can be sold quickly
Example:
A tuckshop has R1 000 in the bank and R500 in stock. Rent is R600. The tuckshop is liquid
because it can pay rent from its cash and stock.
3. Solvency
Solvency is the business’s ability to pay its long-term debts (loans or bonds) when they are
due.
Why It Matters
• Shows if the business can survive in the long run
• Helps build trust with banks and suppliers
• Reduces the risk of bankruptcy
Example:
A company owns equipment and buildings worth R200 000, but only owes R50 000 in loans.
It is solvent because it owns more than it owes.

• Solvency – being able to pay off long-term debts.
Lastly, you learned key terms like income, expenses, assets, liabilities, and profit. These terms help
you understand how a business is doing.
Now you are ready to learn more about managing business finances.
