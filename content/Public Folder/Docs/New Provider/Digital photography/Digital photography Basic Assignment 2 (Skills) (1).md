Assignment 2

Part 1 – Composition and Visual Design

Marks: 20

Composition refers to how elements are arranged within a photograph. Good composition

helps guide the viewer’s eye and makes photographs more visually engaging.

Task 1: Rule of Thirds Composition

Marks: 7

Take a photograph where the main subject is positioned using the rule of thirds instead

of being placed in the centre.

Examples :

●  A person standing to the side of the image

●  A tree placed along one of the grid lines

●  A building positioned off-centre

●  A pet sitting near one of the grid intersections

Important: These examples are only suggestions. You are not limited to these subjects and

may photograph any scene where the rule of thirds can be applied.

This task trains you to:

●  Position subjects effectively within a frame

●  Create balanced photographs

●  Understand how subject placement improves composition

Task 2: Leading Lines Photography

Marks: 7

Take a photograph that clearly uses leading lines to guide the viewer’s eye toward the

subject.

Examples :

●  A road leading toward a building

●  Railway tracks disappearing into the distance

●  A pathway in a park

●  A fence guiding the viewer’s attention

6

Important: These examples are only suggestions. You are encouraged to find your own

creative scenes that contain strong lines.

This task trains you to:

●

Identify natural lines in the environment

●  Direct the viewer’s attention toward the subject

●  Create stronger visual flow in photographs

Task 3: Visual Storytelling Photography

Marks: 6

Take a photograph that captures an activity or moment that tells a story.

Examples :

●  Someone cooking or preparing food

●  A person studying or working

●  Children playing

●  A street vendor selling goods

Important: These examples are only suggestions. You may photograph any activity or

moment that communicates meaning or emotion.

This task trains you to:

●  Observe meaningful moments

●  Capture real-life situations

●  Use photography to communicate stories and emotions

Part 2 – Lighting in Digital Photography

Marks: 20

Lighting plays a major role in photography. The direction, strength, and type of light can

dramatically change how a photograph looks.

Task 4: Natural Light Photography

Marks: 7

Take a photograph using natural daylight.

Examples :

●  A plant near a window

●  A building photographed outdoors

●  A portrait taken outside

●  A street scene during the day

Important: These examples are only suggestions. You may photograph any subject you find

interesting.

This task trains you to:

●  Understand how natural light affects photographs

●  Capture well-lit and clear images

●  Work effectively with available light

Task 5: Backlighting Photography

Marks: 7

Take a photograph where light comes from behind the subject.

Examples :

●  A silhouette during sunset

●  Sunlight shining through leaves

●  A person standing in front of the sun

●  Light shining behind an object

Important: These examples are only suggestions. You are encouraged to experiment with

different lighting situations.

8

This task trains you to:

●  Understand how light direction affects photographs

●  Create dramatic lighting effects

●  Experiment with silhouettes and glow

Task 6: Low-Light Photography

Marks: 6

Take a photograph in low-light conditions such as evening or indoors with limited lighting.

Examples :

●  A room lit by a lamp

●  A street scene at night

●  A candle-lit object

●  A building photographed in the evening

Important: These examples are only suggestions. You may choose any low-light

environment.

This task trains you to:

●  Photograph in challenging lighting conditions

●

Improve camera stability and focus

●  Understand how lighting affects exposure

Part 3 – Digital Workflow and Photo Editing

Marks: 15

Professional photographers edit their photographs and organise their work to maintain a

structured digital workflow.

Task 7: Colour Correction and Contrast Editing

Marks: 10

Take one new photograph of any subject and improve it using a photo editing application.

You must use an editing tool such as:

●  Adobe Lightroom

●

InShot

●  Snapseed

●  Any other photo editing application with colour adjustment tools

You must submit two photographs:

●  The original photograph (before editing)

●  The edited photograph (after editing)

Your edited photograph must clearly show the following adjustments (Your editing

should show your own style or photography preference) :

●  Colour correction (improving or balancing colours)

●  Contrast adjustment (enhancing the difference between light and dark areas)

Examples of subjects may include:

●  A street scene

●  A portrait of a person

●  A landscape

●  Food or product photography

●  A building or architectural detail

Important: These examples are only suggestions. You are not limited to these subjects and

may photograph anything that interests you.

10

This task trains you to:

●  Use editing tools professionally

●

Improve the colour and clarity of photographs

●  Enhance visual detail using contrast

●  Understand how editing improves photographic results

Task 8: Organising Your Photography Portfolio

Marks: 5

Professional photographers organise their images so they can easily find and manage their

work. Create a clearly organised Assignment 2 photo folder on your device. Since folders

cannot be submitted directly, you must take a screenshot showing the folder inside.

The screenshot must clearly show:

●  The folder name

●  The file names of the photographs

Example folder structure (do not copy this exactly – create your own):

Photo_Project2

Composition_Shot

Lines_Shot

Story_Shot

Daylight_Image

Backlight_Image

Night_Image

Original_Edit

Final_Edit

This task trains you to:

●  Organise digital photography files

●  Develop professional workflow habits

●  Manage photographs efficiently
