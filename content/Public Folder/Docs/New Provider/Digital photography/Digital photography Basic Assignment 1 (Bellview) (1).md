Assignment 1

Part 1 – Exploring Your Camera and Environment

Marks: 15

Purpose of this task

Professional photographers must first become comfortable using their camera and observing

the world around them. This task helps you begin seeing everyday scenes as photographic

opportunities. Students must take three original photographs :

Task 1: Everyday Subject Photography

Marks: 5

Choose one everyday subject that interests you and take a clear photograph of it.

Examples may include:

●  A coffee cup

●  A bicycle

●  A plant or flower

●  A book

●  A phone

●  A person performing an activity

Important: These examples are only suggestions. You are not limited to these subjects and

may photograph any everyday object or subject you find interesting.

This task trains you to:

●

Identify a clear subject

●  Practise focusing

●  Frame your subject within the photograph

6

Task 2: Photographing Your Environment

Marks: 5

Choose a location that represents your surroundings and photograph the scene.

Examples may include:

●  Your home

●  A street

●  A park

●  A classroom

●  A market

Important: These examples are only suggestions. You may photograph any environment or

location that you find visually interesting.

This task trains you to:

●  Observe your surroundings like a photographer

●  Capture real-life scenes

●  Begin thinking about visual storytelling

Task 3: Creative Framing

Marks: 5

Take one photograph where you intentionally frame the subject in an interesting way.

You may experiment with:

●  Different camera angles

●  Distance from the subject

●  Subject placement within the frame

Important: You are free to photograph any subject you choose. The goal is to practise

creative framing and composition.

This task trains you to:

●  Control how viewers see the subject

●  Develop your creative perspective

7

Part 2 – Controlling Light and Motion

Marks: 20

Purpose of this task

Light and motion strongly influence the final result of a photograph. Photographers must

learn how to capture movement and work with different lighting conditions.

Students must take four photographs.

Task 4: Freezing Motion and Capturing Movement

Marks: 10

Choose a moving subject and take two photographs:

1.  One photograph where the motion appears frozen.

2.  One photograph where motion blur is visible.

Examples of moving subjects may include:

●  A person walking or running

●  A moving bicycle or car

●  A pet playing

●  Water flowing

Important: These examples are only suggestions. You may photograph any type of

movement that you find interesting.

This task trains you to:

●  Observe movement in real life

●  Practise photographing action

●  Understand how camera settings affect motion

8

Task 5: Working with Different Light Conditions

Marks: 10

Choose one subject and photograph it twice:

●  One photograph in bright light

●  One photograph in lower light or shade

Examples of subjects may include:

●  A plant near a window

●  A person outdoors

●  A building

●  A street scene

Important: These examples are only suggestions. You may choose any subject you wish.

This task trains you to:

●  Understand how light affects photographs

●  Learn to photograph in different lighting conditions

●  Develop awareness of brightness and shadows

9

Part 3 – Focus, Distance, and Perspective

Marks: 20

Purpose of this task

Photographers use focus, distance, and lenses to control what the viewer sees in a

photograph. These skills help photographers highlight subjects and create visual depth.

Students must take four photographs :

Task 6: Close-Up Photography

Marks: 5

Take one close-up photograph of a small subject.

Examples may include:

●  A flower

●  A coin

●  A leaf

●  A piece of jewellery

●  A textured surface

Important: These examples are only suggestions. You may photograph any small object or

subject.

This task trains you to:

●  Practise precise focusing

●  Capture small details

●  Explore close-up photography

Task 7: Creating Depth

Marks: 5

Take one photograph that shows distance or depth in the scene.

Examples may include:

●  A road stretching into the distance

●  Railway tracks

●  A hallway

●  A pathway

10

Important: These examples are only suggestions. Any scene that shows distance or

perspective can be used.

This task trains you to:

●  Create depth in photographs

●  Guide the viewer’s eye through the image

Task 8: Subject and Background

Marks: 5

Take one photograph where the main subject clearly stands out from the background.

Examples may include:

●  A person against a background

●  A flower with blurred surroundings

●  An object on a table

Important: These examples are only suggestions. You may choose any subject you prefer.

This task trains you to:

●  Control focus

●  Highlight the main subject

Task 9: Wide Scene Photography

Marks: 5

Take one photograph that captures a wide scene.

Examples may include:

●  A landscape

●  A street view

●  A building

●  A large indoor space

Important: These examples are only suggestions. You may photograph any wide scene that

interests you.

11

This task trains you to:

●  Capture large environments

●  Practise wide-angle style photography

Reflection

Marks: 5

Write a short reflection, in your reflection, describe:

●  Which photograph you are most proud of

●  What new photography skill you practised

●  What type of photography you may want to explore in the future

●  How did you apply what you learnt in the Digital photography basic textbook

Submission

Students must submit:

●  Original photographs

●  Each photograph to be attached under clearly labelled table with the task number

●  One reflection (30–50 words)

Students are encouraged to experiment creatively and begin discovering their preferred

photography style.
