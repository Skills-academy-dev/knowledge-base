Introduction

Digital photography has become an essential part of everyday communication and creativity.

People use cameras and smartphones to capture memories, document events, and share

experiences with others. Understanding the basic principles of photography helps learners

recognise how images are created and how cameras function. Learning about camera types,

equipment, and digital image basics provides a strong starting point for developing practical

photography skills.

Refer to page 10 in your textbook for more information.

Unit 1 – Understanding Photography

Photography is both a creative and technical process that allows people to capture and

communicate visual information. Learning the fundamentals of photography helps you

understand how images are created and why they are important in many fields. Knowledge

of photography’s development and purpose also helps learners appreciate how images

influence communication and storytelling in modern society.

Refer to page 11–14 in your textbook for more information.

1.1 Definition and purpose of photography

Photography is the process of capturing images using light and a camera. It allows people to

record moments, document events, and communicate ideas visually. Photographs can also

be used for artistic expression, education, marketing, and storytelling.

1.2 Brief history of photography

Early photography began with experiments using light-sensitive materials to capture images.

Over time, technological improvements made cameras more reliable and easier to use.

These developments helped photography become widely used for documentation, art, and

communication.

1.3 Evolution from film photography to digital photography

Film photography required chemical processes to develop and print images. Digital

photography replaced film with electronic sensors that capture and store images as digital

files. This change made photography faster, easier to edit, and more convenient for sharing

images.

9

1.4 The role of photography in modern society

Photography is widely used in journalism, education, advertising, and social media. Images

help communicate information quickly and often have a strong impact on audiences.

Photography also allows individuals to share experiences, preserve memories, and express

creativity.

Unit 2 – Types of Digital Cameras

Digital cameras are designed in different forms to suit various photography needs and skill

levels. Some cameras focus on simplicity and portability, while others provide advanced

controls and higher image quality. Understanding these camera types helps photographers

choose the most suitable equipment for different situations and photographic goals.

Refer to page 15–18 in your textbook for more information.

2.1 Compact digital cameras

Compact digital cameras, often called point-and-shoot cameras, are designed for simplicity

and convenience. They are small, lightweight, and usually use automatic settings to adjust

focus, exposure, and lighting. These cameras are suitable for beginners or casual

photographers.

2.2 DSLR cameras

DSLR (Digital Single-Lens Reflex) cameras offer higher image quality and advanced manual

controls. Photographers can adjust settings such as aperture, shutter speed, and ISO to

achieve specific creative results. DSLR cameras also support interchangeable lenses,

making them versatile for different photography styles.

2.3 Mirrorless cameras

Mirrorless cameras are modern digital cameras that do not use the mirror system found in

DSLR cameras. Instead, images are displayed electronically on a screen or electronic

viewfinder. These cameras provide high image quality while often being smaller and lighter

than DSLR models.

2.4 Smartphone cameras and computational photography

Smartphone cameras have become widely used because they are convenient and always

accessible. Modern smartphones include advanced sensors and multiple lenses for

capturing high-quality images. Computational photography uses software and artificial

intelligence to automatically improve lighting, colours, and image quality.

10

Unit 3 – Parts of a Digital Camera

Digital cameras contain several components that work together to capture and process

photographs. Each part plays an important role in controlling light, focusing the image, and

storing digital data. Understanding these camera components helps photographers operate

their cameras more effectively and improve the quality of their images.

Refer to page 19–21 in your textbook for more information.

3.1 Camera body and image sensor

The camera body is the main structure that holds the camera’s internal and external

components. Inside the body is the image sensor, which captures light and converts it into

digital image data. The quality and size of the sensor influence the clarity and detail of the

final photograph.

3.2 Camera lenses and focal length basics

Camera lenses focus light onto the image sensor to create a clear photograph. Different

lenses allow photographers to capture subjects from different distances and perspectives.

Focal length determines how wide or narrow the camera’s field of view will appear.

3.3 Shutter mechanism and aperture system

The shutter controls how long light enters the camera, while the aperture controls how much

light passes through the lens. These components work together to regulate exposure and

affect the brightness of a photograph. They also influence creative effects such as motion

blur and depth of field.

3.4 Viewfinders, LCD screens and camera controls

Viewfinders and LCD screens help photographers compose and review photographs. The

viewfinder allows the photographer to frame the subject before taking a picture. Buttons,

dials, and menu systems provide control over camera settings such as focus, exposure, and

image playback.

Unit 4 – Essential Photography Equipment

Photography often requires additional equipment to support the camera and improve image

quality. Accessories help photographers store images, stabilise the camera, protect

equipment, and control lighting conditions. Understanding these tools helps photographers

work more efficiently and protect their gear during different shooting situations.

Refer to page 22–24 in your textbook for more information.

11

4.1 Memory cards and digital storage

Memory cards store the digital photographs captured by a camera. They allow

photographers to save, transfer, and organise images on computers or other devices.

Choosing memory cards with suitable storage capacity and speed helps ensure smooth

camera performance and prevents data loss.

4.2 Tripods and stabilisation tools

Tripods and stabilisation tools keep the camera steady while taking photographs. Stable

support reduces blur caused by hand movement, especially in low-light conditions or when

using slow shutter speeds. These tools are commonly used for landscape, night, and

long-exposure photography.

4.3 Camera bags and protective equipment

Camera bags protect photography equipment during storage and transport. They help

organise cameras, lenses, and accessories while preventing damage from dust, moisture, or

accidental impact. Protective equipment such as lens cases and cleaning kits also helps

maintain camera performance.

4.4 Filters and basic camera accessories

Filters are placed in front of a camera lens to control how light enters the camera. They can

reduce reflections, improve colour contrast, or limit the amount of light entering the lens.

Other accessories such as cleaning tools and lens caps help maintain equipment and

protect camera lenses.

Unit 5 – Camera Setup and Basic Operation

Proper camera setup helps photographers capture images efficiently and manage their

photographs effectively. Understanding camera menus, image formats, and file organisation

improves workflow and makes it easier to locate and edit images later. Good camera

handling and care also help protect equipment and ensure reliable performance.

Refer to page 25–27 in your textbook for more information.

5.1 Navigating camera menus and settings

Digital cameras include menu systems that allow photographers to adjust camera settings.

These menus control functions such as image quality, focus modes, exposure settings, and

storage options. Learning how to navigate these menus allows photographers to customise

the camera according to their needs.

12

5.2 Image formats (JPEG and RAW)

Digital photographs are commonly stored in JPEG or RAW formats. JPEG files are

compressed images that require less storage space and are ready to use immediately. RAW

files contain more image data, allowing greater flexibility when editing photographs.

5.3 File naming, metadata and image organisation

Organising image files helps photographers manage large collections of photographs. File

naming systems and folder structures make it easier to locate images later. Metadata stored

in each photograph records details such as camera settings, date, and time.

5.4 Basic camera handling and care

Proper camera handling helps maintain the performance and lifespan of photography

equipment. Cameras should be handled carefully and stored in safe conditions to avoid

damage. Regular cleaning and protection from dust, moisture, and extreme temperatures

help keep the camera functioning properly.

Unit 6 – Understanding Digital Images

Digital photographs are made up of small units of information that combine to form a

complete image. Understanding how digital images are created and stored helps

photographers maintain image quality and manage their files effectively. Knowledge of

resolution, compression, and file formats also helps when preparing images for editing,

sharing, or printing.

Refer to page 28–30 in your textbook for more information.

6.1 Pixels and image resolution

Pixels are the smallest units that form a digital image. Thousands or millions of pixels

combine to create a photograph, and the number of pixels determines the level of detail.

Higher resolution images contain more pixels and usually appear sharper and clearer.

6.2 Image quality and compression

Image compression reduces the file size of digital photographs so they can be stored and

shared more easily. While compression helps save storage space, excessive compression

may reduce image quality. Photographers must balance file size and image detail depending

on how the image will be used.

13

6.3 Digital image file formats

Digital images can be saved in several file formats, each designed for specific purposes.

Formats such as JPEG, RAW, PNG, and TIFF differ in file size, image quality, and editing

flexibility. Choosing the appropriate format helps photographers manage images more

effectively.

6.4 Introduction to image metadata

Metadata is additional information stored within a digital image file. It records details such as

the date and time the photograph was taken, camera settings, and sometimes location data.

Metadata helps photographers organise images and analyse how photographs were

captured.

Introduction

Controlling exposure is one of the most important skills in photography because it

determines how bright or dark a photograph appears. Cameras capture images by allowing

light to reach the image sensor, and the amount of light must be carefully controlled to

produce a balanced photograph. Learning how exposure works helps photographers adjust

camera settings to suit different lighting conditions and subjects. Understanding exposure

also improves image quality and allows photographers to create more visually effective

photographs.

Refer to page 37 in your textbook for more information.

Unit 1 – Understanding Exposure

Exposure controls how light or dark a photograph appears. Cameras capture images by

allowing light to reach the image sensor, and the amount of light determines the brightness

and clarity of the final photograph. Understanding exposure helps photographers adjust

camera settings correctly and produce well-balanced images in different lighting conditions.

Refer to page 38–40 in your textbook for more information.

1.1 Definition of exposure

Exposure refers to the amount of light that reaches the camera’s image sensor when a

photograph is taken. The level of exposure determines whether the image appears bright,

dark, or balanced. Proper exposure helps ensure that important details in the photograph are

clearly visible.

1.2 Correct exposure in photography

Correct exposure occurs when a photograph has a balanced level of brightness and detail.

Both bright and dark areas of the image remain visible without losing important information.

Achieving correct exposure requires adjusting camera settings according to the available

lighting.

1.3 Overexposure and underexposure

Overexposure happens when too much light reaches the camera sensor, making the image

appear too bright and washing out details. Underexposure occurs when too little light enters

the camera, causing the image to appear too dark. Both conditions reduce image quality and

make it difficult to see details clearly.

16

1.4 Exposure value (EV)

Exposure value (EV) is a numerical system used to describe different combinations of

camera settings that produce similar brightness levels. It helps photographers compare

exposure settings and understand how aperture and shutter speed work together. EV can

also assist photographers in adjusting settings when lighting conditions change.

Unit 2 – Aperture and Depth of Field

Aperture is an important camera setting that controls how much light enters the lens. It also

influences how much of the photograph appears sharp or blurred. Understanding how

aperture works helps photographers manage exposure and create different visual effects in

their images.

Refer to page 41-43 in your textbook for more information.

2.1 Understanding aperture and f-stops

Aperture refers to the adjustable opening inside a camera lens that allows light to pass

through to the sensor. The size of this opening is measured using f-stops. Smaller f-stop

numbers represent larger openings, while larger f-stop numbers represent smaller openings.

2.2 Aperture and light control

Aperture controls the amount of light entering the camera through the lens. A wide aperture

allows more light to reach the sensor, making the image brighter. A narrow aperture allows

less light to enter, which results in a darker image.

2.3 Aperture and depth of field

Depth of field describes the area of a photograph that appears sharp and in focus. A wide

aperture produces a shallow depth of field, where the subject is sharp but the background

appears blurred. A narrow aperture creates a deeper depth of field, keeping more of the

image in focus.

2.4 Creative uses of aperture

Photographers use aperture to guide attention within a photograph. A wide aperture is often

used in portrait photography to blur the background and highlight the subject. Smaller

apertures are commonly used in landscape photography to keep the entire scene sharp.

17

Unit 3 – Shutter Speed and Motion

Shutter speed controls how long the camera sensor is exposed to light while taking a

photograph. It also determines how motion appears in the final image. Understanding shutter

speed allows photographers to freeze movement or create motion effects depending on the

situation.

Refer to page 44–46 in your textbook for more information.

3.1 Understanding shutter speed

Shutter speed refers to the amount of time the camera shutter remains open when capturing

a photograph. It is usually measured in fractions of a second, such as 1/500 or 1/60. Faster

shutter speeds expose the sensor for a shorter time, while slower shutter speeds allow more

light to enter.

3.2 Fast shutter speeds and freezing motion

Fast shutter speeds capture moving subjects sharply by reducing the time light reaches the

sensor. This technique freezes motion and prevents blur. It is commonly used when

photographing sports, wildlife, or fast-moving objects.

3.3 Slow shutter speeds and motion blur

Slow shutter speeds allow the camera sensor to collect light for a longer time. Moving

subjects may appear blurred, creating a sense of movement in the photograph. This

technique is often used in creative photography such as capturing flowing water or light

trails.

3.4 Creative effects using shutter speed

Shutter speed can be adjusted to create different artistic effects. Photographers may use

slow shutter speeds to capture motion or fast shutter speeds to freeze action. These

adjustments help add visual interest and storytelling to photographs.

Unit 4 – ISO and Image Sensitivity

ISO is one of the main camera settings used to control exposure in photography. It

determines how sensitive the camera’s image sensor is to light and plays an important role

in capturing clear photographs in different lighting conditions. Understanding ISO helps

photographers adjust image brightness while maintaining good image quality.

Refer to page 47–49 in your textbook for more information.

18

4.1 Understanding ISO

ISO refers to the sensitivity of the camera’s image sensor to light. Lower ISO values mean

the sensor is less sensitive to light, while higher ISO values increase sensitivity.

Photographers adjust ISO depending on how much light is available in the environment.

4.2 ISO and image brightness

ISO directly affects the brightness of a photograph. Increasing the ISO value makes the

image brighter because the sensor becomes more sensitive to light. Lower ISO settings are

typically used in bright conditions, while higher ISO settings are used in darker

environments.

4.3 ISO and digital noise

Digital noise appears as grainy or speckled patterns in a photograph. Higher ISO settings

can increase the amount of digital noise because the camera amplifies the image signal.

Lower ISO values generally produce clearer images with less noise.

4.4 Choosing appropriate ISO settings

Photographers select ISO settings based on lighting conditions and the desired image

quality. Low ISO settings are suitable for bright daylight, while higher ISO values are useful

in low-light environments. Balancing ISO with aperture and shutter speed helps achieve

proper exposure.

Unit 5 – The Exposure Triangle

The exposure triangle explains how three important camera settings work together to control

the brightness of a photograph. These settings are aperture, shutter speed, and ISO.

Understanding their relationship helps photographers adjust their cameras to produce

well-exposed images in different lighting situations.

Refer to page 50–52 in your textbook for more information.

5.1 Relationship between aperture, shutter speed and ISO

Aperture, shutter speed, and ISO work together to determine how much light reaches the

camera sensor. Changing one of these settings affects the others and may require

adjustments to maintain proper exposure. Understanding this relationship helps

photographers control both brightness and creative effects.

19

5.2 Balancing exposure settings

Balancing exposure involves adjusting aperture, shutter speed, and ISO so that the image

has the correct brightness. When one setting changes, another may need to be adjusted to

compensate. This balance allows photographers to control exposure while achieving their

desired visual results.

5.3 Adjusting exposure for different scenes

Different lighting environments require different exposure settings. Bright outdoor scenes

often require lower ISO values and smaller apertures, while darker scenes may require

higher ISO or slower shutter speeds. Evaluating lighting conditions helps photographers

select appropriate settings.

5.4 Practical examples of exposure control

Exposure control is applied in real-world photography situations. Photographers may

increase shutter speed to capture fast movement, adjust aperture for depth of field, or raise

ISO in low-light conditions. Practising these adjustments helps photographers understand

how exposure affects the final image.

Unit 6 – Camera Shooting Modes

Digital cameras provide different shooting modes that help photographers control exposure

and camera settings. Some modes automatically adjust settings for convenience, while

others allow more manual control for creative photography. Understanding these modes

helps photographers choose the most suitable option for different situations.

Refer to page 53–55 in your textbook for more information.

6.1 Automatic mode and scene modes

Automatic mode allows the camera to control most settings such as aperture, shutter speed,

and ISO. Scene modes are preset options designed for specific situations such as portraits,

landscapes, or sports photography. These modes make photography easier for beginners.

6.2 Aperture priority mode

Aperture priority mode allows the photographer to select the aperture value while the camera

automatically adjusts the shutter speed. This mode is useful when controlling depth of field is

important. It is commonly used in portrait and landscape photography.

20

6.3 Shutter priority mode

Shutter priority mode allows the photographer to choose the shutter speed while the camera

adjusts the aperture automatically. This mode is helpful when controlling motion in a

photograph. Photographers often use it to freeze movement or create motion blur.

6.4 Manual shooting mode

Manual mode gives photographers full control over aperture, shutter speed, and ISO

settings. This mode allows precise adjustments based on lighting conditions and creative

goals. Many experienced photographers use manual mode to achieve complete control over

exposure.
