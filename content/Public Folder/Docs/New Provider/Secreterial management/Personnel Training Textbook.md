INTRODUCTION
Welcome to the world of Personnel Training! This textbook is your first step towards
understanding how organisations prepare individuals for success, enhance workforce
productivity, and create opportunities for career growth. Whether you aim to work in human
resources, as a trainer, or simply want to understand how skills development impacts
organisational success, this textbook will provide the foundation you need.
Personnel training focuses on developing the knowledge, skills, and attitudes necessary for
effective workplace performance. It plays a critical role in ensuring that employees are not only
well-prepared for their roles but also continue to grow and adapt in a rapidly changing world. By
studying personnel training, you will understand how training contributes to individual and
organisational success.
Why Study Personnel Training?
Training is essential for any organisation to thrive. It addresses critical questions such as: How
do employees gain the skills they need? How can organisations improve productivity? How can
training contribute to personal and professional growth?
By studying Personnel Training, you will develop a deeper understanding of:
1. How education, training, and development are integrated into workplace practices.
2. The different methods and principles of adult learning.
3. How training programmes are planned, delivered, and assessed for effectiveness.
Whether you aim to become a trainer, work in a managerial role, or simply improve your own
learning skills, this textbook will serve as a valuable resource.
Here’s why learning personnel training is important:
 Building a Skilled Workforce: Training equips individuals with the skills needed to
perform their jobs effectively, ensuring organisations remain competitive and productive.
 Empowering Individuals: Learning opportunities enable individuals to advance in their
careers, boosting their confidence and job satisfaction.
 Adapting to Change: As industries evolve, continuous training ensures that workers stay
updated with new tools, methods, and technologies.

Introduction
Training and development play a vital role in building a skilled and capable workforce, which is
essential for economic growth and social progress. In South Africa, understanding the dynamics of
the labour market, education systems, and manpower policies is crucial for addressing challenges
such as unemployment, low productivity, and urbanisation. This module provides an in-depth
exploration of key concepts related to education, training, and development, while examining the
structure of the labour market and its role in shaping the country's workforce.
You will gain insights into the formal and informal sectors, the importance of adult basic education,
and the differences between labour-intensive and capital-intensive production. The module also
delves into South Africa's manpower policies, including the National Qualifications Framework
(NQF), and explores how mechanisation and technological advancements influence employment.
By understanding these critical elements, you will develop a strong foundation for analysing and
addressing the challenges and opportunities within South Africa's training and development
landscape.
1.1 Relevant Concepts
The concepts and terms discussed in this section form the foundation of this module and the entire
textbook. By understanding these key ideas, you will gain insights into how training and
development contribute to building a skilled and capable workforce.
1.1.1 Education, training and development
Education, training, and development are essential processes for improving the skills and
knowledge of individuals, enabling them to contribute effectively to the workplace and society.
Although these terms are often used together, they have distinct meanings.
1. Education
Education is a formal, systematic process of learning, often delivered in schools or recognised
institutions. It is designed to transmit knowledge, values, and skills that shape intellectual growth
and independent thinking. Education primarily focuses on preparing individuals for future roles in
society and the workplace.
 Definition:
Education involves deliberate and systematic learning that occurs over a long period, typically
in recognised institutions, with the aim of developing intellectual and independent individuals.
 Key Features of Education:
 It is long-term and knowledge-based.
 It develops intellectual capacity and critical thinking.
 It prepares individuals for societal and professional roles.
11 | Page

 Example: Secondary school education in mathematics and science to prepare students for
various career paths.
2. Training
Training is a short-term and targeted process aimed at enhancing specific skills and abilities needed
to perform tasks effectively. It is often technical or job-specific, focusing on equipping individuals
with practical skills to succeed in their roles.
 Definition:
Training is an activity-related exercise that provides individuals with the specific skills and
knowledge required for tasks and workplace activities.
 Goals of Training:
 Enable individuals to acquire new job-specific skills.
 Improve efficiency and performance in tasks.
 Modify behaviour for workplace harmony, e.g., disciplinary training.
 Example: Learning to use a particular software application in a technical training course.
 Effectiveness of Training: For training to be effective, it must have clear, measurable goals.
For instance, after attending a computer skills course, an employee should be able to perform
specific tasks efficiently.
3. Development
Development is a comprehensive, long-term process combining education and training. It aims at
preparing individuals for future needs by addressing both immediate and evolving demands.
 Definition:
Development is a long-term educational process combining training programmes and practical
experience to meet future professional and personal goals.
 Focus of Development:
 Broader skills acquisition.
 Preparing individuals for leadership or advanced roles.
 Addressing evolving industry or organisational needs.
 Example: Attending ongoing leadership workshops to prepare for managerial roles over time.
4. Learning
Learning underpins both education and training. It is the process through which individuals acquire
knowledge, skills, and competencies through study, experience, or instruction.
12 | Page

 Definition:
Learning is the continuous process of acquiring new knowledge or skills, contributing to
personal and professional growth.
 Importance of Learning:
 Encourages adaptability in dynamic environments.
 Supports lifelong personal and professional development.
5. Human Resource Development (HRD)
Human Resource Development is a key concept in the context of workplace training and
development. It focuses on the continuous improvement of employees' skills, starting from their
initial training upon hiring and continuing throughout their careers.
 Definition:
HRD includes all the training and learning opportunities provided to employees, beginning
from induction training to ongoing professional development.
 Example: Providing access to online courses and seminars for employees to improve their
technical skills.
 Quote Reflection: As Nelson Mandela states, "Education is the most powerful weapon you
can use to change the world." This highlights the transformative power of knowledge, skills,
and development, both for individuals and society.
Summary of Key Benefits:
 Education builds a foundation of knowledge for long-term intellectual growth.
 Training enhances specific skills to improve immediate job performance.
 Development prepares individuals for future challenges, ensuring sustained growth.
 Learning is an overarching process that drives continuous improvement and adaptation.
1.1.2 Knowledge, skills and attitude
The triad of knowledge, skills, and attitude (KSA) is integral to designing effective training and
development programmes. Each component contributes uniquely to a well-rounded and competent
individual who can contribute meaningfully to the workplace.
1. Knowledge
Knowledge involves the mental skills required to understand facts, concepts, principles, and
processes. It forms the foundation for any training programme and is typically acquired through
education and learning activities.
13 | Page

 Definition:
Knowledge is the information stored in our brains, such as understanding laws, company
policies, or specific job requirements.
 Examples of Training Areas:
 Organisational procedures and policies.
 Labour relations legislation.
 How to perform tasks like making a presentation or operating software.
Knowledge transfer equips employees with the intellectual tools needed for their roles, ensuring
clarity and confidence in executing their responsibilities.
2. Skills
Skills are the abilities developed through deliberate effort and practice. These enable individuals to
perform tasks effectively and efficiently, aligning with organisational goals.
 Definition:
A skill is an ability acquired through practice and effort, enabling individuals to carry out job
functions.
 Types of Skills:
 Cognitive skills: Involving ideas and thought processes (e.g., decision-making).
 Technical skills: Practical, hands-on abilities (e.g., using machinery).
 Interpersonal skills: Effective communication and teamwork.
In a workplace context, training specific skills enhances productivity. For example:
 Technical skills may include learning how to operate a new computer program.
 Interpersonal skills could involve conflict resolution in team settings.
3. Attitude
Attitude is often overlooked in training but is equally essential as it influences motivation, work ethic,
and interpersonal dynamics.
 Definition:
Attitude is the tendency to respond positively or negatively toward a person, object, or
situation. It shapes an individual's choice of action and governs their reactions.
 Importance in Training:
 Positive attitudes towards training encourage engagement and lead to successful
outcomes.
14 | Page

 Negative attitudes may hinder participation, wasting resources and reducing programme
effectiveness.
Employers and trainers must address attitudes through motivational strategies, fostering a
workplace culture that values learning and growth.
 Impact of Attitude on Training:
 Positive attitude: Employees view training as an opportunity for growth.
 Negative attitude: Employees may resist training, reducing its effectiveness.
Importance of the KSA Triad
The balance between knowledge, skills, and attitude ensures:
 Competence: Employees know what to do (knowledge), how to do it (skills), and are
motivated to perform (attitude).
 Adaptability: Workers can adjust to new challenges and changes in the workplace.
 Organisational Success: A workforce equipped with the right KSA mix contributes to
achieving organisational goals efficiently.
Incorporating KSA into Training
When developing training programmes:
1. Identify knowledge gaps (e.g., insufficient understanding of workplace policies).
2. Develop skills to address job-specific challenges (e.g., problem-solving).
3. Encourage a positive attitude through leadership and communication strategies.
With a strategic focus on knowledge, skills, and attitude, organisations can empower employees to
excel in their roles and contribute to a dynamic and productive work environment.
1.1.3 Formal and informal sectors
The economy and labour market in South Africa are categorised into formal and informal sectors.
These sectors are defined by the nature of work, regulations, taxation, and their contribution to the
overall economy. Understanding the characteristics of these sectors is essential for addressing
unemployment and improving economic productivity.
15 | Page

1. Formal Sector
Definition
The formal sector encompasses all jobs that are well-structured, involve normal working hours, and
provide regular wages. These jobs are taxed, monitored by the government, and contribute to the
gross domestic product (GDP).
Examples:
 Teachers in schools
 Doctors in hospitals
 Clerks in corporate offices
Characteristics of the Formal Sector
1. Structured and Regulated
 Includes industries monitored by government institutions and follows established laws.
 Activities are tracked using administrative systems, income tax legislation, and labour
laws.
2. Diverse Workforce
 Involves both skilled workers (e.g., engineers, architects) and unskilled workers (e.g.,
cleaners, packers).
3. Capital Intensive
 Generates significant economic activity and involves advanced services, such as
technological or medical industries.
4. Economic Contribution
 Plays a pivotal role in the formal economy, contributing to GDP and national
employment rates.
2. Informal Sector
Definition
The informal sector includes jobs or activities that are not officially recognised, taxed, or monitored
by the government. It often includes irregular work arrangements and provides income to those
excluded from the formal sector.
Examples:
 Street vendors
 Babysitters
 Handymen
16 | Page

Characteristics of the Informal Sector
1. Lack of Regulation
 Activities are not taxed or controlled by government regulations.
 No formal contracts or income documentation.
2. Income Opportunities for the Unemployed
 Offers jobs to unskilled and semi-skilled individuals, often with limited formal education.
 Serves as an alternative income source for those unable to access formal employment.
3. Entrepreneurial Activities
 Includes small-scale businesses such as street vending or home-based enterprises.
4. Challenges
 Often characterised by income instability, lack of benefits, and absence of social
security.
Government Role in Enhancing the Informal Sector
To grow the informal sector and integrate its benefits into the economy:
 Focus on training and development programmes to address the skills shortage.
 Promote entrepreneurship and provide resources to uplift informal workers.
Importance of Balancing Both Sectors
 The formal sector drives economic growth and provides stability through structured
employment.
 The informal sector offers flexibility and opportunities for individuals excluded from traditional
labour markets.
 By strengthening training and development, both sectors can work synergistically to reduce
unemployment and improve overall productivity in South Africa.
This balance ensures inclusivity and resilience within the labour market, addressing critical
challenges faced by the country's workforce.
1.1.4 Private and public sectors
Jobs are classified based on ownership into private and public sectors. These sectors differ
significantly in structure, objectives, and the services they provide.
17 | Page

1. Private Sector
 Definition:
The private sector, often referred to as the "citizen sector," is comprised of organisations and
enterprises owned and managed by private individuals or groups. Its primary goal is to
generate profits, and it operates independently of government control.
 Characteristics of the Private Sector:
1. Ownership: Run by private individuals or groups aiming to earn a profit.
2. Autonomy: Sets its own objectives and operational methods within the confines of the
law.
3. Funding: Capital comes from private investors or shareholders.
4. Government Role: The government does not manage or own these organisations.
5. Examples: Retail stores, supermarkets, farms, and service-based businesses like
private clinics.
2. Public Sector
 Definition:
The public sector includes organisations owned, operated, or subsidised by the government.
Its focus is on providing essential services rather than generating profit.
 Characteristics of the Public Sector:
1. Ownership: Managed, owned, or funded by state, provincial, or municipal governments.
2. Purpose: Provides essential and large-scale services, such as healthcare and educa-
tion, which are often costly or impractical for individuals to handle independently.
3. Funding: Operates using taxpayer funds, ensuring services are affordable or free.
4. Non-Profit Nature: These enterprises prioritise societal benefit over profit-making.
5. Examples: Public schools, hospitals, transportation systems, and electricity suppliers
like Eskom in South Africa.
Both sectors play crucial roles in economic and social development, complementing each other to
ensure that citizens' needs are met effectively.

1.1.5 Adult basic education
Adult Basic Education and Training (ABET) is a critical programme designed to address the
challenges of unemployment, illiteracy, and limited skill sets in South Africa. It provides a foundation
for adults to engage in lifelong learning and equips them with the skills necessary to contribute to
the workforce and society.
Definition
ABET refers to structured education programmes targeting adults who lack formal education or
have limited educational qualifications. It focuses on equipping learners with basic literacy,
numeracy, and functional skills necessary for their personal and professional growth.
Components of ABET
1. Adult Basic Education:
 Provides foundational literacy and numeracy skills.
 Enables individuals to carry out essential tasks such as reading, writing, and managing
basic calculations.
2. Further Education and Training (FET):
 Focuses on skill development for employment and personal growth.
 Includes vocational and technical training to improve job prospects.
3. Example: Training participants in computer literacy or financial management.
4. Adult Education and Training (AET):
 Combines the components of ABET and FET into a comprehensive framework.
 Prepares learners for lifelong learning opportunities and career advancement.
Goals/Objectives of ABET
The primary goals of ABET include:
1. Creating a Learning Culture: Encourages lifelong education and personal development.
2. Improving Employment Opportunities: Equips adults with the skills necessary to secure
better jobs and increase their earning potential.
3. Empowering Communities: Provides tools for individuals to improve their social awareness,
engage in civic activities, and contribute to economic development.
19 | Page

Focus Areas of ABET
ABET focuses on two main areas:
1. Basic Literacy and Numeracy Skills:
Aims to help individuals develop the ability to read, write, and perform basic arithmetic.
2. Functional Skills:
Provides training in practical skills required for workplace productivity and income generation.
Importance of ABET
 Addresses skill shortages in the workforce.
 Reduces unemployment by equipping adults with relevant skills.
 Promotes social equity by providing education to marginalised groups.
 Supports economic development by creating a more skilled and competent workforce.
Example of ABET in Action
Consider an adult who has never attended formal schooling. Through an ABET programme, they
can learn basic literacy and numeracy, enabling them to apply for entry-level jobs. With additional
training in areas like computer skills, they can further advance their career prospects.
ABET is not only a tool for individual empowerment but also a cornerstone for national
development, ensuring inclusivity and progress for all.
1.1.6 Labour market
The labour market is a critical aspect of any economy, serving as the space where employers and
employees meet to exchange skills and services. It reflects the dynamics of employment and
workforce development and is influenced by various economic factors.
Definition
The labour market is the place where workers and employers interact to exchange services for
wages. It includes all individuals available and willing to work and the businesses seeking
employees to fill specific roles.
Key Characteristics of the Labour Market
1. Demand for Labour:
 Refers to the number of workers employers require to fulfil organisational needs.
 Demand is determined by the industry’s economic activities, technological
advancements, and the required skill levels.
20 | Page

2. Supply of Labour:
 Refers to individuals offering their skills and time in exchange for employment and
wages.
 Includes skilled, semi-skilled, and unskilled workers.
3. Labour Exchange:
 Employers compete for skilled workers to improve business productivity.
 Employees compete for jobs that offer satisfaction and align with their skill levels.
Challenges in South Africa’s Labour Market
1. Oversupply of Low-Skilled Workers:
 Many job seekers lack the qualifications and expertise required for modern industries.
 This leads to high unemployment rates, particularly among unskilled labourers.
2. Shortage of Skilled Workers:
 There is a significant gap in technological, technical, and scientific skills.
 Employers face difficulties finding qualified professionals for high-demand roles.
3. Imbalance in Supply and Demand:
 Ideally, the labour market should maintain a balance between available jobs and the
workforce.
 In South Africa, this balance is disrupted, causing economic inefficiencies.
Importance of Addressing Labour Market Challenges
 To stimulate economic growth, it is essential to invest in education and training programmes.
 Bridging the skill gap ensures a workforce capable of meeting industry needs.
 Adult Basic Education and Training (ABET) initiatives can play a vital role in upskilling
workers.
Example
A manufacturing company may find itself with an excess of unskilled workers for entry-level tasks
but face a shortage of engineers to design and manage advanced machinery. Such a scenario
highlights the importance of targeted training programmes to equip the workforce with relevant
skills.
By understanding and addressing these dynamics, South Africa can create a more balanced and
effective labour market that supports both economic growth and social development.
