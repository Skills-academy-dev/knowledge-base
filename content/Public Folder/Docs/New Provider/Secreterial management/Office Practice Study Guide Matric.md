MODULE 1

ORIENTATION TO THE SECRETARIAL CAREER

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this module, you should be able to:

  Understand the key roles and responsibilities of a secretary or management assistant.

Identify career progression opportunities in this field.

  Recognise specialised areas within secretarial work.

  Evaluate the impact of technology on the profession.

  Understand factors influencing remuneration in the role.

12

INTRODUCTION

The role of a secretary or management assistant is critical to the smooth functioning of

modern organisations. Once perceived as a purely administrative position, the profession

has evolved to include a broad range of responsibilities that contribute to an organisation’s

success. Secretaries and management assistants serve as the backbone of office opera-

tions, ensuring that tasks are completed efficiently and deadlines are met.

Unit 1: Job Description of the Secretary/Management Assistant

A secretary or management assistant plays a central role in ensuring that organisational
activities are well-coordinated and operate efficiently. This role has transformed over the
years into a position that demands technical skills, organisational expertise, and excellent
communication abilities.

Key Responsibilities:

  Managing communication: Emails, letters, phone calls, and virtual platforms.

  Scheduling and calendar management: Organising appointments and meetings.

  Preparing and managing documents: Drafting and editing professional documents.

  Coordinating events: Planning and managing office functions and events.

Refer to pages 11–13 of your textbook.

Unit 2: The Promotion Route

The secretarial profession offers significant opportunities for career growth and advance-
ment.

Key points:

  Junior Secretary: Entry-level  role handling basic tasks.

  Senior Secretary: Handles more complex  tasks and reports  to higher-level manag-

ers.

  Personal Assistant (PA): Dedicated to a senior  manager, managing their  schedule

and responsibilities.

  Executive Assistant (EA): High-level  role with strategic responsibilities.

Refer to pages 14–15 of your textbook.

13

Unit 3: Specialised Fields of Secretarial Work

Specialisation in secretarial work allows professionals to focus on specific industries.

Examples:

  Legal Secretary: Supporting legal teams with documentation  and court filings.

  Medical Secretary: Managing patient records  and scheduling.

  Financial Secretary: Assisting  with  financial  reporting  and data management.

Refer to pages 16–17 of your textbook.

Unit 4: The Impact of Technological Resources on Performance

Advancements in technology have significantly influenced the efficiency and effectiveness
of secretarial work.

Key impacts:

  Use of software tools for scheduling, document processing, and communication.

  Automation of repetitive tasks, improving productivity.

  Enhanced collaboration through digital platforms.

Refer to pages 19–20 of your textbook.

14

Unit 5: Factors that May Influence the Remuneration of the Management Assistant

The remuneration of a management assistant is influenced by various factors, including:

  Level of experience and education.

Industry or sector of employment.

  Complexity of responsibilities and additional certifications.

Refer to pages 22–25 of your textbook.

Summary

This module provided an overview of the secretarial career, highlighting the importance of

effective communication, organisation, and adaptability in the modern office environment. It

outlined the promotion route, specialised fields, and technological influences while address-

ing remuneration factors for the role.

THE TELEPHONE: TECHNIQUES AND TECHNICAL AIDS

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this module, you should be able to:

  Understand the various types of telephone systems used in business.

  Explain the general uses of the telephone in professional settings.

  Recognise the importance of the telephone in the business world.

  Develop techniques for effective handling of telephone calls.

  Respond appropriately to emergency situations using telephonic communication.

INTRODUCTION

The telephone remains a vital communication tool in the business environment despite the

rise of digital communication platforms. It is essential for secretaries and management as-

sistants to be proficient in using telephone systems effectively, maintaining professionalism,

and handling calls with confidence. This module explores different telephone systems, their

uses, and best practices for managing calls in a business setting.

Unit 1: Different Types of Telephone Systems

Organisations use various telephone systems depending on their size and communication
needs.

Key types:

  Landline systems: Traditional phones for internal and external communication.

  VoIP (Voice over Internet Protocol): Internet-based  systems offering cost-effective

solutions.

  Mobile systems: Portable communication  tools for professionals  on the go.

  PBX (Private Branch Exchange): Internal telephone  networks  for large organisa-

tions.

Refer to pages 31–33 of your textbook.

Unit 2: General Uses of the Business Telephone System

The telephone is indispensable for day-to-day business operations.

Key uses:

Internal communication: Coordinating  tasks within  the office.

  External communication: Interacting with clients,  suppliers,  and stakeholders.

  Customer service: Addressing  inquiries,  complaints,  and feedback.

  Scheduling: Arranging appointments  and meetings.

Refer to pages 34–35 of your textbook.

17

Unit 3: The Importance of the Telephone in the Business World

The telephone plays a critical role in maintaining effective communication and enhancing
customer relationships.

Key points:

  Provides instant connectivity for urgent matters.

  Facilitates personalised interactions, fostering trust and goodwill.

  Serves as a reliable tool for remote business dealings.

Refer to pages 36–37 of your textbook.

Unit 4: Techniques for Receiving Telephone Calls

Handling telephone calls professionally reflects positively on the organisation.

Best practices:

  Answer calls promptly and greet the caller warmly.

  Listen attentively and take accurate messages.

  Use polite language and maintain a calm tone, even in challenging situations.

Refer to pages 38–39 of your textbook.

18

Unit 5: Emergency Situations

In emergencies, clear and effective communication is crucial to ensure timely responses.

Key steps:

  Stay calm and gather essential details from the caller.

  Notify the appropriate authorities or departments immediately.

  Follow the organisation’s emergency protocols.

Refer to pages 40–41 of your textbook.

Summary

This module provided an overview of telephone systems, their uses, and techniques for

managing calls professionally in a business environment. It highlighted the importance of

effective telephonic communication and strategies for handling emergencies.

Meetings and conferences are vital for effective communication and decision-making in or-

ganisations. Management assistants play a key role in ensuring that these events are well-

organised and run smoothly. This module focuses on the preparation, execution, and follow

-up tasks involved in meetings and conferences, providing practical insights into profession-

al event management.

Unit 1: Assisting with Meetings

Meetings are essential for collaboration, problem-solving, and decision-making.

Key preparation steps:

  Prepare and distribute meeting agendas in advance.

  Arrange the meeting venue and necessary equipment.

  Record accurate minutes during the meeting.

Unit 2: The Role of the Management Assistant When Assisting with Meetings

The management assistant ensures that all meeting logistics are handled efficiently.

Key responsibilities:

  Confirm attendance of participants and manage RSVPs.

  Ensure all documentation is available and organised.

  Address any technical issues during the meeting.

Key aspects:

  Prepare materials such as presentations, programs, and attendee packets.

  Congress: A large gathering to discuss specific topics  or issues.

INTRODUCTION

Office supplies are essential for the smooth functioning of any organisation. Efficient man-

agement of office supplies ensures cost control, prevents waste, and supports productivity.

This module focuses on the processes of purchasing, storing, and issuing office supplies,

as well as the principles of stock control and maintaining an organised supplies room. .

Unit 1: Procedures for Purchasing Office Supplies

Proper procurement procedures ensure that office supplies are acquired efficiently and cost
-effectively.

Key steps:

Identify the needs of the office and prepare a requisition list.

  Obtain quotes and select reliable suppliers.

  Place orders and confirm delivery timelines.

Efficient storage practices help maintain the quality and accessibility of office supplies.

Key practices:

  Use clearly labelled shelves or bins for categorising supplies.

  Store items in a clean, dry, and secure location.

  Monitor expiry dates for items like ink cartridges and stationery.

Stock control ensures that sufficient supplies are available without overstocking or wastage.

Key methods:

  Perform regular inventory checks to update stock records.

  Use stock control systems to track usage and replenishment.

  Set reorder levels to avoid running out of essential items.

  Limit access to supplies for accountability.

Refer to pages 86–87 of your textbook.

  Display inventory lists for quick reference.

Refer to pages 88–89 of your textbook.

INTRODUCTION

Efficient document management is essential for the smooth operation of any organisation.

Proper preparation, collection, processing, and filing of documents ensure that information

is easily accessible, well-organised, and secure. This module focuses on the principles of

filing systems, classification, arrangement, and equipment to maintain an efficient filing en-

vironment.

Unit 1: Filing and Indexing Systems

Filing and indexing systems provide the framework for organising documents systematical-
ly.

Key systems:

  Alphabetical filing: Based on names or titles arranged in alphabetical order.

  Numerical filing: Uses numbers  for classification  and retrieval.

  Subject filing: Organises documents  by topic or category.

Refer to pages 112–113  of your textbook.

Unit 2: Classification of Files

File classification ensures that documents are grouped logically for easy retrieval.

Key classifications:

  Active files: Frequently accessed and used for ongoing  tasks.

Inactive files: Retained for reference or legal purposes  but not regularly used.

  Archived files: Permanently stored for historical  or compliance reasons.

Refer to pages 114–115  of your textbook.

37

Unit 3: Arrangement of Files

Arranging files systematically reduces the time spent searching for documents.

Methods of arrangement:

  Chronological order: Organised by date.

  Geographical order: Grouped based on location.

  Departmental order: Sorted according to department or function.

Refer to pages 116–117  of your textbook.

Unit 4: Requirements of a Good Filing System

An effective filing system ensures efficiency, accuracy, and security.

Key requirements:

  Accessibility: Files should be easy to locate and retrieve.

  Simplicity: The system should be straightforward and user-friendly.

  Security: Sensitive information must be protected from unauthorised access.

Refer to pages 118–119  of your textbook.

38

Unit 5: Filing and Indexing Equipment and Basic Filing Procedures

Proper equipment and procedures are essential for maintaining an organised filing system.

Key equipment:

  Filing cabinets, shelves, and storage boxes.

Index cards and digital indexing software.

Basic procedures:

  Label files clearly with consistent naming conventions.

  Conduct regular audits to ensure files are up-to-date.

  Dispose of outdated or unnecessary files according to organisational policies.

Refer to pages 120–121 of your textbook.

Summary

This module provided an in-depth look at the principles of document management, including

filing systems, classification, and arrangement. It also highlighted the requirements of an

effective filing system and the importance of using proper equipment and procedures to
