Nail technology is a professional discipline within the beauty industry that blends art and

science. It focuses on improving the appearance and condition of natural nails, applying

enhancements, and ensuring client wellbeing. Through this module, you will explore how

professionalism, hygiene, and continuous learning form the foundation of success in nail care.

You will also learn about the structure of the industry, professional expectations, and

opportunities for career development.

Introduction

Scientific concepts form the foundation of professional nail care. Every nail technician must

understand how to prevent infections, maintain hygiene, identify nail and skin conditions, and

use products safely. This module explores infection control, anatomy, and product chemistry —

essential knowledge for maintaining salon safety and client wellbeing.

Refer to page 35 of your textbook for detailed information

Unit 1: Infection Control and Sanitation Procedures

Infection control and sanitation are essential to ensure the safety of both clients and

professionals. Nail services often involve close skin contact, so strict hygiene procedures must

be followed to prevent cross-contamination and disease transmission.

1.1 Importance of Infection Control in Nail Technology

Explains the need for infection prevention to protect against bacteria, viruses, and fungi that can

spread through tools, hands, or contaminated surfaces.

1.2 Levels of Infection Control

Describes the three key levels:

●  Sanitation – removal of visible dirt and debris using soap and water.

●  Disinfection – use of chemical agents to destroy harmful microorganisms.

●  Sterilisation – complete elimination of all microbial life using specialised equipment

such as autoclaves.

1.3 Sanitation and Disinfection Practices

Outlines the daily cleaning procedures that maintain salon hygiene, including:

●  Washing hands before and after each client.

●  Cleaning and disinfecting tools, surfaces, and equipment.

●  Disposing of single-use materials safely.

15

1.4 Blood Exposure Procedures

Provides step-by-step guidance for handling incidents involving blood or injury during a service,

including stopping the procedure, disinfecting affected areas, and documenting the incident

according to salon policy.

1.5 Preventing Cross-Contamination

Describes ways to minimise the spread of infection by using clean towels, separating clean and

used tools, avoiding unnecessary contact during services, and using spatulas instead of fingers

for product removal.

1.6 Legal Standards and Compliance

Explains that all nail technicians must comply with national and local hygiene regulations. Non-

compliance can result in fines or closure of the salon. Emphasises the technician’s ethical and

legal responsibility to maintain safety standards.

Unit 3: Product Handling and Storage Procedures

Correct product handling and storage are essential for safety, hygiene, and efficiency in the

salon. Nail products often contain chemicals that can be harmful if misused. Knowing how to

store and manage these items prevents accidents, protects clients, and maintains product

quality.

3.1 Understanding Nail Care Products

Different nail products have specific uses and safety needs. Always keep bottles sealed, store

away from heat, and handle flammable or strong chemicals with care. Test new products on

sensitive skin before full use to avoid allergic reactions.

3.2 Safe Handling Procedures

Follow manufacturer instructions and use protective gear when working with chemicals. Keep

liquids away from electrical equipment, never dip fingers into containers, and label all products

clearly to prevent accidents.

3.3 Storage of Nail Care Products

Store flammable liquids, disinfectants, and gels in cool, dry, and well-ventilated areas. Keep lids

tightly closed, and record expiry dates in a “Product Safety Log” to ensure safe usage.

3.4 Chemical Safety and Label Reading

Read all product labels and Safety Data Sheets (SDS) carefully. Know key warnings such as

flammable, toxic, or irritant and always store dangerous products securely, away from children

and heat.

3.5 Disposal of Waste Products

Dispose of chemical waste safely — never pour it down sinks or drains. Use lined bins for

disposables and separate containers for chemical waste. Follow local waste disposal laws and

empty bins regularly.

3.6 Environmental Responsibility

Support sustainability by using eco-friendly and refillable products, recycling packaging,

reducing single-use plastics, and avoiding product wastage. Environmentally responsible

practices protect both people and the planet.

Unit 4: Safe and Proper E-File Techniques

The electric file (E-File) is an essential modern tool in nail technology that enhances speed,

precision, and service quality. When used correctly, it improves efficiency and results; however,

misuse can cause pain, heat damage, or injury. This unit explains how to operate the E-File

safely, identify bit types, and maintain professional standards.

4.1 Understanding the E-File

An E-File is a motorised tool with adjustable speeds and interchangeable bits for shaping and

refining nails. Key parts include the handpiece, bits, speed control dial, power source, and dust

collector. Always begin at a low speed and increase gradually to avoid friction burns.

4.2 Types of E-File Bits and Their Uses

Each bit serves a specific purpose: mandrel bits smooth surfaces, carbide bits remove acrylic,

diamond bits clean edges, ceramic bits reduce heat, and buffer bits polish nails. Colour rings

indicate grit levels—red for fine, blue for medium, and green for coarse.

4.3 Safety Procedures for E-File Use

Disinfect the handpiece and bits before and after each client. Avoid pressure or working on

damaged nails, and keep the bit moving to prevent heat build-up. Always wear a mask, safety

glasses, and check client comfort regularly. Practise on artificial nails before servicing clients.

4.4 Hygiene and Maintenance

Clean and disinfect bits after each use, store them in labelled containers, and replace worn

parts immediately. Follow manufacturer’s guidelines for servicing and calibration to ensure

safety and durability.

4.5 Professionalism and Employability Skills

Safe and effective E-File use demonstrates professionalism. Employers value technicians who

show technical skill, hygiene awareness, time management, clear communication, and

responsibility in maintaining salon standards.

24

4.6 Common Mistakes to Avoid

Avoid using too much pressure, filing too close to the natural nail, working at high speeds, or

using damaged or dirty bits. Skipping aftercare cleaning reduces hygiene and professionalism.

Unit 5: Client Consultation and Documentation

Client consultation is a vital part of every nail care service. It helps the technician understand

client needs, identify potential health issues, and choose appropriate treatments. A professional

consultation builds trust, ensures safety, and improves client satisfaction and loyalty.

5.1 The Importance of Consultation

Consultations allow you to assess client expectations, identify contraindications, select suitable

products, and create a personalised treatment plan. Always greet clients warmly and

communicate professionally to make a positive first impression.

5.2 Stages of a Client Consultation

Each consultation includes greeting, observation, questioning, recommendations, consent, and

record keeping. This structured approach ensures professionalism, accuracy, and client comfort

throughout the service.

5.3 Information to Record on a Client Card

Client cards should include personal details, medical history, allergies, nail conditions, service

information, and aftercare advice. Keep these records updated after every visit to monitor

progress and maintain accuracy.

5.4 Professional Communication Skills

Good communication—both verbal and non-verbal—is essential. Speak clearly, use positive

language, and avoid gossip. Maintain friendly body language, active listening, and eye contact

to build confidence and comfort.

5.5 Maintaining Confidentiality and Professional Boundaries

Protect client information by keeping records secure and discussing details only with authorised

staff. Avoid personal conversations, respect privacy, and focus on providing a professional, safe

service.

25

5.6 Documentation and Legal Requirements

Accurate documentation, such as consent forms and treatment notes, ensures compliance with

salon policies and legal standards. Proper record keeping protects both the client and the

technician, promoting professionalism and accountability.

5.7 Aftercare Advice

Aftercare advice helps clients maintain nail results and prevents damage. Remind clients to

avoid using nails as tools, wear gloves when cleaning, apply cuticle oil daily, and return for

maintenance. Providing written advice reinforces professionalism and care.

5.8 Employability Link
