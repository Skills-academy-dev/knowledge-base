7 STUDY TIPS FOR SUCCESS

1. Create a Consistent Study Routine

Set aside specific times during the week to focus on your coursework. A steady routine helps

you stay organised and prevents last-minute stress.

2. Break Modules into Manageable Sections

Instead of trying to study an entire module at once, divide it into smaller units. This makes the

content easier to understand and remember.

3. Use Active Learning Techniques

Summarise key concepts in your own words, create mind maps, or practise explaining the

material out loud. Active learning helps you retain information more effectively.

4. Practise Hands-On Skills Regularly

Facial therapy is a practical field — try to apply techniques where possible. Even small

exercises like practising draping, cleansing movements, or tool handling can reinforce your

learning.

5. Review Your Notes After Each Study Session

Revisiting notes soon after learning helps strengthen memory and identify areas that may

need more attention.

6. Ask Questions When You Need Help

If you are unsure about a concept or technique, reach out for support. Clarifying early helps

you avoid confusion later on.

7. Stay Motivated and Pace Yourself

Set small goals, track your progress, and celebrate milestones. Consistent effort is more

effective than long, overwhelming study sessions.

Introduction

This module introduces the foundations of facial therapy and the professional standards

required in the beauty industry. It explains the purpose of facial treatments, the role of the

therapist, hygiene requirements, and how to create a safe and comfortable treatment

environment.

Read page 10 of your textbook for the full module introduction.

Unit 1: Understanding Facial Therapy

This unit explains what facial therapy is, the benefits it provides, and how it supports client

well-being.

Read pages 11–14 of your textbook for more information on the topics below.

1.1 What is facial therapy?

Facial therapy is a specialised treatment that cleanses, nourishes, and improves the condition

of the skin through scientific and therapeutic techniques.

1.2 Benefits of facial treatments

Facial treatments help improve circulation, enhance skin health, support relaxation, and

address a variety of skin concerns.

1.3 Different types of facials: spa, medi, and holistic

Spa facials focus on relaxation, medi-facials use advanced technology for corrective results,

and holistic facials balance both skin and emotional well-being.

1.4 How facial therapy enhances client well-being

Facial therapy promotes physical and emotional wellness by reducing stress, improving

appearance, and boosting confidence.

Unit 2: The Role of the Therapist

This unit outlines the professional expectations, ethics, and behaviours required of a facial

therapist.

Read pages 15–18 of your textbook for more information on the topics below

2.1 Professional conduct and image

A therapist must maintain a neat appearance, behave professionally, and present themselves

in a way that reflects industry standards.

11

2.2 Communication and client comfort

Clear, respectful communication helps clients feel understood, safe, and relaxed throughout

the treatment.

2.3 Confidentiality and ethical responsibilities

Therapists must protect client information and follow ethical guidelines to build trust and

maintain professional integrity.

2.4 Workplace readiness and teamwork

Being prepared, dependable, and cooperative ensures smooth salon operations and positive

client experiences.

Unit 3: Hygiene and Health

This unit explains the hygiene procedures required to protect clients and maintain a safe

working environment.

Read pages 19–23 of your textbook for more information on the topics below.

3.1 Sanitation, disinfection, and sterilisation

These hygiene methods remove dirt, kill harmful microbes, and ensure tools and surfaces

remain safe for client use.

3.2 Personal hygiene and therapist preparation

Therapists must maintain clean hands, tidy appearance, and proper grooming to avoid

transferring contaminants.

3.3 Infection control and cross-contamination prevention

Following infection-control procedures prevents the spread of bacteria and keeps both

therapist and client protected.

3.4 Safe waste disposal and housekeeping

Correct disposal of waste and regular cleaning routines help maintain a tidy, hazard-free

treatment environment.

Unit 4: Setting Up the Treatment Area

This unit explains how to prepare and organise the workspace to ensure safety, comfort, and

professionalism.

Read pages 24–26 of your textbook for more information on the topics below.

12

4.1 Salon/treatment room layout

A well-organised room layout ensures efficient workflow and provides a calm, welcoming

environment for clients.

4.2 Preparing tools, towels, and products

All equipment and materials must be cleaned, arranged, and ready before the client arrives to

ensure smooth treatment delivery.

4.3 Correct use of PPE (personal protective equipment)

Using PPE appropriately protects both client and therapist from contamination and exposure

to harmful substances.

4.4 Client comfort and safety checks

Adjusting lighting, temperature, and bed position helps ensure that clients feel relaxed and

secure throughout the session.

Unit 5: Health and Safety Regulations

This unit outlines the legal and safety requirements that guide professional practice in the

beauty industry.

Read pages 29–32 of your textbook for more information on the topics below.

5.1 Legal responsibilities and safety standards

Therapists must follow government and industry regulations to maintain a safe and compliant

working environment.

5.2 Risk assessments and accident reporting

Risk assessments identify potential hazards, while accurate reporting ensures accountability

and prevents future incidents.

5.3 Equipment and electrical safety

Understanding how to use and maintain electrical devices correctly helps prevent injuries and

equipment damage.

5.4 Emergency procedures and first aid readiness

Knowledge of emergency protocols allows therapists to respond quickly and effectively during

incidents.

Unit 1: Client Consultation Techniques

This unit introduces the consultation process and explains how to gather accurate information

to guide safe and effective treatment planning.

Read pages 39–42 of your textbook for more information on the topics below.

1.1 The purpose of consultation

A consultation helps the therapist understand the client’s needs, concerns, and expectations

before beginning any treatment.

1.2 Building rapport and trust

Creating a comfortable and respectful environment allows clients to feel safe and confident

sharing personal information.

1.3 Completing client record cards

Record cards document essential details such as medical history, skin concerns, and

treatment notes to ensure continuity and safety.

1.4 Gathering lifestyle and health information

Understanding factors such as diet, stress, medication, and routines helps identify influences

on the client’s skin health.

Unit 2: Skin Analysis and Assessment

This unit explains how to identify skin types and conditions using visual, tactile, and

professional assessment tools.

Read pages 43–48 of your textbook for more information on the topics below.

2.1 Skin types (normal, dry, oily, combination, sensitive)

Identifying the client’s skin type guides product selection and ensures appropriate treatment

techniques are used.

16

2.2 Common skin conditions (acne, pigmentation, ageing)

Recognising common conditions helps the therapist adapt treatments to address concerns

safely and effectively.

2.3 Visual and tactile analysis techniques

Observing the skin and gently feeling its texture helps determine hydration levels, congestion,

elasticity, and overall condition.

2.4 Using tools like magnifying lamps and Woods lamps

These tools enhance visibility and help identify issues such as pigmentation, dryness,

oiliness, and underlying skin irregularities.

Unit 3: Understanding the Skin Barrier and Microbiome

This unit explores the protective structure of the skin barrier, the role of the microbiome, and

how to maintain or repair it.

Read pages 49–54 of your textbook for more information on the topics below.

3.1 The structure and function of the skin barrier

The skin barrier prevents moisture loss and shields the skin from environmental irritants and

pathogens.

3.2 What the skin microbiome is and why it matters

The microbiome is the community of microorganisms on the skin that helps protect against

infection and maintain balance.

3.3 Signs and symptoms of a compromised barrier

A weakened barrier may appear as dryness, redness, sensitivity, irritation, or increased

breakouts.

3.4 Common causes of barrier damage

Harsh products, over-exfoliation, environmental stress, and poor lifestyle habits can weaken

the skin barrier.

3.5 Professional strategies to repair and protect the barrier

Therapists can restore the barrier through gentle treatments, hydration, barrier-supporting

products, and reduced irritation.

3.6 Ingredients that support a healthy microbiome (ceramides, niacinamide,

probiotics)

These ingredients strengthen the barrier, improve moisture retention, and promote a

balanced skin environment.

correctly before beginning a facial.

The workstation must be clean, organised, and stocked with all required tools to ensure a

smooth and hygienic treatment process.

1.2 Draping and positioning the client correctly

Proper draping and positioning keep the client comfortable and supported while allowing the

therapist full access to the face.

1.3 Product selection and patch testing

Choosing suitable products and performing patch tests help prevent irritation and ensure

client safety during treatment.

1.4 Maintaining professionalism during preparation

Professional behaviour during setup reassures clients and sets the tone for a safe and

respectful treatment.

This unit describes the cleansing steps and exfoliation methods used to prepare the skin for

deeper treatment.

Read pages 78–81 of your textbook for more information on the topics below.

2.1 Step-by-step cleansing routine (first and second cleanse)

A structured cleansing routine removes surface impurities and prepares the skin for further

treatment.

2.2 Exfoliation techniques: mechanical and chemical

21

Mechanical exfoliation removes dead skin manually, while chemical exfoliation dissolves

buildup using active ingredients.

2.3 Safety precautions for exfoliation

Understanding exfoliation limits helps prevent over-irritation, redness, and damage to the skin

barrier.

2.4 Selecting exfoliants for each skin type

Different exfoliants are chosen based on skin type to ensure safe, effective, and personalised

Unit 3: Steaming and Extraction

This unit explains the purpose of steam, how to apply it safely, and how to perform manual

extractions correctly.

Read pages 82–85 of your textbook for more information on the topics below.

3.1 Purpose and benefits of steam

Steam softens the skin and helps loosen impurities, making extraction easier and more

comfortable.

3.2 Proper steaming distance and duration

Maintaining the correct distance and time prevents burns and avoids overstimulating the skin.

3.3 Manual extraction techniques

Extractions should be performed gently and precisely to remove congestion without causing

trauma.

3.4 Post-extraction hygiene and care

After extraction, the skin must be sanitised and soothed to reduce irritation and prevent

infection.

Unit 4: Facial Massage Techniques

This unit outlines the benefits, steps, and variations of facial massage, as well as tools and

safety considerations.

Read pages 86–95 of your textbook for more information on the topics below.

4.1 Benefits of facial massage

Facial massage improves circulation, relaxation, and the absorption of skincare products.

22

4.2 Step-by-step procedure: effleurage, petrissage, tapotement

These massage movements work together to stimulate tissues, release tension, and improve

skin tone.

4.3 Lymphatic drainage and pressure point application

Gentle drainage techniques and pressure point work support detoxification and balance

within the skin.

4.4 Choosing suitable massage mediums (oils, creams, serums)

Different massage mediums are selected based on skin type, treatment goals, and desired

glide.

4.5 Gua Sha and facial cupping: benefits, tools, and safe application

These advanced tools enhance circulation and sculpting when used correctly with controlled

pressure.

4.6 Hygiene, contraindications, and care of massage tools

Massage tools must be sanitised and used appropriately to prevent irritation, injury, or

contamination.

Unit 5: Mask and Finishing Techniques

This unit explains how to apply masks, remove them correctly, and complete the facial using

finishing products and aftercare advice.

Read pages 96–103 of your textbook for more information on the topics below.

5.1 Different types of masks and their purposes

Masks are selected to hydrate, calm, purify, or revitalise the skin depending on the client’s

needs.

5.2 Correct application and removal methods

Applying masks evenly and removing them gently ensures both comfort and treatment

effectiveness.

5.3 Finishing products (toner, serum, moisturiser, SPF)

These products complete the facial by restoring balance, protecting the skin, and supporting

long-term results.

5.4 Delivering aftercare and home-care advice

Aftercare guidance helps clients maintain their facial results and support ongoing skin

improvement.

Learning Objectives

After completing this module, you should be able to:

●  Explain the difference between standard facial treatments and advanced or

medi-facials.

●  Understand the scientific principles behind advanced exfoliation and skin

rejuvenation techniques.

●

Identify indications, benefits, and contraindications for advanced facial treatments.

●  Describe the procedures, safety measures, and professional boundaries related to

dermaplaning and microneedling.

●  Understand the roles of active ingredients (such as acids, enzymes, and serums)

used in advanced facials.

●  Demonstrate professional knowledge of device-assisted procedures and client

preparation steps.

●  Apply correct pre- and post-care guidelines for advanced skin treatments to ensure

safety and best results.

●  Recognise when to refer clients to medical professionals for specialised care or

treatment complications.

●  Maintain hygiene, recordkeeping, and client communication standards required for

advanced skincare practice.

●

Integrate medi-facial theory with practical skills to deliver customised, results-driven

facial therapies.
