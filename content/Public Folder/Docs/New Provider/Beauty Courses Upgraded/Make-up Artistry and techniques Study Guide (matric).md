7 STUDY TIPS

1. Break Your Study into Short, Focused Sessions

Your brain blends knowledge better when you study in 20–40 minute chunks instead of

marathon sessions. Think quality over quantity!

2. Practise Techniques as You Learn Them

Makeup is a hands-on skill — the more you blend, swatch, sculpt, and shade, the faster your

muscle memory levels up.

3. Take Smart Notes (Not Long Notes)

Write down formulas, steps, must-know terms, and product rules. Keep it concise so you can

revise quickly before assessments.

4. Use Your Phone Camera as a Study Tool

Record yourself doing techniques, compare looks, and track your progress. Photos reveal

things your eyes sometimes miss.

5. Teach Someone Else What You Learned

Explaining undertones, contour placement, or hygiene rules to a friend helps lock the

knowledge into your brain instantly.

6. Build Mini-Practice Sessions into Your Week

Five minutes of brow mapping here, a quick shade-matching drill there — tiny repetitions add

up to huge skill gains.

7. Stay Curious and Experiment

Don’t be afraid to try new colours, textures, or techniques. Creativity is part of the job, and

mistakes are just learning in disguise.

Introduction

Module 1 introduces the foundations of professional makeup artistry, blending the creative

and technical skills needed to enhance, transform, and celebrate natural beauty. It explores

the relationship between facial anatomy, colour theory, skin analysis, and core application

techniques that help artists create balanced and long-lasting looks. This module also

emphasises professional standards such as hygiene, client care, ethical behaviour, and the

ability to adapt makeup techniques within South Africa’s diverse cultural and environmental

context. Through practical guidance and conceptual understanding, learners begin building

the solid base required for more advanced artistry across later modules.

Unit 1: Introduction to the Art and Profession

Read pages 12-15 of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

This unit introduces the foundations of the makeup profession by exploring its history,

purpose, and the responsibilities that define a professional MUA. It explains how makeup has

evolved culturally and technologically, what tools and products form a working kit, and why

professionalism and ethics shape every client experience. Learners gain a clear

understanding of what it means to enhance natural beauty with intention, skill, and respect.

1.1 The Evolution of Makeup Artistry

Explains how makeup practices developed across cultures and eras, showing how historical

traditions influence today’s techniques and industry standards.

1.2 The Role and Responsibility of a Makeup Artist

Outlines the professional expectations of an MUA, including enhancing features, maintaining

hygiene, communicating clearly, protecting client privacy, and upholding ethical conduct.

1.3 Understanding Your Makeup Kit: Tools and Products

Provides an overview of essential tools and product categories, explaining how proper

selection, organisation, and hygiene support effective, safe, and efficient application.

1.4 Professional Conduct and Ethics

Highlights the ethical principles and professional behaviours—such as confidentiality,

honesty, respect, punctuality, and integrity—that shape client trust and define industry

professionalism.

1.5 The Purpose of Makeup: Enhancing Natural Beauty

Explains how makeup enhances rather than hides features by boosting confidence,

harmonising facial structure, and supporting cultural or personal expression through

intentional application.

Unit 2: A Brief History of Cosmetics

Read pages 16-20  of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

Cosmetics have been part of human culture for thousands of years, evolving alongside social,

religious, and technological changes. Understanding this timeline helps makeup artists create

accurate period looks and appreciate the cultural meaning behind various beauty practices.

This unit explores how cosmetic use developed from ancient rituals to the modern beauty

industry we know today.

2.1 Origins of Cosmetic Use in Ancient Civilisations

Explores how early cultures used natural pigments and minerals for protection, symbolism,

beauty rituals, and identity.

2.2 Cosmetic Practices in Classical and Medieval Periods

Describes how makeup reflected shifting attitudes around social class, religion, and gender in

Greek, Roman, and Medieval societies.

2.3 Transformation of Cosmetics in the 18th and 19th Centuries

Highlights the extravagant beauty styles of the 18th century and the rise of mass-produced

cosmetics and early makeup innovations in the 19th century.

2.4 The Emergence of Modern Makeup (1900–1960)

Shows how cinema, photography, and Hollywood shaped refined application techniques and

iconic beauty trends from the 1920s to the 1960s.

2.5 Cosmetics in the Late 20th and Early 21st Century

Explains how technological advances, HD media, social media, and inclusivity movements

revolutionised product performance and shade diversity.

2.6 Summary of Key Historical Milestones

Summarises major cosmetic developments across eras, linking each period to its signature

beauty practices and innovations.

2.7 Why History Matters in Modern Makeup Artistry

Explains how historical awareness strengthens creativity, cultural sensitivity, technical

accuracy, and professional identity in makeup application.

Introduction

This module explores the science of the skin and the hygiene practices essential for safe,

professional makeup application. Learners discover how skin biology, environmental

influences, and lifestyle factors affect makeup performance, and how proper sanitation and

preparation techniques create a smooth, long-lasting finish. By combining skin analysis with

strict hygiene standards, makeup artists develop the technical confidence to choose suitable

products, prevent contamination, and protect client wellbeing.

Unit 1: The Professional Skin Consultation

Read pages 36-41 of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

A professional skin consultation forms the foundation of every makeup application by helping

the artist understand the client’s skin type, conditions, concerns, and goals. Through this

process, the MUA determines which products, techniques, and hygiene precautions are

appropriate. This unit guides learners through analysing the skin accurately and using this

information to create a tailored makeup plan.

1.1 Purpose of a Skin Consultation

Clarifies how analysing skin type, conditions, allergies, and client goals ensures product

suitability, comfort, and the longevity of the final makeup look.

1.2 Skin Types and Their Needs

Explains the key characteristics of oily, dry, combination, normal, and sensitive skin, and how

each type requires targeted preparation and product choices.

1.3 The Professional Skin Prep Routine (The MUA's 4 Steps)

Outlines the four essential preparation steps—cleanse/exfoliate, tone, moisturise/protect, and

prime—that create an even, long-lasting base for makeup.

1.4 Assessing Contraindications and Sensitivities

Identifies allergies, infections, and recent skin treatments that may affect product choice or

require service refusal for client safety.

Introduction

This module explores how professional tools and techniques shape the outcomes of makeup

artistry, showing how brushes, sponges, and specialised applicators influence coverage,

precision, and finish. It also explains how fundamental techniques such as buffing, blending,

stippling, sculpting, and setting allow artists to control texture, enhance structure, and create

polished, cohesive results. By understanding the relationship between tools, product

formulas, and skin, learners develop the skill to adapt their approach across different

environments and client needs.

Unit 1: Anatomy of the Face for Corrective Makeup

Read pages 55-58 of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

This unit introduces the facial structures that guide corrective makeup, explaining how the

bones and muscles influence the placement of contour, highlight, and colour. Through

understanding anatomy, learners gain the ability to work with light and shadow intentionally to

enhance balance, symmetry, and natural definition. Corrective techniques become more

precise when artists know how features are shaped beneath the skin.

1.1 Understanding the Bony Framework of the Face

Explains how facial bones determine natural planes of light and shadow, guiding placement of

contour and highlight for balanced, realistic corrective makeup.

1.2 Understanding Facial Musculature and Its Influence on Makeup

Describes how voluntary and involuntary muscle movements affect product behaviour, crease

formation, and the durability of makeup across expressive areas of the face.

1.3 Applying Anatomy Knowledge to Corrective Makeup

Shows how understanding bone and muscle structure allows MUAs to place highlight,

contour, and blush intentionally for lifting, sculpting, and harmonising facial proportions.
