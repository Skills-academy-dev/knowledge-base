Introduction

This module focuses on two essential planning functions in operations management: forecasting and

inventory management. Forecasting helps predict future demand, allowing businesses to plan resources

and production effectively. Inventory management ensures that materials and products are available when

needed, balancing cost-efficiency with service level requirements.

Together, these functions support efficient operations, reduce waste, and improve decision-making.

Students will explore key concepts, methods, and tools used to anticipate demand and manage stock

effectively across various operational settings.

Unit 1: Mastering the Fundamentals of Forecasting

(pp. 9–33 of textbook)

Key Topics:

  Forecasting purpose and applications (production, finance, HR).

  Types of forecasting:

  Qualitative (Delphi, expert opinion)

  Quantitative (Time series, regression)

  Time series components: trend, seasonality, cyclicality, randomness.

  Forecast accuracy measures:

  MAD (Mean Absolute Deviation)

  MSE (Mean Squared Error)

  MAPE (Mean Absolute Percentage Error)

  Forecasting process: purpose → method → data → forecast → validate → update.

  Challenges: poor data, changes in trends, bias.

This unit introduces the role of forecasting in operations management. It explains how businesses predict

future demand using different methods, such as expert judgment and data-based models. You'll learn

about the components of time series data (trend, seasonality, etc.) and how to evaluate forecast accuracy

using MAD, MSE, and MAPE. The unit also outlines the forecasting process and common challenges.

Unit 2: Strategic Inventory Management Techniques

(pp. 34–46 of textbook)

Key Topics:



Inventory types: raw materials, WIP, finished goods, MRO.

  Functions of inventory: buffer, decoupling, demand satisfaction, cost reduction.



Inventory costs: ordering, holding, stockout, setup.

  EOQ Model: EOQ = √(2DS/H) Where D = demand, S = order cost, H = holding cost

  Reorder Point (ROP) and Safety Stock ROP = (Average Demand × Lead Time) + Safety Stock

  ABC Classification

  A-items: high-value, tight control

  B-items: moderate

  C-items: low-value, simple control

  Just-in-Time (JIT): minimizes waste and inventory, increases dependency on suppliers.

This unit focuses on how organizations manage inventory effectively. It covers different types and roles of

inventory, the costs involved, and key models such as EOQ and Reorder Point. You'll also explore

strategies like safety stock, ABC classification for prioritizing inventory, and Just-in-Time (JIT) systems

that minimize waste by aligning production with actual demand.

Summary

(pp. 47 of textbook)

This module introduced key concepts in forecasting and inventory management, two essential

components of operations planning. Forecasting enables businesses to anticipate future demand using

historical data and analytical methods. We examined both qualitative and quantitative forecasting

techniques, including time series models and regression analysis, and discussed how to measure

forecast accuracy with tools such as MAD, MSE, and MAPE.

Students also learned about the components of time series data, trend, seasonality, cyclicality, and

random variation, as well as the structured forecasting process from identifying the purpose to

continuously refining the model.

Introduction

This module introduces key tools and concepts used to align operations with demand. You will explore

Material Requirements Planning (MRP) to manage dependent demand, Capacity Planning to evaluate

available resources, and Aggregate Planning to bridge medium-term decisions. The module concludes

with Short-Term Scheduling, which focuses on the day-to-day coordination of tasks, jobs, and workflows.

By mastering these techniques, students will be equipped to make informed decisions about production

timing, resource allocation, and workload management, balancing efficiency, flexibility, and customer

service in dynamic operational environments.

(pp. 51–60 of textbook)

  Master Production Schedule (MPS)

  Bill of Materials (BOM)



Inventory Records

  MRP Explosion Process

  Net Requirements and Planned Order Releases

This unit introduces MRP as a system that calculates what materials are needed, in what quantities, and

when. It breaks down product needs using the Master Production Schedule (MPS), the Bill of Materials

(BOM), and inventory records. You’ll learn how to “explode” BOMs, calculate net requirements, and

schedule planned orders.

This unit explores how businesses plan for the capacity they need to meet customer demand over time. It

outlines long-term facility decisions, medium-term workforce planning, and short-term adjustments. You’ll

also learn different strategies for aggregate planning and how they impact labor, inventory, and costs.

  EDD (Earliest Due Date)

This unit focuses on how operations managers schedule tasks and jobs in the short term. It covers

different production environments and introduces scheduling tools like Gantt charts and priority rules.

You’ll examine how scheduling affects resource use, lead time, and customer service.

maximizing resource efficiency.

In today’s competitive and fast-moving global market, organizations must do more than produce efficiently;

they must also eliminate waste and ensure high-quality outcomes across every process. Lean

management and quality management are two complementary approaches that help organizations achieve

these goals by focusing on continuous improvement, customer value, and operational excellence.

Introduction

Operations management is no longer just about efficiency, it’s about adapting, evolving, and leading

improvement in a dynamic world. Organizations must continuously refine their systems while responding

to emerging global trends, digital transformation, and sustainability demands.

In this module, students will explore how companies sustain continual improvement through lean thinking,

quality initiatives, and project management practices. At the same time, they’ll examine the modern

challenges facing operations managers such as technological disruption, supply chain volatility,

environmental pressures, and workforce changes.

(pp. 134 of textbook)
