Introduction

Operations management is at the heart of every organization. Whether a business provides services or

manufactures products, it must plan, organize, and control its operations to satisfy customers efficiently.

This module introduces you to operations management's nature, scope, and significance and lays the

foundation for deeper exploration of operations topics in later modules.

You will learn about the evolution of operations management from its early origins to modern approaches

that include technology, sustainability, and global integration. You will also explore the transformation

process model, operations decision-making levels, and operations managers' key responsibilities.

Unit 1: Nature and Scope of Operations Management

(Textbook Reference: pp. 9-13)

Operations management forms the backbone of every organization's ability to produce goods and deliver

services. This unit introduces the definition, functions, and evolution of operations management and

explores its relationship with other key business functions. You will also examine emerging global trends

that are shaping the modern operations landscape.

Unit 2: The Transformation Process Model

(Textbook Reference: pp. 13–18)

At the core of operations management is the transformation process turning inputs into outputs. This unit

explains how value is added through operations, outlines the types of transformations businesses use,

and highlights the role of feedback and control in maintaining quality and efficiency.

Unit 3: Functions and Decision Areas

(Textbook Reference: pp. 18–24)

Operations management involves a wide range of decision-making responsibilities. These decisions can

be broadly classified as strategic, tactical, or operational. Understanding these levels of decisions and the

key focus areas helps operations managers align daily activities with long-term business goals.

Unit 4: Operations and Business Strategy

(Textbook Reference: pp. 24-28)

Operations management plays a vital role in supporting and achieving an organisation’s business strategy.

It contributes  directly to competitive advantage by aligning operational goals with the overall direction of

the business.

Unit 5: Operations Manager Roles and Responsibilities

(Textbook Reference: pp. 28-36)

Operations managers play a central role in overseeing the day-to-day functioning of the operations

department. Their responsibilities extend from planning and organising to monitoring, improving, and

aligning operations with the business’s overall goals. This unit introduces you to the key activities, skills,

and leadership qualities expected of operations managers in both manufacturing and service

environments.

Module 1 Summary

(Textbook Reference: pp. 36)

This module introduced the basics of operations management, focusing on its role in planning, controlling,

and improving the production of goods and services. You explored the transformation process model,

decision-making levels, the integration of operations with business strategy, and the responsibilities of

operations managers. The module also highlighted global trends shaping modern operations, such as

digitalization and sustainability.

Introduction

Supply chain management is the coordination of all activities involved in sourcing, producing, and

delivering goods and services to customers. This module explores the structure of supply chains, key

drivers of performance, and the challenges of managing logistics, global sourcing, and sustainability. You

will also examine risk management strategies and the importance of strong supplier relationships in

maintaining an effective supply chain.

Unit 1: Introduction to Supply Chains

(Textbook Reference: pp. 41-46)

Supply chains form the backbone of operational efficiency in any business. They represent the entire

network of entities, activities, and resources involved in moving a product or service from supplier to

customer. This unit explains what a supply chain is, how it differs from a value chain, and introduces the

key entities and structures that make up supply networks.

Unit 2: Supply Chain Drivers and Metrics

(Textbook Reference: pp. 46-54)

Efficient supply chains rely on several key drivers that influence their performance. This unit explores the

main drivers of supply chain performance, explains how to measure performance through relevant metrics,

introduces the SCOR model, and discusses how organizations can balance efficiency with responsiveness

to meet different customer needs.

Unit 3: Logistics and Distribution Management

(Textbook Reference: pp. 54-60)

Logistics and distribution are key components of supply chain management, ensuring that goods move

smoothly and efficiently from point of origin to point of consumption. This unit explores the difference

between inbound and outbound logistics, transportation modes, warehousing layouts, packaging, and the

design of distribution networks.

Unit 4: Global and Green Supply Chains

(Textbook Reference: pp. 60-64)

As businesses expand internationally and environmental concerns grow, supply chains must adapt to new

challenges. This unit explores global sourcing risks, cultural and regulatory barriers, and strategies for

creating greener, more sustainable supply chains. It also introduces concepts like circular supply chains

and environmental certifications.

Unit 5: Supply Chain Risks and Relationships

(Textbook Reference: pp. 64-72)

Supply chains face various risks that can disrupt operations and impact performance. This unit explores

different types of risks, strategies to reduce them, and the importance of building strong supplier relation-

ships. It also examines outsourcing decisions and collaborative practices like CPFR to improve coordina-

tion and resilience.

Module 2 Summary

(Textbook Reference: pp. 72)

This module introduces the structure and key components of supply chains and explains how goods and

information flow from suppliers to end customers. It distinguishes between supply chains and value

chains and outlines the roles of suppliers, manufacturers, distributors, and retailers. Learners explore core

supply chain drivers inventory, facilities, transportation, and information and how they influence perfor-

mance. The SCOR model is introduced as a framework to analyse and optimise supply chain processes.

Logistics and distribution are explained in detail, including inbound/outbound logistics, warehousing, and

transportation modes. Finally, the module addresses global supply chain challenges, sustainable practic-

es, and risk management strategies.

Introduction

Design is a critical starting point for operations, influencing cost, quality, and customer satisfaction. This

module focuses on how organizations design goods and services that meet customer needs, remain com-

petitive, and are operationally feasible. It covers both product and service design, highlighting tools like

QFD, design-for-operations principles, and the role of innovation. You will also learn how marketing and

operations teams work together during the design process to align product features with customer

expectations.

Unit 1: Understanding Customer Needs

(Textbook Reference: pp. 76-85)

Design begins with a deep understanding of the customer. This unit introduces how organizations gather,

interpret, and priorities customer needs to inform product and service design. It also explores tools such

as Quality Function Deployment (QFD) and explains how marketing and operations departments

collaborate to deliver customer satisfaction.

Unit 2: Product and Service Innovation

(Textbook Reference: pp. 85-94)

Innovation drives competitiveness and customer satisfaction. This unit explores how new ideas are

generated, evaluated, and developed into successful products and services. It also looks at different types

of innovation, the role of R&D and cross-functional teams, and how organizations manage products

throughout their life cycles while ensuring ethical and legal compliance.

Unit 3: The Design and Development Process

(Textbook Reference: pp. 94-100)

Turning an idea into a finished product or service involves a series of structured steps. This unit outlines

the phases of the design and development process, explains the role of prototyping and testing, and

describes how design review gates help keep the project on track. You’ll also explore value engineering,

cost estimation, and time-to-market considerations.

Unit 4: Design for Operations

(Textbook Reference: pp. 100-106)

Once a product or service idea has been developed, it must be designed in a way that ensures efficient

and effective operations. This unit explores various design approaches that support manufacturing and

service delivery, including principles such as Design for Manufacturability (DFM), Design for Assembly

(DFA), Design for Six Sigma (DFSS), and modular design.

Unit 5: Designing Services

(Textbook Reference: pp. 106-112)

Service design requires a different approach from product design because services are intangible,

variable, and involve direct interaction with customers. This unit explores the key characteristics of service

design, the use of tools like service blueprinting, and strategies to manage service delivery environments

and customer experiences.

Module 3 Summary

(Textbook Reference: pp. 112)

This module focused on how organizations design goods and services that meet customer needs while

ensuring operational efficiency. You explored methods for identifying and prioritizing customer

requirements, using tools like Quality Function Deployment (QFD) and market segmentation. The module

covered innovation strategies, product and service development stages, and the role of R&D and

cross-functional teams. It also examined design-for-operations approaches such as DFM, DFA, DFSS,

and modular design, along with the unique challenges of service design, including blueprinting, customer

touchpoints, layout, and queue management. These principles help organizations deliver value while

managing cost, quality, and customer experience.

Introduction

Efficient processes are the foundation of productive operations. This module introduces you to the design

and management of processes in both manufacturing and service environments. You’ll  explore how

organizations choose process types based on volume and variety, how they measure and manage

capacity and performance, and how they improve processes using structured tools and strategies.

Whether it’s an assembly line or a hospital procedure, good process design is essential for delivering

consistent, high-quality outcomes.

Unit 1: Fundamentals of Process Design

(Textbook Reference: pp. 116-119)

Process design involves planning the sequence of activities that transform inputs into outputs. This unit

introduces the concept of a process, outlines its key characteristics, and explains the objectives of

process design. You  will also explore how volume and variety affect process choices and examine

real-world examples from different industries.

Unit 2: Types of Process Structures

(Textbook Reference: pp. 119-121)

Different operations require different process structures depending on product volume, variety, and

complexity. This unit explores the major types of process structures used in manufacturing and services,

how to select the appropriate structure, and the impact of process design on cost and efficiency. It also

introduces tools like process flow diagrams to help visualize and manage operations.

Unit 3: Strategic Process Choices

(Textbook Reference: pp. 121-130)

Strategic process choices ensure that operations align with the broader business strategy. This unit

explores how different process types support different strategic goals, introduces the process–product

matrix as a decision tool, explains how processes evolve over time, and compares mass customization

with traditional production. It also looks at the decision to produce in-house or outsource.

Unit 4: Managing Capacity and Performance

(Textbook Reference: pp. 130-138)

Managing capacity is essential to ensuring that an organization can meet customer demand without

excessive cost or delay. This unit introduces capacity-related concepts, explains different capacity planning

strategies, and explores tools and techniques to measure and improve performance. Key focus areas

include bottlenecks, cycle time, and throughput.

Unit 5: Process Analysis and Improvement

(Textbook Reference: pp. 138-145)

Processes must be regularly reviewed and improved to ensure they remain efficient, cost-effective, and

aligned with customer expectations. This unit introduces key tools used to analyse and improve processes,

explains how to identify waste and redundancy, and outlines structured improvement methods such as

Kaizen and PDCA.

Module 4 Summary

(Textbook Reference: pp. 145)

This module focused on how organizations design and manage their processes to ensure efficient

operations and support strategic goals. You explored the characteristics of processes using the 4Vs model,

examined different process structures such as job shop, batch, and continuous, and learned how to align

process types with business strategy using the process–product matrix. The module also introduced

concepts of capacity management, including utilization, bottlenecks, and cycle time, and explained tools for

process analysis such as flowcharts, VSM, and SIPOC. Finally, you reviewed methods for continuous

improvement, including Kaizen and PDCA, which help drive operational excellence.
