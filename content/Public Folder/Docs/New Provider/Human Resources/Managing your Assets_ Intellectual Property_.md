Managing your

Assets: Intellectual

Property

https://www.seda.org.za/ServicesOfferings/Factsheets/Pages/Intellectualproperty.aspx

Most successful businesses come up with new ideas, inventions, designs or brands that make them
stand out in the market place. This intellectual property is generally intangible, so you can't lock it
away in a storeroom to keep it safe. But you do have the right to prevent others from 'stealing' your
ideas and using them for their own commercial gain.

The state takes responsibility for registering your ideas - through the Companies and Intellectual
Property Registration Office (CIPRO). The process of registration can be complex, however, so you
may need to employ expert legal advice.

Also, if you need to go to court to defend your intellectual property rights, you have to do that at your
own expense.
There are a number of ways of protecting your intellectual property:

●

●

If you have an invention, you can protect it with a patent. This gives the inventor the exclusive
right - usually for 20 years - to produce and sell the product. During this period, the invention
cannot be commercially made, used, distributed or sold without the owner's consent.
If you are an author or designer, you will generally have automatic protection - or copyright -
of your work, unless you have been specifically commissioned to produce it for an employer
or customer. It is therefore not necessary to apply for copyright (unless the product is a film),
but you need to put the words "Copyright", "Copyright Reserved" or "Copyright [Your Name]
2004" on the work. You can also use the copyright sign: "© [Your Name] 2004".

●

●  As a designer, you can go further than copyright, and register a design. The registration gives
you an exclusive right for 10 years (for aesthetic designs) or 15 years (for functional designs).
The registration has to be renewed annually.
If you've come up with a catchy brand name (like Spar), logo (like the Nike 'tick') or slogan
(like 'Yebo, Gogo'), you can register it as a trade mark. While you can just go to court defend
un-registered names or phrases that your business uses, it is easier to protect them if you
have officially registered them. The registration needs to be renewed every ten years, and
can protect you forever.
