ASSIGNMENT
1. Which phrase best describes service excellence in hospitality?
A. Following company rules only
B. Slightly exceeding guest expectations
C. Offering the lowest prices D. Serving more food portions
2. What is the first foundation of service excellence?
A. Selling more products
B. Avoiding complaints
C. Meeting guest needs consistently D. Using technology
3. According to the textbook, what turns an ordinary stay into a memorable
experience?
A. Strict check-in policies
B. Adding a personal, caring touch
C. Fast service only D. Expensive décor
4. Which of the following is an example of a basic guest expectation?
A. Clean rooms and safety
B. Free gifts and upgrades
C. Luxury entertainment D. Staff flexibility
5. The term guestology means:
A. Understanding and studying guest behaviour and expectations
C. Managing employee schedules
D. Creating financial reports
B. Recording daily bookings
6

6. Why is service excellence vital to the South African hospitality industry?
A. It focuses mainly on advertising
B. It reduces tourism competition
C. It limits international visitors
D. It helps create jobs and build national reputation
7. What is the main benefit of exceeding guest expectations?
A. Lower labour costs
B. Fewer employees
C. Guest loyalty and repeat visits D. Faster check-out times
8. In the SERVQUAL model, tangibles refer to:
A. Physical appearance of facilities and staff
B. Quick service speed
C. Personal relationships
D. Emotional understanding
9. Reliability in hospitality service means:
A. Doing what was promised accurately and on time
B. Smiling all the time
C. Offering random surprises D. Using new technology
10. Which SERVQUAL dimension focuses on care and attention to each guest?
A. Assurance
B. Tangibles
C. Efficiency D. Empathy
11. Why is responsiveness important in service delivery?
A. It allows staff to take longer breaks
B. It reduces service quality
C. Guests value fast and willing assistance D. It makes guests less demanding
7

12. Assurance in service means:
A. Staff knowledge and courtesy that builds confidence
B. Promising unrealistic offers
C. Quick repairs of facilities
D. Giving rewards to loyal guests
13. What is a moment of truth?
A. A key guest interaction that shapes their impression
B. When staff finish their shift
C. When a guest pays the bill D. A marketing event
14. Which factor directly connects good service to business success?
A. Higher building costs
B. Customer satisfaction and loyalty
C. Fewer products D. More uniforms
15. Which psychological skill helps staff handle guest emotions better?
A. Assertiveness
B. Aggression
C. Indifference D. Empathy
16. How do first impressions affect guests?
A. They shape their entire experience
B. They only matter to managers
C. They are easy to ignore D. They have no effect
17. Which action best strengthens a strong service culture?
A. Managers hide information
B. Staff avoid feedback
C. Leaders model the behaviours they expect D. Teams work in silos
8

18. What does the term paradigm shift mean in the service context?
A. A change in building design
B. A major change in thinking and approach
C. A minor system update D. A new staff schedule
19. Moving from “rules to trust” means:
A. Removing all structure
B. Ignoring management
C. Letting guests set rules
D. Empowering employees to make guest-centred decisions
20. The shift from “transactions to relationships” focuses on:
A. Long-term guest loyalty
B. Faster payments
C. Avoiding small talk
D. Only upselling products
21. What is organisational culture?
A. The physical layout of a hotel
B. Company uniforms
C. Guest complaints
D. Shared values and behaviours guiding how staff work
22. A strong service culture is created by:
A. High staff turnover
B. Consistent values and leadership example
C. Strict rule enforcement D. Avoiding feedback
23. Which South African principle encourages respect and teamwork?
A. Competition
B. Independence
C. Materialism D. Ubuntu
9

24. What makes a service leader effective?
A. Leading by example and showing emotional intelligence
B. Using fear and control
C. Focusing only on profits D. Avoiding communication
25. Emotional intelligence helps leaders:
A. Understand and manage emotions
B. Avoid emotional issues
C. Control employees strictly D. Focus only on results
26. What are ethical values in hospitality?
A. Doing what benefits oneself
B. Honesty, fairness, and respect in all actions
C. Competing aggressively D. Avoiding responsibility
27. Ethical leadership encourages:
A. Favouritism and secrecy
B. Ignoring staff opinions
C. Cutting corners
D. Trust, integrity, and fairness in decision-making
28. Hiring for attitude means selecting candidates who:
A. Share service-minded values
B. Have only formal education
C. Demand high pay D. Work alone
29. What motivates employees most effectively?
A. Strict supervision
B. Isolation
C. Recognition and appreciation D. Punishment
10

30. Coaching in service teams helps:
A. Guide, support, and improve performance
B. Replace training
C. Avoid responsibility D. Criticise employees
31. Collaboration and teamwork improve:
A. Costs only
B. Staff turnover
C. Independence
D. Consistency and guest satisfaction
32. Why is communication important in service teams?
A. It ensures understanding and guest satisfaction
B. It reduces staff confidence
C. It slows service down D. It replaces empathy
33. Active listening involves:
A. Paying attention and responding thoughtfully
B. Talking over guests
C. Ignoring body language
D. Ending the conversation early
34. Cross-cultural communication in hospitality requires:
A. Using one language only
B. Ignoring foreign guests
C. Avoiding cultural topics
D. Respect and adaptability to guest differences
35. What is the purpose of service design?
A. Choosing staff uniforms
B. Decorating the property
C. Writing advertisements
D. Planning how services are created and delivered
11

36. What does guest journey mapping help identify?
A. Each step of the guest’s experience
B. Staff training needs
C. Budget shortages D. Food costs
37. The main goal of service blueprinting is to:
A. Decorate guest rooms
B. Replace customer feedback
C. Set prices
D. Show the link between front-stage and back-stage processes
38. What do front-stage activities include?
A. Maintenance work only
B. Accounting tasks
C. Warehouse management
D. Direct guest interactions like check-in and service
39. Back-stage activities refer to:
A. Behind-the-scenes support functions
B. Guest-facing conversations
C. Marketing campaigns D. Room inspections
40. What is the purpose of Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)?
A. Ensuring consistency and quality in service delivery
B. Replacing management
C. Adding new staff titles D. Advertising promotions
41. Personalisation means:
A. Treating all guests exactly the same
B. Using generic responses
C. Avoiding special requests
D. Tailoring experiences to meet each guest’s unique needs
12

42. What is the main difference between personalisation and customisation?
A. Both are identical
B. Customisation is always free
C. Personalisation ignores technology
D. Personalisation focuses on guest identity; customisation focuses on product options
43. Ethical use of guest data means:
A. Selling guest emails
B. Sharing data online
C. Ignoring privacy laws
D. Protecting personal information under POPIA
44. What is the first step in service recovery?
A. Listening carefully to the guest
B. Offering a refund immediately
C. Defending the company D. Ignoring the problem
45. Why should complaints be recorded?
A. To punish staff
B. To advertise online
C. To delete guest data
D. To identify patterns and prevent repeat issues
46. Risk management in hospitality means:
A. Anticipating and preparing for possible problems
B. Avoiding all changes
C. Blaming staff
D. Ignoring safety
47. Innovation in service delivery encourages:
A. Avoiding new ideas
B. Repeating old mistakes
C. Limiting teamwork
D. Continuous improvement and creativity
13

48. How does technology support service excellence?
A. By improving efficiency and convenience for guests
B. By replacing all employees
C. By removing personal contact D. By slowing service
49. Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools are most useful for:
A. Making meals
B. Cleaning rooms
C. Designing décor
D. Automating bookings and responses
50. The main purpose of combining people, process, and technology is to:
A. Reduce costs only
B. Limit creativity
C. Replace teamwork
D. Deliver consistent and memorable guest experiences
