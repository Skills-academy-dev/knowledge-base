INTRODUCTION

All living organisms are made up of cells. These microscopic structures are the smallest units of life

and are responsible for all life processes. Cells form tissues, and tissues form organs that carry out

body functions. This module explores the internal structure of cells, how they function, communicate,

divide, and how they combine to form tissues. It also highlights how tissues heal, regenerate, and

change over time.

Refer to page 24 on the textbook

This unit explores the cell as the basic unit of life, focusing on its internal structures and the special-

ised roles they perform. You will learn how organelles such as the nucleus, mitochondria, and ribo-

somes contribute to essential processes like energy production, protein synthesis, and communica-

tion

INTRODUCTION

The ability to move, sit, stand, or carry objects relies on the skeletal and muscular systems. The

skeleton forms the framework of the body, while muscles contract to produce movement. Togeth-

er, they provide stability, support, and mobility. Bones are more than hard structures — they store

minerals, produce blood cells, and change throughout life. Muscles, on the other hand, generate

the force required to move bones at joints

INTRODUCTION

The human body constantly communicates internally to maintain balance and respond to changes.

Two major systems coordinate this communication: the nervous system, which sends fast electrical

signals, and the endocrine system, which sends slower but longer-lasting chemical signals. This

module explores how these systems work, how they interact, and how they maintain control and bal-

ance across the body’s organs and tissues.

Refer to page 62 on the textbook

functions. You will learn about the central and peripheral nervous systems, their subdivisions, and

the roles of specialised cells such as neurons and glial cells. Understanding this organisation is es-

sential for recognising how the body processes information and responds to internal and external

changes.

Summary

The nervous and endocrine systems are essential communication networks within the body. The

nervous system uses fast electrical impulses to respond quickly to stimuli, while the endocrine sys-

tem uses hormones for long-term control. Together, they help the body respond to the environment,

regulate growth, maintain internal balance, and support survival. Understanding these systems

helps us better manage health, disease, and body function.
