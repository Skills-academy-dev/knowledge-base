## INTRODUCTION

In this module, you will learn what risk means, how it affects personal and organisational objectives, and why it is important to manage it carefully. You will explore the history and development of risk management, from basic safety measures in early societies to modern, structured approaches such as Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) and international standards like ISO 31000. This module also introduces the different types of risks that organisations face today — including strategic, operational, financial, compliance, and environmental risks.

Understanding these categories helps businesses prepare for unexpected challenges and seize new opportunities. You will also examine the importance of risk management in South Africa, where strict legal standards and social expectations encourage organisations to protect their employees, customers, and communities. By the end of this module, you will see risk management as a valuable tool for making better decisions, protecting value, and supporting long-term success.

## INTRODUCTION

Before risks can be managed, they must first be identified and understood. This module focuses on discovering and describing the risks that could affect an organisation or project. You will learn how to identify internal risks, such as human errors and equipment failures, and external risks, such as natural disasters, economic changes, or new regulations. The module introduces useful tools and techniques for risk identification, including brainstorming, checklists, interviews, surveys, and analysing historical data.

You will also explore environmental scanning tools such as PESTLE (Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, and Environmental analysis) and SWOT (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats), which help organisations understand the wider context in which they operate. In addition, you will learn how to document identified risks in a risk register and see the importance of involving different stakeholders, like employees, suppliers, and customers, to make the risk assessment more accurate and complete. This module builds a strong foundation for understanding and capturing risks clearly and systematically.

## INTRODUCTION

After identifying risks, the next critical step is to evaluate and prioritise them. This module explains how to assess the likelihood and potential impact of each risk to determine which ones need the most attention. You will explore two main assessment methods: qualitative assessments, which use descriptive categories (such as high, medium, or low), and quantitative assessments, which rely on numerical data and calculations. The module also covers key concepts such as risk appetite and risk tolerance, which help organisations decide how much risk they are willing to accept and how much variation they can tolerate in their objectives.

You will learn how to use visual tools like risk matrices and heat maps to rank risks and communicate their urgency to decision-makers. Effective prioritisation ensures that time, money, and resources are focused on the most critical risks first. Finally, you will understand how to communicate risk assessments clearly to various stakeholders through reports, charts, and meetings, supporting informed decision-making at every level.

# Module 4: Risk Treatment Strategies

## LEARNING OBJECTIVES

## INTRODUCTION

Once risks have been evaluated and prioritised, organisations must decide how to respond to them. This module introduces the various strategies for treating risks: avoiding them entirely, reducing their likelihood or impact, transferring them to a third party (such as through insurance), or accepting them if they are within acceptable limits. You will learn how to develop detailed action plans that include clear steps, assigned responsibilities, deadlines, and resource requirements.

The module also explains different types of control measures, including preventive controls that stop incidents from happening and detective controls that help identify problems quickly when they do occur. You will explore the role of insurance and financial tools in transferring risk and understand the importance of business continuity and crisis management planning to ensure that essential operations can continue during unexpected events. By mastering these approaches, you will be able to support organisations in managing risks effectively and maintaining stability even during disruptions.
