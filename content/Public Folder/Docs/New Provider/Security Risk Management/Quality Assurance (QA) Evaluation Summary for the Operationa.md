Quality Assurance (QA) Evaluation Summary for the Operational Risk
Management Textbook

Following the detailed QA process using the format and criteria from the "QA REPORT_
Excellence In Customer Service.pdf", here is a full evaluation and scoring for the Operational
Risk Management Textbook.

📚 1. Content Accuracy & Relevance

●  Strengths:

○  Content is highly aligned with international standards (Basel II/III, COSO) and

current operational risk management practices.

○  Extensive South African examples (e.g., Nedbank, Transnet, Telkom) make it

locally relevant.

○  Case studies and scenarios are practical and varied across sectors.

●  Areas for improvement:

○  A few areas could use fresher post-2023 examples, especially in technology risks

and AI (newer incidents).

●  Score: 9 / 10

📝 2. Structure, Organisation & Navigation

●  Strengths:

○  Logical modular design, building from foundational concepts to sector-specific

applications.

○  Good page references in ToC, clear flow from introductions → units →

summaries → exercises.

●  Areas for improvement:

○  Minor inconsistency in module exercise layouts (some scenarios appear before

matchings, elsewhere reversed).

●  Score: 9 / 10

✏ 3. Language & Style

●  Strengths:

○  Consistently uses British English, simple formal style appropriate for 15–18 year

distance learners.

○  Friendly tone with practical tips, “fun facts”, “leadership tips”, and examples

tailored to distance learning.

●  Score: 10 / 10

🎨 4. Visual Quality (Images, Diagrams, Tables)

●  Strengths:

○  Strong use of diagrams (COSO cube, Basel frameworks, heatmaps,

dashboards).

○  Relevant tables and process charts reinforce learning (taxonomy, vendor

management, Lean/Kaizen examples).

●  Areas for improvement:

○  Some images appear pixelated (esp. risk taxonomies on page 19, COSO cube

on page 9).

○  Would benefit from higher-resolution redraws for taxonomy, PDCA cycle on page

52, and risk heatmaps on page 95.

●  Score: 8 / 10

💻 5. Examples, Exercises & Case Studies

●  Strengths:

○  Each module contains varied assessment types (True/False, Fill in Gaps,

Scenario, Match Columns).

○  Good use of localised examples: Eskom, Barings, healthcare in SA, SMEs in

Kenya.

●  Score: 10 / 10

🔍 6. Overall Usability for Distance Learning

●  Strengths:

○  Summaries are concise and effectively wrap up modules.

○  Plenty of scenario-based questions simulate distance learning engagement.

○  Visuals and tables break up long text, enhancing readability.

●  Score: 9 / 10
