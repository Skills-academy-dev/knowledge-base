# Module 2: From Cells to Tissues – The Microscopic Architecture of Life

## LEARNING OBJECTIVES

Identify and describe the structure and function of cell organelles.

Explain transport mechanisms, osmosis, and cellular communication.

Outline the stages of the cell cycle, mitosis, meiosis, and protein synthesis.

Read page 23 on the textbook for more information

## INTRODUCTION

All living organisms are made up of cells. These microscopic structures are the smallest units of life and are responsible for all life processes. Cells form tissues, and tissues form organs that carry out body functions. This module explores the internal structure of cells, how they function, communicate, divide, and how they combine to form tissues. It also highlights how tissues heal, regenerate, and change over time.

## INTRODUCTION

The ability to move, sit, stand, or carry objects relies on the skeletal and muscular systems. The skeleton forms the framework of the body, while muscles contract to produce movement. Together, they provide stability, support, and mobility. Bones are more than hard structures — they store minerals, produce blood cells, and change throughout life. Muscles, on the other hand, generate the force required to move bones at joints

## INTRODUCTION

The human body constantly communicates internally to maintain balance and respond to changes. Two major systems coordinate this communication: the nervous system, which sends fast electrical signals, and the endocrine system, which sends slower but longer-lasting chemical signals. This module explores how these systems work, how they interact, and how they maintain control and balance across the body’s organs and tissues.

This unit explains how the nervous system is structured and divided to control and coordinate body functions. You will learn about the central and peripheral nervous systems, their subdivisions, and the roles of specialised cells such as neurons and glial cells. ## Summary

The nervous and endocrine systems are essential communication networks within the body. The nervous system uses fast electrical impulses to respond quickly to stimuli, while the endocrine system uses hormones for long-term control. Together, they help the body respond to the environment, regulate growth, maintain internal balance, and support survival. Understanding these systems helps us better manage health, disease, and body function.
