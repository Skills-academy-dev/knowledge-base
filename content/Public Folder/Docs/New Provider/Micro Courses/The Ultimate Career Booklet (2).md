### Multiple Choice Questions

Choose the most correct answer and tick the appropriate box on the answer sheet provided.

The most reliable starting point for intentional career choices is to:
 A. Scan job boards at random
 B. Build self-awareness about strengths, values, and goals
 C. Ask friends to pick a path for you
 D. Follow the highest salary only

A quick way to surface genuine strengths is to:
 A. Wait for annual reviews only
 B. Keep a two-week “strengths journal” noting energising moments and wins
 C. Focus on what others dislike doing
 D. Pick three traits from a generic list

When discussing weaknesses constructively, you should:
 A. Hide them on your CV
 B. Present them with the steps you’re taking to improve
 C. Blame past managers
 D. Avoid mentioning them in interviews

A values–career mismatch typically leads to:
 A. Higher long-term motivation
 B. Energy drain and lower engagement
 C. Faster promotions
 D. Easier networking

A SMART short-term goal example is:
 A. “Get a great job soon.”
 B. “Improve Excel eventually.”
 C. “Complete an Excel data-analysis course by 30 June and present one dashboard.”
 D. “Be better at spreadsheets.”

Matching skills to opportunities begins with:
 A. Listing your top hard and soft skills, then comparing to job requirements
 B. Applying for everything in one sector
 C. Choosing jobs by title alone
 D. Waiting for recruiters to contact you

A practical way to build confidence week-to-week is to:
 A. Ignore feedback
 B. Track small wins and rehearse upcoming tasks
 C. Rely on natural talent
 D. Avoid stretch tasks

A helpful check on “strengths overuse” (e.g., perfectionism) is to:
 A. Triple the time spent on details
 B. Set time limits and define “good enough” criteria
 C. Add more reviewers
 D. Avoid deadlines

Industry research helps you:
 A. Replace planning
 B. Spot growing sectors and avoid shrinking ones
 C. Guarantee job offers
 D. Skip qualifications

To understand a role beyond its title, you should prioritise:
 A. Salary only
 B. Daily tasks, required skills, entry routes, and progression
 C. Company perks
 D. Office layout

Labour-market analysis is MOST useful for:
 A. Predicting the exact employer who’ll hire you
 B. Identifying demand trends, skills shortages, and regional opportunities
 C. Replacing interview prep
 D. Negotiating salaries only

A strong use of online resources is to:
 A. Copy someone’s profile
 B. Compare job ads for repeated skills and map your gaps
 C. Post daily for visibility only
 D. Avoid official sources

Informational interviews are primarily for:
 A. Asking directly for a job
 B. Learning about a role/industry and entry pathways
 C. Salary negotiation
 D. Replacing applications

A networking benefit highlighted in the text is access to:
 A. Overtime pay
 B. Unadvertised opportunities via referrals
 C. Free transport
 D. Automatic promotions

A reliable indicator a role may NOT suit you is:
 A. It offers training
 B. Daily tasks clash with your values and energy patterns
 C. Others enjoy it
 D. It has a trendy title

Effective workplace communication includes:
 A. Writing long paragraphs to show effort
 B. Clear structure, plain language, and error checks
 C. Heavy jargon for credibility
 D. Caps for emphasis

Active listening is BEST shown by:
 A. Waiting to speak next
 B. Paraphrasing key points and asking clarifying questions
 C. Maintaining silence only
 D. Taking over to save time

Non-verbal signals that support communication include:
 A. Closed posture and minimal eye contact
 B. Open posture, steady eye contact, and congruent tone
 C. Speaking faster
 D. Louder volume

A solid first step in problem-solving is to:
 A. Implement the first idea
 B. Define the problem precisely with observable evidence
 C. Survey everyone’s opinion
 D. Check last year’s plan only

The 5 Whys technique helps you:
 A. Write longer emails
 B. Test assumptions to find root causes
 C. Speed through brainstorming
 D. Avoid data

A simple decision aid for comparing solutions is a:
 A. Random pick
 B. Decision matrix with agreed criteria (e.g., impact/effort/risk)
 C. Popular vote
 D. Rotating chair

Piloting a change is recommended because it:
 A. Replaces measurement
 B. Lets you test on a small scale with success metrics before wider rollout
 C. Avoids stakeholder input
 D. Guarantees success

Time management improves when you:
 A. Schedule everything back-to-back
 B. Distinguish urgent vs. important (e.g., Eisenhower Matrix)
 C. Respond instantly to all messages
 D. Leave plans open

A sustainable daily plan focuses on:
 A. Ten priorities
 B. Three core outcomes and one optional
 C. Inbox count
 D. Every possible task

Time-boxing routine tasks primarily aims to:
 A. Make them longer
 B. Contain low-value work so it doesn’t sprawl
 C. Replace deadlines
 D. Increase perfectionism

A practical distraction control during focus blocks is to:
 A. Keep all notifications on
 B. Use site/app blockers and silence non-essential alerts
 C. Check messages every five minutes
 D. Work with TV in the background

Teamwork is strongest when members:
 A. Work independently without coordination
 B. Share information, respect differences, and own commitments
 C. Avoid disagreement
 D. Rotate randomly

Healthy conflict handling means:
 A. Focusing on people
 B. Focusing on issues/ideas and resolving respectfully
 C. Avoiding all conflict
 D. Escalating immediately

Accountability in teams is reinforced by:
 A. Vague action items
 B. Clear owner, deliverable, and due date tracked visibly
 C. Optional follow-ups
 D. Private notes only

Lifelong learning is framed as:
 A. One-off qualifications
 B. A continuous learn → apply → get feedback → improve loop
 C. Study without practice
 D. Only formal degrees

Formal learning includes:
 A. Degrees and accredited certificates
 B. Podcasts and articles only
 C. On-the-job tips from peers
 D. Short unassessed workshops only

Non-formal learning is BEST described as:
 A. Unstructured browsing
 B. Structured courses/workshops outside traditional degree programmes
 C. Accidental learning
 D. Only mentoring

Informal learning examples include:
 A. Accredited diplomas
 B. Books, tutorials, shadowing, and peer discussions
 C. Exams only
 D. Lectures with assessment

When choosing a course, the FIRST filter should be:
 A. Campus coffee quality
 B. Fit to your career goal and skill gap
 C. Friends’ opinions
 D. The longest duration

A wise course-selection check is to review:
 A. Entry requirements, outcomes, delivery mode, and assessment
 B. Logo design
 C. Social-media following
 D. Number of electives only

A strong way to fund learning may include:
 A. Hoping for luck
 B. Researching bursaries/scholarships/employer support and planning costs
 C. Ignoring payment deadlines
 D. Paying late fees

Balancing study with responsibilities works better when you:
 A. Study only when free time appears
 B. Time-block study, use buffers, and scale during heavy weeks
 C. Avoid routines
 D. Pull all-nighters

Recognising prior, non-formal learning can help you:
 A. Repeat material
 B. Gain recognition/credit for existing skills and accelerate progress
 C. Avoid assessment forever
 D. Skip practice

To make learning “stick,” the booklet recommends you:
 A. Watch videos only
 B. Apply each concept in small real tasks and seek feedback
 C. Memorise definitions
 D. Wait until exams

A quarterly personal learning system should include:
 A. Random topics
 B. A focused skill target, chosen resources, practice tasks, and review
 C. Only reading
 D. Only certificates

Networking that supports learning and opportunities looks like:
 A. Adding contacts without context
 B. Attending events, joining associations, and arranging informational chats
 C. Asking for jobs immediately
 D. Posting motivational quotes

A sign an industry is future-friendly is:
 A. High recent layoffs and shrinking demand
 B. Growing employment and skills shortages in credible reports
 C. Vague news articles
 D. Celebrity endorsements

When comparing two roles, the MOST decisive evidence is:
 A. Office décor
 B. How the daily tasks, required skills, and growth paths match your strengths/values
 C. The longer title
 D. The friendlier HR email

A clear application of problem-solving in studies/work is to:
 A. Skip measurement
 B. Define success metrics before you pilot a change
 C. Judge options before listing any
 D. Avoid iteration

Effective written updates should lead with:
 A. Backstory
 B. Bottom line, status vs. measures, and explicit next steps
 C. Quotes from leaders
 D. Colourful formatting

A common bias that can derail decisions is:
 A. Considering disconfirming evidence
 B. Confirmation bias (only noticing data that fits your first idea)
 C. Comparing options with a matrix
 D. Asking for a peer review

A simple way to test fit for a new field before committing is to:
 A. Enrol in the longest course
 B. Take a short introductory course and speak to professionals
 C. Quit current work immediately
 D. Buy textbooks only

A practical weekly progress signal for learning is:
 A. Hours logged only
 B. Evidence of applied outcomes (e.g., a working draft, prototype, or mini-project)
 C. Number of videos watched
 D. Number of posts liked

A NOT recommended approach to building employability skills is to:
 A. Practise active listening
 B. Ignore feedback and avoid collaboration
 C. Use checklists and planners
 D. Pilot improvements and document learning

Bringing Modules 1–4 together, the strongest foundation for momentum is to:
 A. Chase every trend simultaneously
 B. Align strengths/values with market-aware goals, build core skills, and sustain learning–practice cycles
 C. Wait for motivation
 D. Focus on certificates instead of application
