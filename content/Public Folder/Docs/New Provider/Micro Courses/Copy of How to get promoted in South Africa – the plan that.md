# **How to get promoted in South Africa – the plan that actually works - notes**

# **1. Keep the “Hard Truths First” Structure**

In Book 1, you begin by telling readers that their current job-search plan is useless, outdated, and self-defeating.

Examples:

* “What you are doing is not working.”
* “Almost zero real jobs are found on the internet.”

### **Do the same in Book 2:**

Open with:
**“You think you know how promotions work. You are wrong.”**

Expose the truth about promotions:

* Hard work alone does *not* get you promoted.
* Doing your job well means you stay *in* your job.
* Promotions are not fair or automatic.
* Being liked, trusted, and visible matters more than being perfect.

Start with a punch in the face, just like Book 1.

# **⭐ 2. Make “Tenacity” the Equivalent Theme – But Recast for Promotion**

In Book 1, tenacity is everything.

Example:

* “Most people give up after six months… you must outlast them.”

### **For promotion, the equivalent is:**

**Consistency beats talent.**Or even better:

**“Most people stop growing the moment they get hired. That is why promotions are actually easy to achieve.”**

You can position the book around:

* Showing up with discipline that other employees simply do not have
* Outlasting your competition inside the company
* Making yourself unavoidable, indispensable, or impossible to ignore

This mirrors the “everyone else gives up” argument in Book 1.

# **⭐ 3. Identify and Destroy the “Wrong Plan”**

Book 1 is structured around demolishing the reader’s current strategy.

Examples:

* “Your plan is not working because you do things that make you FEEL busy.”
* “None of this will get you a job.”

### **Do the same in Book 2:**

Show how most South Africans try to get promoted:

* Waiting to be noticed
* Working harder, not smarter
* Avoiding difficult conversations
* Hiding from visibility
* Being scared to ask
* Confusing loyalty with progress

Then say:
**“None of this gets you promoted.”**

List the behaviours that FEEL productive but are worthless.

This mirror-structure keeps the same unmistakable signature.

# **⭐ 4. Include a Chapter Equivalent to “How People Really Find Jobs”**

In Book 1, you expose that jobs come from contacts, not job sites.

Example:

* “Almost all jobs are found through contacts.”

### **In Book 2:**

Expose how promotions *actually* happen in South Africa:

* Your manager recommends you.
* Someone fights for you when you are not in the room.
* You solve problems nobody else wants to touch.
* You become trustworthy, not talented.
* You improve your boss’s life.
* You take on work that is *above your job description* before being asked.

Structure:

* “How promotions really work”
* “The invisible rules of South African workplaces”
* “Why waiting never works”

This keeps the same “I’m going to tell you what nobody else will tell you” tone.

# **⭐ 5. Add Uncomfortable Advice (Your Signature Style)**

The original books include brutally honest points like:

* “Be willing to work for low wages.”
* “There are no bad jobs.”
* “Maybe you are even spending time dropping off CVs… lolz!”

This humour + harsh truth is core to your voice.

### **In Book 2:**

Use similar “uncomfortable truths”, for example:

* “Stop believing your work will speak for you. It cannot talk.”
* “Your company does not owe you a promotion.”
* “If you hide in the corner, the corner is where you will stay.”
* “You are not promoted for effort. You are promoted for impact.”
* “If your manager is scared of you, threatened by you or irritated by you, you will never move.”
* “You need to become the person your boss brags about.”

This keeps the same real, unfiltered power as Book 1.

# **⭐ 6. Use the Same “Real Action Plan” Format**

In Book 1, your plan is simple, uncomfortable, and guaranteed to work:

“Get a job, any job.
Become their best employee.
All the other opportunities will open.”

### **Book 2 equivalent:**

\*\*“Your promotion plan is:

1. Pick the promotion you want.
2. Do the work of that job now.
3. Make your boss’s life easier.
4. Ask for the promotion.”\*\*

This aligns exactly with your minimalistic, practical formula style.

# **⭐ 7. Bring in AI Skills — But With Your “Real World” Angle**

Book 1 includes early but important mentions of AI tools for job hunting, e.g. LinkedIn, ChatGPT, Bard, etc.
Example: AI is part of “Helpful Technology” and “The proper use of technology.”

### **In Book 2:**

Focus on **AI for promotion**, not generic “future of work” hype.

Show how AI gives a normal worker a manager-level advantage:

* Writing better reports than their peers
* Automating tasks their colleagues still do manually
* Preparing smarter for meetings
* Impressing leadership with speed and clarity
* Becoming the “AI person” in the team
* Producing ideas, analysis, and documents quickly

Make it simple and practical:

* “If you can use WhatsApp, you can use AI.”
* “AI makes you look smarter than you feel.”
* “AI is the shortcut to becoming promotion-ready.”

This aligns perfectly with your audience and your first book’s tone.

# **⭐ 8. Keep the Language Simple, Sharp, and Direct**

Your books use:

* Short sentences
* Repetition for emphasis
* Very direct instructions
* Zero academic language

Examples:

* “Never stop asking people if they know of job openings.”
* “Be humble.”

Book 2 must follow this exact pattern.

# **⭐ 9. Add Lists—You Use Them Constantly**

Your books break everything into:

* Short lists of steps
* Lists of people to contact
* Lists of things not to do
* Lists of enemies (scammers, time-wasters, job sites, etc.)

Replicate this for:

* “People who will block your promotion”
* “Behaviours that keep you stuck”
* “Signs your boss will never promote you”
* “Daily habits of people who get promoted”

# **⭐ 10. End With Motivation + Reality**

Book 1 ends with:

* “The importance of perseverance”
* “Never give up”

Book 2 can end with:

* “Growth takes courage.”
* “You are capable of more than your position.”
* “Promotions go to the brave, not the perfect.”
* “Start today, even if you feel small.”
