Welcome

Welcome to your Interior Design Drawing & Visual Communication Study Guide!

We are excited to support you on this creative learning journey as you develop essential

drawing and visual communication skills used in interior design. This subject is not only about

drawing, but also about communicating ideas clearly through sketches, plans, and visual

presentations. Throughout this course, you will learn how to represent design ideas accurately,

use drawing techniques effectively, and present interior spaces in a clear and professional way.

practical design situations.

How to Use this Study Guide

This study guide is designed to support your learning as a distance learning student. It will help

you understand your Interior Design Drawing & Visual Communication textbook, stay

organised, and prepare effectively for your assignments.

●  Start with the Module Overview: Each module begins with a short introduction. Read

this first to understand what you will learn and why it is important.

●  Read the Unit Summaries: Each module is divided into units with clear summaries.

Use these to revise key concepts or to get an overview before studying the textbook in

detail.

●  Focus on Practical Drawing Skills: Apply what you learn by practising drawing

techniques such as sketching, line work, shading, technical drawing, and visual

presentation.

●  Highlight Important Concepts: Highlight key topics such as visual communication,

types of drawings, scale, orthographic drawings, perspective, and presentation

techniques to support revision.

●  Take Notes and Practise Drawing: Write your own notes and practise sketches

regularly. Drawing is a practical skill, so consistent practice will improve your confidence

and accuracy.

●  Use It for Assignment Preparation: Use the summaries to guide your answers and

apply your knowledge to practical design and drawing tasks.

●  Refer to the Textbook: Use the textbook for detailed explanations, diagrams, and

examples when needed.

●  Manage Your Time: Study one module at a time and create a simple study plan to stay

on track.

●  Practise and Reflect: Apply your skills by observing real spaces and trying to draw

layouts, objects, and perspectives around you.

●  Reach Out for Support: If you need help, contact your tutor or support team. Support

is available to help you succeed in your studies.

Using this study guide together with your textbook will help you build a strong understanding of

interior design drawing and visual communication. Stay organised, practise regularly, and

apply what you learn.

Fundamentals Of Interior Design And

Visual Communication

Introduction

This module introduces the basic concepts of interior design and visual communication. It

explains how designers plan spaces and use drawings to present ideas clearly. These

fundamentals form the foundation for developing drawing skills and understanding the design

process.

Read page 10 of your textbook for more information.

Unit 1: Introduction to Interior Design

Interior design focuses on creating spaces that are both functional and visually appealing. It

involves planning layouts, selecting materials, and understanding how people interact within a

space.

●  1.1 Definition and Purpose of Interior Design: Interior design is the planning and

organisation of indoor spaces to improve both function and appearance. It aims to create

environments that are safe, comfortable, and suitable for their intended use.

●  1.2 Roles and Responsibilities of Interior Designers: Interior designers plan layouts,

choose materials, and present ideas through drawings. They also work with clients and

other professionals to ensure that the design meets practical and aesthetic

requirements.

●  1.3 Types of Interior Design Projects: Design projects vary depending on the space.

These include residential spaces (homes), commercial spaces (offices and shops),

hospitality spaces (hotels and restaurants), and public spaces such as schools and

hospitals.

●  1.4 Overview of the Interior Design Process: Design work follows a structured

process that includes understanding the client’s needs, developing ideas, creating

drawings, and presenting solutions. This process helps ensure clear and effective

results.

Read pages 11—15 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

9

Unit 2: Visual Communication in Interior Design

Clear communication is essential in design. Visual tools such as drawings and images are used

to explain ideas and ensure that designs are understood correctly.

●  2.1 Meaning of Visual Communication: Visual communication refers to the use of

drawings, images, and symbols to present ideas. It makes complex design concepts

easier to understand.

●  2.2 Importance in Interior Design: Using visuals improves clarity, reduces errors, and

helps clients and teams understand the design before it is completed.

●  2.3 Types of Visual Communication Methods: Different methods are used depending

on the stage of the project. These include sketches for ideas, floor plans for layouts,

elevations for vertical views, and 3D visuals for realistic presentations.

●  2.4 Communication with Clients and Stakeholders: Successful design requires clear

communication with clients, contractors, and suppliers. This ensures that everyone

understands the design and their role in the project.

Read pages 16—20 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 3: Drawing as a Design Tool

Drawing plays an important role in developing, testing, and communicating design ideas. It

supports both creativity and problem-solving.

●  3.1 Drawing for Idea Generation: Sketching allows designers to explore different ideas

quickly. It helps in developing creative solutions and testing layouts.

●  3.2 Drawing for Problem Solving: Drawings make it easier to identify design problems

and test possible solutions before final decisions are made.

●  3.3 Drawing as a Communication Tool: Drawings are used to explain ideas clearly to

clients and professionals. They provide visual instructions and reduce

misunderstandings.

●  3.4 Freehand vs Technical Drawing: Freehand drawing is quick and flexible, making it

useful for early ideas. Technical drawing is precise and used to show accurate

measurements for construction.

10

Read pages 21—25 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 4: Introduction to Design Drawings

Different types of drawings are used at different stages of the design process. Each type serves

a specific purpose in communicating ideas.

●  4.1 Concept Drawings and Sketches: Concept drawings are simple and used in the

early stages of design. They help explore ideas and plan layouts.

●  4.2 Technical Drawings (Overview Only): Technical drawings are detailed and

accurate. They show measurements and layout information needed for construction.

●  4.3 Presentation Drawings: Presentation drawings are used to show final design ideas.

They are neat, detailed, and often include colour and texture to help clients visualise the

space.

●  4.4 Manual vs Digital Drawings: Manual drawings are created by hand and are useful

for quick sketches. Digital drawings use software and allow for greater accuracy and

professional presentation.

Read pages 26—29 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Introduction

This module focuses on developing the basic drawing skills needed in interior design. It

introduces the tools, techniques, and methods used to create clear and effective drawings.

These skills form the foundation for both freehand sketches and technical drawings used later in

the course.

Read page 36 of your textbook for more information.

Unit 1: Drawing Tools and Materials

Accurate and neat drawings depend on using the correct tools, materials, and workspace setup.

Understanding how to use and care for these tools improves drawing quality.

●  1.1 Types of Drawing Tools: Interior designers use tools such as pencils, rulers,

erasers, and drawing boards. Each tool has a specific purpose and helps improve

accuracy and control.

●  1.2 Drawing Surfaces and Paper Types: Different types of paper are used for different

drawing purposes. Sketch paper is used for rough ideas, cartridge paper for general

work, and tracing paper for refining drawings.

●  1.3 Tool Handling and Maintenance: Proper care of tools ensures clean and accurate

drawings. Keeping tools clean, pencils sharp, and equipment organised helps improve

drawing results.

●  1.4 Setting Up a Drawing Workspace: A well-organised workspace with good lighting

and a stable surface helps improve focus and drawing accuracy. Comfort and

organisation are important for effective work.

Read pages 37—40 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

14

Unit 2: Line Work and Graphic Language

Line work is the foundation of all drawings. Different types of lines and symbols are used to

communicate information clearly and professionally.

●  2.1 Types of Lines and Their Meanings: Different lines are used to show specific

information. For example, solid lines show visible edges, dashed lines show hidden

features, and dotted lines show centre lines.

●  2.2 Line Weight and Quality: Line weight refers to how thick or thin a line is, while line

quality refers to how smooth and consistent it is. Using the correct line weight improves

clarity and depth.

●  2.3 Drawing Symbols and Conventions: Symbols are used to represent objects

such as doors, windows, and furniture. These standard symbols make drawings easier

to understand.

●  2.4 Visual Clarity and Neatness: Clear and neat drawings are easier to read and look

more professional. Proper spacing, alignment, and clean lines improve communication.

Read pages 41—45 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 3: Shapes, Forms, and Proportion

Understanding shapes, forms, and proportion helps create accurate and realistic drawings.

These elements are used to represent objects and spaces correctly.

●  3.1 Basic Geometric Shapes: Simple shapes such as squares, rectangles, circles,

and triangles form the foundation of most drawings. They are used to build more

complex objects.

●  3.2 Drawing 3D Forms: Three-dimensional forms such as cubes, cylinders, and

spheres add depth to drawings. They help represent objects more realistically.

●  3.3 Understanding Proportion: Proportion refers to the size relationship between

objects. Correct proportion ensures that drawings look balanced and realistic.

●  3.4 Drawing Interior Objects: Interior objects can be drawn by combining basic

shapes and forms. Starting with simple shapes makes it easier to develop accurate

drawings.

Read pages 46—50 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

15

Unit 4: Shading, Texture, and Tone

Shading and texture are used to make drawings look more realistic by showing light, depth, and

surface details.

●  4.1 Shading Techniques: Different techniques such as hatching, cross-hatching,

blending, and stippling are used to show light and shadow in drawings.

●  4.2 Representing Materials: Different materials such as wood, metal, and fabric are

shown using specific shading and line techniques to represent their texture.

●  4.3 Creating Depth and Volume: Depth is created by using shading, light sources, and

overlapping objects. This helps drawings appear more three-dimensional.

●  4.4 Texture in Interior Drawings: Texture shows how surfaces look or feel. It is created

using patterns, lines, and shading to make drawings more detailed and realistic.

Read pages 51—55 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 5: Introduction to Sketching and Digital Drawing

Sketching and digital drawing are both important methods used in interior design. Each method

is used at different stages of the design process.

●  5.1 Freehand Sketching Techniques: Freehand sketching involves drawing without

tools. It is used to quickly capture ideas and explore design concepts.

●  5.2 Conceptual Sketching: Conceptual sketching builds on freehand sketches by

adding more detail. It helps develop and refine design ideas.

●  5.3 Introduction to Digital Drawing Tools: Digital tools such as CAD software and

drawing applications are used to create accurate and detailed drawings. These tools

improve efficiency and presentation.

●  5.4 Manual vs Digital Sketching: Manual sketching is quick and flexible, while digital

sketching allows for precision and easy editing. Both methods are used together in

design work.

Introduction

This module focuses on technical drawing, which is used to communicate design ideas

accurately. It introduces scale, measurements, and orthographic drawings such as floor plans,

elevations, and sections. These drawings are essential for planning and construction, as they

provide clear and detailed information about a design.

Read page 67 of your textbook for more information.

Unit 1: Scales and Measurements

Accurate measurements are essential in technical drawing. Scale is used to represent real-life

sizes in a smaller, manageable format on paper.

●  1.1 Understanding Scale: Scale shows the relationship between the size of a

drawing and the real object. Common scales such as 1:50 or 1:100 are used to reduce

large spaces to fit on paper.

●  1.2 Converting Measurements: Measurements must be converted from real size to

drawing size using the chosen scale. This ensures that drawings are accurate and

consistent.

●  1.3 Drawing to Scale: Drawing to scale involves using correct measurements and

tools to represent objects accurately. This ensures that all elements fit together

correctly.

●  1.4 Common Scale Errors: Errors such as incorrect calculations or inconsistent

scales can affect the accuracy of a drawing. Careful checking helps avoid these

mistakes.

Read pages 68—72 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

19

Unit 2: Introduction to Orthographic Drawings

Orthographic drawings are used to show different views of a space clearly and accurately.

These drawings are essential for communicating detailed design information.

●  2.1 Definition of Orthographic Drawing: Orthographic drawing is a method of showing

an object using separate views, such as top, front, and side views.

●  2.2 Purpose in Interior Design: These drawings provide accurate measurements and

layout details, making them useful for planning and construction.

●  2.3 Types of Orthographic Views: Common views include - Plan view (top view),

Elevation (side view) and Section (cut-through view). Each view shows a different aspect

of the design.

●  2.4 Reading Orthographic Drawings: Understanding symbols, measurements, and

labels is important when reading these drawings. All views must be considered together

for a complete understanding.

Read pages 73—77 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 3: Floor Plans (Plan View)

Floor plans show a space from a top view and are used to plan layouts and organise furniture

within a space.

●  3.1 Elements of a Floor Plan: Floor plans include walls, doors, windows, and room

layouts. These elements show how the space is structured.

●  3.2 Symbols and Conventions: Standard symbols are used to represent features such

as doors, windows, and furniture. These symbols make drawings easier to read.

●  3.3 Furniture Layouts: Furniture layout shows how items are arranged within a space.

Good layout ensures functionality, comfort, and easy movement.

●  3.4 Drawing a Basic Floor Plan: Creating a floor plan involves measuring the space,

applying scale, drawing walls, and adding details such as furniture and openings.

Read pages 78—82 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

20

Unit 4: Elevations and Sections

Elevations and sections provide vertical views of a space, showing heights and interior details.

●  4.1 Interior Elevations: Elevations show one side of a room, including walls, windows,

and design features. They help visualise vertical elements.

●  4.2 Section Drawings: Section drawings show a cut-through view of a space. They

reveal internal details that are not visible in floor plans.

●  4.3 Vertical Measurements and Heights: These drawings include measurements of

height, such as wall height, door height, and ceiling levels.

●  4.4 Detailing Interior Features: Details such as finishes, fixtures, and built-in

elements are shown clearly in elevations and sections.

Read pages 83—87 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 5: Dimensioning, Annotation, and Digital Drafting

Clear labelling and accurate information are essential in technical drawings. Dimensioning and

annotation help communicate details effectively.

●  5.1 Dimensioning Rules: Dimensioning involves adding measurements to drawings.

Correct placement and clear formatting ensure accuracy.

●  5.2 Labelling Drawings: Labels identify different parts of the drawing, such as rooms,

materials, and features.

●  5.3 Notes and Specifications: Notes provide additional information about materials,

finishes, and construction details.

●  5.4 Introduction to CAD Drafting: Computer-Aided Design (CAD) is used to create

precise digital drawings. It improves accuracy and allows easy editing.

Read pages 88—91 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Introduction

This module focuses on how interior spaces are planned and designed. It introduces the

concepts of space, layout, and design principles. Understanding how to organise space and

apply design elements helps create environments that are both functional and visually balanced.

Read page 98 of your textbook for more information.

Unit 1: Understanding Space

Understanding how space works is essential for effective design. Different types of space and

how people move within them must be considered.

●  1.1 Positive and Negative Space: Positive space refers to areas occupied by furniture

and objects, while negative space refers to empty areas. Both are important for balance

and movement.

●  1.2 Spatial Relationships: Spatial relationships describe how objects are positioned in

relation to each other. Good relationships improve functionality and visual flow.

●  1.3 Circulation and Movement: Circulation refers to how people move through a space.

Clear pathways ensure easy and safe movement.

●  1.4 Human Needs in Design: Design must consider comfort, safety, and usability.

Spaces should meet the physical and practical needs of users.

Read pages 99—103 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 2: Space Planning Techniques

Space planning involves organising a space to make it functional and efficient. Different

techniques are used to improve layout and usability.

●  2.1 Zoning Spaces: Zoning divides a space into different areas based on function, such

as living, working, or relaxing areas.

24

●  2.2 Functional Layout Design: Layouts should support the purpose of the space.

Furniture and elements must be arranged to allow easy use.

●  2.3 Ergonomics: Ergonomics focuses on designing spaces that are comfortable and

suitable for human use. It considers body movement and posture.

●  2.4 Planning Different Room Types: Different rooms require different layouts. For

example, kitchens, bedrooms, and offices each have specific design needs.

Read pages 104—107 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 3: Elements of Design

Design elements are the basic components used to create interior spaces. These elements

affect how a space looks and feels.

●  3.1 Line, Shape, and Form: Lines, shapes, and forms define the structure of a space

and influence its style and appearance.

●  3.2 Colour and Light: Colour affects mood and atmosphere, while light highlights

features and improves visibility.

●  3.3 Texture and Patter: Texture refers to the surface quality of materials, while

patterns add visual interest and detail.

●  3.4 Space in Design: Space itself is an important element. Proper use of space

ensures balance and prevents overcrowding.

Read pages 106—112 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 4: Principles of Design

Design principles guide how elements are arranged to create a balanced and visually pleasing

space.

●  4.1 Balance and Harmony: Balance creates stability in a design, while harmony

ensures that all elements work well together.

25

●  4.2 Contrast and Emphasis: Contrast highlights differences between elements, while

emphasis draws attention to key features.

●  4.3 Rhythm and Repetition: Rhythm is created through repeated patterns or elements,

helping guide the viewer’s eye through the space.

●  4.4 Proportion and Scale: Proportion refers to the size relationship between elements,

while scale relates to the size of objects in relation to the space.

Read pages 113—117 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 5: Application of Space Planning

Applying space planning involves using design knowledge to create practical and effective

layouts.

●  5.1 Residential Layouts: Residential layouts focus on comfort and daily living needs.

Spaces must be functional and welcoming.

●  5.2 Commercial Layouts: Commercial layouts focus on efficiency and purpose, such

as office productivity or customer flow in shops.

●  5.3 Furniture Arrangement: Furniture must be arranged to support movement,

function, and visual balance within a space.

●  5.4 Practical Layout Examples: Examples of layouts show how design principles are

applied in real situations. These help improve understanding and practical skills.

Read pages 118—122 of your textbook for more information on this unit.
