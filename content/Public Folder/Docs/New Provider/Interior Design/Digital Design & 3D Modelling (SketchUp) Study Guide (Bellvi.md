Welcome

Welcome to your Digital Design & 3D Modelling (SketchUp) Study Guide!

We are excited to support you on this creative and practical learning journey as you develop

digital design and 3D modelling skills used in modern design industries. This subject focuses on

creating accurate digital models and communicating design ideas clearly using SketchUp and

digital presentation techniques.

Throughout this course, you will learn how to create 2D and 3D models, use SketchUp tools,

apply materials and textures, organise projects professionally, and present design ideas

effectively.

Think of this study guide as your learning companion throughout the course. It works together

with your textbook to help you understand key concepts, stay organised, and prepare for your

assignments.

To succeed in this course, it is important to work consistently through both the textbook and this

study guide. You will need to:

●  Study Modules 1–3 and complete Assignment 1, and

●  Study Modules 4–6 and complete Assignment 2

There are no formal examinations for this course. However, both assignments are compulsory

and must be submitted for assessment. Once submitted, you will receive tutor feedback, which

will help you improve your understanding and further develop your digital design and 3D

modelling skills.

As you work through the modules, you will cover topics such as digital design principles,

SketchUp workspace navigation, drawing and modelling tools, project organisation, materials

and textures, presentation techniques, rendering basics, and professional digital workflows. The

activities, examples, and practical exercises included throughout the course will help you apply

your knowledge in realistic design situations.

Before you begin, make sure you have received:

●  Your Digital Design & 3D Modelling (SketchUp) Textbook

How to Use this Study Guide

This study guide is designed to support you throughout your studies as a distance learning

student. It works together with your Digital Design & 3D Modelling (SketchUp) textbook to help

you understand key concepts, stay organised, and prepare for your assignments.

●  Start with the Module Overview: Read the introduction at the beginning of each

module to understand the topics and learning goals.

●  Use the Unit Summaries: Each unit contains important summaries to help you revise

and understand the main ideas.

●  Practise Your Modelling Skills: Regularly practise creating 2D drawings, 3D models,

layouts, textures, scenes, and presentation views in SketchUp.

●  Highlight Important Concepts: Focus on topics such as drawing tools, modelling tools,

measurements, groups, components, tags, scenes, materials, rendering, and digital

workflows.

●  Take Notes and Practise Often: Writing notes and practising SketchUp tools regularly

will improve your confidence, speed, and accuracy.

●  Use the Guide for Assignment Preparation: The summaries and key points will help

you prepare for Assignment 1 and Assignment 2.

●  Refer to the Textbook: Use the textbook for detailed explanations, examples,

diagrams, and step-by-step modelling activities when needed.

●  Manage Your Time: Study one module at a time and follow a simple study plan to stay

on track.

●  Apply What You Learn: Observe real spaces, furniture, buildings, and layouts, and

think about how they can be recreated using digital models and presentation techniques.

●  Ask for Support: Contact your tutor or support team if you need assistance during your

studies.

Using this study guide together with your textbook will help you build strong digital design and

3D modelling skills for professional design environments.

Introduction

Digital design and 3D modelling are important skills used in many modern industries. SketchUp

helps designers create accurate digital models and present ideas clearly and professionally.

In this module, you will learn the basics of digital design and 3D modelling, explore the

SketchUp software, and understand how to navigate the workspace. You will also learn about

file organisation and workspace management skills used in professional digital design projects.

Read page 10 of your textbook for more information.

Unit 1: Understanding Digital Design and 3D Modelling

Digital design and 3D modelling are used to create visual representations of spaces, objects,

and ideas. These skills are important in industries such as architecture, interior design,

engineering, and product design. Understanding the basics will help you build a strong

foundation for working in SketchUp.

●  1.1 What is Digital Design?: Digital design is the process of using computer software

to create drawings, layouts, models, and presentations. Designers use digital tools to

improve accuracy, make changes easily, and communicate ideas clearly.

●  1.2 Introduction to 3D Modelling: 3D modelling involves creating digital objects that

have width, height, and depth. These models help designers visualise projects

realistically before construction or production begins.

●  1.3 Careers in Digital Design: Many career opportunities use digital design and 3D

modelling  skills.  Examples  include  interior  design,  architecture,  engineering,  product

design, animation, and gaming. These careers combine creativity, problem-solving, and

technical skills.

Read pages 11—17 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

9

Unit 2: Introduction to SketchUp

SketchUp is widely used in design industries because it allows users to create digital models

quickly and accurately. Learning how the software works and how to set it up correctly is an

important step before starting practical modelling activities.

●  2.1 What is SketchUp?: SketchUp is a 3D modelling programme used to create digital

designs and presentations. It includes tools for drawing, modelling, applying materials,

and creating presentation views.

●  2.2 System Requirements and Setup: Good computer performance helps SketchUp

run smoothly. Important requirements include enough memory, suitable graphics

performance, and correct software setup for modelling projects.

●  2.3 Installing SketchUp: Installing SketchUp includes downloading the software,

creating an account, and selecting the correct template settings. Proper installation helps

ensure that all tools and features work correctly.

Read pages 18—26 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 3: The SketchUp Workspace

Understanding the SketchUp workspace helps you work more confidently and efficiently.

Knowing how to use navigation tools, toolbars, and file management systems improves

workflow and reduces mistakes during modelling.

●  3.1 Understanding the Interface: The SketchUp interface includes toolbars, menus,

drawing areas, and panels used during modelling. Learning the layout of the workspace

helps users access tools quickly and work more efficiently.

●  3.2 Navigation Tools: Navigation tools help users move around the workspace and

view models from different angles. Orbit, Pan, and Zoom are commonly used to improve

visibility and control while modelling.

●  3.3 File Management and Organisation: Good file management helps keep projects

organised and safe. Proper naming systems, folder organisation, and regular backups

improve workflow and reduce the risk of losing important work.

Introduction

Drawing and modelling tools are used to create shapes, build 3D objects, and edit digital

models in SketchUp. Understanding these tools helps improve modelling accuracy, workflow,

and presentation quality.

In this module, you will learn how to use important SketchUp drawing and modelling tools. You

will also explore measurements, axes, inference snapping, modification tools, and editing

techniques used to create professional digital models.

Read page 44 of your textbook for more information.

Unit 1: Drawing Tools

Drawing tools help designers create the basic shapes and outlines needed for digital models.

These tools are used to create floor plans, furniture shapes, room layouts, and detailed design

features in SketchUp.

●  1.1 The Line Tool: The Line Tool is used to create straight edges and outlines.

Connected lines can form shapes and surfaces that are later converted into 3D forms.

●  1.2 Rectangle Tool: The Rectangle Tool creates square and rectangular shapes

quickly and accurately. Designers commonly use it to create walls, rooms, floors, and

furniture layouts.

●  1.3 Circle and Polygon Tools: Circle and Polygon tools create round and multi-sided

shapes used in digital models. These tools are useful for creating tables, columns,

decorative objects, and geometric layouts.

●  1.4 Arc and Freehand Tools: Arc and Freehand tools create curved and irregular

shapes. These tools help designers add decorative details and more realistic forms to

digital models.

Read pages 45—49 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

13

Unit 2: Understanding Dimensions and Accuracy

Accurate measurements are important when creating professional digital models. SketchUp

includes tools and guides that help users draw precisely and position objects correctly within a

model.

●  2.1 Using Measurements: Measurements help designers create objects and layouts

with realistic sizes and proportions. Accurate dimensions improve the quality and

reliability of digital models.

●  2.2 Entering Exact Dimensions: SketchUp allows users to type exact dimensions while

drawing. This improves precision and helps designers follow correct technical

specifications.

●  2.3 Understanding Axes: The red, green, and blue axes help users understand

direction and positioning inside the SketchUp workspace. These axes guide movement

and drawing accuracy during modelling.

●  2.4 Inference Snapping: Inference snapping helps users align objects and connect

geometry accurately. Visual guides such as endpoints, midpoints, and axis lines improve

drawing precision.

Read pages 50—55 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 3: Creating 3D Forms

Creating 3D forms allows designers to turn flat shapes into realistic digital objects and

structures. SketchUp includes modelling tools that help add height, depth, and detail to designs.

●  3.1 Push/Pull Tool: The Push/Pull Tool converts flat surfaces into 3D forms by adding

height or depth. Designers use it to create walls, furniture, platforms, and openings.

●  3.2 Offset Tool: The Offset Tool creates duplicate outlines inside or outside existing

shapes. It is commonly used to create wall thickness, borders, and frames.

●  3.3 Follow Me Tool: The Follow Me Tool creates shapes by extending a surface along a

selected path. Designers use it to create pipes, railings, mouldings, and curved features.

●  3.4 Creating Openings and Extrusions: Openings and extrusions add detail and

realism to models. These techniques are commonly used for windows, doors, recessed

panels, and raised features.

14

Read pages 56—61 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 4: Modification Tools

Modification tools allow designers to adjust, move, rotate, resize, and duplicate objects within a

model. These tools improve workflow speed and help create organised and realistic designs.

●  4.1 Move Tool: The Move Tool repositions objects, edges, and faces inside a model.

Accurate positioning helps improve layout organisation and model realism.

●  4.2 Rotate Tool: The Rotate Tool changes the angle of objects around a selected point

or axis. Designers use it to position furniture and decorative objects naturally within a

space.

●  4.3 Scale Tool: The Scale Tool changes the size and proportions of objects. Correct

scaling helps maintain realistic dimensions in digital models.

●  4.4 Copying and Array Functions: Copying and array functions create repeated

objects quickly and accurately. These features are useful when placing furniture,

windows, lights, and decorative elements.

Read pages 62—67 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 5: Selection and Editing

Selection and editing tools help designers improve models, correct mistakes, and manage

geometry efficiently. Understanding these tools helps maintain clean and organised digital

models.

●  5.1 Selecting Objects: The Select Tool allows users to choose objects, faces, edges,

and groups for editing or modification. Different selection methods help users work more

efficiently.

●  5.2 Erasing Geometry: The Eraser Tool removes unwanted edges and geometry from

a model. Cleaning unnecessary lines improves organisation and model appearance.

●  5.3 Undo and Redo: Undo and Redo functions help users correct mistakes quickly

during modelling. These tools improve workflow and reduce the need to rebuild objects.

15

●  5.4 Editing Faces and Edges: Editing faces and edges helps reshape and improve

objects inside a model. Proper editing techniques support accurate and professional

modelling results.

Read pages 68—72 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 1: Setting Up a Project

A good project setup helps designers work more accurately and efficiently from the beginning.

Proper planning, correct settings, and organised workflows improve productivity and reduce

mistakes during modelling.

●  1.1 Choosing Templates: Templates provide pre-set workspace settings such as

measurement units and drawing styles. Choosing the correct template helps create

accurate and organised digital models.

●  1.2 Setting Units: Correct measurement units are important for creating models with

accurate dimensions. SketchUp allows users to work in millimetres, centimetres, metres,

inches, or feet depending on the project requirements.

●  1.3 Project Planning Basics: Planning a project before modelling helps designers

organise ideas, layouts, and modelling steps. Good planning improves workflow and

supports better project management.

●  1.4 Creating a Workflow: A workflow is a step-by-step process followed during a

project. Organised workflows help designers save time, improve consistency, and

manage projects more effectively.

Read pages 80—84 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 2: Groups and Components

Groups and components help keep models organised and prevent geometry from sticking

together. These tools improve editing, simplify workflows, and make models easier to manage.

●  2.1 What are Groups?: Groups combine selected geometry into a single organised

object. Using groups helps prevent accidental editing of other parts of the model.

●  2.2 Creating Components: Components are reusable objects that can be copied and

edited efficiently. Designers use components for furniture, doors, windows, and repeated

design elements.

●  2.3 Reusing Components: Reusing components saves time and improves consistency

in models. Changes made to one component can automatically update all copies of that

component.

●  2.4 Component Libraries: Component libraries store reusable digital objects for future

projects. These libraries help designers work faster and maintain organised workflows.

Read pages 85—90 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 3: Tags and Scenes

Tags and scenes help organise complex models and improve presentations. These tools make

it easier to control visibility, manage layouts, and create professional presentation views.

●  3.1 Understanding Tags: Tags help organise model elements into categories such as

furniture, lighting, or walls. Proper tag organisation improves workflow and project

management.

●  3.2 Organising Layers: Organising layers helps control the visibility of objects within a

project. Good layer management makes complex models easier to work with and

present.

●  3.3 Creating Scenes: Scenes save camera positions, views, and display settings for

presentations. Designers use scenes to create walkthroughs and presentation layouts.

●  3.4 Scene Management: Scene management involves organising and updating scenes

throughout a project. Well-managed scenes improve communication and presentation

quality.

Unit 1: Understanding Visual Design Principles

Strong visual design principles help create balanced, organised, and attractive digital models.

Understanding these principles improves layout planning, presentation quality, and

communication of design ideas.

●  1.1 Balance and Proportion: Balance and proportion help create visually stable and

comfortable spaces. Designers use these principles to organise objects, furniture, and

layouts in a realistic and pleasing way.

●  1.2 Scale in Design: Scale refers to the size relationship between objects and spaces.

Correct scale helps ensure that furniture, rooms, and architectural features appear

realistic within a model.

●  1.3 Colour Theory Basics: Colour theory explains how colours work together to create

different visual effects and moods. Designers use colour combinations to improve

atmosphere, contrast, and visual interest.

●  1.4 Space and Layout: Space and layout planning help organise objects and movement

within a design. Good layouts improve functionality, comfort, and visual balance in

interior and architectural spaces.

Read pages 116—121 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 2: Applying Materials and Textures

Materials and textures improve the realism and presentation quality of digital models. Applying

suitable finishes helps designers create more detailed and visually appealing projects.

●  2.1 Material Libraries: Material libraries contain ready-made textures and finishes used

in digital models. Designers can apply materials such as wood, glass, fabric, and

concrete to different surfaces.

●  2.2 Editing Materials: Editing materials allows users to change colours, brightness,

transparency, and texture settings. Adjusting materials helps improve realism and

presentation quality.

●  2.3 Importing Custom Textures: Custom textures allow designers to add unique

personalised designs.

●  2.4 Texture Positioning: Texture positioning controls how materials appear on surfaces.

Correct positioning helps improve alignment, scale, and realism within a model.

Read pages 122—127 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 3: Lighting and Shadows

Lighting and shadows help create realistic visual effects and improve the mood of digital

presentations. Proper lighting techniques also help designers understand how spaces may

appear in real environments.

●  3.1 Natural Lighting: Natural lighting simulates sunlight entering a space. Designers

use natural light to study brightness, shadows, and the overall feel of interior

environments.

●  3.2 Artificial Lighting: Artificial lighting includes lamps, ceiling lights, and decorative

lighting features used in digital models. Different lighting types help improve visibility and

atmosphere.

●  3.3 Shadows and Time Settings: Shadows and time settings allow users to control how

sunlight affects a model throughout the day. These settings improve realism and help

analyse lighting conditions.

24

●  3.4 Creating Mood and Atmosphere: Mood and atmosphere are influenced by lighting,

colours, materials, and layout choices. Designers use these elements to create

comfortable, dramatic, or professional environments.

Read pages 128—132 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 4: Sustainable and Inclusive Design Basics

Modern design practices focus on sustainability, accessibility, and responsible planning.

Designers must consider environmental impact, user comfort, and inclusivity when creating

spaces and digital models.

●  4.1 Sustainable Design Concepts: Sustainable design focuses on reducing

environmental impact through efficient planning, material choices, and energy-conscious

design solutions.

●  4.2 Efficient Space Planning: Efficient space planning improves movement,

functionality, and comfort within a design. Well-planned spaces make better use of

available areas and resources.

●  4.3 Accessibility in Design: Accessible design ensures that spaces can be used safely

and comfortably by people with different physical needs and abilities. Designers consider

movement, reach, and safety when planning layouts.

●  4.4 Responsible Digital Design Practices: Responsible digital design includes

organised workflows, ethical design choices, and efficient use of digital resources. Good

practices improve professionalism and project quality.
