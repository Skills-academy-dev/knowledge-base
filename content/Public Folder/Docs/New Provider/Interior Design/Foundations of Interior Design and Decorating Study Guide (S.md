Welcome

Welcome to your Foundations of Interior Design and Decorating Study Guide!

We are excited to support you on this creative learning journey as you develop essential

knowledge and practical skills in interior design and decorating. This subject is not only about

making spaces look attractive, but also about planning functional layouts, selecting suitable

materials, and creating environments that are comfortable, safe, and visually appealing.

Throughout this course, you will learn how to design and decorate different types of spaces,

apply design principles, and bring ideas to life in practical ways.

Think of this study guide as your learning companion throughout the course. It works alongside

your textbook to help you understand key concepts, simplify important ideas, and stay

organised as you progress through each module. The guide highlights important topics and

supports you in preparing for your assignments.

To succeed in this course, it is important to work consistently through both the textbook and this

study guide. You will need to:

●  Study Modules 1–4 and complete Assignment 1, and

●  Study Modules 5–7 and complete Assignment 2

There are no formal examinations for this course. However, both assignments are compulsory

and must be submitted for assessment. Once submitted, you will receive tutor feedback, which

will help you improve your understanding and develop your design skills further.

As you work through the modules, you will cover topics such as the basics of interior design and

decorating, the design process, colour theory, space planning, furniture and layouts, materials

and lighting, decorative styling, and residential and commercial applications. The activities and

examples provided will help you apply your knowledge in real-life design situations.

Before you begin, make sure you have received:

●  Your Foundations of Interior Design and Decorating Textbook

●  This Study Guide

●  Assignment 1 and Assignment 2

How to Use this Study Guide

This study guide is designed to support your learning as a distance learning student. It will help

you understand your Foundations of Interior Design and Decorating textbook, stay organised,

and prepare effectively for your assignments.

●  Start with the Module Overview: Each module begins with a short introduction. Read

this first to understand what you will learn and why it is important.

●  Read the Unit Summaries: Each module is divided into units with clear summaries.

Use these to revise or to get an overview before studying the textbook in detail.

●  Focus on Practical Skills and Application: Apply what you learn by focusing on

space planning, colour selection, furniture arrangement, materials, lighting, and design

processes.

●  Highlight Important Concepts: Highlight key topics such as design principles, colour

theory, layouts, materials, lighting, and styling techniques to support revision.

●  Take Notes: Write your own notes, sketches, and examples to help you understand

and remember key ideas.

●  Use It for Assignment Preparation: Use the summaries to guide your answers and

apply your knowledge to real-life design situations.

●  Refer to the Textbook: Use the textbook for detailed explanations, diagrams, and

examples when needed.

●  Manage Your Time: Study one module at a time and create a simple study plan to stay

on track.

●  Be Creative and Reflect: Apply concepts to real spaces around you to deepen your

understanding.

●  Reach Out for Support: If you need help, contact your tutor or support team. Support

is available to help you succeed in your studies.

Using this study guide together with your textbook will help you build a strong understanding of

interior design and decorating. Stay organised, practise regularly, and apply what you learn.

Introduction

This module introduces the basic concepts of interior design and decorating. It explains their

purpose, differences, and importance, as well as the roles, skills, and impact of design in

everyday spaces.

Read page 10 of your textbook for more information.

Unit 1: Understanding Interior Design

This Unit introduces the concept of interior design and explains how it combines creativity and

practical planning to improve spaces.

●  1.1 Definition and Scope of Interior Design: Interior design refers to the process of

planning and improving indoor environments to make them functional, safe, and visually

appealing. It includes layout planning, furniture selection, and material choices across

different types of spaces.

●  1.2 Purpose and Importance of Interior Design: The purpose of interior design is to

enhance how spaces are used and experienced. Well-designed interiors improve

comfort, efficiency, safety, and overall quality of life.

●  1.3 Functional vs Aesthetic Design: A key concept in design is balancing function

and appearance. Functional design ensures a space works well, while aesthetic design

ensures it looks attractive and pleasing.

●  1.4 Interior Design in Everyday South African Spaces: Interior design can be seen

in homes, offices, and public spaces across South Africa. Designers must consider local

conditions such as culture, climate, and available materials.

Read pages 11—16 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

10

Unit 2: Understanding Interior Decorating

Interior decorating focuses on enhancing the visual appeal and atmosphere of a space.

●  2.1 Definition and Scope of Interior Decorating: Decorating involves styling a space

using colours, furniture, and accessories without making structural changes. It works with

existing spaces to improve their look and feel.

●  2.2 Role of Aesthetics in Interiors: Aesthetics play an important role in creating

attractive and comfortable spaces. It influences how a space looks, feels, and affects the

mood of its users.

●  2.3 Decorative Elements and Styling: Styling involves arranging items such as

cushions, rugs, artwork, and plants to create a balanced and visually appealing

environment.

●  2.4 Decorating Across Different Cultures: Cultural influences shape decorating styles,

including the use of colours, patterns, and materials. Designers must respect and reflect

cultural diversity in their work.

Read pages 17—23 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 3: Roles and Responsibilities

This unit explains the different roles within the design field and the skills needed to perform them

effectively.

●  3.1 Role of an Interior Designer: An interior designer plans and organises spaces,

focusing on layout, materials, lighting, and functionality. They often work with other

professionals during projects.

●  3.2 Role of an Interior Decorator: An interior decorator focuses on styling and

improving the appearance of a space by selecting colours, furniture, and decorative

elements.

●  3.3 Skills Required (Creative, Technical, Communication): Success in this field

requires a combination of creativity, technical knowledge, and strong communication

skills to develop ideas and work with clients.

11

●  3.4 Professional Ethics and Client Relationships: Professional behaviour includes

honesty, respect, and clear communication. Building strong client relationships ensures

successful and satisfactory design outcomes.

Read pages 24—29 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 4: Interior Design as a Profession

A career in interior design offers various opportunities across different industries.

●  4.1 Career Opportunities (Freelance, Retail, Corporate): Designers may work

independently, in retail environments, or for companies, depending on their skills and

interests.

●  4.2 Industry Overview in South Africa: The South African design industry includes

residential, commercial, and public projects, with growing demand for modern and

functional spaces.

●  4.3 Working with Clients: Designers must understand client needs, preferences, and

budgets, and communicate ideas clearly to ensure expectations are met.

●  4.4 Introduction to Design Projects: Design projects involve a structured process,

from understanding the brief to developing and presenting a final solution.

Read pages 30—35 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 5: Impact of Interior Spaces

This Unit explores how interior environments influence people and the world around them.

●  5.1 Human Comfort and Wellbeing: Interior spaces affect both physical comfort and

emotional wellbeing through factors such as lighting, layout, and furniture.

●  5.2 Cultural and Social Influences: Design is influenced by cultural traditions, social

values, and lifestyle choices, which shape how spaces are used and decorated.

12

●  5.3 Environmental and Sustainability Awareness: Sustainable design focuses on

reducing environmental impact by using eco-friendly materials and energy-efficient

solutions.

●  5.4 Designing for Diverse Users: Inclusive design ensures that spaces are accessible,

safe, and suitable for people of different ages, abilities, and backgrounds.

Read pages 36—40 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Introduction

Module 2 explains the step-by-step process used to plan, develop, and complete interior design

and decorating projects. It focuses on understanding client needs, applying design methods,

and using tools to present ideas effectively.

Read page 47 of your textbook for more information.

Unit 1: Understanding Client Needs

This unit focuses on gathering important information before starting any design project.

●  1.1 Client Brief and Requirements: A client brief outlines the client’s needs,

preferences, and expectations for the space. It provides clear guidance for the design

process and helps ensure that the final outcome meets the client’s goals.

●  1.2 Budget Planning and Constraints: Budget planning determines what can be

achieved within financial limits. Designers must balance quality and cost by selecting

suitable materials and prioritising key design elements.

●  1.3 Lifestyle and Functional Needs: Design decisions must reflect how the client uses

the space daily. Considering routines, activities, and personal preferences ensures the

space is practical and comfortable.

●  1.4 Site Analysis and Measurements: Site analysis involves studying the space,

including its size, layout, lighting, and features. Accurate measurements are essential to

create correct and functional design plans.

Read pages 48—57 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

15

Unit 2: The Interior Design Process

The interior design process provides a structured approach to completing projects successfully.

●  2.1 Programming and Research: This stage involves collecting information about the

client, the space, and design requirements. Research helps designers make informed

decisions and identify possible challenges.

●  2.2 Concept Development: Designers develop ideas and themes based on research

findings. This includes choosing styles, colours, and the overall direction of the design.

●  2.3 Design Development: At this stage, ideas are refined into detailed plans. Designers

create layouts, select materials, and ensure that all elements work together effectively.

●  2.4 Implementation and Project Execution: The final stage involves carrying out the

design plan. This includes purchasing materials, coordinating work, and completing the

space according to the design.

Read pages 58—62 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 3: The Decorating Process

This unit explains how a space is styled and visually enhanced.

●  3.1 Theme and Style Selection: A theme or style is chosen to guide all decorating

decisions. This ensures consistency and creates a clear visual identity for the space.

●  3.2 Colour and Material Selection: Colours and materials are selected to suit the

purpose and mood of the space. The right combination enhances both comfort and

appearance.

●  3.3 Furniture and Accessories Planning: Furniture and decorative items are chosen

and arranged to support functionality and style. Proper planning prevents overcrowding

and improves usability.

●  3.4 Final Styling and Finishing: Final styling involves making adjustments and adding

finishing touches. This step ensures the space looks complete, balanced, and ready for

use.

Read pages 63—67 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

16

Unit 4: Planning Tools and Techniques

Designers use various tools to organise ideas and visualise spaces.

●  4.1 Mood Boards and Inspiration Boards: Mood boards present selected colours,

textures, and materials, while inspiration boards show a variety of ideas to guide the

design direction.

●  4.2 Bubble Diagrams (Space Planning): Bubble diagrams are simple drawings that

show how different areas of a space relate to each other and how movement flows

between them.

●  4.3 Sketching and Concept Drawings: Sketches are used to quickly visualise ideas

and communicate concepts. They allow designers to explore and refine designs before

finalising them.

●  4.4 Measuring and Recording Spaces: Accurate measurements and records ensure

that designs fit the space correctly and help avoid costly mistakes during

implementation.

Read pages 68—73 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 5: Presenting Design Ideas

Presenting ideas clearly is an important part of the design process.

●  5.1 Design Boards and Layouts: Design boards combine images, colours, and

materials to show the overall concept, while layouts show how furniture and elements

are arranged.

●  5.2 Basic Digital Tools (PowerPoint, simple apps): Digital tools are used to create

organised and professional presentations that are easy for clients to understand.

●  5.3 Client Feedback and Revisions: Client feedback helps improve the design.

Designers make changes based on suggestions to ensure the final design meets

expectations.

●  5.4 Final Presentation Techniques: The final presentation involves clearly explaining

the design, using visuals, and answering questions professionally.

Read pages 74—79 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Introduction

Module 3 introduces the basic design theory used in interior design. It focuses on the elements

and principles of design, as well as colour theory, helping you understand how to create

balanced, attractive, and effective interior spaces.

Read page 86 of your textbook for more information.

Unit 1: Elements of Design

This unit explains the basic building blocks used to create any interior design.

●  1.1 Line: Lines guide the eye and create structure within a space. They can be

horizontal, vertical, or curved, and each type creates a different visual effect.

●  1.2 Shape and Form: Shapes are two-dimensional, while forms are three-dimensional.

Both are used to define objects and spaces in interior design.

●  1.3 Texture: Texture refers to how a surface feels or appears to feel. It adds depth,

interest, and contrast to a space.

●  1.4 Space: Space is the area around and within objects. Good use of space ensures

balance, movement, and functionality.

Read pages 87—91 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 2: Principles of Design

The principles of design explain how the elements are arranged to create a visually pleasing

result.

●  2.1 Balance: Balance refers to the even distribution of visual weight in a space. It can

be symmetrical, asymmetrical, or radial.

●  2.2 Rhythm: Rhythm is created through repetition of elements such as colour, shape,

or pattern, which guides the viewer’s eye through a space.

20

●  2.3 Emphasis: Emphasis creates a focal point that draws attention, such as a feature

wall or statement furniture piece.

●  2.4 Harmony and Proportion: Harmony ensures that all elements work well together,

while proportion ensures that sizes and relationships between objects are appropriate.

Read pages 92—97 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 3: Introduction to Colour Theory

Colour theory helps designers understand how colours work and how to use them effectively.

●  3.1 Colour Wheel: The colour wheel is a visual tool that shows the relationship

between colours and helps in selecting colour combinations.

●  3.2 Primary, Secondary, and Tertiary Colours: Primary colours are the base colours,

secondary colours are created by mixing them, and tertiary colours are combinations of

both.

●  3.3 Colour Properties (Hue, Value, Saturation): Hue refers to the colour itself, value

refers to its lightness or darkness, and saturation refers to its intensity.

●  3.4 Warm and Cool Colours: Warm colours create energy and warmth, while cool

colours create calmness and relaxation.

Read pages 98—103 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 4: Colour Schemes

Different colour schemes are used to create harmony and contrast in design.

●  4.1 Monochromatic: A monochromatic scheme uses one colour in different shades

and tones for a simple and unified look.

●  4.2 Complementary: Complementary colours are opposite each other on the colour

wheel and create strong contrast.

●  4.3 Analogous: Analogous colours are next to each other on the colour wheel and

create a harmonious effect.

21

●  4.4 Triadic: A triadic scheme uses three evenly spaced colours to create balance and

vibrancy.

Read pages 104—110 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 5: Psychological Effects of Colour

This Unit explores how colours influence emotions and behaviour in interior spaces.

●  5.1 Emotional Impact of Colours: Colours can affect feelings, such as calmness,

energy, or warmth.

●  5.2 Colour and Mood in Spaces: The choice of colour can change how a space feels,

making it more relaxing, stimulating, or inviting.

●  5.3 Cultural Colour Meanings: Colours may have different meanings in different

cultures, which must be considered in design.

●  5.4 Application in Interiors: Designers use colour carefully to create the desired mood

and function in a space.

Read pages 111—116 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Introduction

Module 4 focuses on how to organise interior spaces effectively. It covers space planning,

furniture selection, layout design, and basic technical drawings to ensure spaces are both

functional and visually balanced.

Read page 123 of your textbook for more information.

Unit 1: Understanding Space

This unit explains how space is used and organised in interior design to support both function

and movement.

●  1.1 Types of Spaces (Private, Public, Functional): Interior spaces can be classified

based on their use. Private spaces offer privacy, public spaces are shared, and

functional spaces are designed for specific activities.

●  1.2 Circulation and Movement: Circulation refers to how people move through a

space. Good design ensures easy, safe, and comfortable movement between areas.

●  1.3 Spatial Relationships: Spatial relationships describe how different areas within a

space connect and interact. Proper planning ensures that spaces work together

effectively.

●  1.4 Zoning: Zoning involves dividing a space into different functional areas, such as

living, working, or relaxing zones.

Read pages 124—129 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 2: Space Planning Techniques

Various techniques are used to organise space efficiently and improve usability.

●  2.1 Bubble Diagrams: Bubble diagrams are simple visual tools used to show how

different areas relate to each other before creating detailed layouts.

25

●  2.2 Flow and Movement: Designing for flow ensures that movement within a space

is smooth and logical, avoiding obstacles or congestion.

●  2.3 Ergonomics and Comfort: Ergonomics focuses on designing spaces that are

comfortable and easy for people to use based on their physical needs.

●  2.4 Anthropometrics (Human Measurements): Anthropometrics uses human body

measurements to ensure that spaces, furniture, and layouts are suitable and practical.

Read pages 130—135 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 3: Furniture Types and Styles

Furniture plays an important role in both function and design.

●  3.1 Storage Furniture: Storage furniture, such as cupboards and shelves, helps

keep spaces organised and clutter-free.

●  3.2 Seating Furniture: Seating furniture includes chairs, sofas, and benches

designed for comfort and use.

●  3.3 Surface Furniture: Surface furniture, such as tables and desks, provides work

and display areas.

●  3.4 Furniture Styles (Traditional, Modern, Contemporary): Furniture styles vary in

design and appearance, influencing the overall look and feel of a space.

Read pages 136—140 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 4: Furniture Arrangement

This Unit focuses on arranging furniture to create a balanced and functional layout.

●  4.1 Layout Planning: Layout planning involves deciding where furniture should be

placed to maximise space and usability.

●  4.2 Creating Focal Points: A focal point is a central feature that draws attention,

such as a fireplace or a statement piece of furniture.

26

●  4.3 Balance and Proportion: Balance ensures that furniture is evenly distributed, while

proportion ensures that items are the correct size in relation to the space.

●  4.4 Small Space Solutions: Small spaces require creative solutions such as

multi-functional furniture and smart storage to maximise usability.

Read pages 141—145 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

details.
