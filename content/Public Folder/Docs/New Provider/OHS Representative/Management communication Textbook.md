communication is key to building strong relationships, improving teamwork, and achieving
success in the workplace.
In this textbook, you will explore different forms of communication, from written correspondence
to verbal presentations, and learn how to apply these skills effectively. Whether you are
engaging in workplace discussions, writing reports, or delivering presentations, mastering
communication will enhance your professional confidence and efficiency.
Why Study Communication?
Strong communication skills are vital in every industry and profession. Studying communication
will enable you to:
• Express ideas clearly and professionally in written and verbal communication.
• Improve workplace interactions, teamwork, and collaboration.
• Develop confidence in public speaking and presentations.
• Understand how leadership styles influence communication in organisations.
• Apply ethical and responsible communication practices in a business setting.
• Enhance career opportunities by mastering professional communication techniques.
Employers value individuals who can communicate effectively, as it improves productivity and
fosters a positive work environment. By developing these skills, you will be better prepared for
any professional role.

Introduction
Effective workplace communication is essential for maintaining productivity, fostering
collaboration, and ensuring smooth business operations. This module explores key aspects of
communication within an organisation, including corporate culture, leadership styles, and the
role of communication in decision-making and conflict resolution.
You will learn about internal and external communication systems, the impact of information
technology on business communication, and how different organisational structures influence
the flow of information. The module also covers formal and informal communication networks,
management terminology, and the importance of briefings, interventions, and negotiations in
workplace communication.
In addition, this module highlights the significance of work ethics and corporate social
responsibility (CSR) in maintaining professionalism and ethical business practices. By the end
of this module, you will have a solid understanding of how workplace communication functions
and how to apply best practices to enhance workplace efficiency.
1.1 Corporate Culture, Leadership Styles, and Change
Management
An organisation’s success depends on its corporate culture, leadership styles, and ability to
manage change effectively. Corporate culture shapes the values, behaviours, and expectations
within a workplace, while leadership styles influence how employees communicate, collaborate,
and perform their duties.
In today’s fast-paced business world, organisational change is inevitable. Businesses must
adapt to new technologies, market conditions, and internal developments. Effective change
management ensures smooth transitions and minimises resistance from employees.
This section explores how corporate culture, leadership, and change management impact
workplace communication and productivity. Understanding these concepts will help you
navigate different work environments and become a more effective communicator.
1.1.1 Definition of Corporate Culture
Corporate culture is the shared values, beliefs, attitudes, and behaviours that define how an
organisation operates. It influences how employees interact, make decisions, and carry out
tasks. A strong corporate culture fosters teamwork, efficiency, and employee satisfaction, while
a weak culture can lead to confusion and low morale.

The Impact of Corporate Culture on Productivity
A strong corporate culture improves efficiency, teamwork, and employee motivation. In
contrast, a weak culture can lead to low morale, high turnover, and lack of commitment.
Example:
A company that promotes open communication and teamwork will encourage employees to
share ideas, leading to increased innovation and collaboration. Conversely, an organisation
with a toxic culture of fear and micromanagement may experience high absenteeism and low
productivity.

1.1.4 Change Management
Change is a necessary part of business operations, whether due to market shifts, technology
updates, organisational restructuring, or competition. Change management is the structured
approach used to transition individuals, teams, and organisations from a current state to a desired
future state.
Purpose of Change Management
• To minimise resistance to organisational changes.
• To improve adaptability to new technologies and processes.
• To enhance efficiency and competitiveness in the industry.
• To reduce employee uncertainty by clearly communicating goals.

1.2 External Business Communication and Technology
External business communication refers to the exchange of information between an organisation
and external stakeholders such as customers, suppliers, investors, government agencies, and the
public. It plays a crucial role in business success by establishing relationships, maintaining
reputation, and ensuring transparency.
Technology has transformed external communication, allowing businesses to engage with global
audiences instantly through emails, video conferencing, social media, and automated customer
service systems. Understanding external communication methods and their impact on business
operations is essential for professional success.
1.2.1 Definition of External Communication
External communication is the process of transmitting information between an organisation and
external parties. It is used to share business-related information, promote products or services, and
manage public relations.

External communication serves multiple purposes in business operations. Some of the main

shape public perception.
