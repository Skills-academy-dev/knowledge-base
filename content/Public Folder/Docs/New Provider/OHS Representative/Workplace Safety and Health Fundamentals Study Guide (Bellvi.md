How to Use this Study Guide

This study guide is designed to support your learning as a distance learning student. It will help

you understand your Workplace Safety and Health Fundamentals textbook, stay organised,

and prepare effectively for your assignments.

●  Start with the Module Overview: Each module begins with a short introduction. Read

this first to understand what you will learn and why it is important for workplace safety

and health.

●  Read the Unit Summaries: Each module is divided into units with clear summaries.

Use these summaries to revise your work or to get an overview before studying the

textbook in detail.

●  Focus on Practical Skills and Application: This is a practical subject, so it is

important to apply what you learn. Pay attention to hazard identification, risk

assessment, safety procedures, incident reporting, and safe work practices.

●  Highlight Important Concepts: Highlight key topics such as hazards, risks, safety

responsibilities, OHSA, PPE, workplace inspections, and safety culture. This will help

you during revision and when completing assignments.

●  Take Notes: Write your own notes, examples, and explanations. This will help you

understand key concepts and remember important safety processes.

●  Use It for Assignment Preparation: Use the summaries to guide your assignment

answers. Make sure you include all key points and apply your knowledge to real

workplace situations.

●  Refer to the Textbook: Use the textbook for more detailed explanations, diagrams, and

case studies when needed. The study guide supports the textbook, but does not replace

it.

●  Manage Your Time: Work through one module at a time and create a study plan. Allow

enough time to revise and practise applying safety concepts.

●  Reach Out for Support: If you need help, contact your tutor or support team. Support is

available to help you succeed in your studies.

Using this study guide together with your textbook will help you develop a strong understanding

of workplace safety and health. Stay organised, keep practising, and focus on applying what

you learn in real workplace environments.

Introduction

This module provides the foundation for understanding workplace safety and health. It

introduces essential concepts, explains why safety is important, and helps you recognise

hazards, responsibilities, and safe behaviours in different work environments.

Read page 9 of your textbook for more information.

Unit 1: Introduction to Workplace Safety and Health

Workplace safety and health form the basis of all safe working environments. This unit

introduces the core ideas needed to understand how safety is managed and why it is important.

●  1.1 Definition of Occupational Safety and Health

Occupational Safety and Health (OSH) focuses on protecting workers from hazards that

may cause injury or illness. It includes both physical safety and the overall wellbeing of

employees in the workplace.

●  1.2 Scope and Nature of Workplace Safety and Health

The scope of workplace safety includes equipment, working conditions, processes, and

human behaviour. It is a dynamic field that changes as new technologies and industries

develop.

●  1.3 Key Concepts (Hazard, Risk, Incident, Accident)

Understanding these key terms helps you identify and manage workplace dangers. A

hazard is a source of harm, while risk refers to the likelihood of that harm occurring.

●  1.4 The Importance of Safety and Health in Daily Work

Daily tasks become safer when workers follow procedures and remain aware of hazards.

Good safety practices improve productivity and protect workers from harm.

Read pages 9—14 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

9

Unit 2: The Importance of Workplace Safety and Health

This unit explains the different reasons why workplace safety is essential and how it benefits

both individuals and organisations.

●  2.1 Moral Reasons for Safety and Health

Protecting workers is an ethical responsibility. Every employee has the right to work in a

safe environment and return home without injury.

●  2.2 Legal Reasons for Safety and Health

Workplace safety is required by law. Employers must follow regulations such as the

Occupational Health and Safety Act to ensure safe conditions.

●  2.3 Financial and Business Impact

Accidents can result in high costs, including medical expenses and lost productivity.

Effective safety practices help reduce these costs and improve efficiency.

●  2.4 Social and Community Impact

Workplace incidents can affect families and communities by causing financial and

emotional strain. Safe workplaces contribute to stronger and more stable communities.

Read pages 14—18 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 3: Roles and Responsibilities in the Workplace

Safety in the workplace depends on everyone understanding and fulfilling their responsibilities.

Each role contributes to maintaining a safe environment.

●  3.1 Employer Responsibilities

Employers are responsible for providing a safe workplace, including proper equipment,

training, and supervision. They must also ensure compliance with safety laws.

●  3.2 Employee Duties

Employees must follow safety procedures, use equipment correctly, and report hazards.

Their actions play a key role in preventing accidents.

10

●  3.3 Supervisors and Management Roles

Supervisors ensure that safety rules are followed daily. They guide workers, monitor

behaviour, and correct unsafe practices.

●  3.4 Health and Safety Representatives

Health and safety representatives act as a link between workers and management. They

help identify hazards and promote safe working conditions.

Read pages 19—22 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 4: Introduction to Workplace Hazards

This Unit focuses on recognising hazards and understanding how they can affect workers in

different environments.

●  4.1 Types of Hazards (Physical, Chemical, Biological, Ergonomic)

Workplace hazards can take many forms, such as noise, chemicals, bacteria, or poor

posture. Each type requires different control measures.

●  4.2 Sources of Hazards in South African Workplaces

Hazards may come from machinery, environmental conditions, or unsafe behaviour.

Different industries face different types of risks.

●  4.3 Basic Hazard Identification Methods

Hazards can be identified through inspections, observations, and reviewing past

incidents. Early identification helps prevent accidents.

●  4.4 Examples from Different Industries

Different workplaces have unique hazards. For example, construction sites may involve

falling objects, while offices may have ergonomic risks.

Read pages 23—28 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

11

Unit 5: Accidents, Incidents, and Occupational Illnesses

Recognising how incidents occur helps improve safety and prevent future harm.

●  5.1 Definitions and Differences

Accidents result in injury or damage, while incidents may not cause harm but still

indicate risk. Near misses are important warning signs.

●  5.2 Causes of Workplace Incidents

Most incidents are caused by unsafe actions, unsafe conditions, or lack of proper

training and supervision.

●  5.3 Occupational Diseases and Illnesses

Long-term exposure to hazards, such as chemicals or noise, can lead to occupational

illnesses that develop over time.

●  5.4 Reporting and Recording Basics

Reporting incidents ensures that problems are addressed and prevents similar events in

the future. Accurate records are also required for legal compliance.

Read pages 28—32 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 6: Introduction to Safety Culture

A positive safety culture helps create a workplace where safety is a shared priority.

●  6.1 Definition of Safety Culture

Safety culture refers to the attitudes, values, and behaviours that influence how safety is

managed in an organisation.

●  6.2 Characteristics of a Positive Safety Culture

A strong safety culture includes open communication, leadership commitment, and

active participation from employees.

●  6.3 Worker Behaviour and Attitudes

Safe behaviour reduces risks, while unsafe actions increase the likelihood of accidents.

Attitudes towards safety play an important role.

12

●  6.4 Building Awareness in the Workplace

Awareness can be improved through training, communication, and regular reminders.

When workers are aware of risks, they are more likely to act safely.

Read pages 33—37 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Read page 44 of your textbook for more information.

Unit 1: Overview of South African OHS Legislation

Workplace safety in South Africa is guided by laws that protect workers and ensure safe working

conditions. This unit introduces the most important legislation and its purpose.

●  1.1 Occupational Health and Safety Act (OHSA) 85 of 1993

The OHSA is the main law that regulates workplace safety. It requires employers to

provide safe environments and places responsibility on both employers and employees.

●  1.2 Compensation for Occupational Injuries and Diseases Act (COIDA)

COIDA provides financial compensation to workers who are injured or become ill due to

workplace conditions. It ensures support during recovery.

●  1.3 Key Regulations under OHSA

Various regulations support the OHSA by focusing on specific risks such as chemicals,

machinery, and construction safety.

●  1.4 Purpose and Scope of Legislation

These laws aim to prevent accidents, protect workers, and promote safe work practices

across different industries.

Read pages 44—49 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

15

Unit 2: Duties and Responsibilities under the Law

This unit explains what is legally expected from employers, employees, and other stakeholders

in maintaining workplace safety.

●  2.1 Duties of Employers

Employers must provide a safe working environment, proper training, and suitable

equipment. They are legally responsible for managing risks and ensuring safety

compliance.

●  2.2 Duties of Employees

Employees are required to follow safety rules, use equipment correctly, and report

hazards. Their cooperation is essential for maintaining safety.

●  2.3 Duties of Manufacturers and Suppliers

Manufacturers and suppliers must ensure that equipment and materials are safe to use.

They must also provide clear instructions and warnings.

●  2.4 Shared Responsibilities in the Workplace

Safety is a shared responsibility. Effective communication and teamwork between all

parties help reduce risks and improve safety outcomes.

Read pages 50—54 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 3: Workplace Compliance and Documentation

Compliance ensures that organisations follow safety laws and maintain proper safety systems.

This unit focuses on documentation and record keeping.

●  3.1 Safety Policies and Procedures

Safety policies outline the organisation’s commitment to safety, while procedures provide

clear steps for safe work practices.

●  3.2 Legal Documentation and Record Keeping

Accurate records, such as training logs and inspection reports, help demonstrate

compliance and support safety management.

16

●  3.3 Incident Reporting Requirements

All workplace incidents, including near misses, must be reported and documented. This

helps identify risks and prevent future accidents.

●  3.4 Compliance Monitoring

Regular inspections, audits, and reviews are used to ensure that safety rules are

followed and to identify areas for improvement.

Read pages 55—60 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 4: Worker Rights and Protection

Workers are protected by law and have specific rights that support their safety and wellbeing in

the workplace.

●  4.1 Right to a Safe Workplace

Every worker has the right to a safe and healthy working environment where risks are

properly controlled.

●  4.2 Right to Refuse Dangerous Work

Workers can refuse to perform tasks that they believe are unsafe, without fear of

punishment.

●  4.3 Right to Information and Training

Employees must be informed about hazards and receive training to perform their duties

safely.

●  4.4 Protection Against Victimisation

Workers are protected from unfair treatment when they report hazards or raise safety

concerns.

Introduction

This module focuses on identifying workplace hazards and assessing risks to prevent accidents.

It introduces practical methods for recognising dangers, evaluating their impact, and applying

control measures to ensure a safe working environment.

Read page 84 of your textbook for more information.

Unit 1: Hazard Identification Techniques

Recognising hazards is the first step in preventing workplace accidents. This unit explains how

hazards can be identified using different techniques and systems.

●  1.1 Types of Workplace Hazards

Workplace hazards can be physical, chemical, biological, or ergonomic. Understanding

these types helps workers recognise potential dangers in their environment.

●  1.2 Methods of Hazard Identification

Hazards can be identified through inspections, observations, and employee reporting.

Using multiple methods improves accuracy.

●  1.3 Workplace Inspections

Regular inspections involve checking equipment, processes, and environments to

identify unsafe conditions before they cause harm.

●  1.4 Hazard Reporting Systems

Reporting systems allow workers to communicate hazards quickly. These systems

encourage participation and improve safety awareness.

Read pages 84—89 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

21

Unit 2: Understanding Risk and Its Impact

This unit explains what risk is and how it relates to workplace hazards. It also highlights how

risks can affect workers and organisations.

●  2.1 Definition of Risk

Risk refers to the likelihood that a hazard will cause harm and the severity of that harm.

●  2.2 Relationship Between Hazard and Risk

A hazard becomes a risk when there is exposure. The level of risk depends on how often

and how severely workers are exposed.

●  2.3 Risk Factors and Exposure

Risk increases when workers are exposed to hazards for longer periods or under unsafe

conditions.

●  2.4 Examples of Workplace Risks

Real-life examples, such as slippery floors or faulty equipment, help illustrate how risks

occur in different workplaces.

Read pages 89—94 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 3: The Risk Assessment Process

Risk assessment is a structured process used to identify and control risks. This unit explains

each step involved in assessing workplace risks.

●  3.1 Steps in Risk Assessment

The process includes identifying hazards, assessing risks, and deciding on control

measures.

●  3.2 Identifying Who May Be Harmed

Different groups of workers, including vulnerable employees, must be considered during

risk assessment.

●  3.3 Evaluating and Prioritising Risks

Risks are evaluated based on their likelihood and severity, allowing organisations to

prioritise the most serious risks.

22

●  3.4 Recording and Communicating Findings

Findings must be documented and shared to ensure that all workers are aware of risks

and control measures.

Read pages 95—98 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 4: Risk Control Measures

Once risks are identified, control measures must be applied. This unit explains different methods

used to reduce or eliminate risks.

●  4.1 Hierarchy of Controls

The hierarchy of controls ranks safety measures from most effective (elimination) to least

effective (PPE).

●  4.2 Elimination and Substitution

Removing a hazard or replacing it with a safer alternative is the most effective way to

reduce risk.

●  4.3 Engineering and Administrative Controls

Engineering controls modify equipment or processes, while administrative controls

involve procedures and training.

●  4.4 Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

PPE provides protection when other controls are not sufficient. It includes items such as

helmets, gloves, and masks.

Read pages 99—104 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

23

Unit 5: Monitoring and Reviewing Risks

Risk management is an ongoing process. This unit focuses on how risks are monitored and

reviewed to ensure continuous safety.

●  5.1 Continuous Monitoring

Regular monitoring ensures that hazards remain controlled and that new risks are

identified.

●  5.2 Reviewing Risk Assessments

Risk assessments must be reviewed regularly to ensure they remain relevant and

effective.

●  5.3 Updating Control Measures

Control measures should be improved when new risks are identified or when existing

measures are ineffective.

●  5.4 Learning from Incidents

Past incidents provide valuable information that can be used to prevent similar events in

the future.

Read pages 104—108 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Unit 6: Practical Applications of Risk Assessment

Applying risk assessment in real situations helps improve workplace safety. This unit focuses on

practical tools and techniques.

●  6.1 Job Safety Analysis (JSA)

JSA involves breaking down tasks to identify hazards and determine safe working

procedures.

●  6.2 Industry-Based Risk Assessments

Different industries require specific risk assessments based on their unique hazards.

●  6.3 Use of Risk Assessment Tools

Tools such as checklists and risk matrices help evaluate and manage risks effectively.

24

●  6.4 Case-Based Risk Evaluation

Case studies allow learners to apply risk assessment knowledge to real workplace

scenarios.

Read pages 109—113 of your textbook for more information on this unit.

Introduction

This module explains how workplace safety is managed through structured systems, policies,

and procedures. It focuses on how organisations plan, implement, monitor, and improve safety

practices to reduce risks and ensure a safe working environment.

Read page 120 of your textbook for more information.

Unit 1: Safety Management Systems

Safety management systems provide a structured approach to managing workplace safety. This

unit introduces how these systems work and why they are important.

●  1.1 Definition and Purpose

A safety management system is a formal framework used to manage risks and ensure

safe work practices across an organisation.

●  1.2 Key Elements of Safety Systems

These systems include policies, procedures, training, communication, and monitoring to

ensure safety is effectively managed.

●  1.3 Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) Cycle

The PDCA cycle is a continuous improvement process used to plan safety measures,

implement them, check results, and make improvements.

●  1.4 Benefits of Effective Systems

Effective safety systems reduce accidents, improve efficiency, and ensure compliance

with legal requirements.
