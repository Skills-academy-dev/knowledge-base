Assignment 2
Task 1: Materials and Finishes Investigation
Module 4: Materials, Finishes and Construction Systems
Marks: 20
Interior Architects must understand how materials and finishes affect the appearance,
durability, sustainability, and performance of interior spaces.
Instructions
Select one room in your home, workplace, educational institution, or public building.
Examples include:
• Bedroom
• Living room
• Classroom
• Office
• Reception area
• Library
Part A: Materials Audit (12 Marks)
Create a table identifying the following:
Interior Element Material Used
Flooring
Wall Finish
Ceiling Finish
One Furniture Item
For each material identified:
• Name the material. (1)
• Briefly describe its appearance or characteristics. (2)
(3 × 4 = 12)
6

Part B: Material Selection Analysis (4 Marks)
Explain why you think each material was selected for the space.
Consider factors such as:
• Durability
• Cost
• Appearance
• Maintenance
• Comfort (4)
Part C: Sustainability Recommendation (4 Marks)
Choose one material from your audit and recommend a more sustainable alternative.
Explain:
• The alternative material.
• Why is it more environmentally responsible? (4)
Task Total: 20 Marks
Task 2: Environmental Systems and Sustainability Review
Module 5: Environmental Systems and Sustainable Design
Marks: 20
Environmental systems influence comfort, health, energy efficiency, and user experience
within interior spaces.
Instructions
Evaluate an existing room in your home, workplace, or educational institution.
7

Part A: Environmental Assessment (12 Marks)
Assess the following aspects of the room:
Aspect What to Assess Marks
Natural Lighting Describe how natural daylight enters the room and evaluate 3
whether it is sufficient for the activities carried out in the
space.
Artificial Lighting Identify the artificial lighting used and evaluate whether it 3
provides adequate illumination for the room's purpose.
Ventilation and Air Explain how fresh air enters and moves through the room and 3
Circulation evaluate the effectiveness of the ventilation.
Thermal Comfort Assess whether the room is comfortable in terms of 3
temperature and explain the factors that influence comfort
levels.
Part B: Sustainability Evaluation (4 Marks)
Identify and discuss two sustainable features currently present in the room.
Examples:
• Natural daylight
• Opening windows
• LED lighting
• Recycled materials
• Energy-efficient appliances
(2 × 2 = 4)
Part C: Sustainability Improvements (4 Marks)
Recommend two improvements that would make the room more sustainable.
For each improvement:
• Describe the recommendation.
• Explain how it would improve environmental performance.
(2 × 2 = 4)
Task Total: 20 Marks

Task 3: Integrated Interior Architecture Design Proposal
Module 6: Professional Practice, Collaboration and Integrated Design
Marks: 20
This task allows you to apply the knowledge gained throughout the course by developing a
small Interior Architecture proposal.
Scenario
A community centre would like to convert an unused room into a small study and reading
space for learners and community members.
Part A: Project Brief (4 Marks)
Write a short project description explaining:
• The purpose of the space.
• The intended users.
• The activities that will take place in the space. (4)
Part B: Concept Statement (4 Marks)
Write a concept statement of approximately 50–100 words describing your design vision.
Your statement may include:
• Atmosphere
• Functionality
• User comfort
• Sustainability
• Accessibility (4)
Part C: Concept Layout Plan (6 Marks)
Create a simple floor plan or sketch showing the location of:
• Reading tables
• Seating
• Storage
• Lighting
• Circulation paths
All items must be clearly labelled. (6)
9

Part D: Design Justification (6 Marks)
Explain:
1.  Why you selected the proposed furniture and layout. (2)
2.  How the design supports users and activities. (2)
3.  How sustainability principles have been incorporated into the proposal. (2)
Task Total: 20 Marks
