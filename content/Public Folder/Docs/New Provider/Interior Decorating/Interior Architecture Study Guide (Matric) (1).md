Introduction to Interior Architecture Study Guide

Welcome to your Interior Architecture Study Guide!

You are beginning an exciting journey into the world of Interior Architecture. This course

explores how interior spaces are planned, designed, and transformed to improve the way

people live, work, learn, and interact. You will learn about space planning, materials,

construction systems, sustainability, digital technologies, and professional practice. The

course also examines how design decisions influence human experience and wellbeing.

Think of this study guide as your learning companion throughout the course. It works

alongside your textbook to simplify important concepts and summarise key ideas from each

module. The content is organised in a clear and easy-to-follow format to support revision and

independent study. Use the summaries and activities regularly to strengthen your

understanding of the subject.

To succeed in this course, it is important to work through both the textbook and this study

guide carefully. You will need to:

●  Study Modules 1–3 and complete Assignment 1.

●  Study Modules 4–6 and complete Assignment 2.

There are no formal examinations for this course, but both assignments are compulsory.

Once submitted, your assignments will be marked and returned with feedback. This

feedback will help you improve your understanding of Interior Architectural concepts, design

processes, and professional practices.

As you progress through the modules, you will explore topics such as human-centred

design, spatial planning, technical documentation, construction systems, sustainability,

adaptive reuse, and integrated design. The activities and exercises are designed to reinforce

learning and encourage critical thinking. These tasks will help you apply theoretical

knowledge to practical Interior Architectural situations.

Before you begin, make sure you have received:

●  Your Interior Architecture Textbook

●  This Study Guide

●  Assignment 1

●  Assignment 2

Approach this course with confidence, curiosity, and creativity. The knowledge and skills you

develop will help you understand the relationship between people and space while preparing

you for future opportunities in Interior Architecture and the built environment industry.

Introduction

Interior Architecture focuses on creating interior environments that support human activities,

wellbeing, and experiences. It combines design creativity, technical knowledge, and an

understanding of how people interact with space. Interior Architects shape spaces that are

functional, safe, and visually appealing. The discipline is influenced by history, culture,

technology, and environmental factors. Understanding these influences helps designers

create meaningful and effective environments. This module introduces the key concepts that

form the foundation of Interior Architectural practice.

Refer to page 8 of your textbook for more information.

Unit 1: Introduction to Interior Architecture

Interior Architecture is concerned with the planning, design, and transformation of interior

spaces. The discipline combines technical knowledge, creative thinking, and human-centred

design principles. Interior Architects work to improve the functionality, quality, and experience

of interior environments.

Refer to pages 9-15 of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

1.1 Definition, Scope and Identity of Interior Architecture

Interior Architecture focuses on shaping interior environments to improve how people use

and experience space. It combines architectural thinking with design, technology, and

human needs. The discipline addresses both new developments and the adaptation of

existing spaces.

Main Features of Interior Architecture

●  Focuses on the relationship between people and interior spaces.

●

Involves the planning and transformation of environments.

●  Combines functional, aesthetic, and technical considerations.

●  Supports user wellbeing and comfort.

1.2 Interior Architecture vs Interior Design vs Architecture

These disciplines work together but focus on different aspects of the built environment.

Interior Architecture focuses on spatial intervention, Architecture focuses on entire buildings,

and Interior Design focuses on interior aesthetics and furnishings. Understanding these

differences helps clarify professional roles.

9

Differences Between the Disciplines

●  Architecture addresses building design and construction.

●

Interior Architecture focuses on interior spatial organisation.

●

Interior Design focuses on appearance and user experience.

●  Collaboration is common on design projects.

1.3 Roles, Ethics and Responsibilities of Interior Architects

Interior Architects have a responsibility to create safe, accessible, and sustainable

environments. Professional practice requires ethical behaviour and accountability. Designers

must consider both client requirements and user needs.

Professional Responsibilities

●  Protect health and safety.

●  Promote accessibility and inclusion.

●  Follow ethical and professional standards.

●  Consider environmental sustainability.

1.4 Contemporary Interior Architectural Practice

Modern Interior Architecture responds to changing social, environmental, and technological

needs. Designers increasingly focus on flexibility, sustainability, and user-centred solutions.

Technology plays an important role in contemporary practice.

Current Areas of Practice

●  Sustainable design solutions.

●  Adaptive reuse of buildings.

●  Technology-driven environments.

●

Inclusive and flexible spaces.

Introduction

Human-centred design focuses on creating spaces that respond to the needs, behaviours,

and experiences of users. Effective space planning ensures that environments are

functional, comfortable, and easy to navigate. Interior Architects consider circulation,

accessibility, ergonomics, and environmental comfort when developing design solutions.

Different building types require unique planning approaches based on their purpose and

users. Research and programming help designers understand client requirements and

spatial needs before the design process begins. This module explores the principles and

methods used to create people-focused interior environments.

Refer to page 52 of your textbook for more information.
