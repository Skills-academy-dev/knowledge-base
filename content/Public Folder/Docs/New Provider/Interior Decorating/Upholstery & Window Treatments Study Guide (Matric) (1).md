Introduction

Upholstery and window treatments are essential in creating comfortable and attractive

interior spaces. They influence how furniture feels and how a room looks and functions.

Choosing suitable materials and styles improves both usability and design. These elements

also help manage light, privacy, and temperature. Interior design principles guide how

everything works together. Learning these basics will help you build a strong foundation in

interior decorating.

Refer to page 10 of your textbook for more information.

Unit 1: Understanding Upholstery

Upholstery focuses on covering furniture with padding, fabric, or leather to improve comfort

and appearance. It plays a key role in how furniture supports the body and how long it lasts.

Understanding upholstery helps in selecting suitable furniture for different environments.

This unit introduces the core ideas behind upholstered furniture.

Refer to pages 11-14 of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

1.1 Definition and Purpose of Upholstery

Upholstery refers to the process of covering furniture using materials such as fabric, leather,

padding, and springs. Its main purpose is to improve comfort by adding softness and

support. It also protects the furniture frame and extends its lifespan. Upholstery adds visual

appeal through colour, texture, and design.

1.2 Role of Upholstery in Interior Design

Upholstered furniture contributes to the overall style and atmosphere of a room. Colours,

patterns, and textures help create harmony within a space. These elements can highlight a

theme or serve as a focal point. Upholstery also supports functionality by ensuring comfort

and usability.

1.3 Types of Upholstered Furniture

Common upholstered items include sofas, armchairs, ottomans, and beds. Each type serves

a specific purpose depending on the room and use. Designs vary based on comfort,

durability, and style. Materials and construction methods also differ across furniture types.

1.4 Basic Upholstery Terminology

Key terms include frame, padding, springs, webbing, and fabric. These terms describe the

structure and layers of upholstered furniture. Understanding this terminology helps in

communication and learning practical skills. It also supports repair and design work.

Unit 2: Understanding Window Treatments

Window treatments are coverings used to manage light, privacy, and decoration. They play

both a functional and decorative role in interiors. Different types offer different benefits

depending on the space. This unit introduces the main concepts related to window

coverings.

Refer to pages 15-19 of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

2.1 Definition and Purpose of Window Treatments

Window treatments include curtains, blinds, shades, and shutters. They help control the

amount of light entering a room and provide privacy. They can also improve insulation and

reduce heat or glare. In addition, they enhance the overall design of a space.

2.2 Types of Window Coverings

Different window coverings are used based on function and style:

●  Curtains and Drapes: Soft fabric panels that add warmth and decoration

●  Blinds: Adjustable slats for flexible light control

●  Shades: Fabric coverings that roll or fold neatly

●  Shutters: Solid structures offering durability and strong control

●  Layered Treatments: Combination of coverings for improved performance

2.3 Hard vs Soft Window Treatments

Hard treatments include materials such as wood, aluminium, or plastic, often used in blinds

and shutters. Soft treatments are fabric-based, such as curtains and drapes, which add

softness to a room. Each type has its own advantages in terms of durability and style.

Combining both allows better control of light and design.

2.4 Basic Window Treatment Terminology

Common terms include rod, panel, valance, lining, and track. These describe different parts

and features of window treatments. Knowing these terms helps in planning and installation. It

also improves communication in design projects.

Unit 3: Functions of Upholstery and Window Treatments

Upholstery and window treatments serve both practical and decorative purposes. They

improve comfort, control environmental conditions, and enhance appearance. These

functions are important in both homes and commercial spaces. Understanding them helps

create effective interior designs.

3.1 Comfort and Ergonomics

Padding and cushioning provide support and make furniture comfortable to use. Proper

design helps maintain good posture and reduces strain. Ergonomic furniture improves

everyday use and long-term comfort. Materials play a role in how supportive the furniture

feels.

3.2 Privacy and Security

Window treatments help limit visibility from outside, protecting personal space. Adjustable

options allow control over how much can be seen. Heavier fabrics and layered designs

provide more privacy. Covering windows also adds a level of security.

3.3 Light and Temperature Control

Window coverings regulate the amount of natural light entering a room. They reduce glare

and help manage heat from sunlight. Some materials improve insulation by keeping rooms

warm or cool. This contributes to better energy efficiency.

3.4 Aesthetic and Decorative Value

Colours, patterns, and textures enhance the visual appeal of a space. Upholstery and

window treatments help define the overall style of a room. They create mood and

atmosphere through design choices. These elements bring a space together.

4.4 Role of Upholstery and Window Treatments in Design

Introduction

Furniture construction and materials play an important role in the quality of upholstered

items. Strong frames and suitable materials ensure comfort and durability. Different fabrics

and components are used depending on the purpose of the furniture. Sustainability is also

becoming more important in modern design. Designers must consider both performance and

environmental impact when choosing materials. This module helps you understand how

materials and construction affect design and function.

Refer to page 43 of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

Unit 1: Furniture Frames and Construction

Furniture frames form the base of upholstered items and determine their strength and

durability. Construction methods influence how long furniture lasts. Understanding these

structures helps in identifying quality pieces. This unit focuses on how furniture is built and

evaluated.

Refer to pages 44-46 of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

1.1 Types of Furniture Frames

Furniture frames are made from materials such as solid wood, plywood, or metal. Solid wood

frames offer strength and long-term durability, while engineered wood provides a more

affordable option. Metal frames are often used for modern designs and added strength. The

type of frame affects both cost and performance.

1.2 Traditional vs Modern Construction

Traditional construction uses strong joints, hardwood, and detailed craftsmanship. Modern

construction often relies on machines and lighter materials for faster production. Traditional

methods usually offer higher durability, while modern methods focus on efficiency and

affordability. Both approaches are used depending on design needs.

1.3 Frame Strength and Durability

Strong frames support weight and maintain their shape over time. The quality of joints,

materials, and construction methods all influence durability. Weak frames may loosen or

break with regular use. Durable construction ensures long-lasting furniture.

1.4 Identifying Quality Furniture

Quality furniture feels stable, strong, and well-balanced. Tight joints, solid materials, and

good finishing indicate better construction. Poor-quality items may creak, wobble, or feel

lightweight. Careful inspection helps in making better choices.

Unit 2: Upholstery Components and Materials

Upholstery is made up of several layers that work together to provide comfort and support.

Each component plays a specific role in the structure. Understanding these materials helps

in selecting and repairing furniture. This unit focuses on the main upholstery components.

Refer to pages 47-50 of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

2.1 Webbing and Springs

Webbing forms the base support layer and is usually made from strong woven material.

Springs are added to provide flexibility and comfort when sitting. These components work

together to distribute weight evenly. Proper installation ensures long-term support.

2.2 Padding and Cushioning Materials

Padding materials such as foam, fibre, and feathers create softness and shape. Different

materials provide different levels of firmness and comfort. High-quality padding maintains its

shape over time. Choosing the right material improves seating comfort.

2.3 Fabric Layers and Structure

Upholstery is built in layers, starting with a base layer, followed by padding, and finished with

outer fabric. Each layer supports the next and contributes to the final structure. Proper

layering improves durability and appearance. A well-structured design ensures long-lasting

results.

2.4 Modern Upholstery Materials

Modern materials include high-density foam, synthetic fibres, and advanced adhesives.

These materials improve durability and make production more efficient. Many modern fabrics

are also stain-resistant and easy to maintain. Innovation continues to improve upholstery

performance.

Unit 3: Upholstery Fabrics and Leather

Different fabrics and materials are used based on function, style, and durability. Choosing

the right material affects comfort and maintenance. Both natural and synthetic options are

widely used. Leather and its alternatives offer additional choices.

Refer to pages 51-54 of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

17

3.1 Natural Fabrics

Natural fabrics include cotton, linen, wool, and silk. These materials are breathable and

comfortable to use. They often provide a soft and natural appearance. However, they may

require more care and maintenance.

3.2 Synthetic Fabrics

Synthetic fabrics include polyester, nylon, and acrylic. These materials are durable and

resistant to stains and fading. They are suitable for high-use furniture. Maintenance is

usually easier compared to natural fabrics.

3.3 Leather and Alternatives

Genuine leather is strong, durable, and develops character over time. Alternatives such as

faux or bonded leather offer a more affordable option. These materials vary in quality and

lifespan. Choice depends on budget, style, and maintenance needs.

3.4 Fabric Durability and Performance

Fabric performance depends on strength, resistance to wear, and ease of cleaning. High-use

areas require durable materials. Testing methods such as rub counts measure fabric

durability. Choosing the right fabric ensures long-term use.

Unit 4: Sustainable and Eco-Friendly Materials

Sustainability is an important part of modern interior design. Material choices can reduce

environmental impact. Eco-friendly options are becoming more popular. This unit focuses on

responsible material selection.

Refer to pages 55-58 of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

4.1 Sustainable Fabric Options

Sustainable fabrics include organic cotton, linen, and hemp. These materials are produced

with less environmental impact. They are renewable and biodegradable. Choosing these

fabrics supports eco-friendly design.

4.2 Recycled and Low-Impact Materials

Recycled materials reuse existing resources to reduce waste. Low-impact materials are

produced using less energy and fewer chemicals. These options help reduce pollution. They

support sustainable production methods.

18

4.3 Environmental Impact of Materials

Material production can affect the environment through pollution and resource use. Synthetic

materials may take longer to break down. Natural materials are often more environmentally

friendly. Understanding impact helps in making better choices.

4.4 Choosing Sustainable Options for Clients

Clients may prefer eco-friendly solutions based on their values and budget. Designers must

balance sustainability with durability and cost. Clear communication helps clients make

informed decisions. Responsible choices improve long-term impact.

Unit 5: Colour, Pattern, Texture, and Design Application

Design elements such as colour, pattern, and texture influence how furniture looks in a

space. These elements must work together to create a balanced design. Careful selection

improves visual appeal. This unit focuses on applying these elements effectively.

Refer to pages 59-62 of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

5.1 Colour Selection Principles

Colour affects mood and atmosphere in a space. Light colours create a calm feeling, while

bold colours add energy. Colour should match the overall design theme. Proper selection

improves harmony.

5.2 Pattern Types and Scale

Patterns include stripes, florals, and geometric designs. Large patterns stand out, while

small patterns create subtle detail. Pattern scale must suit the size of the furniture. Proper

use prevents visual clutter.

5.3 Texture and Visual Impact

Texture adds depth and interest to furniture. Smooth and rough surfaces create contrast.

Texture also affects how materials feel. Combining textures improves design quality.

5.4 Coordinating Upholstery with Interior Themes

Upholstery should match the overall style of the room. Colours, patterns, and textures must

work together. Consistent design creates a cohesive look. Good coordination enhances the

space.

Unit 6: Evaluating, Repairing, and Preparing Furniture

Furniture must be assessed and prepared before upholstery work begins. Understanding its

condition helps in making repair decisions. Proper preparation improves final results. This

Refer to pages 63-66 of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

6.1 Assessing Furniture Condition

Furniture should be checked for damage, wear, and stability. Frames, padding, and fabric

must be inspected. Identifying issues early helps prevent further damage. Proper

assessment guides repair decisions.

6.2 Repair vs Replacement Decisions

Deciding whether to repair or replace depends on cost and condition. Valuable or strong

furniture is often worth repairing. Poor-quality items may be replaced instead. Practical

decisions save time and money.

6.3 Identifying Structural Issues

Structural problems include loose joints, broken frames, and worn supports. These issues

affect safety and comfort. Repairs must be completed before new upholstery is applied.

Strong structure ensures durability.

6.4 Preparing Furniture for Upholstery

Preparation includes removing old fabric and cleaning the frame. Surfaces must be smooth

and ready for new materials. Proper preparation improves the final finish. Careful work leads

to better results.

Introduction

Upholstery involves a step-by-step process that requires skill, accuracy, and attention to

detail. Each stage, from removing old materials to applying new fabric, affects the final

result. Proper techniques ensure that furniture is both comfortable and durable. Careful

planning helps reduce mistakes and material waste. Practical skills are important for

achieving a professional finish. This module focuses on developing the techniques needed

for successful upholstery work.

Refer to page 73 of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

Unit 1: Removing and Preparing Old Upholstery

Removing old upholstery is the first step before any repair or reupholstering work begins.

Careful removal protects the frame and allows reuse of structural components. Preparation

ensures a clean and stable base for new materials. This unit focuses on safe and effective

removal techniques.

Refer to pages 74-76 of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

1.1 Stripping Fabric Safely

Old fabric is removed carefully to avoid damaging the frame underneath. The process

focuses on working step by step rather than pulling material off quickly. Safe handling
