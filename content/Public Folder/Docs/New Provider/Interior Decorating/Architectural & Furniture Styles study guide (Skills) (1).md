Introduction to the Architectural & Furniture Styles Study Guide

Welcome to your Architectural & Furniture Styles Study Guide! We’re excited to have

you here and you are about to explore the fascinating world of architectural history, furniture

design, and interior decoration. Architectural and furniture styles are important in

understanding how people throughout history designed their homes, public buildings, and

interior spaces. Studying these styles will help you recognise design characteristics,

understand cultural influences, and develop practical interior decorating knowledge that can

be applied in modern spaces.

Think of this study guide as your support tool throughout the course. It works together with

your textbook to help you understand concepts in a simple and structured way. The guide

highlights key ideas, summarises important content, and helps you focus on the most

important information in each module. It is designed to make your learning more

manageable, organised, and effective.

To succeed in this course, it is important to work consistently through both the textbook and

this study guide. You will need to:

●  Study Modules 1–3 and complete Assignment 1, and

●  Study Modules 4–6 and complete Assignment 2.

There are no formal examinations for this course. However, both assignments are

compulsory and must be completed and submitted for assessment. Once submitted, your

assignments will be returned with tutor feedback. This feedback will help you identify areas

for improvement, strengthen your understanding of design styles, and build confidence in

applying architectural and furniture knowledge in practical interior decorating situations.

As you progress through the modules, you will explore important topics such as ancient and

classical styles, Medieval and Renaissance interiors, European decorative movements,

Modern and Contemporary design, furniture history, colour and decorative styling, space

planning, lighting, and professional interior decorating practice. You will also learn how to

analyse interior spaces, identify architectural features, and apply style knowledge in

real-world design contexts. The activities and case studies included in the course will help

you develop practical and professional design skills.

Before you begin studying, make sure you have received:

●  Your Architectural & Furniture Styles Textbook

●  This Study Guide

●  Assignment 1 and Assignment 2

Introduction

Architectural and furniture styles form the foundation of interior decorating and design.

Different societies and historical periods developed unique buildings, furniture, and

decorative traditions that reflected their culture, technology, and lifestyle. Understanding

these styles helps designers recognise important visual features and apply suitable design

ideas in modern interiors. Knowledge of materials, design principles, and construction

methods also supports better planning and creative decision-making. Interior decorators

must learn how spaces, furnishings, colours, and ornamentation work together to create

functional and attractive environments. This module introduces the core concepts and

professional skills needed to study architectural and furniture styles successfully.

Refer to page 9 in your textbook for more information.

Unit 1: Introduction to Architectural & Furniture Styles

Architectural and furniture styles help identify the visual appearance, construction methods,

and decorative features of buildings and furnishings from different historical periods.

Designers study styles to understand how interiors developed over time and how design

influences continue today. Architecture and interiors work together to shape the overall

atmosphere and function of a space. Knowledge of styles also helps interior decorators

make informed design choices for clients and projects.

Refer to page 10-16 in your textbook for more information.

1.1 Definition of Architectural Styles

Architectural styles refer to the distinctive design features used in buildings during specific

historical periods or cultural movements. Note the importance of materials, structure, roof

shapes, windows, columns, and decorative details when identifying styles. Different styles

reflect social, religious, political, and technological influences. Recognise that architectural

styles often influence interior layouts and decoration.

1.2 Definition of Furniture Styles

Furniture styles describe the appearance, construction, materials, and decorative details of

furniture from different periods. Identify features such as carving, upholstery, leg shapes, and

ornamentation when studying furniture styles. Furniture design often reflects the artistic

movements and lifestyles of a specific time. Observe how furniture styles contribute to the

overall character of an interior space.

9

1.3 Relationship Between Architecture and Interiors

Architecture and interiors work together to create balanced and functional spaces. Note how

wall finishes, ceilings, furniture, lighting, and decorative elements complement the

architectural structure. Interior spaces often follow the style and proportions of the building

itself. Successful interiors maintain harmony between architecture and decoration.

1.4 Importance of Style Knowledge in Interior Decorating

Style knowledge helps decorators choose suitable colours, furniture, materials, and layouts

for different projects. Understand how historical styles continue to influence modern interiors

and furniture design. Knowledge of styles also improves communication with clients and

suppliers. Recognising design styles supports better visual analysis and creative planning.

Unit 2: History and Evolution of Interior Spaces

Interior spaces changed over time as societies developed new building methods, materials,

and decorative traditions. Early shelters focused mainly on survival and protection before

interiors became more comfortable and decorative. Cultural exchange and artistic

movements influenced the appearance of homes and furniture across different regions.

Understanding historical development helps designers appreciate the origins of modern

interiors.

Refer to page 17-23 in your textbook for more information.

2.1 Early Human Shelters and Interiors

Early shelters included caves, huts, and simple structures made from natural materials. Note

how people used stone, mud, wood, and animal skins for protection and comfort. Interiors

remained basic and functional during early periods. Simple furniture and storage solutions

developed gradually over time.

2.2 Development of Domestic Interiors

Domestic interiors became more organised as societies advanced and permanent

settlements developed. Identify the growth of separate living, sleeping, cooking, and dining

areas in homes. Decorative finishes, textiles, and furniture became more important in daily

life. Wealth and social status often influenced interior decoration.

10

2.3 Evolution of Decorative Arts

Decorative arts include furniture, ceramics, textiles, metalwork, and surface decoration used

in interiors. Observe how artistic movements influenced interior decoration across different

historical periods. Handcrafted ornamentation and detailed craftsmanship became important

features in many styles. Decorative arts continue to influence contemporary interior design

today.

2.4 Cultural Influences on Design

Different cultures developed unique design traditions based on climate, religion, materials,

and lifestyle. Note how African, Asian, European, and Middle Eastern cultures influenced

architecture and interiors worldwide. Cultural identity often appears through patterns,

colours, furniture, and decorative objects. Designers must respect cultural influences when

planning interior spaces.

Unit 3: Elements and Principles of Design

The elements and principles of design help designers organise interior spaces effectively.

These concepts guide decisions about colour, texture, balance, and spatial arrangement.

Understanding design principles improves creativity and visual communication. Designers

use these principles to create attractive and functional interiors.

Refer to page 24-30 in your textbook for more information.

3.1 Elements of Design

The elements of design include line, shape, form, colour, texture, pattern, light, and space.

Identify how each element affects the appearance and mood of an interior. Lines can create

movement, while colour influences atmosphere and emotion. Texture and pattern add visual

interest and depth.

Important Design Elements

●  Line

●  Colour

●  Texture

●  Shape and form

●  Space and light

11

3.2 Principles of Design

The principles of design guide how elements are arranged within a space. Note the

importance of balance, rhythm, emphasis, proportion, harmony, and contrast. These

principles help create visually pleasing interiors. Designers combine principles carefully to

achieve unity and function.

3.3 Proportion, Scale, and Balance

Proportion refers to the relationship between objects within a space. Scale compares the

size of objects to the room and to people using the space. Balance creates visual stability

through symmetrical, asymmetrical, or radial arrangements. Correct proportion and balance

improve comfort and appearance.

3.4 Harmony and Spatial Composition

Harmony creates a sense of unity between colours, furniture, materials, and decorative

features. Observe how spatial composition affects movement and organisation within

interiors. Well-planned spaces feel comfortable and visually connected. Harmony helps

interiors appear organised and professional.

Unit 4: Materials, Construction, and Ornamentation

Materials and construction methods influence the appearance, strength, and function of

interiors and furniture. Traditional and modern materials provide different textures, colours,

and structural qualities. Ornamentation adds decoration and visual identity to buildings and

furnishings. Designers must also consider sustainability when selecting materials today.

Refer to page 31-36 in your textbook for more information.

4.1 Traditional Building Materials

Traditional materials include wood, stone, clay, brick, marble, and plaster. Note how local

climate and available resources influenced material selection historically. These materials

often provided durability and natural beauty. Many traditional materials remain popular in

contemporary design.

4.2 Furniture Construction Techniques

Furniture construction techniques affect strength, quality, and appearance. Identify methods

such as joinery, carving, bending, veneering, and upholstery. Skilled craftsmanship was

highly valued in historical furniture production. Modern manufacturing now combines

traditional and technological methods.

12

4.3 Decorative Ornamentation

Ornamentation includes carving, mouldings, metalwork, painting, inlays, and decorative

patterns. Observe how ornamentation reflects cultural and historical influences. Some styles

use heavy decoration while others focus on simplicity. Decorative details help define the

identity of a design style.

4.4 Sustainable and Contemporary Materials

Modern designers increasingly use sustainable materials to reduce environmental impact.

Note the use of recycled materials, engineered wood, bamboo, and energy-efficient products

in contemporary interiors. Sustainable design focuses on durability, safety, and responsible

production. Environmental awareness now plays an important role in design practice.

Unit 5: Design Theory and Visual Analysis

Design theory helps designers understand how interiors communicate meaning and visual

impact. Visual analysis allows decorators to identify styles, materials, and design features

accurately. Careful observation improves creative thinking and professional judgement.

Designers use analysis skills to evaluate both historical and contemporary interiors.

Refer to page 37-41 in your textbook for more information.

5.1 Design Theory and Interpretation

Design theory explains how people understand and respond to visual environments. Note

how colour, form, symbolism, and layout influence emotions and behaviour. Different

theories guide creative approaches and design decisions. Interpretation helps designers

communicate ideas more effectively.

5.2 Visual Literacy and Style Identification

Visual literacy refers to the ability to interpret and understand visual information. Identify

style characteristics through observation of furniture, materials, ornamentation, and

architectural details. Strong visual literacy improves design research and communication

skills. Designers regularly use photographs, sketches, and drawings during analysis.

5.3 Reading Interior Spaces

Interior spaces communicate information through layout, colour, lighting, and furniture

arrangement. Observe how movement, function, and atmosphere influence user experience.

Different spaces create different emotional and practical responses. Designers study

interiors carefully before making design decisions.

13

5.4 Analysing Architectural Features

Architectural analysis focuses on structure, proportion, materials, and decorative details.

Note the importance of doors, windows, columns, ceilings, and floor plans when identifying

styles. Architectural features often reveal the historical period and purpose of a building.

Careful analysis improves design understanding and style recognition.

Unit 6: Research and Professional Design Skills

Research and presentation skills are important in professional interior decorating and design

practice. Designers use research to develop ideas, understand client needs, and explore

style influences. Organised presentation methods help communicate concepts clearly.

Professional portfolios also support career development and creative growth.

Refer to page 42-47 in your textbook for more information.

6.1 Design Research Methods

Design research includes books, journals, case studies, online resources, and visual

references. Note the importance of collecting reliable and relevant information during

projects. Research helps designers understand historical styles and current trends. Good

research improves planning and creativity.

6.2 Mood Boards and Concept Development

Mood boards help organise colours, textures, furniture, materials, and visual inspiration.

Identify how concept boards communicate the overall design direction for a project.

Designers use sketches, samples, and images to present ideas clearly. Concept

development supports creative planning and client communication.

6.3 Academic Referencing and Research Ethics

Academic referencing gives credit to original authors and sources. Note the importance of

honesty, accuracy, and avoiding plagiarism in research work. Ethical research strengthens

professionalism and academic quality. Correct referencing also improves the credibility of

design projects.

6.4 Portfolio Development Fundamentals

A portfolio presents a designer’s skills, creativity, and project experience. Include sketches,

photographs, mood boards, drawings, and research examples in a professional portfolio.

Well-organised portfolios help designers communicate their abilities effectively. Portfolio

development is important for both study and career opportunities.

Introduction

Ancient and early world styles laid the foundation for many architectural and interior design

traditions used today. Civilisations such as the Egyptians, Greeks, Romans, Byzantines, and

various African and Asian cultures developed distinctive approaches to buildings, furniture,

decoration, and spatial planning. These styles reflected religious beliefs, climate, available

materials, social systems, and artistic traditions. Many historical design features, such as

columns, arches, mosaics, geometric patterns, and handcrafted furniture, continue to

influence modern interiors. Understanding these early styles helps designers recognise

cultural influences and appreciate the historical development of interior spaces. This module

explores important ancient and early world design traditions and their lasting impact on

contemporary design practice.

Refer to page 53 in your textbook for more information.

Unit 1: Egyptian Style

Egyptian design is one of the earliest recorded architectural and decorative traditions in

history. Egyptian interiors and buildings reflected religious beliefs, power, and strong

symbolic meaning. Large stone structures, decorative carvings, and painted surfaces

became important design features. Egyptian influences continue to appear in furniture,

ornamentation, and luxury interiors today.

Refer to page 54-60 in your textbook for more information.

1.1 Egyptian Architecture

Egyptian architecture is known for pyramids, temples, tombs, and monumental stone

construction. Note the use of large columns, massive walls, and symmetrical layouts in

Egyptian buildings. Architecture often focused on religion, royalty, and the afterlife. Stone

was the primary building material because of its durability.

1.2 Egyptian Furniture and Decoration

Egyptian furniture included stools, chairs, beds, chests, and tables made from wood and

decorated with carvings or inlays. Identify the use of animal motifs, gold finishes, and

decorative patterns in furniture design. Wealthy Egyptians used luxurious materials such as

ebony, ivory, and linen. Furniture was often carefully crafted and highly decorative.

17

1.3 Symbolism, Colour, and Ornamentation

Egyptian decoration used symbols connected to religion, nature, and royalty. Observe the

use of lotus flowers, scarabs, falcons, and hieroglyphics in ornamentation. Colours such as

gold, blue, red, and black carried symbolic meaning. Decorative surfaces often included

murals, carvings, and painted reliefs.

1.4 Egyptian Revival in Modern Interiors

Egyptian Revival styles became popular during the nineteenth and twentieth centuries. Note

the continued use of columns, geometric decoration, and luxurious finishes inspired by

ancient Egypt. Modern interiors sometimes use Egyptian motifs to create dramatic and

elegant spaces. Egyptian-inspired furniture and accessories remain popular in luxury design.

Unit 2: Greek Style

Greek design focused on balance, proportion, harmony, and simplicity. Greek architecture

introduced important column systems and mathematical design principles that influenced

Western architecture for centuries. Interiors remained elegant and functional with carefully

planned proportions. Greek furniture and decoration reflected order and craftsmanship.

Refer to page 61-66 in your textbook for more information.

2.1 Greek Architectural Orders

Greek architecture is famous for the Doric, Ionic, and Corinthian column orders. Identify the

differences in column shape, decoration, and proportions between these orders. Greek

temples used symmetry and balance to create visual harmony. Columns became one of the

most influential architectural features in history.

●  Corinthian

2.2 Greek Domestic Interiors

Greek homes often included open courtyards, simple furnishings, and painted walls. Note

the importance of natural light, airflow, and functional space planning in Greek interiors.

Decorative pottery and textiles added colour and pattern to interiors. Domestic spaces

focused on comfort and practicality.

18

2.3 Greek Furniture Forms

Greek furniture included stools, couches, benches, and elegant chairs with curved forms.

Observe the use of carved wood, bronze details, and balanced proportions in furniture

design. Furniture remained lightweight and functional. Greek forms later influenced Roman

and neoclassical furniture styles.

2.4 Classical Proportion and Symmetry

Greek designers used mathematical proportions to achieve visual balance and beauty.

Understand how symmetry and proportion created harmony in architecture and interiors.

These principles continue to guide modern design practice today. Classical proportion

remains important in interior planning and furniture arrangement.

Unit 3: Roman Style

Roman design combined Greek influences with advanced engineering and large-scale

construction. Roman interiors became more luxurious and decorative over time. Public

buildings, villas, baths, and entertainment spaces demonstrated Roman wealth and power.

Roman innovations strongly influenced later Western architecture and interiors.

Refer to page 67-72 in your textbook for more information.

3.1 Roman Architecture and Engineering

Roman architecture introduced arches, vaults, domes, and concrete construction. Note the

importance of roads, aqueducts, amphitheatres, and public baths in Roman engineering.

Roman builders focused on durability and large-scale construction. Architectural innovation

allowed the Romans to create complex structures.

3.2 Roman Interior Spaces

Roman interiors included villas, courtyards, bathhouses, and richly decorated rooms. Identify

the use of mosaics, frescoes, marble, and columns in interior decoration. Wealthy Romans

created luxurious interiors filled with art and decorative objects. Public and private spaces

often reflected social status.

3.3 Roman Furniture and Decorative Arts

Roman furniture included couches, tables, cabinets, and decorative seating. Observe the

use of marble, bronze, carved wood, and upholstered surfaces in furniture production.

Decorative arts included mosaics, sculpture, pottery, and wall paintings. Roman interiors

combined comfort with visual richness.

19

3.4 Roman Influence on Western Design

Roman architecture and decoration strongly influenced Renaissance, neoclassical, and

modern design. Note the continued use of arches, domes, columns, and classical

ornamentation in Western architecture. Roman planning principles also influenced public

buildings and urban design. Many classical interior features originated from Roman

traditions.

Unit 4: Byzantine and Early Christian Styles

Byzantine and Early Christian styles focused mainly on religious architecture and sacred

interiors. Interiors became richly decorated with mosaics, domes, and symbolic imagery.

Religious symbolism strongly influenced colour, materials, and decorative surfaces. These

styles created visually dramatic spiritual environments.

Refer to page 73-78 in your textbook for more information.

4.1 Religious Architecture and Symbolism

Early Christian and Byzantine architecture focused on churches, basilicas, and domed

structures. Note the importance of spiritual symbolism in building layouts and decoration.

Domes often represented heaven and divine authority. Religious spaces were designed to

inspire worship and reflection.

4.2 Mosaics and Decorative Surfaces

Mosaics became one of the most important decorative features in Byzantine interiors.

Identify the use of coloured glass, stone, and gold tiles in walls and ceilings. Decorative

surfaces often displayed religious figures and symbolic patterns. Rich decorations created

dramatic visual effects.

4.3 Furniture and Sacred Interiors

Furniture in sacred interiors included thrones, altars, screens, and ceremonial seating.

Observe the use of carved wood, gold finishes, and religious symbols in furniture design.

Sacred interiors focused more on symbolism than domestic comfort. Decorative

craftsmanship played an important role in religious spaces.

4.4 Colour and Material Use

Byzantine interiors used rich colours such as gold, blue, purple, and red. Note the use of

marble, mosaics, silk, and precious materials in decoration. Colour symbolised spirituality,

wealth, and religious authority. Luxurious surfaces created visually impressive interiors.
