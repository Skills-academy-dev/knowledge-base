Assignment 1

1. A coastal resort has strong social-media engagement, but repeat bookings are

falling because the delivered experience does not match its promotional promise.

Which response best reflects strategic tourism marketing?

A. Align employee training, service delivery and marketing promises around a clear value

proposition.

B. Increase posting frequency to maintain visibility.

C. Introduce a short-term price discount before reviewing operations.

D. Transfer the entire marketing budget to influencer partnerships.

2. A destination organisation must decide whether to enter a new international

market. Which evidence set provides the strongest basis for a strategic decision?

A. The marketing manager’s intuition and last year’s visitor total.

B. Competitors’ advertisements and the destination’s current follower count.

C. A single survey of recent visitors and national arrival statistics.

D. Market attractiveness, organisational capability, competitor intensity and long-term

strategic fit.

3. Which situation most clearly represents the creation of customer, business and

destination value at the same time?

A. A community-guided heritage experience gives visitors meaningful learning, earns

sustainable income and supports local conservation.

B. A hotel reduces room rates below cost to increase occupancy for one month.

C. A tour operator replaces local suppliers with cheaper imports to improve its margin.

D. A destination attracts more visitors while local residents carry the environmental costs.

4. A lodge’s managers repeatedly copy competitors’ campaigns without examining

their own capabilities or target market. Which strategic weakness is most evident?

A. Excessive market orientation.

B. Overinvestment in scenario planning.

C. An overly differentiated product portfolio.

D. An absence of strategic orientation and organisational fit.

6

5. A new visa restriction reduces arrivals from a lodge’s main overseas market. In a

strategic environmental analysis, this development should primarily be treated as:

A. An internal weakness caused by poor employee performance.

B. an external political factor whose implications must be assessed against the lodge’s

market dependence.

C. proof that all international marketing should permanently stop.

D. a tactical pricing issue that requires no change in market strategy.

6. Which analysis most effectively converts a macro-environmental trend into a

strategic implication?

A. Listing inflation as an economic factor without assessing its consequences.

B. Comparing the organisation’s logo with those of its competitors.

C. Measuring employee satisfaction independently of market conditions.

D. Estimating how rising transport costs may alter demand, segment profitability and product

design.

7. A manager identifies hotels, self-catering apartments and visiting friends and

relatives as alternatives competing for a traveller’s holiday budget. What does this

demonstrate?

A. Tourism competition includes direct competitors, substitutes and alternative uses of

customer resources.

B. Competition exists only between businesses in the same formal category.

C. Suppliers are always more influential than customers.

D. Macro-environmental forces are the only source of competitive pressure.

8. What is the most defensible use of scenario analysis in an uncertain tourism

market?

A. To predict one future accurately enough to eliminate risk.

B. To replace environmental scanning with managerial judgement.

C. To prepare coherent responses for several plausible market conditions.

D. To justify continuing the existing strategy regardless of change.

7

9. A KwaZulu-Natal operator develops alternative plans for strong international

demand, weak international demand and faster domestic growth. The main strategic

benefit is that the operator can:

A. adapt resource allocation and marketing actions more quickly as conditions develop.

B. guarantee that each scenario will occur in the planned sequence.

C. avoid monitoring market signals once the plans are approved.

D. use the same marketing mix in every possible future.

10. A small cultural-tour business has expert local guides, trusted community

relationships and experiences that competitors cannot readily reproduce. Which

competitive strategy best exploits these resources?

A. A broad cost-leadership strategy based on continuous price cutting.

B. An undifferentiated strategy aimed at every visitor segment.

C. A focused differentiation strategy aimed at travellers who value authentic, expert-led

experiences.

D. A distribution-only strategy that delegates the experience to intermediaries.

11. Which benchmarking practice is strategically sound?

A. Copying the highest-rated competitor’s packages in full.

B. Comparing only prices because service quality is difficult to measure.

C. Treating current competitor performance as a permanent industry standard.

D. Comparing relevant performance dimensions, identifying capability gaps and adapting

lessons to the organisation’s position.

12. A once-distinctive online-booking facility is now offered by nearly every

competitor. What is the strongest conclusion?

A. The facility should be removed because it no longer differentiates the business.

B. Technology can never provide competitive advantage in tourism.

C. The feature has become a market expectation, so differentiation must be renewed

through harder-to-copy value.

D. The organisation should increase prices to signal that its booking facility remains superior.

8

13. Which action is most likely to sustain a service-based competitive advantage?

A. Reducing training so that competitors cannot recruit experienced employees.

B. Embedding specialist knowledge in recruitment, training, culture and consistent operating

systems.

C. Relying on one highly skilled guide without transferring knowledge.

D. Changing the value proposition whenever a competitor launches a campaign.

14. A destination wants to reduce its dependence on one peak international season.

Which option is a strategic decision rather than a tactical action?

A. Publishing three social-media posts about winter activities.

B. Offering a two-week discount through one travel agent.

C. Changing the wording of an email subject line.

D. Prioritising the domestic short-break market to diversify demand across the year.

15. Which objective is most suitable for strategic marketing control?

A. Increase direct domestic off-season bookings by 15% within 12 months while maintaining

the target contribution margin.

B. Improve awareness among everyone as soon as possible.

C. Become the best-known lodge in Southern Africa.

D. Post more frequently than competitors during the next season.

16. A tour operator has limited funds and three possible target markets. What is the

best strategic resource-allocation principle?

A. Divide the budget equally to demonstrate fairness among segments.

B. Fund the largest market even if the operator lacks the capabilities to serve it.

C. Concentrate resources where market attractiveness, strategic fit and expected value are

strongest.

D. Select the cheapest media channels before defining objectives.

17. Bookings increase after a campaign, but profit declines and complaints rise.

Which control response is most appropriate?

A. Evaluate a balanced set of financial, customer, operational and brand indicators before

adapting the strategy.

B. Declare the campaign successful because bookings increased.

C. Ignore complaints until the annual planning cycle.

D. Increase campaign spending to preserve momentum.

9

18. A wellness resort reports “weak performance” and immediately launches a visitor

survey. What should management do first to improve the research design?

A. Select a larger sample before deciding what information is needed.

B. Collect competitor prices because they are easy to quantify.

C. Combine all available data without distinguishing the decision to be made.

D. Define the management problem and convert it into precise research objectives.

19. Which research objective is the most strategically useful?

A. Find out whether customers like the website.

B. Collect as many opinions as possible about tourism.

C. Determine which functional, emotional and service barriers cause mobile users to

abandon direct bookings before payment.

D. Prove that the redesigned booking page will increase revenue.

20. A researcher needs to estimate how common booking abandonment is and

understand why travellers abandon. Which research design is strongest?

A. Only qualitative interviews, because numerical prevalence is irrelevant.

B. A mixed-method design combining behavioural or survey data with qualitative enquiry.

C. Only website analytics, because observed behaviour explains motivation.

D. A staff workshop followed by management intuition.

21. A survey about off-season travel is conducted only among guests staying during

the December peak. What is the most serious threat to the findings?

A. The sample may not represent the market relevant to the decision.

B. The survey contains numerical questions.

C. The organisation used primary rather than secondary data.

D. The data cannot be analysed using any statistical method.

22. What distinguishes a marketing information system from an occasional research

project?

A. It relies exclusively on external syndicated research.

B. It systematically integrates ongoing internal and external information for recurring

decisions.

C. It is designed only for senior managers’ annual planning.

D. It eliminates the need for interpretation by automatically selecting a strategy.

10

23. A hotel combines booking patterns, website behaviour, guest feedback and

competitor rates. What is the principal advantage of this integration?

A. Relationships across demand, behaviour, experience and competition can reveal more

actionable patterns.

B. Each data source becomes accurate simply because it is combined.

C. Competitor information can substitute for customer information.

D. Managers no longer need to define the decision context.

24. Bookings decline while competitors’ bookings rise and reviews increasingly

mention slow confirmation. Which interpretation is most defensible?

A. Competitor growth proves that the destination’s total demand is declining.

B. The combined evidence suggests that booking convenience may be weakening

competitiveness and should be investigated.

C. Slow confirmation is certainly the sole cause of every lost booking.

D. The organisation should reduce prices before testing the booking process.

25. Customers who book cultural tours also tend to spend more on local food

experiences. Why should the operator avoid immediately claiming that cultural tours

cause higher food spending?

A. Other characteristics may influence both behaviours, so correlation alone does not

establish causation.

B. Customer spending cannot be analysed quantitatively.

C. Cross-selling is inappropriate in experience design.

D. The relationship is useful only if competitors observe it too.

26. A traveller selects an eco-lodge partly because friends approve, the brand fits the

traveller’s self-image and environmental responsibility is personally important. Which

explanation is strongest?

A. The decision is explained entirely by disposable income.

B. The traveller has skipped the information-search stage.

C. The decision reflects interacting social, psychological and value-based influences.

D. Cultural and social influences cannot affect individual travel decisions.

11

27. A customer discovers a destination on social media, checks reviews on a mobile

device, compares prices on an OTA and books directly using a laptop. What should

the marketer infer?

A. The final booking channel deserves all marketing credit.

B. OTAs have no role because the final purchase was direct.

C. Social media should be evaluated only through direct conversions.

D. The journey is cross-device and multi-touch, requiring consistent information and careful

attribution.

28. Which action best addresses the evaluation stage of the tourism customer

journey?

A. Sending a generic awareness advertisement after the trip.

B. Providing credible reviews, transparent comparisons and clear evidence of value and fit.

C. Removing detailed information to shorten the website.

D. Focusing exclusively on loyalty rewards for past guests.

29. Travellers increasingly value smaller groups, local interaction and credible

sustainability practices. What is the strongest strategic response?

A. Use sustainability language without changing operations.

B. Assume the change applies equally to all market segments.

C. Validate the pattern in priority segments, redesign relevant experiences and provide

verifiable evidence.

D. Replace all existing products immediately.

30. Why is post-purchase behaviour strategically important in tourism?

A. It matters only when a refund is requested.

B. It shapes satisfaction, reviews, advocacy, complaints and repeat purchases, which

influence future demand.

C. It occurs after revenue is earned and therefore falls outside marketing.

D. It is controlled entirely by digital platforms.

12

31. Which segment definition is most actionable for a premium weekend nature

retreat?

A. All adults who enjoy travel.

B. Visitors aged 18 to 65 from any location and income group.

C. People who liked mountain photography online.

D. South African professionals seeking short restorative nature breaks, valuing convenience

and willing to pay for guided quality.

32. A segment is large and growing, but reaching it is expensive, competitors are

entrenched and the organisation lacks the expected service capabilities. What should

management conclude?

A. Size and growth automatically outweigh all other considerations.

B. The segment should be targeted before competitors react.

C. Attractiveness must be discounted because accessibility, competitive intensity and

organisational fit are weak.

D. The organisation should target every segment to spread risk.

33. What is the main strategic purpose of a perceptual map?

A. To compare customer perceptions of competing offerings on relevant dimensions and

identify positioning opportunities.

B. To calculate the exact profitability of each booking channel.

C. To determine customer demographics without primary research.

D. To display management’s desired identity regardless of customer opinion.

34. A guesthouse is perceived as a low-cost overnight stop but wants to attract

higher-value leisure breaks. Which repositioning approach is most credible?

A. Adopt a luxury slogan while keeping every element of the experience unchanged.

B. Increase the price immediately so customers infer premium quality.

C. Avoid researching current perceptions because they may delay the campaign.

D. Change the product experience and supporting evidence before consistently

communicating the new value position.

13

35. A mountain resort relies heavily on hiking, which creates seasonal vulnerability.

Which portfolio decision offers the strongest strategic logic?

A. Remove hiking because dependence on one product is always unacceptable.

B. Add unrelated products whenever competitors introduce them.

C. Develop complementary wellness, cultural or indoor experiences that fit target needs and

organisational capabilities.

D. Discount hiking throughout the year rather than evaluate the portfolio.

36. Which statement best captures advanced tourism experience design?

A. It manages the customer journey before, during and after the visit so that touchpoints

deliver coherent functional and emotional value.

B. It concentrates on the physical core product because supporting services do not affect

memory.

C. It adds as many activities as possible, irrespective of target-market relevance.

D. It treats promotion as the experience and operations as a separate responsibility.

37. An established sightseeing tour faces declining demand, but research shows

continuing interest in its theme and dissatisfaction with its format. What is the

strongest product-life-cycle response?

A. Withdraw immediately because all mature products inevitably fail.

B. Rejuvenate the offering through evidence-based redesign before deciding whether

withdrawal is necessary.

C. Increase capacity before changing the experience.

D. Keep the tour unchanged and intensify advertising.

38. A cultural village-tour operator considers launching an unrelated luxury transport

service in a new market. This is best classified as:

A. diversification requires careful assessment of market knowledge and capability fit.

B. market penetration with low strategic risk.

C. product development for the existing target market.

D. market development using the current product portfolio.

14

39. A lodge enjoys high awareness, strong quality associations, repeat guests and

customers’ willingness to pay a premium. Collectively, these assets indicate:

A. A narrow distribution network.

B. temporary promotional effectiveness only.

C. strong brand equity with strategic commercial value.

D. a multiple-brand architecture.

40. An accommodation group operates a premium safari lodge and a budget urban

hostel for distinct segments. Which brand-architecture issue is most important?

A. Every property must use the same name and price.

B. The relationship between corporate and property brands must create clarity without

transferring conflicting associations.

C. The group must avoid any connection between the properties.

D. Brand architecture has no effect on customer expectations.

41. A tour company promises “expert, safe and welcoming adventures”, but guides

apply inconsistent safety and service standards. What is the core brand problem?

A. Brand awareness is too high for the company’s capacity.

B. The brand personality is necessarily inappropriate for adventure tourism.

C. The company needs more sub-brands rather than operational alignment.

D. Brand identity and communication are not being translated into a consistent brand

experience.

42. Which response best protects brand reputation after a credible service failure

becomes visible online?

A. Delete criticism wherever possible and increase promotional volume.

B. Respond promptly, acknowledge the issue, correct the cause and demonstrate consistent

recovery actions.

C. Change the logo so that customers separate the failure from the brand.

D. Wait until attention disappears before contacting affected guests.

15

43. A luxury safari lodge prices primarily by adding a standard percentage to

operating costs, despite guests valuing rare access, expertise and exclusivity. Which

limitation is most significant?

A. Cost information is never relevant to pricing.

B. Premium customers always prefer the highest available price.

C. The lodge cannot use seasonal prices if it considers costs.

D. The approach may ignore perceived value and willingness to pay.

44. A hotel changes rates as demand, remaining capacity, booking time and market

conditions change. This is an example of:

A. dynamic pricing used as part of revenue management.

B. cost-plus pricing with a fixed margin.

C. undifferentiated pricing that ignores capacity.

D. brand repositioning through distribution.

45. Demand is high during school holidays but weak on selected midweek dates.

Which action best supports yield optimization?

A. Apply the same discount to all dates and segments.

B. Use targeted midweek offers and suitable packages while protecting high-demand-period

rates.

C. Close direct channels during low-demand periods.

D. Maximise occupancy regardless of price and contribution.

46. Why can indiscriminate discounting damage long-term tourism performance?

A. It always reduces total demand.

B. It prevents any use of intermediaries.

C. It converts a fixed-capacity service into a storable product.

D. It may weaken reference prices, brand perceptions and contribution without addressing

the actual demand problem.

16

47. A small lodge uses its website for direct domestic bookings and selected OTAs for

overseas reach. Which channel principle does this illustrate?

A. All channels should target identical customers on identical terms.

B. Direct channels make intermediary performance irrelevant.

C. A channel mix can allocate different roles according to reach, customer access, cost and

strategic control.

D. The lowest-commission channel should receive every room.

48. An OTA supplies significant demand but charges commission and limits access to

customer information. What is the strongest managerial response?

A. Exit the OTA immediately because commission always destroys value.

B. Evaluate the OTA’s incremental reach and net value while strengthening an attractive

direct-booking capability.

C. Depend entirely on the OTA because it provides market access.

D. Offer lower public prices on every other channel without checking agreements or

profitability.

49. Which direct-booking proposition is strategically strongest?

A. “Book direct because intermediaries are untrustworthy.”

B. A direct price that changes after the customer enters payment details.

C. A simple, secure process with clear value, reliable support and relevant direct benefits.

D. A website that hides cancellation terms to reduce comparison.

50. A hotel compares channels only by gross booking revenue. Which additional

analysis is most necessary?

A. The number of advertisements used by each channel.

B. Net contribution, customer acquisition cost, cancellation behaviour, market reach and

customer relationship value.

C. The age of the channel’s technology provider.

D. Whether the channel is classified as traditional or digital.
