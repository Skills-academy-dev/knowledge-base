Assignment 1

1. Which statement best describes a tourism product?

A. A physical item that a visitor takes home after travelling

B.  A  combination  of  goods,  services,  attractions,  activities  and  experiences  that  satisfies

visitor needs

C. Accommodation provided by hotels only

D. An advertisement designed to attract tourists

2.  A  Cape  Winelands  holiday  includes  accommodation,  wine  tasting,  local  cuisine,

scenic attractions and guided tours. What does this example demonstrate?

A. A tourism product consists mainly of accommodation.

B. Tourism products should be marketed individually.

C. Attractions are more important than services.

D. A tourism product often combines several components into a complete visitor experience.

3.  A  private  game  reserve  introduces  guided  night  safaris  after  customer  feedback

shows  strong

interest

in  new  wildlife  experiences.  Which  activity

is  being

demonstrated?

A. Product development and innovation

B. Market withdrawal

C. Crisis communication

D. Distribution management

4. A tourism attraction has reached a stage where visitor numbers have stabilised and

competition has increased. According to the Product Life Cycle, which stage is this?

A. Introduction

B. Growth

C. Maturity

D. Decline

5.  Which  action  would  most  likely  help  extend  the  life of a tourism product that has

reached maturity?

A. Stop monitoring visitor preferences.

B. Introduce new activities or improve the existing visitor experience.

C. Remove all promotional activity.

D. Keep the product unchanged regardless of competition.

6

6. Why is branding important to a tourism organisation?

A. It helps create a distinctive identity and differentiate the offering from competitors.

B. It guarantees that customers will pay any price.

C. It removes the need for quality service.

D. It prevents competitors from entering the market.

7. Which statement best describes a tourism brand?

A. Only the logo used by a tourism business

B. Only the name of a tourism destination

C. A legal document registering a tourism business

D.  The  image,  reputation,  identity,  experiences and expectations associated with a tourism

offering

8.  A  lodge  wants  customers  to  perceive  it  as  an  environmentally  responsible,

high-quality safari business. This desired perception is most closely related to:

A. package pricing.

B. physical evidence.

C. brand positioning.

D. sales promotion.

9. Which action is most likely to strengthen a tourism brand over time?

A. Delivering consistent quality and keeping the brand promise

B. Changing the brand message every week

C. Ignoring customer reviews

D. Promising services that cannot consistently be delivered

10. Which list correctly identifies the seven elements of the tourism marketing mix?

A. Product, Price, Place, Positioning, People, Process and Profit

B. Product, Price, Place, Promotion, People, Process and Physical Evidence

C. Product, Planning, Promotion, People, Profit, Process and Positioning

D. Product, Price, Promotion, Planning, People, Packaging and Performance

11.  A  resort  introduces  lower  midweek  room  rates  during  quieter  periods.  Which

element of the marketing mix is being adjusted?

A. Product

B. Promotion

C. Place

D. Price

7

12.  A  hotel  improves  its  online  booking  procedure so customers can reserve rooms

more quickly and easily. Which element of the 7Ps is being improved most directly?

A. Promotion

B. Physical Evidence

C. Process

D. Product

13. A tourism business allows visitors to book through its own website, travel agents

and  Online  Travel  Agencies.  Which  element  of  the  marketing  mix  is  most  directly

involved?

A. Place

B. People

C. Promotion

D. Physical Evidence

14. What is one major advantage of digital marketing for tourism organisations?

A. It prevents customers from comparing alternatives.

B.  It  allows  businesses  to  reach  and  engage  potential  visitors  through  multiple  digital

channels.

C. It guarantees positive customer reviews.

D. It removes the need for customer research.

15. Why is a mobile-friendly tourism website important?

A. It replaces all other marketing channels.

B. It prevents customers from using search engines.

C. It makes printed brochures compulsory.

D. Many travellers use mobile devices to research, plan and book tourism experiences.

16.  A  guesthouse  publishes  destination  guides,  local  travel  tips,  photographs  and

videos to attract potential visitors. Which approach is being used?

A. Content marketing

B. Personal selling

C. Package pricing

D. Internal marketing

8

17.  A  tourism  organisation  wants  its  website  to  appear  more  prominently  when

travellers  search  online  for  accommodation  in  its  area.  Which  digital  marketing

activity is most relevant?

A. Public relations

B. Personal selling

C. Search engine optimisation

D. Direct distribution only

18. Why is social media useful in tourism marketing?

A. It guarantees every post will reach all travellers.

B.  It  allows  tourism  organisations  to  share  content  and  engage  directly  with  potential  and

existing visitors.

C. It prevents customers from posting opinions.

D. It replaces the need for a marketing strategy.

19.  A  destination  considers  working  with  a travel influencer. Which factor should be

considered most carefully?

A. Whether the influencer posts the greatest number of times per day

B. Whether the influencer has the most expensive camera

C. Whether the influencer uses only one social media platform

D.  Whether  the  influencer's  audience,  credibility  and  content  are  relevant  to  the  target

market

20.  A  hotel  receives  a  negative  online  review  about  a  long  check-in  delay.  Which

response is most appropriate?

A. Respond professionally, acknowledge the concern and investigate the problem.

B. Publicly argue with the guest.

C. Ignore all negative reviews.

D. Remove the hotel's social media pages.

21. Why should tourism organisations monitor online reviews?

A. Reviews only affect existing employees.

B. Reviews determine all tourism prices.

C. Reviews can influence reputation, customer perceptions and booking decisions.

D. Reviews make customer service unnecessary.

9

22. What is the main purpose of Customer Relationship Management (CRM)?

A. To replace all tourism employees

B. To manage customer information and relationships in order to improve service and loyalty

C. To prevent visitors from booking elsewhere

D. To increase prices for repeat customers

23.  A  hotel  records  returning  guests'  room preferences and uses this information to

provide more relevant offers. This is an example of:

A. Mass marketing.

B. market withdrawal.

C. personalised marketing.

D. public relations.

24. Which use of artificial intelligence is most relevant to modern tourism marketing?

A. Providing automated customer assistance and personalised travel recommendations

B. Removing the need for accurate tourism information

C. Guaranteeing that every website visitor makes a booking

D. Preventing travellers from comparing destinations

25.  A  tourism  marketer  compares website visits, advertisement clicks, enquiries and

completed bookings. Why is this information useful?

A. It determines the number of employees required in every department.

B. It guarantees future visitor numbers.

C. It replaces the need for marketing objectives.

D.  It  helps  the  organisation  analyse  customer  behaviour  and  evaluate  digital  marketing

performance.

26.  An  online  advertising  campaign  receives  many  clicks  but  produces  very  few

bookings. What should the tourism marketer do?

A. Assume the campaign is successful because many people clicked on it.

B. Investigate why customer interest is not converting into bookings.

C. Stop measuring the campaign.

D. Increase all tourism prices immediately.

10

27. What is the main purpose of marketing communication in tourism?

A. To communicate the value of a tourism offering to relevant audiences and encourage an

appropriate response

B. To replace the tourism experience itself

C. To guarantee that customers remain loyal

D. To eliminate competing tourism businesses

28. Which statement best describes Integrated Marketing Communication (IMC)?

A. Selecting only the least expensive communication method

B. Using social media as the organisation's only communication channel

C. Allowing different departments to communicate unrelated messages

D.  Coordinating  communication  channels  so  that customers receive a clear and consistent

message

29.  A  tourism  organisation  pays  to  promote  a  holiday  package  through  online

advertisements, radio and outdoor posters. Which promotional tool is primarily being

used?

A. Public relations

B. Advertising

C. Personal selling

D. Market research

30. Which activity is the best example of public relations in tourism?

A. Giving every customer a 20% booking discount

B. Paying sales employees a commission

C. Working with media and stakeholders to build and maintain a positive public image

D. Increasing accommodation prices during peak season

31. A resort offers customers a 15% discount when they book before a specified date.

Which promotional method is being used?

A. Sales promotion

B. Public relations

C. Personal selling

D. Market research

11

32.  A  travel  consultant  discusses  a  customer's  budget  and  interests  before

recommending  a  suitable  holiday  package.  Which  promotional  method  is  being

demonstrated?

A. Advertising

B. Public relations

C. Sales promotion

D. Personal selling

33.  Why  should  tourism  organisations  adapt  communication  for  different  target

audiences?

A. Every target market responds to exactly the same message.

B.  Different  audiences  may  have  different  needs,  interests  and  preferred  communication

channels.

C. Adapting communication allows marketers to make inaccurate claims.

D. Target markets do not influence communication decisions.

34.  A  hotel,  restaurant,  tour  operator  and  local  attraction  jointly promote a weekend

tourism package. What is a major benefit of this partnership?

A. The organisations no longer need individual identities.

B. Competition within tourism disappears.

C. The organisations can combine resources and increase their marketing reach.

D. Customers are prevented from selecting individual services.

35.  Why  might  a  tourism  organisation  participate  in  a  tourism  exhibition  or  travel

trade event?

A. To showcase its offering and communicate with potential customers or industry partners

B. To avoid direct contact with the tourism industry

C. To eliminate the need for all other marketing activities

D. To prevent competitors from promoting their products

36.  Incorrect  information  about  a  serious  safety  incident  begins  circulating  online

about a tourism attraction. What should the organisation do?

A. Wait until the story disappears on its own.

B. Publicly attack everyone who shared the information.

C. Stop communicating with customers.

D.  Verify  the  facts  and  provide  accurate,  timely  information  through  appropriate  official

channels.

12

37. Which practice best demonstrates ethical tourism marketing communication?

A. Hiding important terms and conditions from customers

B. Providing accurate information and avoiding misleading claims

C. Using photographs that deliberately misrepresent the actual destination

D. Promising services that the organisation cannot deliver

38. A promotional campaign reaches thousands of potential visitors, but management

wants  to  determine  whether  it resulted in actual bookings. Which measure would be

most useful?

A. Number of staff meetings held during the campaign

B. Number of desks in the marketing office

C. Booking or conversion response generated by the campaign

D. Age of the tourism organisation

39.  What  distinguishes  destination  marketing  from  the  marketing  of  an  individual

tourism business?

A. Destination marketing promotes a place and its combined visitor experiences rather than

only one organisation.

B. Destination marketing promotes accommodation only.

C. Destination marketing cannot use digital channels.

D. Destination marketing targets international visitors only.

40. What is an important role of a Destination Marketing Organisation (DMO)?

A. Determining each tourist's personal travel budget

B. Owning every tourism business in a destination

C. Coordinating and promoting the destination to relevant visitor markets

D. Preventing communities from participating in tourism

41. Why is stakeholder collaboration particularly important in destination marketing?

A. Every destination contains only one tourism product.

B.  The  overall  visitor  experience  depends  on  several  businesses,  communities  and

public-sector stakeholders.

C. Collaboration removes the need for destination branding.

D. The government provides every service used by visitors.

13

42.  Two  destinations  have  similar  natural  attractions,  but  one  provides  stronger

infrastructure, higher-quality visitor experiences and a more recognisable brand. The

second destination is likely to have:

A. greater seasonality.

B. weaker positioning.

C. lower customer value.

D. stronger destination competitiveness.

43. Which approach best demonstrates sustainable destination marketing?

A.  Attracting  visitors  while  considering  environmental,  social,  community  and  long-term

destination impacts

B. Maximising visitor numbers regardless of environmental damage

C. Excluding local communities from tourism development

D. Encouraging visitors to ignore conservation requirements

44. A rural community develops guided cultural experiences, employs local residents

and  sells  locally  produced  crafts  to  visitors.  Which  approach  is  most  clearly

demonstrated?

A. Mass tourism production

B. Crisis marketing

C. Community-based tourism

D. Price skimming

45. Which example best represents the use of smart tourism technology?

A. Removing digital technology from visitor services

B. Using mobile applications and visitor data to provide useful travel information and improve

the visitor experience

C. Providing tourists with printed information only

D. Preventing customers from making online bookings

46. How can climate change affect tourism destinations?

A. It affects tourism only in countries without cities.

B. It removes the need for responsible tourism.

C. It guarantees increased tourism demand.

D.  It  can  affect  natural  attractions,  weather  conditions  and  travel  patterns,  requiring

destinations to adapt.

14

47.  Travellers

increasingly  expect  convenient  digital  booking,  authentic  local

experiences  and  environmentally  responsible  tourism  options.  What  should tourism

marketers do?

A. Adapt tourism offerings and marketing approaches as traveller expectations change.

B. Continue using exactly the same approach regardless of market changes.

C. Remove digital booking facilities.

D. Focus exclusively on traditional mass advertising.

48.  A  destination  introduces  a  mobile  application  containing digital maps, attraction

information, event updates and booking facilities. What is the main benefit to visitors?

A. Visitors become unable to travel without tour operators.

B. Attractions no longer need employees.

C. Visitors can access useful destination information and services more conveniently.

D. The destination no longer needs tourism businesses.

49.  Why  is  continuous  professional  development  important  for  future  tourism

marketers?

A. Tourism marketing methods remain unchanged over time.

B. Marketers need to update their knowledge and skills as technology, traveller expectations

and industry practices evolve.

C. Training eliminates the need for practical experience.

D. Digital skills are required only by senior managers.

50.  A  South  African  destination  wants  to  remain  competitive  and  attractive over the

long term. Which approach provides the strongest response?

A. Concentrate only on increasing visitor numbers every year.

B. Replace stakeholder involvement with automated technology.

C. Continue using existing marketing methods without evaluating change.

D.  Combine  customer-focused  marketing,  sustainability,

innovation,  stakeholder

collaboration and appropriate technology.
