Assignment 1

1. Which statement best describes tourism?

A. Permanent relocation from one area to another

B. Temporary travel outside a person's usual place of residence for a limited period and for

various purposes

C. Travel that must involve crossing an international border

D. Travel undertaken only for holidays

2.  A  family  from  Johannesburg  spends  five  days  in  Durban  during  the  school

holidays. Why is this considered tourism?

A. They are temporarily travelling outside their usual place of residence.

B. They are travelling only because it is a school holiday.

C. They are travelling within South Africa.

D. They are paying for accommodation.

3.  Which  example  best  demonstrates  the  interconnected  nature  of  the  tourism

industry?

A. A hotel introduces new staff uniforms.

B. A restaurant changes its menu.

C. A tourist purchases a souvenir.

D. A visitor uses transport, accommodation, attractions and restaurants during one trip.

4.  Which  tourism  stakeholder  is  most  directly  responsible  for  developing  tourism

policies, regulations and public infrastructure?

A. Visitors

B. Travel agents

C. Government

D. Hotel guests

5. A community tourism project creates jobs for local guides and increases sales for

local craft businesses. Which benefit of tourism is demonstrated most clearly?

A. Economic development in the local community

B. Removal of competition between tourism businesses

C. Elimination of environmental impacts

D. Prevention of seasonal demand

6

6. Which statement best distinguishes marketing from selling?

A. Selling begins by studying customer needs, whereas marketing begins after production.

B.  Marketing  focuses  broadly  on  understanding  and  satisfying  customers,  while  selling

focuses more directly on encouraging purchases.

C. Marketing and selling are exactly the same activity.

D. Selling includes market research while marketing does not.

7.  A  lodge  researches  what  eco-conscious  travellers  expect  before  developing  its

packages. Which marketing philosophy is being demonstrated?

A. Production orientation

B. Selling orientation

C. Product orientation

D. Marketing orientation

8. Which action provides the strongest evidence of customer-focused marketing?

A. Increasing advertising without studying customers

B. Copying every product offered by competitors

C. Identifying visitor needs and designing suitable experiences around them

D. Concentrating only on reducing operating costs

9.  Which  development  is  identified  in  the  textbook  as  an  emerging  opportunity  in

tourism marketing?

A. Using artificial intelligence to provide personalised recommendations

B. Replacing online booking systems with postal bookings

C. Reducing the use of customer information completely

D. Moving from mobile technology back to printed catalogues

10.  A  visitor  believes  that  a  R3  000  safari  provides  excellent  service,  memorable

activities,  convenience  and  quality  compared  with  the  amount  paid.  This judgement

relates mainly to:

A. market size.

B. customer value.

C. organisational culture.

D. tourism regulation.

7

11.  Which  tourism  marketing  activity  best  reflects  the  modern  movement  from

one-way communication to interactive customer engagement?

A. Printing the same brochure every year

B. Removing customer review facilities

C. Using only roadside advertising

D. Communicating with travellers and responding to them through social media

12. Why is customer satisfaction important to a tourism organisation?

A. It guarantees that customers will accept any price.

B. It eliminates competing businesses.

C. Satisfied customers may return, recommend the organisation and leave positive reviews.

D. It prevents changes in tourism demand.

13. Which example represents domestic tourism?

A. A Pretoria resident visiting Cape Town for a holiday

B. A South African resident travelling to Botswana

C. A German visitor travelling through the Garden Route

D. A Namibian visitor staying in Johannesburg

14. Why is trust especially important when tourists book accommodation or holidays

before travelling?

A. Tourism organisations cannot advertise their products.

B. Visitors often purchase tourism experiences before they can experience them.

C. Tourists always pay only after the trip.

D. Tourism products are physical goods that can be tested beforehand.

15. Why can an unsold hotel room for tonight create a problem for a hotel?

A. The hotel can store tonight's room and sell it twice tomorrow.

B. The room can be converted into a physical product.

C. The room automatically becomes more valuable tomorrow.

D. The unused service opportunity for tonight cannot be stored for later sale.

16.  Two  guests  receive  slightly  different  experiences  because  they  are  assisted  by

different employees. What does this illustrate about tourism services?

A. Tourism services are always identical.

B. Tourism services can be stored for future use.

C.  Customer  experiences  may  vary  depending  on  service  quality  and  who  delivers  the

service.

D. Visitors become owners of the service.

8

17.  Why  should  tourism  organisations  monitor  stakeholders  such  as  government,

communities, suppliers and tourism businesses?

A. Their actions can influence the visitor experience and tourism marketing success.

B. They all have identical responsibilities.

C. Only stakeholders determine tourism prices.

D. Stakeholders replace the need to understand customers.

18. Why should tourism marketers study consumer behaviour?

A. To make every tourist behave in the same way

B. To understand why travellers make particular travel and purchasing decisions

C. To eliminate the need for customer research

D. To guarantee that every campaign succeeds

19. A traveller chooses an adventure holiday after friends recommend the experience

and members of their social group post positive photographs online. Which factor is

most evident?

A. Internal financial control

B. Organisational structure

C. Family and social influence

D. Distribution strategy

20. A traveller chooses a wellness retreat because they want relaxation and relief from

everyday stress. What most directly explains the decision?

A. Travel motivation

B. Pricing policy

C. Distribution method

D. Physical evidence

21. What normally happens after a traveller recognises a need or desire to take a trip?

A. The traveller automatically becomes loyal to a destination.

B. The traveller immediately evaluates the completed holiday.

C. The tourism organisation selects a target market.

D. The traveller searches for information about possible destinations and tourism products.

9

22.  Naledi  compares  guest  houses  according  to  price,  location,  facilities  and online

reviews  before  deciding  where  to  stay.  Which  stage  of  the  tourist  decision-making

process is she completing?

A. Need recognition

B. Evaluation of alternatives

C. Post-travel evaluation

D. Customer loyalty

23.  After  returning  home,  a traveller considers whether the holiday met expectations

and posts an online review. Which stage is this?

A. Information search

B. Market targeting

C. Post-travel evaluation

D. Positioning

24. Which statement best describes tourism market research?

A.  Collecting  and  analysing  information  to  understand  a  specific  tourism  market  and  its

customers

B. Selling tourism products directly to visitors

C. Preparing advertisements without customer information

D. Dividing employees into departments

25. A lodge distributes questionnaires to recent guests to determine their satisfaction

with its services. The information collected directly from the guests is:

A. industry data.

B. historical secondary information.

C. competitor information.

D. primary research data.

26.  A  tour  company  examines  government  tourism  statistics,  published  industry

reports and existing research before developing a new package. These are mainly:

A. observation records.

B. secondary research sources.

C. focus-group findings.

D. personal-selling information.

10

27. A tourism organisation wants detailed information about how visitors feel about a

new cultural experience. Which research method would be most suitable?

A. Interviews

B. Financial statements

C. Competitor pricing only

D. Staff scheduling

28.  A  destination  distributes  a  questionnaire  to  1  000  visitors  to  gather  information

about  their  travel  purpose  and  preferred  activities.  Which  research  method  is being

used?

A. Observation

B. Focus group

C. Survey

D. Secondary research

29. What is the main purpose of market segmentation?

A. To market the same product to every traveller

B.  To  divide a broad market into smaller groups with similar characteristics, needs or travel

behaviour

C. To eliminate competing tourism businesses

D. To divide employees into departments

30.  A  tour  operator  groups  customers  according  to  age,  income,  occupation  and

family size. Which form of segmentation is being used?

A. Geographic

B. Behavioural

C. Psychographic

D. Demographic

31.  A  tourism  organisation  groups  potential  visitors  according  to  the  countries,

provinces and cities where they live. This is:

A. geographic segmentation.

B. psychographic segmentation.

C. demographic segmentation.

D. behavioural segmentation.

11

32.  A  safari  operator  groups  travellers  according  to  lifestyle,  interests,  values  and

preference for adventure. Which form of segmentation is most appropriate?

A. Geographic

B. Demographic

C. Psychographic

D. Financial

33.  After  identifying  several  market  segments,  a  lodge  decides  to  concentrate  its

marketing  resources  on  environmentally  conscious  domestic  couples.  This  process

is known as:

A. market research.

B. targeting.

C. service recovery.

D. environmental scanning.

34.  A  boutique  hotel  wants

travellers

to  view

it  as  an  affordable

luxury

accommodation option compared with nearby competitors. This relates to:

A. positioning.

B. primary research.

C. segmentation.

D. environmental scanning.

35.  Why  should  a  tourism  organisation  consider  more  than  the  size  of  a  market

segment when selecting a target market?

A. Large market segments cannot purchase tourism products.

B. Government selects target markets for every tourism business.

C. Small market segments are always more profitable.

D.  The  organisation  must  also  consider  factors  such  as  growth  potential,  profitability,

competition and available resources.

36.  A  guest  expected  a  clean  room,  accurate  information  and  friendly  service.  The

business delivered all three as promised. Which outcome is most likely?

A. Market segmentation

B. Automatic price reduction

C. Customer satisfaction

D. Competitive analysis

12

37. Which action is most likely to strengthen customer loyalty?

A. Delivering consistent service, recognising repeat visitors and responding to feedback

B. Ignoring complaints from returning customers

C. Changing the target market after every booking

D. Making promises that cannot consistently be delivered

38. What is the main purpose of a tourism marketing plan?

A. To eliminate the need for future decisions

B. To provide a structured roadmap for achieving marketing objectives

C. To ensure every competitor follows the same approach

D. To focus only on advertising

39. Which activity is most clearly an example of operational marketing?

A. Deciding the organisation's long-term market direction

B. Selecting the market the business will serve over the next five years

C. Establishing a long-term competitive advantage

D. Implementing a seasonal social media campaign and promotional offer

40. Which objective best follows the SMART approach?

A. Increase direct online bookings by 15% over the next 12 months.

B. Increase bookings as much as possible.

C. Become the best tourism business soon.

D. Make every visitor completely satisfied.

41.  A  safari  lodge  identifies  experienced  guides  as  an  advantage  and  limited  room

capacity as a problem. In a SWOT analysis, these are examples of:

A. opportunities and threats.

B. political and economic factors.

C. strengths and weaknesses.

D. customer segments and target markets.

42.  A  tourism  organisation  examines  inflation,  exchange  rates  and  changing

employment levels. Which PESTLE factor is it analysing?

A. Social

B. Economic

C. Technological

D. Environmental

13

43. What is the main purpose of competitive analysis?

A. To understand competitors and identify ways to differentiate the tourism offering

B. To copy competitors exactly

C. To avoid studying customer reviews

D. To replace customer research

44.  The  government  introduces  new  visa  regulations  for  visitors  from  an  important

source market. Which PESTLE factor is most directly involved?

A. Social

B. Technological

C. Environmental

D. Political

45. A hotel combines booking records, customer reviews, website analytics, research

findings and sales reports to support marketing decisions. What is it using?

A. SWOT analysis

B. Customer persona

C. Marketing Information System

D. Distribution channel

46. A resort uses previous booking levels, school holiday dates, weather patterns and

special events to estimate visitor numbers for December. This is an example of:

A. Customer positioning.

B. forecasting tourism demand.

C. service recovery.

D. brand development.

47. A hotel wants to know what percentage of visitors to its website actually complete

a booking. Which KPI would provide the most useful information?

A. Number of staff members

B. Customer age

C. Number of brochures printed

D. Website conversion rate

48.  A  tourism  organisation  compares  actual  booking  results  with  its  marketing

objective  and  then  changes  an  underperforming  campaign.  What  process  is  being

demonstrated?

A. Market segmentation

B. Product development

C. Customer profiling

D. Evaluating marketing performance

49.  A  guesthouse  advertises  every  room  as  having  an  uninterrupted  sea  view  even

though several rooms overlook a car park. Which ethical marketing principle has been

violated?

A. Geographic segmentation

B. Truthful and accurate communication

C. Demand forecasting

D. Competitive analysis

50.  A  tour  operator  collects  personal  information  when  customers  make  online

bookings. Which practice is most responsible?

A. Sharing the information with unrelated companies automatically

B. Publishing customer contact details online

C. Storing customer information securely and using it responsibly

D. Sending promotional messages without permission
