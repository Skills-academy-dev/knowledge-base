Assignment 2

1. A destination wants to increase domestic winter visits. Management begins by

defining the target audience, communication objective, value proposition and desired

customer response. Which principle is being applied?

A. Strategic communication planning aligned with broader marketing objectives.

B. Media scheduling based primarily on the lowest advertising cost.

C. Promotional standardisation across all visitor markets.

D. Tactical message repetition without audience analysis.

2. A lodge promotes itself as a quiet wellness retreat on its website but advertises

loud entertainment packages on social media. What is the greatest strategic risk?

A. The lodge will depend too heavily on owned media.

B. Inconsistent messages may weaken positioning and create uncertain customer

expectations.

C. Social media will automatically attract an unsuitable international market.

D. The lodge will be unable to measure campaign reach.

3. Which communication decision most clearly demonstrates effective

audience-message-channel alignment?

A. Sending the same technical brochure to families, adventure travellers and business

tourists.

B. Choosing social media because competitors use it frequently.

C. Advertising premium retirement tours through youth-focused entertainment platforms.

D. Presenting convenient family activities through channels commonly used by parents

planning domestic holidays.

4. A tourism business pays for search advertisements, publishes information on its

website and receives unpaid coverage from a travel journalist. These channels are

best classified as:

A. shared, paid and direct media respectively.

B. earned, owned and shared media respectively.

C. paid, owned and earned media respectively.

D. paid, earned and owned media respectively.

6

5. A visitor posts an enthusiastic video about a tour, and other travellers share it

widely. Why should the tourism business not treat this communication in the same

way as its own website content?

A. Shared media cannot contribute to brand awareness.

B. The organisation has less control over how customer-created content is interpreted and

redistributed.

C. Customer-created content is always less credible than paid advertising.

D. Shared content cannot influence purchase decisions.

6. Which campaign reflects integrated tourism marketing communication most

effectively?

A. Website, email, social media and partner messages support one positioning objective

while adapting content to each channel.

B. Every channel publishes exactly the same content, format and wording.

C. Each department develops an independent message for the same target market.

D. The organisation selects communication channels before defining the campaign

objective.

7. A Garden Route operator receives strong engagement on inspirational content but

few bookings. What should management examine before increasing promotional

spending?

A. Whether competitors post content more frequently.

B. Whether the campaign has generated enough social-media reactions.

C. Whether the customer journey provides clear information, persuasive value and a simple

route from interest to booking.

D. Whether the campaign uses both photographs and videos.

8. Which statement best describes a digital tourism ecosystem?

A. A tourism organisation’s website and booking engine only.

B. A list of social-media platforms used by the marketing department.

C. A database that stores customer contact details independently of other systems.

D. A connected network of platforms, organisations, technologies and touchpoints

influencing the traveller’s journey.

7

9. A traveller discovers a destination through social media, searches for

accommodation, checks reviews, uses a map and completes a mobile booking. What

is the strongest strategic implication?

A. Digital touchpoints should provide consistent, connected information throughout the

customer journey.

B. Only the final booking platform contributes to marketing performance.

C. Search engines become irrelevant once social-media awareness is achieved.

D. Each touchpoint should communicate an independent value proposition.

10. A tourism organisation wants its online content to support both search visibility

and customer decision-making. Which approach is strongest?

A. Repeat high-volume keywords even when they make the content difficult to read.

B. Develop useful, relevant content around traveller needs while applying sound search

optimisation.

C. Publish inspirational images without practical information or booking links.

D. Produce the greatest possible volume of content regardless of quality.

11. A mobile user moves between a destination website, messaging service, booking

platform and physical visitor centre. What would an effective omnichannel strategy

provide?

A. Different prices and information at each touchpoint to encourage comparison.

B. A requirement that the visitor restart the enquiry whenever the channel changes.

C. A coherent experience in which information and assistance remain connected across

channels.

D. Exclusive dependence on the channel used at the awareness stage.

12. A tour operator has high mobile website traffic but a low mobile conversion rate.

Which action is most strategically appropriate?

A. Increase desktop advertising because mobile users are unlikely to book.

B. Remove detailed product information to reduce page length.

C. Introduce more booking steps to collect additional customer data.

D. Investigate mobile speed, navigation, availability, trust signals, calls to action and

checkout complexity.

8

13. A hotel collects permitted guest preferences, previous-stay information and

service feedback. Which use best reflects strategic customer relationship

management?

A. Sending every guest identical promotions regardless of previous behaviour.

B. Using relevant information to improve service, communication, retention and long-term

customer value.

C. Selling the data to unrelated organisations without customer knowledge.

D. Retaining all available customer information indefinitely because it may become useful.

14. A business compares two customer groups. Group X generates frequent

low-margin bookings and high service costs, while Group Y books less often but

purchases profitable packages and recommends the business. Which measure would

best support the comparison?

A. Social-media reach.

B. Total website traffic.

C. Customer lifetime value.

D. Gross booking volume only.

15. Which retention strategy is most likely to create genuine customer loyalty?

A. Making it difficult for customers to cancel or move to competitors.

B. Offering points that customers cannot realistically redeem.

C. Sending frequent messages without considering relevance.

D. Consistently delivering valued experiences, recognising suitable preferences and

maintaining meaningful relationships.

16. A hotel guest posts a justified complaint after a serious service failure. Which

response best supports service recovery and electronic word-of-mouth management?

A. Acknowledge the problem, respond promptly, offer an appropriate remedy and correct the

underlying cause.

B. Ask the guest to delete the review before investigating the problem.

C. Publish a promotional offer to move attention away from the complaint.

D. Use an automated response stating that all service complaints are subjective.

9

17. An AI chatbot can answer routine questions but sometimes provides uncertain

information about safety requirements. What is the strongest management response?

A. Allow the chatbot to answer every inquiry because automation reduces labour costs.

B. Stop using digital customer support entirely.

C. Define accuracy controls and escalation rules so complex or high-risk enquiries receive

human oversight.

D. Remove warnings so customers are not discouraged from booking.

18. Why should a destination be managed as a complex tourism product rather than

merely promoted as a geographical place?

A. Visitors experience an interconnected combination of attractions, services, infrastructure,

communities and environmental conditions.

B. A destination marketing organisation owns every tourism product within its boundaries.

C. Destination competitiveness depends only on the quality of its advertising.

D. Visitors assess individual businesses without forming an overall destination impression.

19. Which activity most clearly belongs to destination management rather than

destination promotion alone?

A. Designing a social-media advertisement for a festival.

B. Coordinating visitor flows, infrastructure, stakeholder services and environmental capacity

during the festival.

C. Paying an influencer to attend the festival.

D. Producing a brochure highlighting the festival programme.

20. A destination marketing organisation launches a campaign that successfully

attracts far more visitors than local transport, waste services and attractions can

manage. What is the main strategic failure?

A. The campaign used too many owned communication channels.

B. The destination selected a market that was too geographically concentrated.

C. The campaign focused on visitor satisfaction rather than brand awareness.

D. Marketing demand was not aligned with destination capacity and management.

10

21. Which governance arrangement is most likely to support effective destination

marketing?

A. Clear stakeholder roles, shared objectives, appropriate coordination and accountable

decision-making.

B. Exclusive control by the largest accommodation provider.

C. Independent campaigns with no common destination strategy.

D. Short-term promotional decisions made without community participation.

22. A coastal destination experiences overcrowding during one short summer period

while inland attractions remain underused. Which strategic response is strongest?

A. Increase summer beach advertising to protect the destination’s best-known image.

B. Use product development, visitor information and market targeting to spread demand

across places and seasons.

C. Stop marketing the destination until overcrowding disappears.

D. Reduce information about inland attractions to avoid confusing visitors.

23. Which distinction between destination identity and destination image is correct?

A. Identity is created entirely by visitors, while image is controlled by the destination.

B. Identity and image are interchangeable because both refer to advertising.

C. Identity concerns how the destination defines its characteristics and values, while image

concerns how audiences perceive it.

D. Image refers only to visual branding, while identity refers only to physical attractions.

24. A destination promotes responsible nature tourism, but visitors regularly

encounter environmental damage and poorly managed facilities. What is the most

likely consequence?

A. The advertising will eventually replace negative visitor experiences.

B. The destination’s identity will automatically become more sustainable.

C. Visitors will evaluate the campaign independently of the actual experience.

D. The gap between the brand promise and delivered experience may weaken image and

reputation.

11

25. Which factor provides the strongest basis for sustainable destination

competitiveness?

A. Distinctive resources supported by accessibility, service quality, infrastructure,

management and a coherent visitor experience.

B. A large advertising budget regardless of product quality.

C. Dependence on one famous attraction that competitors cannot advertise.

D. Continuous discounting across all tourism products.

26. A destination benchmarks itself only against the international destination with the

highest visitor numbers. What is the main limitation?

A. Visitor numbers can never be used in destination benchmarking.

B. International destinations cannot be compared with domestic destinations.

C. Benchmarking should focus only on social-media performance.

D. The comparator and measures may be unsuitable for the destination’s markets,

objectives, scale and strategic challenges.

27. Which stakeholder network is most likely to strengthen a multi-day regional

tourism route?

A. One hotel develops the route independently and prevents other businesses from

participating.

B. Accommodation providers, attractions, guides, transport operators, communities and

destination organisations coordinate complementary experiences.

C. Local government manages every commercial tourism activity directly.

D. Businesses participate only when all partners offer identical products.

28. A public-private tourism partnership has funding and enthusiastic participants but

no agreed responsibilities or performance measures. What is the greatest risk?

A. The partnership will necessarily focus too heavily on community participation.

B. Private organisations will automatically take control of the destination brand.

C. Unclear accountability may produce duplicated work, conflict and weak results.

D. Public-sector involvement will prevent any marketing innovation.

12

29. Which approach best reflects meaningful community participation in destination

marketing?

A. Communities help shape tourism stories, products, benefits and decisions rather than

appearing only in promotional images.

B. Marketers select cultural stories without consulting the people represented.

C. Residents participate only after the destination campaign has been finalised.

D. Community involvement is limited to providing unpaid entertainment at events.

30. Several tourism businesses cooperate on a shared campaign but also need to

retain their individual identities. Which approach is most appropriate?

A. Allow every partner to use an unrelated destination message.

B. Coordinate a central campaign proposition while allowing suitable partner-level

adaptation.

C. Require all partners to use identical products, prices and branding.

D. Exclude smaller businesses because they have limited promotional budgets.

31. A rural destination identifies growing interest in guided adventure experiences.

What should decision-makers do before investing in major new facilities?

A. Assess demand, target-market requirements, competition, local capabilities,

environmental implications and commercial feasibility.

B. Build facilities immediately because market growth guarantees success.

C. Copy the leading international adventure destination.

D. Promote the concept widely before determining whether it can be delivered.

32. A niche tourism segment is relatively small but demonstrates strong interest,

suitable spending and close alignment with the destination’s distinctive resources.

What is the most defensible conclusion?

A. The segment is unsuitable because advanced strategies require mass markets.

B. The segment may be strategically attractive despite its smaller size.

C. The destination should serve the segment only by lowering prices.

D. Niche markets do not require research because their interests are already specific.

13

33. A town creates a cultural festival that attracts visitors but causes severe

congestion, excludes local traders and has no activities beyond the event venue.

Which strategic weakness is most evident?

A. The town has used an event as a destination-branding tool.

B. The festival attracts visitors from outside the destination.

C. The event programme includes cultural experiences.

D. Event marketing has not been integrated with destination management, community value

and wider visitor experiences.

34. A coastal destination depends heavily on summer beach tourism. Which

diversification strategy is most appropriate?

A. Develop credible complementary experiences and markets that extend demand while

protecting the destination’s core resources.

B. Replace beach tourism entirely with an unrelated industry.

C. Promote the same beach product more aggressively during the existing peak.

D. Enter every available niche market regardless of destination fit.

35. Which decision best integrates sustainability into tourism marketing strategy?

A. Add a green symbol to existing advertising without reviewing operations.

B. Target the largest possible visitor market regardless of capacity.

C. Align target markets, product design, communication and visitor behaviour with

environmental and community priorities.

D. Separate sustainability planning from marketing decisions.

36. A lodge reduces water use but imports most supplies, provides few local jobs and

encourages large visitor volumes in a sensitive area. What is the strongest

assessment?

A. The lodge should evaluate environmental, socio-cultural and economic effects together

rather than treating one positive action as complete sustainability.

B. Reduced water use proves that the lodge is fully sustainable.

C. Local economic participation is unrelated to responsible tourism marketing.

D. High visitor volumes necessarily compensate for negative community impacts.

14

37. Which marketing practice best supports community value?

A. Presenting residents as decorative features in promotional material.

B. Encouraging tourists to observe communities without local interaction.

C. Replacing local suppliers with standardised national suppliers.

D. Involving local enterprises in suitable products, supply opportunities, storytelling and

benefit-sharing.

38. A resort claims to be “100% environmentally harmless” but provides no evidence

and continues activities that damage a wetland. This is best described as:

A. responsible market positioning.

B. greenwashing through an unsupported and misleading sustainability claim.

C. destination diversification.

D. sustainability benchmarking.

39. Which sustainability claim is most credible?

A. “We are the greenest lodge in Africa.”

B. “Our holidays have no effect on the environment.”

C. “Every guest automatically supports complete community development.”

D. “Metered water use decreased by 18% this year; the measurement method and

remaining improvement targets are available.”

40. A tourism company wants to evaluate a campaign intended to increase profitable

direct bookings. Which KPI set is most strategically relevant?

A. Direct conversions, acquisition cost, contribution, cancellation behaviour and customer

quality.

B. Impressions, likes and follower growth only.

C. Number of advertisements produced and staff hours used.

D. Competitors’ posting frequency and total destination arrivals.

41. A campaign generates high reach and engagement but no measurable increase in

enquiries, bookings or customer quality. What should management conclude?

A. The campaign is successful because attention metrics always predict revenue.

B. Awareness may have improved, but performance against decision-relevant outcomes

remains weak or unproven.

C. All digital metrics should be removed from future reports.

D. Marketing objectives are unnecessary when engagement is high.

15

42. Which statement about marketing return on investment is most accurate?

A. Any increase in bookings after a campaign proves that marketing caused the increase.

B. Return on investment is calculated by dividing social-media reach by advertising

expenditure.

C. Marketing accountability requires comparison of attributable value and costs while

recognising other influences on performance.

D. Marketing expenditure should be judged only against competitor expenditure.

43. What is the primary strategic purpose of a marketing dashboard?

A. To organise selected indicators so managers can monitor performance, identify changes

and support decisions.

B. To display every piece of available data regardless of relevance.

C. To replace analysis with automated charts.

D. To ensure that favourable indicators receive greater attention than negative results.

44. A dashboard displays a sharp decline in mobile conversion while desktop

performance remains stable. What should management do next?

A. Report the average conversion rate without separating devices.

B. Increase all advertising expenditure immediately.

C. Investigate the mobile journey, validate the data and identify the point at which customers

abandon.

D. Remove mobile performance from the dashboard because it distorts results.

45. A coastal tourism business identifies severe weather, transport disruption and

dependence on one booking platform as possible threats. Which action best

demonstrates risk management?

A. Wait for a disruption before deciding how to communicate.

B. Assess likelihood and impact, define warning indicators and prepare response

alternatives.

C. Transfer all bookings to the existing platform.

D. Avoid discussing risks because this could harm staff confidence.

16

46. Severe flooding closes access to an attraction. Which communication response is

most appropriate?

A. Continue normal promotional messages until every detail is confirmed.

B. Remove all online information and avoid media enquiries.

C. Blame local authorities before investigating the situation.

D. Provide timely, accurate and empathetic updates, explain what is known and offer

practical guidance.

47. After a regional wildfire, several unaffected attractions remain open, but

customers believe the entire destination is closed. What should recovery marketing

prioritise?

A. Rebuild confidence with verified information, coordinated stakeholder communication and

clear evidence of accessible experiences.

B. Offer deep discounts without addressing safety perceptions.

C. Claim that the crisis had no effect on the region.

D. Resume ordinary advertising without acknowledging customer concerns.

48. Which capability most strongly supports long-term marketing resilience?

A. Reliance on one target market and one booking channel.

B. A fixed annual plan that cannot be changed after approval.

C. Avoiding post-crisis reviews because they may expose mistakes.

D. Market and channel diversification, scenario planning, organisational learning and flexible

resource allocation.

49. A destination introduces connected visitor information, real-time transport

updates and digital itinerary support. Which concept does this best illustrate?

A. Traditional mass communication.

B. Smart tourism that uses connected information and technology to improve visitor and

destination outcomes.

C. Cost-based destination pricing.

D. Cooperative print advertising.

17

50. Which approach best reflects an adaptive, future-focused tourism marketing

strategy?

A. Adopt every emerging technology before evaluating its value or risks.

B. Predict one future and commit all resources to it.

C. Monitor important changes, develop scenarios, test suitable innovations and retain

human, ethical and strategic oversight.

D. Preserve the existing strategy until competitors prove that change is unavoidable.
