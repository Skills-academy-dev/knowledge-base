Chapter 1: Structuring the Sales

Force

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this chapter, students should be able to:

1.  Describe what a sales organisation is and explain its purpose in a business.

2.  Demonstrate an understanding of how to organise a sales team effectively.

3.  Identify different forms of specialisation within a sales team.

4.  Examine the various elements that affect the number of subordinates a manager can

oversee.

5.  Explain the roles of reporting lines and managerial supervision.

6.  Evaluate several types of sales structures and their respective advantages.

7.  Outline key influences when determining how a sales team should be structured.

8.  Discuss the benefits and drawbacks of decentralised training models.

9.  Consider how specialisation improves efficiency across different sales environments.

10. Differentiate between formal and informal structures in an organisational context.

Introduction

Unit 1: The Concept of a Sales Organisation

●  Purpose and role of a structured sales force

●  Connection between sales and marketing functions

●

Importance of structure for achieving organisational objectives

●  Characteristics of formal and informal structures

●  Features of a well-functioning sales team

Unit 2: Core Principles of Organising a Sales Department

●  Hierarchy of authority and clear lines of reporting

●  The concept and application of unity of command

●  Defining and understanding span of control

●  Differences between centralised and decentralised organisations

●  Principles supporting effective sales team structures

Unit 3: Sales Team Specialisation Models

●  Geographic specialisation: working by regional territories

●  Product/service-based specialisation for technical sales

●  Client or customer-based team organisation

●  Function-based divisions such as technical or service roles

●  Matrix structure combining multiple specialisation strategies

Unit 4: Strengths and Weaknesses of Specialisation

●  Benefits of personalisation in customer and client-based teams

●  Geographic focus and improved local responsiveness

●

In-depth product knowledge and expertise in product-based models

●  Potential issues with communication and role duplication

●  Flexibility and coordination challenges in matrix organisations

Unit 5: Elements That Shape Sales Team Design

●

Impact of company size and strategic goals on team structure

●  Customer base characteristics and product complexity

●  Role of available resources and distribution methods

●

Influence of management style and staff abilities

●  Considerations of cost, control, and scalability

Unit 6: Management Supervision and Control Factors

●  The role of unity of command in leadership clarity

●  Determinants of an optimal span of control

●  Delegation of responsibilities and authority

●  Relationship between control span and performance outcomes

●  Practical application of reporting structures in sales

Unit 7: Decentralised Structures and Training Practices

●  Characteristics of decentralised sales departments

●  Advantages of localised training and supervision

●  Drawbacks and inconsistencies in decentralised systems

●  Examples of companies using regional sales teams

●  Comparison of decentralised vs centralised training models

Chapter 2: Recruiting and Selecting

the Sales Force

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this chapter, students should be able to:

1.  Explain the importance of recruitment in building an effective sales team.

2.  Describe the main steps in the selection process for sales personnel.

3.  Identify both internal and external sources of recruitment.

4.  Analyse the purpose and structure of job descriptions and job specifications.

5.  Discuss the role of application forms, interviews, and reference checks in selection.

6.  Evaluate different types of psychological and aptitude assessments used during hiring.

7.  Understand ethical and professional considerations in recruitment practices.

8.  Explain how realistic performance expectations are set during recruitment.

9.  Discuss challenges involved in hiring from competitors and external markets.

10. Describe how effective selection leads to long-term performance and lower staff

turnover.

Introduction

Unit 1: Understanding Recruitment in Sales Management

●  Definition and purpose of recruitment in the sales context

●  Objectives of recruitment in relation to sales targets

●

Impact of poor recruitment on business outcomes

●  Link between recruitment and sales team performance

●  Strategic importance of planning the recruitment process

Unit 2: Sources of Recruitment for Sales Roles

●

Internal recruitment: promotions, transfers, and referrals

●  External recruitment: advertising, job fairs, recruitment agencies

●  Sourcing candidates from competitor and non-competitor firms

●  Online platforms and professional networks

●  Evaluating the pros and cons of each recruitment source

Unit 3: Sales Job Descriptions and Specifications

●  Elements of a detailed job description (duties, responsibilities)

●  Components of a job specification (skills, experience, qualifications)

●  The role of job analysis in creating accurate descriptions

●  Customising descriptions for specific sales positions

●

Importance of clarity in setting candidate expectations

Unit 4: The Salesperson Selection Process

●  Overview of the full selection process (from application to appointment)

●  Screening and shortlisting applications

●  Conducting initial and final interviews

●  Performing background and reference checks

●

Issuing offers and onboarding successful candidates

Unit 5: Application Forms and Interview Techniques

●  Purpose of standardised application forms in the selection process

●  Key information collected from applications (education, experience, skills)

●  Differences between structured and unstructured interviews

●  Techniques for conducting fair and effective interviews

●

Importance of interview preparation and follow-up

Unit 6: Psychological Testing and Evaluation Tools

●

Introduction to psychological and aptitude testing in recruitment

●  Types of assessments: personality, intelligence, and attitude tests

●  How testing predicts future sales performance

●  Ethical considerations and reliability of tests

●  Using test results alongside other evaluation methods

Unit 7: Ensuring Fairness and Fit During Selection

●  Communicating role expectations and performance standards

●  Assessing candidate alignment with organisational culture

●  Avoiding bias and discrimination in the hiring process

●  Evaluating motivation, attitude, and long-term potential

●  Retaining talent through careful hiring decisions

for Success

3.  Identify different methods used to assess training needs.

4.  Describe the content typically included in a sales training programme.

5.  Compare centralised and decentralised training approaches.

6.  Discuss the role of sales managers in delivering and supporting training.

7.  Evaluate the advantages and limitations of on-the-job training.

8.  Understand how training supports long-term performance improvement.

9.  Identify common challenges that affect training effectiveness.

10. Explain how training helps reduce mistakes and improve customer service.

Unit 1: The Importance of Sales Training

●  Definition and purpose of sales training in the business context

●  The link between training and company objectives

●  Benefits of training for individuals and the organisation

●

Impact of training on customer relations and service delivery

●  Role of training in adapting to market and technology changes

Unit 2: Identifying Sales Training Needs

●  Methods of identifying skill and knowledge gaps

●  Analysing performance feedback and customer complaints

●

Involving sales managers and staff in training evaluations

●  Aligning training needs with sales goals and strategies

Unit 3: Designing a Sales Training Programme

●  Core content areas: product knowledge, company values, and customer service

●  Structuring training for new recruits vs. experienced staff

●  Setting objectives and learning outcomes

●  Selecting training tools and instructional methods

●  Planning timelines, schedules, and resource allocation

Unit 4: Training Delivery Methods

●  Centralised vs. decentralised training: definitions and comparisons

●  Face-to-face classroom sessions and workshops

●  Online and blended learning formats

●  Mentorship, coaching, and shadowing as informal training

●  Choosing suitable methods based on learner and organisational needs

Unit 5: Role of Managers in Sales Training

●  Sales manager responsibilities in the training cycle

●  Providing feedback and performance reviews

●  Encouraging a learning culture and motivation

●  Leading by example: managers as trainers and role models

●  Defining on-the-job training (OJT) and when it is applied

●  Limitations and risks of informal training environments

●  Tips for improving OJT quality and supervision

Unit 7: Overcoming Training Challenges and Evaluating Impact

●  Common barriers to training (e.g. time, cost, motivation)

●  Criteria for evaluating training success

●  Tools for measuring performance improvements

●  Addressing low engagement and learning gaps

●  Making training sustainable and performance-driven

Chapter 4: Rewarding and Retaining

the Sales Force

Learning Outcomes

 By the end of this chapter, students should be able to:

1.  Explain the purpose of compensation in motivating and managing the sales team.

2.  Identify key objectives of a well-designed compensation system.

3.  Compare different types of financial and non-financial incentives.

4.  Describe various compensation methods, including salary, commission, and combination

plans.

5.  Understand the organisation’s perspective on sales compensation.

6.  Discuss the salesperson’s viewpoint on fair and motivating remuneration.

7.  Explain the role of fringe benefits and non-monetary rewards.

8.  Evaluate how compensation plans influence sales behaviour and performance.

9.  Understand the challenges in designing flexible yet effective compensation plans.

10. Identify factors that affect compensation decisions in different sales environments.

Introduction

Unit 1: Understanding the Purpose of Sales Compensation

●  Defining compensation and its importance in sales management

●  Objectives of compensation plans: motivation, control, and performance

●  Aligning compensation with company goals and strategies

●  Role of compensation in attracting and retaining talent

●  Linking compensation to productivity and customer satisfaction

Unit 2: Components of a Compensation Package

●  Direct financial rewards: salaries, commissions, and bonuses

●

Indirect financial benefits: allowances and fringe benefits

●  Non-financial rewards: recognition, promotion, and flexibility

●  Combining rewards to suit different sales roles and goals

●  Balancing financial and non-financial elements for long-term impact

Unit 3: Compensation Methods in Sales Management

●  Fixed salary (straight salary): features, pros, and cons

●  Straight commission: characteristics and use in performance-driven environments

●  Combination plans: blending salary and commission for flexibility

●  Bonuses and incentive schemes linked to targets or milestones

●  Choosing appropriate compensation methods for different industries

Unit 4: The Organisation’s Perspective on Compensation

●  Motivating the sales team to meet organisational objectives

●  Controlling sales activities and aligning efforts with company strategy

●  Ensuring fairness, competitiveness, and cost-effectiveness

●  Adapting to economic changes and business performance

●  Supporting compliance, ethics, and internal equity

Unit 5: The Salesperson’s Perspective on Compensation

●  Earning a stable and fair income

●  Receiving recognition for high performance

●  Opportunities for career progression and personal development

●  Preferences for flexibility and independence in earning

●  Ensuring transparency and understanding of how pay is calculated

Unit 6: Fringe Benefits and Non-Monetary Incentives

●  Definition and examples of fringe benefits (e.g. medical aid, company car)

●  Role of benefits in improving job satisfaction and loyalty

●  Types of non-monetary incentives: awards, time off, travel perks

●  Supporting work-life balance through non-financial rewards

●  Offering benefits that align with employee needs and values

Unit 7: Factors Influencing Compensation Decisions

●

Internal factors: company policies, budget, and sales structure

●  External factors: market conditions, industry standards, and legislation

●  Sales role type: technical, consultative, or transactional selling

●  Employee preferences and performance history

●  Adjusting compensation strategies for evolving business needs

Chapter 5: Monitoring and Measuring

Sales Success

Learning Outcomes

 By the end of this chapter, students should be able to:

1.  Explain the importance of performance evaluation in sales management.

2.  Identify different methods used to measure sales performance.

3.  Understand how to analyse sales volume data by product, region, and customer type.

4.  Describe how marketing costs are grouped, compared, and interpreted.

5.  Explain the use of performance metrics and targets in evaluation.

6.  Assess individual performance of sales representatives.

7.  Understand how evaluation tools guide sales team development.

8.  Discuss how evaluation helps in planning and budgeting.

9.  Identify the challenges in interpreting performance data.

10. Recommend improvements based on evaluation outcomes.

Unit 1: Purpose and Benefits of Sales Performance Evaluation

●  Goals of evaluation: accountability, improvement, motivation

●  Link between evaluation and team productivity

●  How evaluation supports decision-making and planning

●  Benefits for both sales managers and salespeople

Unit 2: Sales Volume Analysis Techniques

●  Analysing overall sales performance by volume

●  Measuring sales by product line and category

●  Comparing performance across different sales territories

●  Evaluating customer-specific sales data

●  Using market share data in volume analysis

Unit 3: Measuring and Categorising Marketing Costs

●  Defining marketing cost analysis in sales management

●  Grouping marketing expenses into relevant categories

●  Comparing cost data with budgets and previous performance

●

Interpreting cost-to-profit ratios

●

Identifying areas of unnecessary or high costs

Unit 4: Tools and Indicators for Sales Evaluation

●  Key performance indicators (KPIs) for measuring success

●  Setting realistic and measurable sales targets

●  Using sales growth, conversion rates, and profit margins

●  Analysing sales call effectiveness and client acquisition

●  Comparing actual results with planned outcomes

Unit 5: Evaluating Individual Sales Representatives

●  Using self-assessments and peer reviews

●  Conducting performance reviews and appraisals

Unit 6: Using Evaluation Results for Team Development

●  Guiding training and coaching based on evaluation

●  Adjusting team structure and territory assignments

●  Rewarding high performers and managing underperformance

●  Planning for promotions and career progression

●  Aligning evaluation outcomes with sales strategy

Unit 7: Budgeting and Planning Based on Performance Data

●  Role of performance data in preparing sales budgets

●  Forecasting future sales based on current trends

●  Allocating resources to productive areas

●

Identifying underperforming products or regions

●  Using evaluation to support strategic planning
