Unit 1.1: What is Management?

Definition: Quality  can  be understood as  the degree to which a set  of  inherent  char-
acteristics fulfills requirements. This includes meeting or exceeding customer expectations,
creating value, and ensuring reliability, efficiency, and satisfaction.

Key Points:

  Customer Expectations: Quality is about fulfilling or surpassing what customers

expect.

  Value Creation: Delivering  products  and  services  that  are worth  more to  custom-

ers than they cost.

  Reliability and Efficiency: Consistently providing products or services that func-

tion as intended without failures.

Reference: Read pages 12-13 of your textbook for more detailed definitions and exam-

ples.

1.2 Understanding Quality Management

Core  Principles:  Quality  management  integrates  various  principles  to  ensure excel-
lence in products and services. These principles include:

  Customer Focus:  Meeting  the needs  and  expectations  of  customers.

  Leadership: Establishing  a unified  direction  and  purpose.

  Engagement of People: Involving all  employees  in the process  of quality improve-

ment.

  Process  Approach:  Understanding  activities  as  processes  that  link  together.



Improvement: Continually  improving  processes  and  systems.

  Evidence-Based Decision Making: Making decisions  based on data and analysis.

  Relationship Management: Managing relationships with interested parties for

sustained success.

Reference: Read pages 13-14 of your textbook for a deeper dive into these principles.

1.3 Development of Quality Thinking

Historical Development: The concept of quality has  evolved over  time, moving
through various stages:

  Craftsmanship: Early  quality  was  ensured  by  individual  artisans.



Industrial Revolution: Introduction of mass production and the need for standard-
ization.

  Quality Control: Use of statistical methods to control quality during manufactur-

ing.

  Total Quality Management (TQM): Holistic approach  integrating  all  organizational

functions.

  Quality Standards and Awards:  Development  of  international  standards  (like ISO

9000) and quality awards (like the Malcolm Baldrige National Quality Award).

  Modern Initiatives: Methods such as Lean and Six Sigma focus on continuous im-

provement and waste reduction.

Key Theorists:

  Walter A. Shewhart: Introduced  statistical  quality control.

  W. Edwards Deming: Developed  the 14 Points  for  Management  and  the System  of

Profound Knowledge.

  Joseph M. Juran: Emphasized  the human  dimension  of  quality management.

  Philip B. Crosby:  Advocated  for  " zero  defects"  and  conformance to  requirements.

Reference: Pages 14-17 cover these developments and contributions in detail.

INTRODUCTION

Before delving into what Quality Management entails and how it can be implemented, it is
crucial to understand why it is necessary. Implementing Quality Management involves sig-
nificant effort and change, but the reasons for this investment are compelling. Generally,
the rationale for adopting Quality Management can be categorized into two main areas:

  Critiquing Existing Practices: Analyzing current methods to highlight inefficien-

cies, risks, and the limitations of maintaining the status quo. Without improvement, an
organization may fall behind in terms of efficiency, customer satisfaction, and competi-
tiveness.

  Highlighting Benefits of Change: Focusing on the advantages that Quality Man-

agement brings, including enhanced customer satisfaction, lower costs due to reduced
waste, improved organizational efficiency, and higher employee morale. It also empha-
sizes long-term gains in market share and reputation from consistent quality improve-
ments.

Understanding these reasons helps stakeholders see the value in committing to Quality
Management initiatives and supports the case for the necessary resources and changes
involved.

2.1 What is Wrong with Traditional Approaches?

Traditional approaches to management and production often lag behind the needs of mod-
ern, dynamic business environments. These outdated methods typically focus on maintain-
ing order and consistency at the expense of flexibility, innovation, and genuine engagement
with both customers and employees. Below are some deficiencies identified in traditional
approaches and why a shift towards more contemporary Quality Management strategies is
critical:

Lack of Leadership:

Traditional management often revolves around rigid structures and strict adherence to pro-
cedures. Effective leadership requires a vision that embraces change, encourages innova-
tion, and inspires the entire organization.

Short-Term Focus:

A prevalent issue in traditional business models is the focus on short-term financial goals,
which can prevent essential investments in technology, personnel, and process improve-
ments.

Lack of Customer Focus:

The 'Product-Out' mindset prioritizes product specifications over customer satisfaction.
Modern markets demand a 'Market-In' approach where customer feedback and needs drive
the development process.

Cost/Quality Trade-off:

The myth that quality is always costly prevents organizations from investing in quality im-
provement initiatives that could save money in the long run. Emphasizing preventive
measures rather than corrective actions can significantly reduce overall costs.

Lack of Systems Thinking:

Traditional compartmentalization within organizations hinders effective communication and
process integration, leading to inefficiencies and missed opportunities for quality improve-
ments.

2.2 Tangible Benefits

Quality Management offers several tangible benefits that can significantly improve an or-
ganization's performance and competitiveness. These benefits include:

Cost Savings:

Reducing waste and inefficiencies through process optimization and error minimization can
decrease production costs and increase profitability.

Enhanced Productivity:

Implementing quality management principles helps streamline processes and eliminate non
-value-added activities, leading to higher productivity levels.

Improved Customer Satisfaction:

Delivering high-quality products and services that meet or exceed customer expectations
can enhance customer satisfaction, leading to repeat business and positive word-of-mouth.

Market Share and Competitiveness:

Consistently high-quality offerings can improve an organization's market share and compet-
itive position by differentiating it from competitors.

Improved Workplace Atmosphere:

A focus on quality fosters a culture of excellence, where employees are motivated to contrib-
ute to continuous improvement efforts.

Stronger Brand Reputation:

Consistently delivering high-quality products and services can build a strong brand reputation,
making it easier to attract new customers and retain existing ones.

Higher Employee Morale:

Engaging employees in quality improvement initiatives can boost their morale and job satis-
faction, leading to lower turnover rates and higher productivity.

INTRODUCTION

Dr. W. Edwards Deming is often considered a founding father of the modern quality move-
ment, thanks to his transformative vision that goes beyond systematic approaches to em-
brace a comprehensive reform of management practices. Deming argued that many busi-
ness problems are due to ineffective management rather than poor workmanship, suggest-
ing that management's approach can either make or break quality improvement efforts.

Deming's prominence in the field began with his work in Japan post-World War II, where he
helped rebuild the country's shattered economy. His principles led Japanese businesses to
dominate the global manufacturing scene for decades, a success story often referred to as
the "Chain Reaction." In recognition of his impact, Japan established the Deming Prize,
awarded to individuals and companies that excel in quality improvement.

Deming's key contributions to Quality Management include:

  The Fourteen Points for Transformation of an Organisation: Guidelines to help

businesses improve operations and quality.

  The Seven Deadly Diseases of Organisations: Common management obstacles

that hinder a company's success.

  The System of Profound Knowledge: A philosophy that encourages  understand-
ing the interdependencies within an organization to effectively manage for quality.

Reference: Read pages 30-31 of your textbook for a deeper introduction.

3.1 The 14 Points

Deming’s philosophy on Quality Management is encapsulated in 14 key principles, crucial
for fundamentally transforming management practices in any type of organization, regard-
less of size, sector, or scope. These principles are designed not just to tweak existing pro-
cedures but to revolutionize the entire approach to management, leading to profound im-
provements in quality and efficiency.

Create Constancy of Purpose: Focus on long-term goals and sustainable growth rather
than short-term financial gains.

Adopt the New Philosophy: Embrace quality  in  every  aspect  of  the business.

Cease Dependence on Mass Inspection: Build quality into the process from the be-
ginning.

End the Practice of Awarding Business on Price Tag Alone: Consider total cost of
ownership.

Improve Constantly and Forever: Seek continuous improvement in every aspect of
production and services.

Institute  Training  on  the  Job:  Ensure  all  employees  understand  their  roles  and  quali-
ty standards.

Institute Leadership: Inspire and  guide employees  rather  than  just  supervise.

Drive Out Fear: Create a workplace where employees  feel  safe to  express  concerns.

Break Down Barriers Between Departments: Encourage interdepartmental  collabora-
tion.

Eliminate Slogans and Targets:  Set  realistic, achievable goals.

Eliminate Numerical Quotas: Focus  on  quality  and  the processes  that  create it.

Remove Barriers to Pride of Workmanship: Provide the right tools and a supportive
environment.

Institute a Vigorous Programme of Education and Self-Improvement: Invest in con-
tinuous learning.

Take Action to Accomplish the Transformation: Align the entire organization with
new principles.

Reference: Read pages 32-34 of your textbook for detailed explanations of these princi-

ples.

3.2 The Deadly Diseases

Dr. W. Edwards Deming identified several significant obstacles, termed "Deadly Diseases,"
that can severely hamper an organization's progress towards becoming world-class. Un-
derstanding these is crucial for any organization aiming for long-term success.

Lack of Constancy of Purpose: Focusing too much on short-term gains prevents sus-
tained improvement.

Emphasis on Short-Term Profits: Neglecting long-term strategic benefits for quick finan-

Evaluation of Performance – Annual Review: Annual reviews can discourage innova-

3.3 The System of Profound Knowledge

Dr. Deming introduced a holistic framework called the System of Profound Knowledge (SoPK)
for managing organizations. This system emphasizes a deep understanding and integration
of four key areas:

Systems Thinking: Viewing  the organization  as  a whole and  understanding  how  differ-
ent elements interact and influence each other.

Variation:  Understanding  and  managing  variation  to  improve  process  reliability.

Psychology: Addressing psychological factors  to  ensure employee engagement  and
satisfaction.

Theory of Knowledge: Continuously testing  and  adapting  strategies  based  on  learned
knowledge.
