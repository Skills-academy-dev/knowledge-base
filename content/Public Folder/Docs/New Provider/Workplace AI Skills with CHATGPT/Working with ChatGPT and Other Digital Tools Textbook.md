Here’s a simple 3-step formula to guide your prompt:
Step 1: State the Purpose Clearly
Begin your email by explicitly stating its main purpose so the recipient immediately understands
the reason for the communication. Indicate whether you are making a request, sending an
invitation, filing a complaint, providing a response, or addressing another matter. A concise and
direct opening line can set the tone for the entire message and help avoid confusion. For
example, you could write, "I am writing to request your assistance with..." or "This email serves
as a formal response to..."
Step 2: Identify the Audience and Tone
Think about who will be reading your email and tailor your tone accordingly. If you're writing to a
supervisor, client, or someone you don’t know well, a formal and respectful tone is appropriate.
If you're addressing a colleague or team member with whom you have a good rapport, a polite
and friendly tone may be more effective. The tone you choose should reflect the context of the
message and help foster clear and constructive communication.

The Importance of Plain Language in Communication
Plain language is a style of communication that prioritizes clarity and simplicity, making it
accessible to a broader audience. The goal is to ensure that the message can be understood by
anyone, regardless of their background, expertise, or level of familiarity with the subject matter.

Using plain language helps eliminate confusion, reduces misunderstandings, and makes your
message more inclusive.
In today’s fast-paced world, people often have limited time to read through long, convoluted
emails or documents. As such, plain language is not just about simplifying words it’s about
communicating efficiently, respecting your audience's time, and making your content as
user-friendly as possible.
Key Principles of Plain Language
1. Use Short, Simple Sentences:
Break down long, complex sentences into shorter, more digestible ones. This helps
readers follow your message without getting lost in excess information. Aim for
sentences that are straightforward and to the point.
Example:
○ Complex: “Given the intricate nature of this project, it is imperative that we
carefully assess all potential risks before proceeding with any further action.”
○ Plain: “We need to assess all potential risks before continuing with the project.”
2. Avoid Jargon and Technical Terms (When Possible):
Whenever possible, replace industry-specific jargon with simpler alternatives. While
technical terms may be necessary in some contexts, always consider whether your
reader will understand them.
Example:
○ Jargon: “We will implement a multi-phase agile process for this development
cycle.”
○ Plain: “We will use a step-by-step process to complete this development
21 | Page

3. Use Active Voice:
Active voice makes sentences clearer and more direct. Passive voice often makes the
subject of the sentence unclear or vague. Use active voice to put the focus on the action
and who is performing it.
Example:
○ Passive: “The report will be reviewed by the manager tomorrow.”
○ Active: “The manager will review the report tomorrow.”
4. Be Concise and Remove Unnecessary Words:
Eliminate words that don’t add value to the message. The shorter and more focused the
sentence, the clearer it will be.
Example:
○ Wordy: “Because the meeting is scheduled to take place tomorrow, we would like
to ask for your confirmation.”
○ Concise: “Please confirm your attendance for tomorrow’s meeting.”
5. Use Bullet Points and Headings for Structure:
Plain language is also about structure. By breaking down your information into bullet
points or using headings, you make your message easier to read and understand at a
glance.
Example:
○ Before: “The meeting will cover the following points: the status update, team
feedback, and next steps.”

1.2.2 Restructure Sentences for Flow and Clarity
Just because a sentence is grammatically correct doesn’t mean it’s easy to read. Sometimes,
writing feels stiff, clunky, or hard to follow, especially when you're trying to cram too many ideas
into one paragraph or struggling to connect thoughts naturally. That's where ChatGPT can
shine: it helps you untangle complex or awkward sentences and rephrase them into smoother,
clearer versions.
Restructuring isn't about changing your message; it's about delivering that message in a way
that flows logically, feels natural to read, and gets straight to the point. Whether you're writing for
colleagues, customers, or leadership, a clear structure can make your content more persuasive
and easier to digest.
Why Sentence Restructuring Matters:
● Improves reader comprehension and reduces cognitive load
● Makes writing sound more professional and confident
● Helps your ideas come across in the right order and tone
● Increases engagement, especially in long or information-heavy emails
Prompt Example:
“Can you rephrase this paragraph to improve the flow and clarity while keeping the
meaning the same?”
What ChatGPT Can Do:
● Break up long sentences into smaller, more manageable ones
Before: “We have received your application, and after reviewing your qualifications, we
have decided to move forward with the interview process, which will begin next week.”
After: “We’ve received your application. After reviewing your qualifications, we’ve decided
to move forward with the interview process, starting next week.

● Link disconnected ideas with transition words and logical connectors.
“The report is complete. It hasn’t been approved yet.” → “The report is complete, but it
hasn’t been approved yet.”
● Eliminate redundancy and filler words.
“We are currently in the process of working on the development of the new project.” →
“We’re currently developing the new project.”
● Clarify vague or generic language.
“I think this might be a bit of an issue.” → “This could cause delays in the project
timeline.”

Clear writing is effective writing. When your message is packed with technical terms, industry
jargon, or overly formal language, it can confuse or even alienate your audience, especially if
they’re not subject-matter experts. Whether you're communicating across departments, writing
for clients, or creating public-facing content, it’s important to make your ideas as easy to
understand as possible.
ChatGPT can act as your translator, converting complex or specialized language into plain
English without watering down your message. It’s like having an editor who’s always ready to
help you explain things in a way that resonates with everyone.
