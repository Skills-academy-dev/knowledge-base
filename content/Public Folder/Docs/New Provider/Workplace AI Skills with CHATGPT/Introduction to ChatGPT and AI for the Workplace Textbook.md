1.1.1 Definition of AI

Artificial Intelligence (AI) refers to machines or computer systems that are built to copy human

intelligence. These systems can:

•

•

•

•

Solve problems

Understand language

Recognise images or voices

Learn from data to improve their actions

AI does not “think” like a human. Instead, it processes large amounts of information, finds

patterns, and uses logical rules to make decisions.

Key characteristics of AI:

•

•

•

•

Learning – AI can improve its performance over time by using new data. This is known

as machine learning.

Problem-solving – AI can analyse problems and suggest or choose the best solution.

Reasoning – AI uses logic and rules to make choices.

Understanding Language – AI systems can read or listen to human language and

respond meaningfully.

It’s important to understand that AI is not a human brain. It doesn’t have emotions, beliefs, or

1.1.2 Types of AI (narrow, general)

AI can be divided into two main types, depending on how powerful it is and what it can do:

1.  Narrow AI (Also called Weak AI)

This is the most common form of AI today. It is created to do one specific task—and it does that

task very well.

Examples of Narrow AI:

•

•

•

•

Google Maps – Finds the fastest route for your journey.

Netflix – Recommends shows and movies you might like.

Face ID – Unlocks your phone by recognising your face.

Email spam filters – Moves unwanted emails to your spam folder.

2.  General AI (Also called Strong AI)

This type of AI does not exist yet. It would be able to think, learn, and make decisions like a

human being—and even do multiple different tasks.

Example (not yet real):

•  A robot that can teach a class, clean the house, drive a car, and write a book—all by itself.

information to create new text when asked.

1.3.1 How it “talks” Using Patterns

ChatGPT does not think like a person. It doesn’t know things. Instead, it uses patterns in

language to guess what words should come next in a sentence.

Simple Explanation: ChatGPT is like a super-powered version of predictive text on your

phone. It finishes your thoughts based on examples it has seen before.

Example: It works like a very advanced version of the predictive text on your phone. When

you type “Thank you for...”, it might suggest “your help” or “your time” based on what usually

comes next in similar sentences.

What Happens When You Type a Message

1.3.2 Language Models in Simple Terms

ChatGPT is a language model, which means it is trained to understand and generate text. It

uses a method called machine learning to study how language is used and to create sentences

that sound natural.

Simple Definition: A language model is like a super reader and writer. It studies how people

use language, and then uses that knowledge to create its own writing.

 Language Model = AI that understands and creates human-like text

1.3.3 The Role of Prompts and Responses

A prompt is your question, instruction, or message to ChatGPT. A response is what ChatGPT

gives you in return. The quality of the response depends on how clear and detailed your prompt

is. Just like giving instructions to a friend or colleague, the more specific you are, the better the

results.

If your prompt is clear and specific, ChatGPT can give you accurate, useful, and well -structured

responses. But if your prompt is too short, vague, or general, the answer might be confusing,

too broad, or not what you were expecting.

ChatGPT works by taking your typed message, analysing it, and then giving a

human-like response. It all starts with a good prompt.

Why Prompts Matter

ChatGPT is only as helpful as your instructions. If your prompt is:

•

•

Clear and specific – The response will be accurate and useful.

Vague or general – The answer may be too broad, off-topic, or confusing.
