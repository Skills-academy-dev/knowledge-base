Unit 1: What is Chat GPT?

ChatGPT is an AI-powered tool that uses something called a "Large Language Model" to understand what people type and give helpful answers. Think of it like a very smart robot that can have conversations, answer questions, explain ideas, and even write essays. Developed by OpenAI, ChatGPT has been trained on a massive amount of information from books, websites, and other texts so that it can respond in a human-like way.

Many distance learning students use ChatGPT to save time, ask questions they’re too shy to ask a teacher, or get help understanding difficult topics. Since ChatGPT works through typing, it can be accessed from a phone, laptop, or tablet—making it perfect for studying from home or while on the go. It’s like having a virtual study buddy who never gets tired.

10 | Page

It’s important to know that ChatGPT doesn’t know everything, and it doesn’t think like a human. It doesn’t have feelings or opinions. Instead, it predicts what to say based on patterns it has learned. This means sometimes it gives answers that are incorrect or outdated, especially if the question is unclear or too broad.

Even with its limits, ChatGPT is still very helpful. It’s especially good at summarizing texts, answering questions about general knowledge, helping improve writing, and giving examples or explanations when you’re stuck. In the rest of this unit, we’ll look at the different parts that make up ChatGPT and how they work together to support learning.

ChatGPT is built on a Large Language Model (LLM)

The Large Language Model is what gives ChatGPT its power. It’s like the brain of the system, trained on billions of words so it can understand and generate human-like language. This means that when a question is asked, it can "predict" the best possible answer based on what it has learned. It does not search the internet like Google; instead, it uses what it already knows from its training.

For example, if asked, "Explain how photosynthesis works," ChatGPT will give a response based on textbooks and articles it has read during training. However, it won’t know if a new discovery about photosynthesis was made last month, because its training only goes up to 2023.

For distance learners, this is helpful because it allows for fast and easy explanations of concepts found in textbooks. It’s like having a mini tutor who explains things in plain language. But remember, it’s still important to check the information using your own notes or other sources.

It uses Natural Language Processing (NLP) to understand your questions

Natural Language Processing, or NLP, helps ChatGPT break down words and understand meaning. It allows ChatGPT to turn a question into something it can work with. For example, if asked, “Can you help me write a paragraph about climate change?” it knows this is a request for writing help on a specific topic.

NLP also lets ChatGPT adjust to different tones. If asked to explain something like you’re five years old, it will use simple words and examples. This is great for revising difficult topics in a way that makes sense. If asked in a more formal way, it can also match that tone and structure.

You can use NLP to your advantage by being specific. The clearer the question or instruction is, the better the answer will be. For instance, instead of saying, "Tell me about math," say, "Explain how to calculate percentages using an example."

ChatGPT interacts with you through conversation-based responses

One of the best things about ChatGPT is that it’s interactive. You can ask follow-up questions or ask it to explain something in a different way. This back-and-forth process makes it feel like a real conversation.

For example, if ChatGPT gives an answer that is unclear, you can reply with, "Can you explain that more simply?" or "Give me a real-world example." It will try to adjust its answer to meet your needs. This makes it very flexible and personal, just like talking to a tutor.

Distance learners often feel isolated or unsure. With ChatGPT, there’s no need to wait for someone to reply to an email. You can get help instantly and keep the conversation going until you feel confident.

It is trained to be helpful, but it has limitations

ChatGPT is not perfect. It sometimes gives wrong answers, especially if the question is confusing or very new. It may also give answers that sound correct but are made up—this is called “hallucination.” For example, it might say a famous person said something they never did.

That’s why it’s important to check the answers you get and not just copy them. Always compare ChatGPT’s responses with your study material, and ask a tutor if something doesn’t feel right. Use ChatGPT as a guide, not the final answer.

As you learn to use ChatGPT, you’ll get better at spotting when an answer is helpful and when it needs a little more checking. This is part of becoming a smart, independent learner— which is what distance learning is all about.

In this unit, we explored what ChatGPT is and why it’s useful for distance learning. We looked at its main parts: the Large Language Model, Natural Language Processing, interactive conversation style, and its limitations. Each of these features supports learning, but only if used wisely.

Key Components of ChatGPT – OpenAI Model

1\. Transformer Architecture

• Based on the Transformer model introduced by Vaswani et al. (2017).

• Uses self-attention mechanisms to process and generate language by weighing the importance of each word in context.

• Core engine powering GPT (Generative Pretrained Transformer).

2\. Pre-training Phase

• Trained on a massive corpus of publicly available text (books, websites, forums, etc.).

• Objective: Predict the next word in a sentence based on prior words (unsupervised learning).

18 | Page

• This phase teaches ChatGPT the “language of the internet.”

3\. Tokenization

• Breaks down text into manageable units called tokens (words or parts of words).

• GPT-4 can handle longer sequences of tokens than earlier versions.

• Essential for processing and understanding language in chunks.

4\. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)

• After pre-training, the model is fine-tuned using human feedback.

• Humans rank responses to guide the model toward producing more helpful, safe, and accurate answers.

• This step teaches the model manners, safety, and user preference.

5\. Prompt-Response Framework

• Operates by receiving a user prompt and predicting the best continuation.

• No memory between sessions (unless enabled in tools like ChatGPT Plus with custom instructions).

• Conversation happens one message at a time, with each prompt steering the reply.

6\. Fine-Tuning for Safety and Instruction Following

• Extra training using custom datasets to help the model follow instructions better.

• Also includes content filtering and bias reduction layers.

• This is why it avoids harmful or inappropriate content.

7\. Context Awareness (within session)

• ChatGPT uses the text in the conversation history to maintain context and coherence.

• However, it has a context window limit (e.g., \~8k–32k tokens for GPT-4).

• It remembers within a session, but not across sessions unless memory is active.

19 | Page

8\. No Live Web Access

• ChatGPT (base model) has no real-time internet browsing.

• It relies on static knowledge from its training data (cut-off date varies by version).

• Unless browsing is enabled (e.g., via plugin), it can't fetch current news or updates.

9\. Multimodal Capabilities (GPT-4 Only)

• GPT-4 can process both text and images (in tools that support it).

• Enables richer, visual question answering and image analysis.

• Used in ChatGPT Plus and some enterprise apps.

10\. Limitations and Hallucinations

• Can produce inaccurate, biased, or fictional responses.

• Doesn't “know” or “understand” like humans—it predicts plausible text.

• Always needs human oversight for critical tasks.

Unit 2: The Anatomy of a Good Prompt

In the world of AI interaction, the quality of the response is often determined by the quality of the prompt. A well-structured, clear, and detailed prompt helps ChatGPT understand your intent and generate useful responses. This becomes especially important in distance learning, where students rely on the tool for clarity, explanation, and tailored guidance. Knowing how to craft a good prompt is a foundational skill that empowers learners to take full advantage of AI technology.

Understanding the anatomy of a good prompt means more than just asking questions. It involves thinking about how information is framed, the kind of context you provide, and how specific you are in your instructions. Much like asking a teacher for help in class, vague questions often lead to vague answers. The clearer you are, the more valuable the help you receive.

This unit will explore the structure of effective prompts, offering practical examples and comparing them with poorly formed ones. Learners will discover the impact that small changes in wording or detail can make, and how to troubleshoot when ChatGPT gives unhelpful or generic answers. These skills are particularly helpful for students using ChatGPT for writing, studying, and research.

By the end of this unit, you’ll be able to write stronger prompts that improve your learning experience. Whether you're drafting essays, solving maths problems, or practicing for exams, the ability to communicate clearly with AI tools like ChatGPT will help you study more efficiently and confidently.

Clarity is Key

A clear prompt sets the foundation for a useful answer. When asking ChatGPT a question or giving it a task, it's important to avoid vague or overly broad language. For example, instead of saying "Tell me about history," it’s better to say "Explain the causes of World War I in simple terms." Clarity helps the AI focus on what you're really asking and reduces the chances of receiving irrelevant or overly general information.

23 | Page

Clear prompts also save time. Rather than needing multiple follow-up questions, you’re more likely to receive the right answer the first time. This is especially helpful in distance learning, where students may be studying alone without immediate access to a tutor. If your prompt is clear, you can rely more on ChatGPT to fill in learning gaps or explain confusing concepts.

Additionally, clear prompts reduce the likelihood of misinterpretation. Since AI models don’t think like humans, they rely entirely on the input they are given. A lack of clarity can lead to unexpected or even incorrect answers, which could confuse or mislead the student. Therefore, choosing words carefully and being specific improves your overall interaction.

A good way to test the clarity of your prompt is to imagine someone reading it with no background knowledge. If it still makes sense and includes enough detail, it's likely to be a strong prompt. If not, adding just a few more words or examples can make a big difference in the quality of the response.

Be Specific with Instructions

Specific prompts guide ChatGPT more effectively. When you say exactly what you want, the AI can tailor its response accordingly. For instance, asking "Write a summary of Chapter 3 in 150 words" is far better than simply asking for "a summary." The more specific you are with the format, length, tone, or focus, the more useful the output becomes.

This also applies to tone and style. If you need a formal email, a creative story, or a simple explanation for a Grade 8 learner, including that in the prompt ensures the response matches your needs. In distance learning, where assignments and tasks vary widely, being specific helps you get responses that suit the situation.

Moreover, specificity reduces the number of edits or retries. If your first prompt gives you exactly what you need, you save time and energy. This can help reduce frustration for students and encourage independent learning, since they’ll see results quicker and be more likely to use ChatGPT again for help.

24 | Page

Examples of specific prompts include: "Explain the water cycle using a diagram and short bullet points," or "Write a two-paragraph answer using academic language." These types of instructions give ChatGPT clear boundaries, helping it generate content that is not only correct but also appropriately formatted and focused.

Use Examples

In your prompts Include examples to help ChatGPT understand the kind of answer you're expecting. For example, if you ask, "Write a summary like this one..." and include a sample, the AI can mirror the tone, structure, and style more closely. This technique is particularly helpful for learning formats, such as essays, reports, and study notes.

In distance learning, students may not always know how to format their work or write in a specific tone. By showing ChatGPT what you want, you can get responses that are more aligned with your school or college expectations. Examples make your request less abstract and more grounded in a real outcome.

This is also a great way to train ChatGPT over time. If you regularly show it how you want things done, it starts to produce content that fits your personal learning style. This makes it a more personalized and effective study assistant.

When using this approach, be sure the example you include is well-written and relevant. A weak or unclear example can confuse the AI and result in poor output. But a strong, focused sample can elevate the quality of what you receive in return.

Avoid Overloading the Prompt

While being detailed is important, too much information in a single prompt can be overwhelming and reduce the clarity of the request. For example, asking ChatGPT to "Explain photosynthesis, compare it to cellular respiration, and write a test question, all in one go" may result in an unfocused or messy response. It’s better to break complex requests into smaller, manageable parts.

In distance learning, students often try to maximize the value of their questions by including too many tasks at once. This usually backfires, as the AI might prioritize one part and neglect the others. Segmenting your requests improves the accuracy and relevance of the information you receive.

25 | Page

Also, when you give too many instructions at once, ChatGPT may generalize its response to cover all bases, making it less useful. The goal is precision, not overload. Asking one clear question at a time ensures that each part of your learning is handled properly.

A good strategy is to use follow-up questions. Begin with one part of your request, get the response, then ask for the next section. This keeps the conversation focused and ensures each part is addressed thoroughly, which is ideal for studying or preparing assignments.

Understanding how to write a good prompt is a key skill in unlocking the full potential of ChatGPT for distance learning. Clear, specific, and well-structured prompts lead to better, more relevant responses that support academic success. By learning to use examples and avoiding overloaded requests, students can communicate more effectively with AI tools. This helps build confidence, encourages independent learning, and enhances overall digital literacy in the modern classroom.

Ask for a Specific Role or Persona

Prompting ChatGPT to “act as” a particular expert or persona can drastically change the quality of the response. For example, you can say:

“Act as a maths teacher and explain fractions to a Grade 7 learner.”

This sets the tone and expertise level expected, which is especially useful in distance learning where students may want simplified or expert-level explanations.

Set the Goal or Output Type

Make it clear what type of output you need: a list, a paragraph, a table, a script, or a mind map. For instance:

“List five pros and cons of online learning in table format.”

Defining the format helps avoid confusion and gives you exactly what you need the first time around.

Include Keywords or Concepts to Focus On

Sometimes you want the answer to revolve around specific ideas. You can include a phrase like:

“Focus on the economic and environmental impact of tourism.”

This ensures that ChatGPT doesn’t drift too far from the main theme or go off on a tangent.

26 | Page

Ask for Step-by-Step Explanations

If you’re solving a problem or learning something new, you can ask ChatGPT to break it down:

“Explain the steps of photosynthesis one by one with examples.”

This works great for technical topics or step-based procedures.

Good Prompt Example (Built with All Key Steps)

“Act as a Business Studies tutor. I’m preparing for a test on entrepreneurship and need a summary of the key traits of successful entrepreneurs. Please provide a summary in bullet point format (no more than 7 points), using simple language suitable for a Grade 11 learner. Include one local South African entrepreneur as an example, and make sure the explanation is easy to understand for someone revising for an exam.”

Unit 3: Using Context to Improve Responses

When interacting with AI like ChatGPT, context plays a vital role in determining the quality and usefulness of the response. Context helps the AI understand who you are, what you’re trying to do, and what kind of information you’re looking for. Without it, the AI has to guess — and that can lead to generic, confusing, or incomplete answers. Providing clear context allows the AI to tailor its replies more accurately to your needs.

In distance learning, where students often work independently, giving context helps bridge the gap between learner and instructor. If a student simply types, “Explain this,” the AI won’t know what “this” refers to unless the previous conversation or subject is included. Context clarifies not only the topic, but also the level of difficulty, format, and purpose of the request. This is especially important when preparing assignments or revising for exams.

Good context transforms a vague question into a focused conversation. It gives the AI a reference point, like telling a teacher, “This is what I’ve learned so far,” before asking for more help. It also allows the AI to maintain continuity in longer conversations — keeping track of what has been said and building on it instead of starting fresh each time.

This unit will explore how to give context effectively when using ChatGPT. You’ll learn how to frame your requests in a way that improves understanding, how to use previous questions and answers to shape your next prompt, and how to make your learning experience smoother and more productive with well-applied context.

For example, if you’re working on a project and previously asked about the history of tourism, your next prompt can be: “Based on the history you just provided, how has tourism changed in South Africa since 1994?” This kind of prompt makes the conversation feel continuous and intelligent.

Referencing previous input prevents repeated explanations and builds a more logical flow. This is especially helpful in subjects like Business Studies, Life Sciences, or Literature where concepts often build on one another. Instead of re-explaining the topic, ChatGPT can focus on moving forward — which mirrors a good classroom discussion.

In distance learning, this continuity makes learning feel more natural and less robotic. Students can revisit earlier questions and use them as foundations for new ones. For example, after learning about budgeting, a student might ask, “How would this budget apply to a small tourism business in Limpopo?”

The more you refer to what was said before, the more intelligent and helpful the AI seems. It also shows that you’re actively engaging in your learning process — connecting ideas and deepening your understanding.

Specify Your Learning Goal or Purpose

Telling ChatGPT what you want to achieve helps it focus its response. For instance, if you say, “I need to write a paragraph about climate change for my Geography exam,” the AI understands that you’re looking for academic, exam-ready content. Without that goal, you might get a casual or overly complex response that doesn’t help you study effectively.

Learning goals create a direction for the conversation. Are you writing a summary? Trying to solve a math equation? Preparing a speech? ChatGPT can adapt its style, language, and structure to meet your objective — but only if it knows what that objective is. Including this detail improves the match between your need and the response you receive.

This is also a great way to use ChatGPT in self-study. Instead of just asking for definitions, you can say, “Help me revise for my test on food chains by explaining how energy flows between organisms.” This not only gives context but also makes your learning more active.
