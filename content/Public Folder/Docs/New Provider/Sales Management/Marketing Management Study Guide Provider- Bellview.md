PRODUCT AND SERVICE POLICY
Learning Outcomes
By the end of this module, you should be able to:
1. Define the role of product and service policies in marketing.
2. Explain the difference between products and services.
3. Identify the stages of the product life cycle and their impact on marketing strategies.
4. Describe the importance of branding in product differentiation.
5. Understand the role of customer needs and expectations in product development.
6. Analyze how packaging and labeling influence consumer decisions.
7. Examine the significance of product positioning and competitive advantage.
8. Identify various product classifications and their characteristics.
9. Discuss the role of innovation in product and service marketing.
10. Evaluate strategies for managing product portfolios.
11. Apply knowledge of market research to improve product and service offerings.
12. Assess ethical considerations in product and service development.

Introduction:
Marketing is more than just selling a product—it is about creating value, understanding consumer
needs, and developing strategies that build strong relationships between brands and customers. In
today’s competitive business landscape, companies must continuously innovate to stay relevant and
meet evolving consumer demands. This module introduces the fundamentals of marketing manage-
ment, exploring essential concepts such as market research, consumer behavior, and strategic
planning. By the end of this module, learners will have a strong foundation in marketing principles,
setting the stage for more advanced topics ahead.
Unit 1: Product Development & Innovation (Page 9-18, Marketing Man-
agement Advance Textbook)
● Definition of Product Development: The process of designing and launching new products.
● Stages of New Product Development (NPD): Idea generation, screening, concept testing, proto-
typing, market testing, and commercialization.
● Innovation in Marketing: The importance of innovation in maintaining competitive advantage.
● Factors Influencing Product Development: Market research, technological advancements, and
consumer preferences.
● Real-World Applications: Case studies of businesses that have successfully introduced innova-
tive products .
Unit 2: Product Life Cycle & Market Adaptation (Page 19-22, Marketing
Management Advance Textbook )
● Understanding the Product Life Cycle (PLC): The four stages—Introduction, Growth, Maturity,
and Decline.
● Marketing Strategies for Each Stage: Adapting product, pricing, promotion, and distribution at
different PLC stages.
● Extending the Product Life Cycle: Strategies such as rebranding, entering new markets, or
modifying product features.
● Market Adaptation Techniques: Adjusting marketing strategies to fit different consumer seg-
ments and industry trends.
● Case Studies: Examples of brands that successfully extended their product life cycle .

Introduction (Pages 47-48, Marketing Management Advance Textbook)
Distribution plays a crucial role in marketing, ensuring that products and services reach consumers
efficiently. Businesses must choose the right distribution strategies based on their target market,
product type, and logistical capabilities.
This module explores different distribution channels, including direct, indirect, digital, and omni-
channel approaches. It also examines key aspects of supply chain management, logistics, and
business expansion strategies such as franchising and international market entry. By the end
of this module, students will have a thorough understanding of how distribution policies impact busi-
ness success.
Unit 1: Understanding Distribution Channels & Strategies (Pages 48-49,
Marketing Management Advance Textbook)
● Definition of Distribution Channels: The role of distribution in moving products from manufactur-
ers to consumers.
● Types of Distribution: ○ Direct Distribution: Selling directly to consumers via company-owned
stores or online platforms.
○ Indirect Distribution: Using intermediaries such as wholesalers, retailers, and agents.
○ Omnichannel Distribution: Integrating online and offline sales channels for seamless cus-
tomer experiences.
● Strategic Considerations: Cost, efficiency, customer preferences, and competitive advantage.
Unit 2: Retail & Wholesale Distribution Models (Pages 53-54, Marketing
Management Advance Textbook)
● Retail Distribution: Selling directly to end consumers through physical stores, e-commerce plat-
forms, or pop-up shops.
● Wholesale Distribution: Selling products in bulk to retailers, who then sell to consumers.
● Hybrid Models: Many businesses use a mix of retail and wholesale approaches to optimize mar-
ket reach.
Unit 3: Supply Chain & Inventory Management (Pages 58-59, Marketing
Management Advance Textbook)
● Logistics & Transportation: Managing the movement of goods from production to the end con-
sumer.

Introduction:
Pricing is one of the most critical elements of the marketing mix because it directly impacts a compa-
ny's profitability, competitive positioning, and customer perception. Businesses must determine
their pricing strategies based on costs, competition, market demand, and consumer psychology.
This module explores different pricing models, including cost-based, value-based, and psychologi-
cal pricing. It also examines market entry pricing strategies, premium pricing for brand posi-
tioning, price adjustments, and digital pricing models such as dynamic pricing and subscription-
based approaches. By the end of this module, students will have a thorough understanding of pricing
as a strategic tool in marketing.
Unit 1: Pricing Strategies & Market Demand (Pages 83-85, Marketing Man-
agement Advance Textbook)
● Definition of pricing strategies and their importance in marketing.
● Factors influencing market demand: ○ Consumer preferences & perceived value.
○ Price elasticity of demand.
○ Competitor pricing and market conditions.
● Aligning pricing decisions with business objectives, market trends, and customer expectations.
Unit 2: Cost-Based vs. Value-Based Pricing (Pages 86-88, Marketing Man-
agement Advance Textbook)
● Cost-Based Pricing: ○ Cost-plus pricing, break-even pricing, and target return pricing.
○ Advantages: Ensures profitability, simple to apply.
○ Disadvantages: Ignores consumer perception, lacks flexibility.
● Value-Based Pricing: ○ Prices are set based on perceived value, brand image, and demand.
○ Common in premium, luxury, and high-tech industries.
○ Advantages: Maximizes profitability, strengthens brand loyalty.
