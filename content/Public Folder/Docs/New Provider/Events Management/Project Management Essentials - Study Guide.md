INTRODUCTION

In this module, we will explore the exciting world of project management. You will learn

what management entails, understand the vital role of a project manager, discover the

reasons behind undertaking projects, and examine the stages of the project life cycle.

Unit 1.1: What is Management?

Management is the process of planning, organising, leading, and controlling resources to

achieve specific goals. In this unit, you will learn how these functions are essential in

helping organisations succeed and how they can be applied to managing projects

effectively.

Unit 1.2: The Role of the Project Manager

Every project needs a leader, and this is where a project manager comes into play. In this

unit, we will discuss the duties and responsibilities of a project manager. You will

understand how their leadership and decision-making skills are crucial for steering projects

towards their successful completion.

Read pages 24—26 of your textbook for more information.

Unit 1.3: Why Are Projects Undertaken?

Projects are initiated for various reasons—from solving complex problems to meeting

specific goals within a timeframe. This unit will explore different motivations for starting

projects and how they can bring about change or add value to an organisation.

Unit 1.4: The Project Life Cycle

Every project goes through a series of stages from start to finish, known as the project life

cycle. This unit will introduce you to these stages, which include initiation, planning,

execution, monitoring and controlling, and closing. Understanding these stages will help

you see how a project evolves from an idea into reality.

Read pages 37—40 of your textbook for more information.

Module Summary

functions of management—planning, organizing, leading, and controlling resources to

achieve desired outcomes. We then examined the crucial role of a project manager,

emphasising their leadership and decision-making skills essential for guiding projects to

success. We also discussed the motivations behind initiating projects, highlighting their

potential to solve problems and add value within an organisation. Lastly, we reviewed the

project life cycle stages, from initiation to closure, providing a clear framework for

understanding how projects evolve from concepts to reality. This foundational knowledge

prepares you for deeper insights into project management in the subsequent modules.

INTRODUCTION

This final module covers the last phase of the project life cycle: project closure. You'll

discover the importance of closing projects efficiently, which includes finalising details,

evaluating successes and challenges, and learning from the project experience.

Unit 5.1: Phase 4: Project Closure

Project closure is not just about completing tasks—it's also about wrapping up the project in

a structured way. This unit will guide you through the steps necessary to formally close a

project. You will learn how to complete project documentation, finalise project deliverables,

release project resources, and conduct a post-project evaluation. Additionally, we will

discuss how to capture lessons learned and how these insights can improve future

projects.

Read pages 119—123 of your textbook for more information,

Module Summary

In Module 5, you learned about the final phase of the project management life cycle:

closing projects. This module taught you how to effectively wrap up projects, ensuring all

components are finalised, goals are met, and benefits are secured. You explored key steps

such as completing a project checklist that includes reviewing successes, transferring

responsibilities, and closing financial accounts. The importance of thorough documentation

and reporting was emphasised, alongside the need for reflecting on the project process and

compiling lessons learned to improve future projects. Tools like Weighted Critical

Measurements helped ensure that projects met initial requirements and achieved desired

outcomes. By the end of this module, you were equipped to efficiently close projects and

use your experiences to enhance your skills as a project manager.
