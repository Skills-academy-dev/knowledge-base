What is Leadership?

Leadership involves guiding and influencing a group of people to achieve a common goal. In the
context of project management, leadership is about setting the vision for the project, motivating the
team, and ensuring that project objectives are met. Effective leadership requires a combination of
strategic thinking, emotional intelligence, and the ability to inspire and motivate others. It involves
making decisions that align with the project's goals and communicating those decisions clearly to
the team. Leaders must also be adaptable, able to respond to changes and challenges as they
arise.

What is Project Leadership?

Project leadership is the art and science of guiding and empowering a team to successfully achieve
project objectives by inspiring, building relationships, navigating challenges, and balancing both the
technical and human aspects of project management.

The Four Leadership Tasks

Planning

Setting the direction for the project. This involves defining the project’s vision, mission, and
goals. Planning also includes developing strategies to achieve these goals and anticipating fu-
ture challenges and opportunities. Effective planning requires thorough research and analysis,
as well as the ability to think ahead and foresee potential obstacles.

Organising

Arranging resources and tasks in a structured manner. This ensures that everything is in place
for smooth project execution. Organising involves creating a clear structure for the project, defin-
ing roles and responsibilities, and establishing processes and workflows. It also includes re-
source allocation, ensuring that team members have the tools and materials they need to com-
plete their tasks effectively.

Leading

Guiding and motivating the team towards the project goals. Leading involves communicating the
vision and goals to the team, inspiring and motivating them to achieve these goals. It requires
strong interpersonal skills, including the ability to build relationships, manage conflicts, and pro-
vide feedback and support. Effective leaders are also good at delegating tasks and empowering
team members to take ownership of their work.

Controlling

Monitoring and assessing the project’s progress. This involves tracking performance against the
project plan, identifying any deviations or issues, and making necessary adjustments. Control-
ling also includes setting performance standards, measuring actual performance, and taking cor-
rective actions when needed. Regular monitoring and evaluation help ensure that the project
stays on track and meets its objectives.

Leadership vs. Management

While leadership and management are often used interchangeably, they involve different skill
sets and roles within an organisation. Leadership focuses on setting a new direction or vision for
a project, inspiring and motivating the team, and emphasising innovation and change. Manage-
ment, on the other hand, focuses on planning and coordinating tasks to achieve the vision, or-
ganising resources and processes, and emphasising stability and efficiency. Both roles are es-
sential for the success of a project, but they require different approaches and skills.

Leadership:

Focuses on setting a new direction or vision for a project. Leaders create a vision and inspire
people to achieve it. They focus on the bigger picture, thinking strategically about the future and
setting long-term goals. Leadership involves innovation, creativity, and the ability to inspire and
motivate others.

Management:

Focuses on planning and coordinating tasks to achieve the vision. Managers are responsible for
implementing the vision set by the leaders. They focus on the details, creating plans, organising
resources, and ensuring that tasks are completed efficiently. Management involves analytical
and organisational skills, as well as the ability to manage time and resources effectively.

Difference:

Leadership involves creating a vision and inspiring people to achieve it. It focuses on innovation
and change, inspiring and motivating others to work towards a common goal. Management, on
the other hand, involves planning, organising, and coordinating tasks to achieve the vision. It
focuses on stability and efficiency, ensuring that tasks are completed on time and within budget.

Example

Consider a project manager leading a team to develop a new software application. As a leader,
the manager sets a vision for the project, inspiring the team with a clear idea of the end goal—an
innovative, user-friendly software. The leader communicates the vision, motivates the team, and
creates a positive work environment. As a manager, the same person would plan the project
timeline, organise resources, assign tasks, and ensure that each phase of the project is complet-
ed on time and within budget. The manager monitors progress, identifies any issues, and makes
necessary adjustments to keep the project on track.

Characteristics of Effective Leaders: Effective leaders possess various qualities that enable them to

1.2 Five Habits of Highly Effective Conflict Resolvers

Conflict is inevitable in project management due to diverse backgrounds and perspectives within a
team. It can arise from differences in values, attitudes, needs, expectations, perceptions, resources,
and personalities. Here are five habits of highly effective conflict resolvers:

Understanding the Nature of Conflict

Effective conflict resolvers take the time to understand the underlying issues and the
perspectives of all parties involved. They do not jump to conclusions but seek to com-
prehend the root causes of the conflict. By understanding the nature of the conflict,
they can address the real issues rather than just the symptoms.

Example: In a project team, two members may have a disagreement about the best
approach to solving a problem. The effective conflict resolver would listen to both
sides, understand their viewpoints, and identify the core issue causing the disagree-
ment. By addressing the root cause, the conflict resolver can help find a solution that
satisfies both parties.

Structured Resolution

Implement structured methods for resolving conflicts, such as mediation and negotia-
tion. This helps ensure that conflicts are resolved in a fair and constructive manner.
Structured resolution involves setting ground rules, creating an agenda, and following
a step-by-step process to address the issues. This methodical approach helps pre-
vent emotions from escalating and keeps the focus on finding a solution.

Example: A project manager may set up a mediation session with clear guidelines,
allowing each party to speak without interruption and working towards a mutually
agreeable solution. The structured approach ensures that both parties feel heard and
respected, leading to a more effective resolution.

Active Listening

Active listening is crucial for effective conflict resolution. This involves paying full at-
tention to the speaker, acknowledging their feelings, and responding appropriately.
Active listening helps build trust and understanding between conflicting parties. It al-
so ensures that all relevant information is considered before making a decision.

Example: During a team meeting, an effective leader listens to all team members'
concerns, acknowledges their feelings, and provides thoughtful responses to address
the issues. By actively listening, the leader can better understand the root causes of
the conflict and work towards a resolution that satisfies everyone involved.

Collaborative Problem-Solving

Effective conflict resolvers focus on finding mutually beneficial solutions. They en-
courage collaboration and creativity in problem-solving, ensuring that all parties are
involved in the process and that the solutions address everyone's concerns. Collabo-
rative problem-solving fosters a sense of ownership and commitment to the resolu-
tion.

Example: When two departments are in conflict over resource allocation, an effective
leader facilitates a brainstorming session where both sides contribute ideas to find a
solution that benefits the entire project. By involving everyone in the problem-solving
process, the leader ensures that the final solution is accepted and supported by all
parties.

Emotional Intelligence:

Managing one's own emotions and understanding the emotions of others are key to
resolving conflicts effectively. Emotional intelligence helps in remaining calm, empa-
thetic, and objective during conflict resolution, which fosters a positive environment
for resolving disputes. Leaders with high emotional intelligence can navigate conflicts
without letting emotions cloud their judgement.

Example:  An  emotionally  intelligent  leader  remains  calm  during  heated  discussions,
empathises with team members' frustrations, and guides the conversation towards a
constructive resolution. By managing their own emotions and understanding those of
others, the leader can create a safe and respectful environment for conflict resolution.

Case Study

A project team at a construction company is facing conflict due to delays in the supply of materials.
The project manager, using the five habits of highly effective conflict resolvers, listens to the con-
cerns of the procurement and construction teams, understands the root cause of the delay, and fa-
cilitates a collaborative meeting to develop a solution that includes finding alternative suppliers and
adjusting the project timeline. By addressing the conflict constructively, the project manager helps
the team find a solution that minimises delays and keeps the project on track.

Delegation is the process of assigning tasks and responsibilities to team members, along with the
authority and resources needed to complete those tasks. It involves entrusting team members with
specific duties, empowering them to make decisions, and holding them accountable for the out-
comes. Effective delegation is a critical skill for project managers, as it allows them to focus on high-
er-priority tasks and ensures that the workload is distributed efficiently among team members.

Importance of Delegation

Allows project managers to focus on high-priority tasks: By delegating routine or lower-priority
tasks, project managers can concentrate on strategic planning, problem-solving, and decision-
making, which are essential for the project's success.

Empowers team members by giving them responsibility: Delegation provides opportunities for
team members to take ownership of their work, develop their skills, and gain confidence in their
abilities.

Enhances team members’ skills and confidence: When team members are given challenging
tasks and the authority to complete them, they can develop new skills, build confidence, and
grow professionally.

Promotes a sense of ownership and accountability among team members: Delegation fosters a
sense of responsibility and accountability, as team members are held accountable for the tasks
they are assigned.

Increases overall efficiency and productivity of the team: Delegation helps distribute the work-
load more evenly, allowing the team to work more efficiently and complete tasks more quickly.

Steps for Effective Delegation

Explain Why: Explain why the task is important and what the expected outcome is. This helps
the team member understand the significance of their contribution. Providing context and ex-
plaining the impact of the task on the overall project can motivate team members and help them
see the value of their work.

Example: A project manager explains to a team member that creating a detailed pro-
ject timeline is crucial for tracking progress and ensuring that all tasks are completed
on time. By understanding the importance of the task, the team member is more like-
ly to be motivated and committed to completing it successfully.

Define Authority: Clearly define the authority of the person you are delegating to. This includes
resources they can access and decisions they can make. Clearly defining authority helps pre-
vent confusion and ensures that the team member knows what they can and cannot do.

Example: The project manager provides the team member with access to project
management software and authority to coordinate with other team members to gather
necessary information. By defining the team member's authority, the project manager
ensures that they have the resources and autonomy needed to complete the task.

Agree on a Deadline: Set a clear deadline for completing the task. Make sure the team member
understands the importance of meeting this deadline. Setting deadlines helps ensure that tasks
are completed on time and keeps the project on track.

Example: The project manager sets a deadline for the project timeline to be complet-

member knows when the task needs to be completed and why it is important.
