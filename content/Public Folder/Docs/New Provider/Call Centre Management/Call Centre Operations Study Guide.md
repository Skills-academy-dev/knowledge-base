# Call Centre Operations Study Guide

## MODULE 1: INTRODUCTION TO CALL CENTRES

Starts on Page 11

### Module Introduction

Call centres are an important part of how businesses support their customers. This module introduces what a call centre is, why it exists, the different types of call centres, and how the industry has grown both globally and in South Africa. You’ll also explore how call centres have changed over time and why they are important for business success and job creation.
[Page 11]

### Unit 1.1: What is a Call Centre?

This unit covers the meaning of a call centre and its main functions. You’ll learn how call centres help customers by solving problems, giving information, and offering support. This unit also explains what makes call centres unique and why many companies rely on them.
[Page 12]

#### Subtopics:

1.1.1 Definition of a Call Centre
A central place where agents handle calls for customer service, sales, or technical support.

1.1.2 Basic Functions and Services
Services include customer support, technical assistance, order handling, sales, billing, surveys, and appointment scheduling.

### Unit 1.2: The Purpose and Role of Call Centres

This unit explores why call centres are used in businesses. It explains how they support communication, customer satisfaction, and business growth. You will understand how call centres are key in solving problems and building trust with customers.
[Page 16]

#### Subtopics:

1.2.1 Customer Communication
How call centres connect businesses and customers by answering questions and resolving complaints.

1.2.2 Supporting Sales and Services
Using call centres to promote products, support customer loyalty, and strengthen business success.

### Unit 1.3: History and Growth of Call Centres

In this unit, you will explore how call centres began and how they have grown with new technology. From switchboards to computer systems, you’ll learn how this industry has become more efficient and professional over time.
[Page 19]

#### Subtopics:

1.3.1 Early Development
Call centres started with simple phones and switchboards for handling customer calls.

1.3.2 Rise of Technology in Call Centres
Introduction of computers, databases, live chats, and CRM systems that improved speed and service.

### Unit 1.4: Types of Call Centres (Inbound, Outbound, Blended)

This unit explains the three main types of call centres: inbound, outbound, and blended. You’ll learn the differences between them and how each one is used for different customer and business needs.
[Page 23]

#### Subtopics:

1.4.1 Inbound: Receiving Calls
Customers phone in for help with orders, accounts, or problems.

1.4.2 Outbound: Making Calls
Agents call customers for sales, promotions, or surveys.

1.4.3 Blended: Doing Both
Centres that handle both incoming and outgoing calls for flexible support.

### Unit 1.5: Call Centres in South Africa

This unit explores the growth of the call centre industry in South Africa. You will discover why the country is seen as a global leader in business process outsourcing (BPO), and how this creates job opportunities for local youth.
[Page 27]

#### Subtopics:

1.5.1 Growth of the Local Industry
More companies are setting up call centres in cities like Cape Town, Johannesburg, and Durban.

1.5.2 South Africa as a Global BPO Destination
South Africa is popular because of friendly workers, lower costs, and strong communication skills. The government also supports training and job creation.

### Module Summary

This module introduced the basics of call centres and their importance in customer service and business operations.

Defined what a call centre is and the services it provides

Explained the purpose and role of call centres in customer support and sales

Outlined the history and growth of the industry

Described the types of call centres: inbound, outbound, and blended

Highlighted South Africa’s development as a leading call centre destination

## MODULE 2: CALL CENTRE ENVIRONMENT AND TEAMWORK

Starts on Page 36

### Module Introduction

Working in a call centre is not just about answering phones—it’s about being part of a busy team that works together to help customers. This module introduces the day-to-day environment in a call centre. You will explore different job roles, how shift work is planned, and how to stay healthy and safe at work. The module also explains the importance of teamwork and how staying motivated with a positive attitude can help you succeed in a call centre role.

### Unit 2.1: Common Job Roles in a Call Centre

Page 37
This unit covers the most important jobs you’ll find in a call centre. It explains what a call centre agent does, the role of a team leader or supervisor, and the responsibilities of support staff like IT, HR, and Admin. Understanding each role helps you see how everyone contributes to keeping the call centre running well.

#### Subtopics:

2.1.1 Call Centre Agent
Agents speak directly to customers, solve problems, log calls, and follow scripts. Skills needed include good communication, computer literacy, and patience.

2.1.2 Team Leader and Supervisor
Supervisors support agents, give feedback, manage schedules, and report to management. They also help with difficult calls.

2.1.3 Support Roles (IT, HR, Admin)
These roles include fixing technical issues, managing staff matters, and organising records. Each job supports the team behind the scenes.

### Unit 2.2: Shift Work and Daily Operations

#### Subtopics:

2.2.1 Roster Systems and Schedules
Describes early, mid, late, and night shifts. Schedules are made to ensure full coverage.

2.2.2 Managing High Call Volumes
Explains how technology and teamwork are used to keep call wait times low and service fast.

### Unit 2.3: Workplace Health, Safety, and Comfort

#### Subtopics:

2.3.1 Ergonomics and Desk Setup
How to set up your chair, screen, and workspace to avoid pain and fatigue.

2.3.2 Eye Strain and Voice Care
Tips for protecting your eyes and voice during long hours at the desk.

### Unit 2.4: Teamwork and Working with Others

Page 46
This unit explains the importance of teamwork in a call centre. You’ll learn how good communication, respect, and helping others can make the workplace more positive and successful.

#### Subtopics:

2.4.1 Communicating with Team Members
Includes team briefings, feedback sessions, and polite, helpful communication.

2.4.2 Respect and Cooperation
Covers being on time, keeping workspaces clean, supporting colleagues, and avoiding gossip.

### Unit 2.5: Motivation and Positive Attitudes at Work

Page 48
This unit looks at how rewards, recognition, and a positive attitude can help you and your team stay happy and productive at work. It also offers ways to handle stress and stay calm during difficult shifts.

#### Subtopics:

2.5.1 Rewards and Recognition
Explains how small rewards, praise, and thank-you notes can boost morale.

2.5.2 Handling Stress and Staying Positive
Offers advice on stress relief, taking breaks, and keeping a good mindset.

## MODULE 3: COMMUNICATION AND CALL HANDLING

Starts on Page 57

### Module Introduction

In a call centre, communication is the most important skill. This module will teach you how to speak clearly, listen carefully, and handle different types of calls with confidence. You’ll learn how your tone of voice and posture affect the way customers feel—even over the phone. You will also discover how to use polite language, follow call scripts, ask helpful questions, take messages, and transfer calls correctly. Whether helping a caller from Port Shepstone or Mokopane, your communication will shape their experience.

### Unit 3.1: Verbal and Non-verbal Communication

Page 58
This unit explores the difference between what you say (verbal) and how you say it (non-verbal). It shows how both can build trust with the customer—even when you are not face to face.

#### Subtopics:

3.1.1 Voice Tone and Clarity
Customers listen for confidence, friendliness, and calmness in your voice. You’ll learn how speed, volume, and tone can affect the message you send.

3.1.2 Body Language and Posture (Even on the Phone)
Sitting up straight and breathing properly improves how you sound. Posture affects clarity, confidence, and energy—even when unseen.

### Unit 3.2: Telephone Etiquette

Page 61
This unit teaches how to start and end calls professionally, and how to speak with kindness and respect at all times. It includes common polite phrases and tips for handling calls with care.

#### Subtopics:

3.2.1 Greeting and Ending a Call
First impressions matter. You’ll learn how to open with a friendly tone and close the call politely.

3.2.2 Polite and Professional Language
Using calm, respectful language helps avoid conflict and shows the customer they matter.

### Unit 3.3: Active Listening and Asking Questions

Page 64
In this unit, you’ll practise listening without interrupting and asking questions to better understand the customer. You will discover how these skills help you solve problems quickly and respectfully.

#### Subtopics:

3.3.1 Listening Without Interrupting
Good listening builds trust. You’ll learn to stay silent, take notes, and let the customer finish speaking.

3.3.2 Using Clarifying Questions
Clear questions help avoid confusion and save time. You’ll learn to ask both open and closed questions to get the right information.

### Unit 3.4: Using Call Scripts and Handling Calls

Page 66
This unit explains how to use call scripts to stay professional while still sounding natural. It also shows how to handle unexpected questions or objections calmly.

#### Subtopics:

3.4.1 Following Call Scripts
Scripts help agents remember greetings, important steps, and how to end a call properly.

3.4.2 Handling Objections or Enquiries
You’ll learn how to stay calm and polite when customers raise concerns or need more help.

### Unit 3.5: Taking Messages and Transferring Calls

Page 70
In this unit, you’ll learn how to take clear, complete messages and know when and how to transfer a call to the correct department or person.

#### Subtopics:

3.5.1 Writing Clear Messages
Record key information like name, number, and reason for the call. Repeat details to confirm accuracy.

3.5.2 Knowing When and How to Transfer
Learn the right time to transfer a call and how to do it politely, ensuring the customer isn’t left confused.

Taught proper telephone etiquette for greeting, ending, and maintaining polite language

Covered how to take clear messages and transfer calls correctly

## MODULE 5: CALL CENTRE TOOLS AND TECHNOLOGY

Starts on Page 101

### Module Introduction

Modern call centres rely on technology to provide fast, professional service. In this module, you’ll explore the tools that help agents manage calls, store customer details, record conversations, and keep systems secure. These tools include CTI, IVR, ACD, CRM software, and cybersecurity systems. You’ll also learn what to do when things go wrong—such as how to fix basic issues or report problems to IT. Whether you’re working from home or in a busy contact centre, knowing how to use these tools will help you succeed as a call centre agent.

### Unit 5.1: Overview of Call Centre Technology (CTI, IVR, ACD)

Page 102
This unit introduces three key technologies used in call centres: CTI, IVR, and ACD. You’ll learn what each one does and how they work together to improve customer service and agent efficiency.

#### Subtopics:

5.1.1 What Each Tool Does

CTI connects phones to computers, letting agents take calls and view customer info on-screen.

IVR lets customers choose options like “Press 1 for Billing”, helping route calls efficiently.

ACD distributes incoming calls to the right agent based on skills, language, or availability.

5.1.2 How They Work Together
These systems work as a team: IVR answers, ACD sends to the right agent, and CTI provides information instantly. Together, they make service faster and reduce mistakes.

### Unit 5.2: CRM Software and Customer Databases

Page 105
This unit explains how agents use CRM (Customer Relationship Management) software to keep track of customer details and service history.

#### Subtopics:

5.2.1 Storing and Accessing Customer Information
CRM systems let agents view customer profiles, past interactions, and notes. This helps provide personalised service.

5.2.2 Updating and Maintaining Records
It’s important to update records after each call to keep information accurate and helpful for future agents.

### Unit 5.3: Call Recording and Monitoring Tools

Page 108
This unit looks at why calls are recorded and how supervisors use monitoring tools to ensure quality service and support training.

#### Subtopics:

5.3.1 Why Calls are Recorded
Recordings help settle disputes, train new agents, and improve customer service.

5.3.2 How Supervisors Use Monitoring
Supervisors listen to live or recorded calls to give feedback and improve team performance.

### Unit 5.4: Data Protection and Cybersecurity

Page 111
This unit highlights the importance of keeping customer information safe using data protection laws like POPIA and basic online safety practices.

#### Subtopics:

5.4.1 Importance of Privacy (POPIA)
POPIA is a South African law that protects personal information. Agents must handle all data carefully.

5.4.2 Safe Passwords and Secure Browsing
Use strong passwords, log out when away, and avoid clicking unsafe links to keep systems protected.

### Unit 5.5: Troubleshooting Basic System Issues

Page 114
This unit teaches you what to do when technology stops working. You’ll learn how to try simple fixes first and know when to call IT support.

#### Subtopics:

5.5.1 Common Technical Problems
Issues include slow systems, frozen screens, and headset errors.

5.5.2 When to Call IT Support
If your fixes don’t work, report the problem with clear details so IT can help quickly.

### Module Summary

This module introduced the technology used in call centres and how it supports effective service delivery.

Described the function of CTI, IVR, and ACD systems

Explained how CRM software helps store and manage customer data

Covered call recording and monitoring tools for training and quality control

Emphasised the importance of data protection, POPIA, and cybersecurity practices

Provided steps for troubleshooting basic technical problems and contacting IT support

## MODULE 6: PERFORMANCE AND CAREER DEVELOPMENT

Starts on Page 124

### Module Introduction

Working in a call centre is not only about handling customer calls—it’s also about growing as a professional. This module explores how your performance is measured, how you can improve through feedback and training, and how to manage time and stress in a busy work environment. It also shows how call centre experience can lead to exciting career opportunities, both within the industry and beyond.

### Unit 6.1: Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

Page 125
This unit introduces KPIs—tools that help measure how well call centre agents are doing their jobs. You’ll learn why they are used and what common KPIs mean.

#### Subtopics:

6.1.1 What is a KPI?
A KPI is a measurable target such as call duration or customer satisfaction, used to track agent performance fairly and clearly.

6.1.2 Common KPIs (AHT, CSAT, FCR)

AHT (Average Handle Time): Measures how long calls take.

CSAT (Customer Satisfaction Score): Shows how happy customers are.

FCR (First Contact Resolution): Tracks how often issues are solved on the first call.

### Unit 6.2: Quality Assurance and Feedback

Page 128
This unit explains how supervisors and QA teams review calls and give feedback to help you improve and stay professional.

#### Subtopics:

6.2.1 Call Scoring and Evaluation

6.2.2 Receiving and Using Feedback
