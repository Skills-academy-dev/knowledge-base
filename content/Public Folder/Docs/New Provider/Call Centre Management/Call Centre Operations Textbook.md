1.1 What is a Call Centre?
Call centres are essential parts of many businesses. They help companies talk to their
customers and solve problems quickly. If you’ve ever phoned your bank, mobile network, or an
online store, you’ve most likely spoken to a call centre agent.
In this unit, we explore the meaning of a call centre, what it does, and how it helps businesses
run smoothly.
1.1.1 Definition of a Call Centre
A call centre is a central office or department where specially trained people—called agents—
handle large numbers of phone calls from or to customers. These calls are often related to
sales, customer service, technical support, or general enquiries.
Main features of a call centre:
• High volume of calls: Agents manage many calls per day.
• Structured environment: Agents follow scripts and company procedures.
• Technology-based: Uses phones, computers, and software to manage
tasks.
Did You Know?
Some call centres now also handle live chat, emails, SMS, and WhatsApp messages. These
are called contact centres because they use more than just calls.
1.1.2 Basic Functions and Services
Call centres offer a wide range of services depending on the needs of the business and its
customers. The main goal is to provide fast, friendly, and helpful communication between the
company and its clients.

1.2 The Purpose and Role of Call Centres
Call centres do more than just answer phone calls—they are an important part of how a
business runs and grows. They help keep customers happy, solve problems quickly, and
support business goals such as sales, loyalty, and product support.
In this unit, you will learn why call centres exist and how they play a valuable role in both
customer service and business success.
1.2.1 Customer Communication
One of the main purposes of a call centre is to make communication between customers and
businesses faster and easier. A company that communicates well with its customers builds
trust, improves loyalty, and keeps customers coming back.
Common ways call centres help with communication:
• Answering product or service questions, e.g. “When will my package be delivered?”
• Helping customers understand information, e.g. “Can you explain my latest bill?”
• Fixing problems or complaints, e.g. “My internet is not working—please help.”
• Making changes to an account, e.g. “I want to update my phone number.”
Real-Life Example:
Zanele calls her mobile provider because her airtime disappeared. The call centre agent
checks her data usage and explains the charges. Zanele is satisfied and continues using the
network.
Benefits of clear communication in a call centre:
• Reduces confusion and frustration
• Builds good customer relationships
• Helps businesses understand customer needs

1.3 History and Growth of Call Centres
Call centres have not always looked the way they do today. The idea of businesses speaking to
customers through a central team has been around for a long time—but it has changed a lot with
the help of technology.
In this unit, we explore how call centres started, how they grew, and how they became the
busy, high-tech environments we know today.
1.3.1 Early Development
The earliest form of a call centre began with the invention of the telephone in the late 1800s.
Businesses started using phones to connect with customers, but they were limited by the
technology of the time.
By the 1960s and 1970s, large companies started using PBX systems (Private Branch
Exchange), which allowed many phones to connect to one central system. This made it easier
for companies to receive and transfer multiple calls.
Example: A bank could now receive many calls at once, with each one going to a different
department like accounts, loans, or customer service.

1.3.2 Rise of Technology in Call Centres
As computers and software became more advanced, so did call centres. The 1990s and 2000s
introduced tools that made customer service faster, smarter, and more personal.
Key Technologies That Changed Call Centres:
• CTI – Computer Telephony Integration
Allowed phones and computers to work together so agents could see customer info while
on a call.
• ACD – Automatic Call Distributor
Sent incoming calls to the right agent or department based on the type of question.
• IVR – Interactive Voice Response
Allowed customers to press buttons or speak to get help before reaching an agent.
• CRM – Customer Relationship Management software
Stored customer details, previous call history, and preferences in one place.
Did You Know?
CRM systems allow agents to greet returning customers by name and quickly check their
previous interactions. This makes the service feel more personal!

1.4 Types of Call Centres (Inbound, Outbound,
Blended)
Call centres are not all the same. Each one has a specific role depending on the type of service
it provides. Some focus on answering incoming calls, others focus on making outgoing calls,
and some do both.
In this unit, you will learn about the three main types of call centres:
1.4.1 Inbound: Receiving Calls
An inbound call centre receives calls from customers who need help, information, or want to
complete a task like placing an order or reporting a problem.
Common reasons customers call inbound centres:
• To ask a question about a product or service.
• To report a technical problem.
• To check a delivery or account balance.
• To update personal information.
Skills needed for inbound agents:
• Clear speaking and listening.
• Problem-solving and patience.
• Ability to stay calm during busy times or with angry customers.
Real-Life Example
Khanyisa works at a call centre in Cape Town that supports a cellphone company. She
receives more than 80 calls a day from people who need help with airtime, SIM card issues,
or network problems. She uses polite greetings, listens carefully, and logs each case into
the system.

1.4.2 Outbound: Making Calls
An outbound call centre is where agents make calls to customers or potential clients. These
calls are usually planned and follow a script.
Common reasons for outbound calls:
• Selling a product or service.
• Reminding customers about payments or appointments.
• Conducting surveys and collecting feedback.
• Updating records or promoting new offers.
Did You Know?
Some call centres use predictive diallers to automatically dial phone numbers from a list. This
saves time and allows agents to talk to more people per hour.
Skills needed for outbound agents:
• Confidence and persuasion.
• A good speaking tone and positive attitude.
• Handling rejection without losing motivation.

1.4.3 Blended: Doing Both
A blended call centre handles both inbound and outbound calls. This means that agents may
switch between receiving calls and making calls during their shift.
How blended centres work:
• In the morning, agents may help with account queries (inbound).
• In the afternoon, they may call customers to follow up on a complaint (outbound).
• Sometimes, the system automatically changes their tasks based on the call volume.
Tip:
Blended agents must be flexible and have skills in both customer service and sales. Time
management is very important.

1.5 Call Centres in South Africa
South Africa is one of the world’s fastest-growing locations for call centre services. With its
large number of English-speaking, friendly, and hardworking people, the country has become a
popular place for both local and international businesses to set up call centres.
In this unit, you will learn how the South African call centre industry has developed and why it
continues to grow.
1.5.1 Growth of the Local Industry
Over the past two decades, call centres in South Africa have grown quickly. What started with
local companies serving only their own customers has turned into a global business
opportunity. Many of the jobs created in this industry now serve international markets,
especially English-speaking countries like the United Kingdom, Australia, and the United
States.
Reasons for Growth in South Africa:
• High unemployment among youth, leading to government support for job creation.
• Strong English skills and neutral accents that are easy for global customers to
understand.
• Time zone advantages, especially for working with Europe and the UK.
• Reliable technology and growing internet infrastructure.
• Well-developed training centres to prepare call centre agents for different roles.
Real-Life Example
Lindiwe, a 22-year-old from the Eastern Cape, joined a free government-funded training
programme for call centre work. Within three months, she was hired by an international
travel agency’s Cape Town call centre to handle UK customer bookings.
Major Cities for Call Centres in South Africa:
• Cape Town – Known for international call centres and excellent English skills.
• Durban – Offers a growing youth workforce and friendly customer service.
• Johannesburg – The business hub with large call centre operations for finance,
telecoms, and insurance.

1.5.2 South Africa as a Global BPO Destination
The term BPO stands for Business Process Outsourcing. This means that companies from
around the world hire South African call centres to handle their customer service, sales, or
technical support.
South Africa has become a top BPO destination because of:
• Skilled and friendly workers.
• Lower costs than running a call centre in Europe or the USA.
• Quality customer service training.
• Supportive government policies.
Government Support Includes:
• Free training programmes for unemployed youth.
• Incentives for companies that open call centres in the country.
• Public-private partnerships to grow the industry further.
Did You Know?
In 2021 and 2022, South Africa was named the number one offshore location in the world for
customer service, beating India and the Philippines. This ranking came from the Ryan Strategic
Advisory global survey.
