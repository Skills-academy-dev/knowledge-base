INTRODUCTION

Call centres are an important part of how businesses support their customers. This module

introduces what a call centre is, why it exists, the different types of call centres, and how

the industry has grown both globally and in South Africa. You’ll also explore how call

centres have changed over time and why they are important for business success and job

creation.

Unit 1.1: What is a Call Centre?

This unit covers the meaning of a call centre and its main functions. You’ll learn how call

centres help customers by solving problems, giving information, and offering support. This

unit also explains what makes call centres unique and why many companies rely on them.

Read pages 12— 15 of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

•

1.1.1 Definition of a Call Centre

A central place where agents handle calls for customer service, sales, or technical

support.

•

1.1.2 Basic Functions and Services

Services include customer support, technical assistance, order handling, sales, billing,

surveys, and appointment scheduling.

Unit 1.2: The Purpose and Role of Call Centres

This unit explores why call centres are used in businesses. It explains how they support

communication, customer satisfaction, and business growth. You will understand how call

centres are key in solving problems and building trust with customers.

Read pages 16— 19 of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

•

1.2.1 Customer Communication

How call centres connect businesses and customers by answering questions and

resolving complaints.

•

1.2.2 Supporting Sales and Services

Using call centres to promote products, support customer loyalty, and strengthen

business success.

INTRODUCTION

Working in a call centre is not just about answering phones—it’s about being part of a busy

team that works together to help customers. This module introduces the day-to-day

environment in a call centre. You will explore different job roles, how shift work is planned,

and how to stay healthy and safe at work. The module also explains the importance of

teamwork and how staying motivated with a positive attitude can help you succeed in a call

centre role.

Unit 2.1: Common Job Roles in a Call Centre

This unit covers the most important jobs you’ll find in a call centre. It explains what a call

centre agent does, the role of a team leader or supervisor, and the responsibilities of

support staff like IT, HR, and Admin. Understanding each role helps you see how everyone

contributes to keeping the call centre running well

Read pages 37—40 of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

•

2.1.1 Call Centre Agent

Agents speak directly to customers, solve problems, log calls, and follow scripts. Skills

needed include good communication, computer literacy, and patience.

•

2.1.2 Team Leader and Supervisor

Supervisors support agents, give feedback, manage schedules, and report to

management. They also help with difficult calls.

•

2.1.3 Support Roles (IT, HR, Admin)

These roles include fixing technical issues, managing staff matters, and organising

records. Each job supports the team behind the scenes.

Unit 2.2: Shift Work and Daily Operations

This unit explains how shift work operates in a call centre. You’ll learn how rosters are used

to schedule workers and how call centres handle large numbers of customer calls. It also

touches on tools like ACD systems that help manage the workload.

INTRODUCTION

In a call centre, communication is the most important skill. This module will teach you how

to speak clearly, listen carefully, and handle different types of calls with confidence. You’ll

learn how your tone of voice and posture affect the way customers feel—even over the

phone. You will also discover how to use polite language, follow call scripts, ask helpful

questions, take messages, and transfer calls correctly. Whether helping a caller from Port

Shepstone or Mokopane, your communication will shape their experience.

Unit 3.1: Verbal and Non-verbal Communication

This unit explores the difference between what you say (verbal) and how you say it

(non-verbal). It shows how both can build trust with the customer—even when you are not

face to face.

Read pages 58—60 of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

•

3.1.1 Voice Tone and Clarity

Customers listen for confidence, friendliness, and calmness in your voice. You’ll learn

how speed, volume, and tone can affect the message you send.

•

3.1.2 Body Language and Posture (Even on the Phone)

Sitting up straight and breathing properly improves how you sound. Posture affects

clarity, confidence, and energy—even when unseen.

Unit 3.2: Telephone Etiquette

This unit teaches how to start and end calls professionally, and how to speak with kindness

and respect at all times. It includes common polite phrases and tips for handling calls with

care.

Read pages 61— 63 of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

•

3.2.1 Greeting and Ending a Call

First impressions matter. You’ll learn how to open with a friendly tone and close the

call politely.

•

3.2.2 Polite and Professional Language

Using calm, respectful language helps avoid conflict and shows the customer they

matter.

INTRODUCTION

Excellent customer service is at the heart of every successful call centre. This module

teaches you how to understand customer needs, offer friendly and professional service,

and manage complaints calmly. You’ll learn how to build trust by being helpful, respectful,

and clear. Whether you’re helping a pensioner with a lost payment or a business owner

with a delivery issue, the same customer service principles apply: listen, care, and solve

the problem quickly and politely.

Unit 4.1: Understanding Customer Needs

learn how to use empathy and personalised service to meet their needs and make them

feel valued.

solve problems efficiently.

•

INTRODUCTION

Modern call centres rely on technology to provide fast, professional service. In this module,

you’ll explore the tools that help agents manage calls, store customer details, record

conversations, and keep systems secure. These tools include CTI, IVR, ACD, CRM

software, and cybersecurity systems. You’ll also learn what to do when things go wrong—

such as how to fix basic issues or report problems to IT. Whether you’re working from

home or in a busy contact centre, knowing how to use these tools will help you succeed as

a call centre agent.

Unit 5.1: Overview of Call Centre Technology (CTI, IVR, ACD)

This unit introduces three key technologies used in call centres: CTI, IVR, and ACD. You’ll

learn what each one does and how they work together to improve customer service and

agent efficiency.

Read pages 102—105 of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

•

5.1.1 What Each Tool Does

•

CTI connects phones to computers, letting agents take calls and view customer
