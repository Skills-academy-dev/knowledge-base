MODULE 1 LAW OF AGENCY

Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, students will be able to:

1. Define and explain the concept of agency.

2. Identify the different methods of creating an agency relationship.

3. Discuss the rights and duties of agents and principals.

4. Analyze the implications of various types of authority in agency relationships.

5. Describe the circumstances under which agency relationships may be terminated.

INTRODUCTION

The Law of Agency is a key area in business law that governs the relationship where one party (the

agent) is legally authorized to act on behalf of another party (the principal). This module covers the

legal aspects of agency relationships, including how they are created, the responsibilities of agents

and principals, and how such relationships may be terminated.

UNIT 1: DEFINITION AND NATURE OF AGENCY

This unit provides an overview of what constitutes an agency relationship, explaining the nature and

legal recognition of agency relationships in business. It explores the essential characteristics of an

agency and how it functions within legal frameworks.

Refer to pages 12–15 in your  Mercantile Law  Textbook  for  more details  on  the following:

1.1 What is an Agency?

1.2 Characteristics of Agency

1.3 Legal Basis of Agency

UNIT 2: CREATION OF AN AGENCY RELATIONSHIP

This unit explores the various ways an agency relationship can be formed, including both formal and

informal agreements. It details the different methods through which an agency relationship may arise

and the legal principles governing these formations.

Refer to pages 15–20 in your Mercantile Law Textbook for more details on the following:.

2.1 Express and Implied Agency

2.2 Agency by Estoppel

2.3 Agency by Necessity

2.4 Operation of Law

UNIT 3: RIGHTS, DUTIES, AND TERMINATION OF AGENCY

This unit covers the legal rights and duties of both agents and principals in an agency relationship. It

explains the obligations that arise from agency contracts and details the circumstances under which an

agency relationship can be legally terminated.

Refer to pages 20–24 in your  Mercantile Law  Textbook  for  more details  on  the following:

3.1 Rights and Duties of Agents

3.2 Rights and Duties of Principals

3.3 Termination by Mutual Agreement

3.4 Termination Due to Breach or Expiry

3.5 Effects of Termination

SUMMARY

The law of agency allows businesses to operate efficiently by appointing agents to act on their behalf.

This module covers the nature and creation of agency relationships, the rights and responsibilities of

agents and principals, and the conditions for terminating agency agreements.

For more details, refer to pages 24–25 in your Mercantile Law textbook.

INTRODUCTION

Suretyship  is  a  legal  arrangement  where  a  third  party  (surety)  guarantees  the  obligations  of  a

principal  debtor  to  a  creditor.  If  the  principal  debtor  fails  to  fulfill  their  obligations,  the  surety

assumes  responsibility.  This  module  covers  the  legal  principles  of  suretyship,  including  how  it  is

created,  its  characteristics,  rights,  and  obligations  of  the  involved  parties,  and  how  agreements

can be enforced or terminated.

and contractual obligations.
