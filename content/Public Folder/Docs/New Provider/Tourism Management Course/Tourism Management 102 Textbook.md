experience.

Introduction
This module is designed to introduce you to the essential roles and responsibilities involved in
managing tourism businesses effectively. As tourism is a crucial part of South Africa's economy,
understanding how to manage these businesses successfully is key to sustaining and growing the
industry.
In this introduction, we'll explore the core management tasks that are pivotal to operating tourism
businesses—planning, organising, leading, and controlling. Each task plays a vital role in ensuring
that a tourism business operates efficiently and can adapt to the ever-changing market demands.
Management in tourism occurs at three distinct levels: the individual business, the destination, and
the country. This chapter focuses on the individual business level, providing a foundation for
understanding how effective management practices can influence the success of tourism
enterprises.
You will learn about the various strategies involved in planning and how these can be tailored to
meet the specific needs of the tourism sector. We will also discuss the importance of organisational
structures that support effective management and leadership techniques that are crucial for guiding
and motivating teams.
By the end of this module, you should have a clear understanding of the fundamental concepts of
management within the tourism industry and be equipped with practical skills to apply these
concepts effectively in real-world scenarios. Let's embark on this educational journey to enhance
your abilities and prepare you for success in the vibrant world of tourism management.
10.1 What is management?
Management involves coordinating an organisation's resources—both human and physical—to
achieve its goals. This means that managers need to effectively use everything from staff to
finances, ensuring that the organisation not only survives but thrives.
In any tourism business, managers are constantly making decisions and engaging with their teams.
Their role involves a wide range of tasks such as planning, organising, leading, and controlling.
These tasks are critical as they help in directing the organisation towards its goals.

The Four Management Tasks:
1. Planning
Planning is about setting the direction for the tourism organisation. It involves deciding on the
organisation’s vision, mission, and goals, and figuring out the resources needed to achieve
these goals. Planning helps anticipate future challenges and opportunities within the
ever-changing tourism industry, ensuring the organisation is prepared for what lies ahead.
2. Organising
Once planning is done, organising takes over. This task involves arranging resources and
tasks in a structured manner. It ensures that everything is in place so that the organisation can
operate smoothly. Organising might mean setting up teams or distributing resources across
the business according to the plan.
3. Leading
Leading is all about guiding and motivating the team to work towards the organisation's goals.
Effective leadership is key in making sure that all team members are inspired and focused.
Managers need to communicate well and motivate their staff, ensuring that everyone is pulling
in the same direction.
4. Controlling
The final task, controlling, is about monitoring and assessing the work that has been done. It
checks if the tasks and plans are being followed and are effective. This step is crucial for
ensuring that the organisation meets its objectives and can make necessary adjustments if
things aren’t going as planned.
10.2 Planning
Planning is defined by Boone and Kurtz (2012) as the process of anticipating future events and
deciding in advance the actions needed to achieve organisational goals. It’s a way to prepare for
both expected opportunities and unforeseen challenges, ensuring that the tourism business remains
competitive and efficient.
Planning plays a vital role in the success of any tourism business. According to Doswell (1997), an
effective plan is best implemented by those who were involved in its creation. This involvement
gives everyone a sense of ownership and motivates them to achieve the goals set out in the plan.

10.2.1 The Importance of Planning
Understanding why planning is crucial can transform how a tourism business operates. These
aspects are essential for maintaining a competitive edge and achieving long-term success.
 Direction and Guidelines: Planning provides clear guidelines for management, helping
everyone understand their responsibilities and the organisation’s expectations.
 Future-Oriented: It ensures the organisation is prepared for the future, adapting to changes
and opportunities in the industry.
 Performance Standards: Planning allows management to set performance standards and
find ways to measure success.
 Facilitates Control: By having clear plans, the organisation can more effectively monitor
progress and make necessary adjustments.
10.2.2 Approaches to Planning
Different types of planning cater to various needs within a tourism organisation. Each type plays a
unique role in supporting the organisation’s vision and handling daily operations as well as
unexpected events.
 Strategic Planning: This is usually handled by top management and involves setting
long-term goals that align with the organisation's vision. For example, an airline might plan to
expand its market share over the next five years.
 Tactical Planning: Middle management takes on tactical planning, which includes more
specific plans for one to three years that support the strategic goals.
 Operational Planning: This involves very specific, short-term plans that deal with the day-to-
day operations of the business, managed by lower levels of management.
 Contingency Planning: It's crucial for management to also have contingency plans. These
plans help the business quickly adapt to sudden changes or crises, ensuring minimal
disruption.

10.2.3 The Planning Process
The planning process in tourism management is a series of logical steps designed to help an
organisation realise its vision, mission, and goals. Here's how it works:
1. Situation Analysis
This first step involves analysing both the internal workings and the external environment of
the tourism business. This analysis helps identify any changes that might affect the business,
pinpointing opportunities and challenges that need planning.
2. Establishing Goals
Based on the vision of the organisation, goals are set. These goals translate the broader
vision into specific objectives that the business aims to achieve. These goals provide a clear
direction and targets for the business.
3. Plan Formulation
After setting goals, the next step is to develop detailed plans to achieve these objectives. This
involves choosing the best strategies and actions that will help meet the goals based on the
situation analysis.
4. Implementation
Once plans are formulated, they need to be put into action. This involves organising resources
and directing teams to execute the plans through effective leadership and management.
5. Periodic Review
The final step in the planning process is the periodic review. This involves monitoring the
progress of the implemented plans and evaluating if the goals are being met. Adjustments are
made to the plans as necessary to ensure the success of the organisation

10.3 Organising
While planning sets the stage in management, organising puts these plans into action. As noted by
Cronjé, Du Toit, and Motlatla (2003), organising involves developing mechanisms to execute
strategies effectively. This means determining what activities will take place, which resources are
needed, and who will carry them out. This crucial step helps align daily operations with the
organisation’s strategic goals, ensuring everyone knows their responsibilities and how to fulfil them.
10.3.1 The Importance of Organising
Organising is crucial for several reasons:
 Facilitates Coordination: It ensures all parts of the organisation work together smoothly,
enhancing efficiency and effectiveness.
 Clear Communication Channels: Establishes definitive pathways for information flow,
crucial for maintaining operational clarity across the organisation.
 Defines Roles and Responsibilities: Ensures that every team member understands their
specific duties and how they contribute to the organisation’s objectives.
 Resource Allocation: Helps in the effective distribution of resources, ensuring that every
department has what it needs to function optimally,
 Fosters Relationships: Encourages strong bonds between different departments, and
between the organisation and its external partners, like suppliers and customers.
10.3.2 Organisational Structure
The tourism industry comprises various types of organisations, each requiring a tailored
organisational structure. For instance, the structure of a small guesthouse, where the owner might
handle multiple roles from bookings to marketing, differs significantly from that of a large hotel that
might have a broad management team including a general manager, department heads, and
specialised staff.

10.3.3 Determining the Type of Organisational Structure
Several factors influence the type of organisational structure suitable for a tourism business:
 Business Size and Scope: Larger businesses often require a more complex structure to
manage various functions and operations.
 Technology and Environment: The level of technology used and the environmental context
in which the business operates can dictate organisational needs.
 Corporate Culture and Staffing: The values of the organisation and the number and type of
employees also shape the structure.
 Business Strategy: The organisation’s past activities and future growth plans determine how
it should be structured for optimal performance.
By understanding these factors, tourism managers can better coordinate various functions within
the organisation and effectively guide the business towards achieving its goals.
10.4 Leading
Leadership in tourism management is about guiding others towards achieving the organisation's
goals. It involves empowering others, creating a clear vision, and making key decisions. Effective
leadership is recognised as a cornerstone of successful management, playing a pivotal role in
ensuring that the strategies developed during planning are executed properly.
10.4.1 Characteristics of Effective Leaders
Effective leaders in tourism need various qualities:
 Motivation: They inspire others to strive towards common goals.
 Empowerment: They equip their teams with the tools and information needed to succeed.

 Clear Communication: They set clear tasks and expectations.
 Acknowledgment: They recognise and reward team achievements.
 Integrity and Honesty: They display genuine and ethical behaviour
 Adaptability: They are flexible and responsive to changes and challenges.
 Conflict Management: They handle disputes effectively and maintain team harmony.
These traits help leaders not only inspire but also maintain a motivated and cohesive team.
10.4.2 Motivation
Motivation is crucial in the tourism sector, particularly because of its dynamic nature. A motivated
leader not only pursues personal goals but also inspires their team to achieve collective objectives.
They need to understand what drives each team member to perform their best, which in turn helps
the entire organisation to succeed.
10.4.3 Communication
Strong communication skills are essential in tourism management. Effective communication goes
beyond mere information exchange; it involves making sure that messages are not only delivered
but also understood and implemented effectively. This is especially critical in South Africa, where
the diverse cultural landscape and multiple official languages add layers of complexity to
communication processes. Leaders must ensure that communication is clear, direct, and tailored to
the audience to prevent misunderstandings that could lead to operational inefficiencies or customer
dissatisfaction.
Example 1: Effective Communication
Imagine a guest at a hotel reports an issue. How quickly and effectively the staff communicate the
resolution to this issue can significantly impact the guest's experience and satisfaction. Effective
communication in such scenarios ensures that problems are resolved efficiently, improving the
overall guest experience.
In the tourism industry, where interactions often happen under fast-paced and sometimes stressful
conditions, the ability to communicate calmly and persuasively can make a significant difference in
service delivery. Effective communication also fosters an environment of trust and cooperation,
which are essential for achieving the organisation's goals and enhancing the overall guest
experience. This involves not only verbal interactions but also non-verbal cues, written instructions,
and digital communications, each tailored to suit the situation and the audience's needs.
By mastering these communication skills, tourism leaders can better navigate the complexities of
the industry, leading to improved team performance and increased customer satisfaction.

10.5 Control
Control is a fundamental management function used to ensure that the activities of a tourism
organisation meet its goals. It involves setting performance standards, monitoring actual
performance, and taking corrective actions when necessary. This systematic process helps to close
any gaps between what is planned and what is actually happening.
10.5.1 The Importance of Control
Control is crucial in tourism management for several reasons:
 Goal Alignment: It verifies that all activities within the organisation contribute effectively
towards achieving its objectives.
 Adaptability: It allows the organisation to adjust to changes in the business environment by
continually assessing performance against set goals.
 Efficiency: Proper control systems enable managers to delegate tasks with confidence,
knowing that these tasks are monitored and will align with organisational goals.
 Problem Identification: Regular monitoring and feedback enable early detection of issues,
allowing for timely corrective actions.
 Risk Reduction: Systematic control reduces errors and minimises costs and delays,
enhancing the overall efficiency of operations.
 Guest Satisfaction: It ensures that all operations work towards providing an exceptional
visitor experience by consistently meeting guest expectations.
10.5.2 The Control Process
Effective control within a tourism organisation involves several key steps:
 Determine Performance Standards: Standards are set during the planning stage. These
should be clear, measurable, and aligned with the organisation’s goals.
 Monitor Actual Performance: Regularly check the actual performance against the
established standards. This can involve financial reports, customer feedback, and operational
assessments.
 Compare Performance with Standards: Evaluate if the performance meets, exceeds, or
falls below the expected standards.
 Take Corrective Action: If discrepancies are found, implement corrective measures to align
performance with goals. This might include training employees, adjusting strategies, or
revising processes

10.5.3 Control Methods
Various methods are used to control operations in a tourism organisation:
 Budgets and Financial Reports: Monitor financial health and resource allocation.
 Statistical Data: Analyse data to identify trends and patterns that can guide decision-making.
 Personal Observation and Interviews: Gain direct insights into daily operations and
employee satisfaction.
 Reports by Department Heads: Regular updates from each department help track progress
and address issues promptly.
10.6 Levels of management
In most organisations, management is structured into three primary levels: top, middle, and lower
management. Each level plays a distinct role in the hierarchy and contributes uniquely to the
organisation's success.
10.6.1 Top Management
Top management includes senior-level executives such as the CEO, Managing Director, and Board
of Directors. These leaders are responsible for setting the organisation's vision and mission. They
engage in long-term strategic planning and make high-level decisions that guide the entire
organisation towards its goals.
10.6.2 Middle Management
Middle management consists of managers like Branch Managers, Division Managers, and
Department Heads, such as Financial Managers and Marketing Managers. This level focuses on
interpreting the strategies set by top management and developing plans and policies to implement
them. They are crucial for translating high-level strategies into actionable tasks that can be
performed by teams.
