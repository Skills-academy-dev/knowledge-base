INTRODUCTION

This module introduces the basic ideas of business finance. It helps you understand what

finance is, why it is important for businesses, and how financial decisions are made. You

will also learn about different business structures, the importance of financial ethics, and

the role of good governance. This module lays the foundation for understanding more

complex finance topics later in the course.

Unit 1.1 What Is Finance?

This unit explains what finance means in the business world. It describes how finance is

used to manage money, plan for the future, and support business goals. You will explore

the basic idea of financial management and why finance is essential in both personal and

professional settings.

Read pages 10—13 of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

1.1.1 Definition of Finance

Explains finance as the study and management of money, including earning, saving,

spending, and investing.

1.1.2 The Objectives of Finance

Describes goals of personal and business finance like saving, planning, reducing

risks, and growing wealth.

1.1.3 Finance in Personal vs. Business Context

Compares how finance is used differently by individuals and businesses, such as in

budgeting and investment.

Unit 1.2: The Role of Finance in a Business

Finance helps a business to grow, survive, and make smart decisions. This unit shows how

finance supports different areas like planning, budgeting, investment, and operations. It

also explains how finance helps a business to stay successful in the long term.

Read pages 13—16 of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

1.2.1 Supporting Daily Operations

Describes how businesses use money to pay for expenses like rent, wages, and

stock.

INTRODUCTION

Every business must decide how to spend its money wisely, especially when it involves

large amounts for long-term goals. These big financial decisions are known as capital

investment decisions. This module introduces the concept of capital budgeting—a tool used

to decide whether a project is worth investing in. You’ll  learn how to calculate and evaluate

different investment options, estimate cash flows, and assess potential risks. Real

examples from South African businesses are included to show how to apply these tools in

real life.

Unit 4.1: Introduction to Capital Budgeting

Before a business spends money on big purchases like equipment or new services, it must

check whether the investment is worth it. This unit introduces capital budgeting and

explains how it helps businesses make smart choices that support long-term growth.

Read pages 101—104 of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

4.1.1 Definition and Purpose

Introduces capital budgeting as the process of planning and deciding on long-term

investments.

4.1.2 Capital vs. Operating Expenses

Explains the difference between once-off investment costs and everyday business

expenses.

Unit 4.2: Investment Appraisal Techniques

Not all investments are good ones. This unit introduces simple but powerful methods for

checking whether an investment is likely to bring profit. You’ll  explore tools like Payback

Period, Net Present Value (NPV), Internal Rate of Return (IRR), and Accounting Rate of

Return (ARR)

Read pages 105—107 of your textbook for more information on the topics below:

4.2.1 Payback Period

Measures how long it takes for an investment to pay back the money spent.

Integrated Business Finance | Study Guide | Bellview Institute Publishing

27

4.2.2 Net Present Value (NPV)

Calculates the current value of future cash flows to decide if an investment is

profitable.

4.2.3 Internal Rate of Return (IRR)

Shows the interest rate an investment earns over time, used to compare options.
