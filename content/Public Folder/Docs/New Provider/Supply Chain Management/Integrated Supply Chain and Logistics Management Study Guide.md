How to Use This Study Guide

This Study Guide helps you study Integrated Supply Chain and Logistics Management in a

7 Study Tips

Studying Integrated Supply Chain and Logistics Management can be rewarding when you

use the right strategies. These tips will help you stay organised, understand the content clearly,

and prepare confidently for your assessments.

1.  Set clear study goals.

Decide which module or unit you want to complete each week to stay focused and

manage your time effectively.

2.  Break the work into smaller parts.

Study one concept at a time, such as customer service, inventory control, supply chain

planning, or logistics activities.

3.  Rewrite key ideas in your own words.

Making your own notes helps you remember important supply chain concepts and

improves understanding.

4.  Create quick summary sheets.

Write down key terms, diagrams, and main ideas from each module to support quick

revision.

5.  Practice with questions and case studies.

Use the exercises and case studies in the textbook to test your understanding and

prepare for assessments.

6.  Use real-life business examples.

Think about how organisations manage supply chains, logistics, and customer service to

connect theory to practice.

7.  Revise regularly.

Short, consistent study sessions help you remember more than last-minute studying.

Using these tips consistently will help you stay focused and build confidence in Integrated

Supply Chain and Logistics Management.

Introduction

This module introduces the basic concepts of logistics and supply chain management. It

explains how goods move from suppliers to customers and why coordination between

businesses is important. The module uses simple explanations and South African examples to

help you understand how supply chains operate in real life.

Refer to pages 10 of the textbook for more details

Unit 1: Overview of Logistics and Supply Chain Management

This unit provides an overview of logistics, supply chains, and supply chain management. It

explains how products move through different stages before reaching customers and why

managing these activities effectively is important.

1.1 Meaning of Logistics

Logistics focuses on moving and storing goods so that products reach the right place at the right

time.

1.2 Meaning of a Supply Chain

A supply chain describes the full journey of a product from raw materials to the final customer.

1.3 Supply Chain Management

Supply chain management involves planning and controlling all supply chain activities to meet

customer needs efficiently.

1.4 Flow of Goods, Information and Funds

This section explains how products move physically, how information is shared, and how money

flows through the supply chain.

1.5 Why Logistics and Supply Chain Management Are Important

Logistics and supply chain management help businesses reduce costs, improve delivery, and

satisfy customers.

1.6 Logistics and Supply Chains in Everyday Life

This section links supply chains to everyday products and local business examples.

1.7 Key Takeaways

The unit reinforces the basic roles of logistics, supply chains, and supply chain management.

Unit 2: Supply Chain Systems and Network Design

This unit focuses on how supply chains operate through connected systems and networks. It

explains how businesses plan, organise, and coordinate supply chain activities to ensure the

smooth flow of goods, information, and funds between different organisations.

2.1 Supply Chain Systems

Supply chain systems describe the organised way in which supply chain activities are planned

and managed so that all parts of the supply chain work together effectively.

2.2 Supply Chain Networks

Supply chain networks explain how businesses are connected through multiple suppliers,

transport routes, warehouses, and customers rather than operating in a simple linear chain.

2.3 Network Design in Supply Chains

Network design refers to decisions about where to locate suppliers, warehouses, and transport

routes to reduce costs and improve service levels.

2.4 Information Flow in Supply Chain Systems

This section highlights the importance of sharing accurate information, such as order details and

stock levels, to support planning and coordination across the supply chain.

2.5 Supply Chain Systems in Local and Emerging Markets

This section briefly considers how supply chain systems help businesses manage challenges

common in South Africa and other emerging markets, such as infrastructure limitations and

transport delays.

2.6 Key Takeaways

The unit reinforces how supply chain systems and networks support coordination, flexibility, and

efficient operations.

Unit 3: Flow of Goods, Information and Funds

This unit explains the three main flows that operate within a supply chain: goods, information,

and funds. Understanding how these flows work together helps businesses coordinate activities,

reduce delays, and improve overall supply chain performance.

3.1 Flow of Goods

The flow of goods refers to the physical movement of products from suppliers to customers

through production, storage, and distribution stages.

3.2 Flow of Information

The flow of information involves sharing data such as customer orders, stock levels, delivery

schedules, and demand forecasts to support planning and coordination.

3.3 Flow of Funds

The flow of funds refers to the movement of money through the supply chain, including

payments from customers to businesses and payments to suppliers and service providers.

3.4 Importance of Managing Supply Chain Flows

Managing these flows effectively helps reduce delays, control costs, improve visibility, and

ensure that customer needs are met.

3.5 Key Takeaways

The unit summarises how the flow of goods, information, and funds must work together to

support efficient and reliable supply chain operations.

Refer to pages 19–21 of the textbook for more details

Introduction

Managing supply chain costs is essential for business success. Costs such as transport,

storage, and handling can be high if not carefully controlled, while performance levels must

remain strong to satisfy customers. This module introduces the key types of supply chain costs

and explains how performance is measured and improved, with reference to South African

business environments.

Refer to page 58 of the textbook for more details

Unit 1: Components of Supply Chain Costs

This unit explains the different types of costs involved in moving products from suppliers to

customers. It highlights why identifying and managing these costs is important for improving

efficiency and profitability.

1.1 Meaning of Supply Chain Costs

Supply chain costs include all expenses related to sourcing, producing, storing, transporting,

and delivering goods.

1.2 Transport Costs

Transport costs relate to moving goods between locations and include fuel, vehicle

maintenance, labour, and insurance.

1.3 Warehousing and Storage Costs

These costs arise from storing goods and include warehouse rental, utilities, security,

equipment, and labour.

1.4 Inventory Holding Costs

Inventory holding costs refer to the cost of keeping stock over time, including storage,

insurance, risk of damage, and obsolete stock.

1.5 Administrative and Handling Costs

Administrative and handling costs relate to managing supply chain activities, such as order

processing, documentation, and goods handling.
